My First Million Podcast: Best Episodes & Business Ideas (2026)

Published March 13, 2026 | Reading time: 9 min

My First Million has become essential listening for entrepreneurs, aspiring founders, and business enthusiasts. Hosts Shaan Puri and Sam Parr brainstorm business ideas, analyze successful companies, and interview founders with refreshing honesty and genuine curiosity. Their chemistry, pattern recognition, and willingness to share both wins and failures make every episode valuable for anyone building businesses.

Meet the Hosts

Shaan Puri

Former head of product at Twitch, sold Blab to Amazon, angel investor. Known for creative business ideas and enthusiasm for new ventures.

Sam Parr

Founded The Hustle (sold to HubSpot for $27M), Hampton community creator. Direct, practical, focused on execution over theory.

Essential My First Million Episodes

1. "Business Idea: AI Resume Builder" (E412)

Why It's Essential: Perfect example of how Shaan and Sam spot opportunities in existing markets using new technology. They break down market size, competition, and go-to-market strategy in real-time.

Key Lessons: Opportunity sizing, AI application to mundane tasks, speed to market

Best Quote: "The best businesses solve boring problems with new technology."

2. "How Andrew Wilkinson Built a $1B+ Holding Company" (E201)

Why It's Essential: Andrew Wilkinson breaks down his holding company strategy, buying boring businesses, and living a great life while building wealth. Masterclass in alternative paths to wealth.

Key Lessons: Holding company model, buying vs building, lifestyle design

Best Quote: "I wanted Berkshire Hathaway for the internet age."

3. "Shaan's $10M Side Hustle" (E324)

Why It's Essential: Shaan reveals his newsletter business Hampton/Milk Road story with actual numbers. Rare transparency on building and exiting a content business.

Key Lessons: Newsletter economics, audience building, acquisition strategies

Best Quote: "Newsletters are the best business model you've never heard of."

4. "The Trends Business Model" (E178)

Why It's Essential: Sam explains how The Hustle's Trends product worked, what subscribers paid for, and why it was valuable. Blueprint for premium content businesses.

Key Lessons: Premium content pricing, community value, information arbitrage

Best Quote: "People will pay for pattern recognition and time savings."

5. "Brainstorming 50 Business Ideas" (E299)

Why It's Essential: Pure idea generation episode showing their thought process. Even if you don't build these exact ideas, you learn how to spot opportunities.

Key Lessons: Idea generation frameworks, market analysis, validation approaches

Best Quote: "Ideas are worthless without execution, but execution without ideas is just hard work."

Recurring Segments Worth Following

"Shaan Bets" & "Sam Bets"

What Makes MFM Unique

1. Real Numbers

Unlike most business podcasts, Shaan and Sam share actual revenue figures, costs, and margins when discussing businesses.

2. Pattern Recognition

They've seen enough businesses to identify patterns—what works, what's saturated, where opportunity exists.

3. Authentic Friendship

Their genuine friendship creates chemistry. They challenge each other, joke around, and think out loud.

4. Action-Oriented

Every episode includes ideas you could actually start building. Not just theory or inspiration—actionable opportunities.

The MFM Framework for Evaluating Ideas

Market Size Questions:

  • How many potential customers exist?
  • What's the total addressable market (TAM)?
  • Is the market growing or shrinking?

Competition Analysis:

  • Who are the incumbents?
  • Are they vulnerable?
  • Can you be 10x better or different?

Execution Difficulty:

  • How long to MVP?
  • Capital requirements?
  • Skills needed?

Personal Fit:

  • Would you enjoy this daily?
  • Do you have unfair advantages?
  • Can you commit for 2-3 years?

Learning from MFM

For Aspiring Entrepreneurs:

Focus on episodes about starting with limited capital, service businesses, and content creation. Learn how to build before raising money.

For Active Founders:

Listen to growth strategy episodes, founder interviews, and market breakdowns. Pattern match your business against theirs.

For Investors:

Pay attention to market analysis, trend predictions, and company breakdowns. They often spot trends early.

Common Themes Across Episodes

1. Boring Businesses Win

The most profitable businesses often solve mundane problems nobody talks about. Waste management, HVAC services, boring SaaS tools.

2. Speed Beats Perfection

Shipping quickly and iterating beats planning forever. Test ideas fast, kill losers, double down on winners.

3. Audience is Currency

Building an audience (email, social, community) creates optionality. Every business becomes easier with distribution.

4. Copycats Can Win

Don't need novel ideas—executing better on existing concepts often works. Look at successful models in other markets.

How to Listen to MFM Effectively

Take Action Notes:

For each episode, write down:

  • 1 business idea to research further
  • 1 tactic to implement this week
  • 1 person/company to study

Follow Up on Ideas:

When an idea resonates, spend 2-3 hours researching it. Many listeners have built the businesses Shaan and Sam brainstormed.

Join the Community:

MFM listeners congregate on Reddit (r/myfirstmillion), Twitter (#MFMpod), and various Discord servers. Compare notes and collaborate.

MFM's Evolution

Early Days (2019-2020):

Scrappy energy, lots of brainstorming, less polish. Great for raw idea generation.

Growth Phase (2021-2022):

Better guests, more founder interviews, refined format. Production quality improved.

Current Era (2023-2026):

High-profile guests, deeper company analyses, more personal stories. Matured while keeping authenticity.

Criticisms and Limitations

Success Bias:

Shaan and Sam have exits and wealth—their advice may not apply to true beginners or different contexts.

Idea Overload:

So many ideas that listeners can become paralyzed by choice rather than executing on one.

Execution Gap:

Ideas sound easier than they are. Remember: Shaan and Sam have experience, networks, and capital.

Final Thoughts

My First Million stands out in business podcasting for its authenticity, idea density, and actionability. Shaan and Sam don't gate-keep—they share the playbook, actual numbers, and honest assessments.

The show's value comes from their genuine friendship, complementary skills, and willingness to think out loud. You're not listening to a polished production—you're overhearing two successful entrepreneurs brainstorming and analyzing opportunities.

Whether you're building your first business, scaling an existing one, or just fascinated by entrepreneurship, MFM offers weekly MBA-level education disguised as friendly conversation.

Start with an episode on a topic you care about. Take notes. Act on one idea. That's how MFM listeners turn podcast episodes into actual businesses.
