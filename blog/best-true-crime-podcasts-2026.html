Best True Crime Podcasts: 20 Gripping Shows (2026)

True crime podcasts dominate the charts for a reason—they're addictive, compelling, and often deliver justice or closure to unsolved cases. From investigative journalism to storytelling to comedy-meets-murder, here are the best true crime podcasts.

Best True Crime Podcasts Overview

1. Serial

Best for: Deep-dive investigative journalism

The podcast that launched the true crime boom. Sarah Koenig investigates one case per season with obsessive detail. Season 1 (Adnan Syed) is legendary.

2. My Favorite Murder

Best for: Comedy + true crime

Karen Kilgariff and Georgia Hardstark tell murder stories with dark humor and personal anecdotes. "Stay sexy and don't get murdered." Massive fanbase ("Murderinos").

3. Criminal

Best for: Short, compelling crime stories (30 min)

Phoebe Judge's soothing voice tells fascinating stories about crime, justice, and people caught in extraordinary circumstances. Not always murder—sometimes heists, cons, or legal oddities.

4. Casefile True Crime

Best for: Serious, no-frills investigative storytelling

Anonymous Australian host delivers meticulously researched cases with no banter or comedy. Just the facts, brilliantly told. International cases.

5. Dateline NBC

Best for: TV-quality true crime investigations

NBC's legendary show in podcast form. Keith Morrison's voice, gripping storytelling, and the full resources of a major network.

6. Morbid: A True Crime Podcast

Best for: Detailed research with personality

Autopsy technician Alaina and hairdresser Ash combine deep research with conversational storytelling. Long episodes (2-3 hours) with humor and empathy.

7. Crime Junkie

Best for: Weekly true crime with consistent format

Ashley Flowers delivers a new case every Monday. Clean storytelling, respectful to victims, and reliably engaging. Controversy over sourcing, but still hugely popular.

8. Bear Brook

Best for: DNA genealogy and cold cases

Investigative journalist Jason Moon unravels a cold case using cutting-edge DNA technology. Season 1 is a masterclass in investigative podcasting.

9. In the Dark

Best for: Investigative journalism exposing injustice

APM Reports investigates cases where the justice system failed. Season 1: Jacob Wetterling case. Season 2: Curtis Flowers wrongful conviction. Season 3: police accountability.

10. Dr. Death

Best for: Medical malpractice thrillers

Wondery's series on dangerous doctors. Season 1 follows neurosurgeon Christopher Duntsch, who maimed 33 patients. Terrifying and gripping.

11. Sword and Scale

Best for: Dark, unflinching true crime

Mike Boudet presents the darkest crimes with 911 calls, interrogations, and courtroom audio. Warning: extremely graphic and disturbing content.

12. S-Town

Best for: Southern Gothic mystery

From the Serial team, Brian Reed investigates a murder in small-town Alabama. What starts as a crime story becomes something much deeper and stranger.

13. The Teacher's Pet

Best for: Australian true crime investigation

Journalist Hedley Thomas investigates the 1982 disappearance of Lynette Dawson. The podcast helped reopen the case and led to a conviction.

14. Up and Vanished

Best for: Real-time cold case investigations

Payne Lindsey investigates unsolved disappearances while recording the podcast. Season 1 (Tara Grinstead) led to arrests and convictions.

15. RedHanded

Best for: British humor + international cases

Suruthi Bala and Hannah Maguire cover true crime from around the world with research, dark humor, and British wit. Well-researched and entertaining.

16. True Crime Garage

Best for: Beer-drinking buddies discussing cases

Nic and The Captain discuss true crime while drinking craft beer in Nic's garage. Conversational, detailed, and covers lesser-known cases.

17. Someone Knows Something

Best for: Canadian investigative journalism

CBC's David Ridgen investigates unsolved Canadian disappearances. Immersive, empathetic, and beautifully produced.

18. Your Own Backyard

Best for: Obsessive local investigation

Chris Lambert investigates the 1996 disappearance of Kristin Smart from Cal Poly. The podcast reignited interest and led to arrests 25 years later.

19. 48 Hours

Best for: CBS investigative reporting

CBS's long-running show in podcast form. High production values, thorough investigations, and decades of archive material.

20. They Walk Among Us

Best for: UK true crime

British true crime covering UK cases with respect, detail, and no sensationalism. Perfect for fans of British crime documentaries.

How to Choose the Right True Crime Podcast

For investigative depth:

  • Serial (one case, entire season)
  • Bear Brook (DNA genealogy)
  • In the Dark (wrongful convictions)
  • S-Town (mystery in the South)

For comedy + true crime:

  • My Favorite Murder (original comedy-crime blend)
  • Morbid (research + personality)
  • RedHanded (British humor)

For serious, respectful storytelling:

  • Casefile (no-frills, international)
  • Criminal (short, diverse stories)
  • Someone Knows Something (empathetic Canadian journalism)

For weekly new cases:

  • Crime Junkie (Mondays)
  • Dateline NBC (ongoing series)
  • Morbid (2x weekly)

For dark, graphic content:

  • Sword and Scale (911 calls, interrogations)
  • True Crime Garage (detailed, nothing off-limits)

For podcasts that solved cases:

  • The Teacher's Pet (led to conviction)
  • Up and Vanished (Season 1 led to arrests)
  • Your Own Backyard (reignited investigation, arrests)

True Crime Ethics

Good true crime podcasts:

  • Center victims, not glorify killers
  • Do original research or properly credit sources
  • Show respect for families affected
  • Provide context and avoid sensationalism

Avoid podcasts that exploit tragedy for entertainment without care or research.

Final Thoughts

True crime podcasts satisfy our curiosity about human nature, justice, and mystery. The best ones balance compelling storytelling with respect for victims and rigorous research.

Start with Serial Season 1 or Criminal if you're new to true crime. Once you're hooked, branch into investigative series (Bear Brook, In the Dark) or weekly shows (Crime Junkie, Casefile).

Warning: True crime can be addictive and emotionally heavy. Balance with lighter content.
