SmartLess vs Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend: Which Comedy Podcast Is Better?

SmartLess and Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend are two of the funniest podcasts alive — both hosted by beloved comedians, both packed with celebrity guests. But they're very different listening experiences. Here's everything you need to know to pick the right one (or binge both).

🎙️ SmartLess

What It Is

SmartLess launched in 2020 and became an instant hit. The concept is deceptively simple: Jason Bateman, Sean Hayes, and Will Arnett take turns surprising each other — and the listener — with a mystery guest. Nobody knows who's coming until they show up. The result is genuine, unscripted reactions from three of Hollywood's funniest people.

Strengths

  • The reveal format: The surprise guest hook is brilliant. You never know if it'll be a comedian, a rock star, or a sitting President — and the hosts don't either.
  • Three-way chemistry: Bateman, Hayes, and Arnett have been friends for decades. Their banter feels lived-in and effortless.
  • A-list guests: Bradley Cooper, Paul McCartney, Barack Obama, Reese Witherspoon, Tom Hanks — the guest list reads like the Oscar after-party.
  • Genuine warmth: Unlike many celebrity podcasts, SmartLess feels like three friends being nice to someone they're excited to see.
  • Consistently funny: The hosts constantly roast each other. The guest is almost secondary to the three of them riffing.

Weaknesses

  • Doesn't go deep: SmartLess is not a place for profound conversations. It's fun and surface-level by design.
  • Three hosts can crowd the guest: Sometimes the guest barely gets a word in while the hosts riff off each other.
  • Celebrity-bubble: Topics stay safely Hollywood: movies, TV, family, favorite vacations. Don't expect controversial opinions.
  • Inconsistent episodes: The show is only as good as the guest chemistry. Some guests click; some don't.

Best Episodes to Start

  • Paul McCartney: Surreal. Three guys who grew up idolizing the Beatles ask him everything.
  • Barack Obama: Surprisingly funny and warm. Obama gives as good as he gets.
  • Bradley Cooper: Chaotic. Cooper and the hosts have known each other forever and it shows.
  • Jennifer Aniston: One of the most listened-to episodes — pure star power and chemistry.

Who Should Listen

✅ Hollywood fans who love celebrity gossip done warmly
✅ People who want easy laughs without heavy topics
✅ Fans of Jason Bateman, Sean Hayes, or Will Arnett
✅ Anyone who misses the feeling of eavesdropping on a great dinner party

🧠 Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend

What It Is

After leaving TBS in 2021 and ending a 28-year TV run, Conan O'Brien leaned fully into podcasting — and turned it into one of the medium's funniest shows. Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend features Conan interviewing a guest while his producer Sona Movsesian and writer Matt Gourley serve as perfect foils. The premise — that Conan is desperately searching for real friends — is the running joke that never gets old.

Strengths

  • Conan is elite: He may be the funniest interviewer alive. His ability to find the absurd angle in any story is unmatched.
  • The Sona dynamic: Sona mercilessly mocks Conan to his face while he pretends to be horrified. It's a perfect bit that never wears out.
  • Varied guest list: Conan books comedians, athletes, musicians, and actors. Less Hollywood royalty than SmartLess, more eclectic mix.
  • Self-deprecating genius: Conan makes himself the butt of the joke constantly — his failed friendship quest, his pale skin, his Irish Catholic guilt.
  • Running gags pay off: Long-time listeners are rewarded with callbacks and evolving bits across episodes.
  • Shorter premium segments: "Conan O'Brien Needs a Fan" episodes feature regular people, which are often the funniest of all.

Weaknesses

  • Conan-centric: If you don't love Conan's specific brand of humor, this won't work for you. There's no escaping his voice.
  • Lightweight on depth: Like SmartLess, this isn't a place for serious conversations. It's a comedy show that happens to have guests.
  • Some premium content is paywalled: The best bonus segments require a subscription.
  • Acquired taste: Conan's humor is very specific — Harvard intellectual meets vaudeville — and not everyone gets it immediately.

Best Episodes to Start

  • Bill Burr: Two comedians who genuinely respect each other. Electric chemistry.
  • Norm Macdonald (recorded before his death): A masterclass in two comedians who communicate in pure subtext.
  • Niall Horan: Surprisingly hilarious. Conan's attempts to bond with a pop star are wonderful.
  • Seth Rogen: Two Hollywood veterans getting deeply silly about everything.
  • Any "Conan O'Brien Needs a Fan" episode: Conan calls superfans and the results are always unexpected gold.

Who Should Listen

✅ Conan fans who miss him on TV
✅ People who love absurdist, self-deprecating comedy
✅ Listeners who appreciate comedic craft and timing
✅ Anyone who enjoys when the host is funnier than the guest

🥊 Head-to-Head

Humor Style

SmartLess: Warm, goofy, lovably chaotic. Three friends horsing around.
Conan: Dry, absurdist, intellectually silly. One genius making everyone his straight man.
Winner: Tie — completely different styles, both excellent.

Guest Quality

SmartLess: Consistently A-list. Paul McCartney and Barack Obama are hard to top.
Conan: More eclectic — sometimes bigger, sometimes more niche, always interesting.
Winner: SmartLess — pure star power.

Host Entertainment Value

SmartLess: Three great comedians who elevate each other.
Conan: One singular comedic mind who needs no help.
Winner: Conan O'Brien — purely as a solo performer, Conan is one of a kind.

Consistency

SmartLess: Varies by guest — great guests = great episodes, dull guests = dull episodes.
Conan: More consistent because Conan himself carries every episode regardless of the guest.
Winner: Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend

Listenability While Multitasking

Both win here. Neither requires full attention. Both are perfect for commutes, gym sessions, or cooking.

Replay Value

SmartLess: Episodes are fun but don't hold up as well on repeat.
Conan: Running gags and callbacks reward long-term listeners.
Winner: Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend

🎯 Which One Should You Choose?

Choose SmartLess if you want:

  • The biggest celebrity guests in Hollywood
  • Three-way comedy chemistry and playful chaos
  • Warm, feel-good entertainment with no edge
  • Episodes where the guest is a genuine star
  • Easy listening that anyone in the room will enjoy

Choose Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend if you want:

  • A singular comedic voice with decades of craft behind it
  • Absurdist humor and self-deprecating wit
  • A show that rewards loyal listeners with callbacks and running bits
  • Conan's unique perspective on fame, failure, and friendship
  • Episodes where the host is funnier than the guest

Why Not Both?

SmartLess and Conan complement each other perfectly:

  • SmartLess: Star power and three-way chaos
  • Conan: Comedic depth and singular wit

Listen to SmartLess when you want to feel like you're at a Hollywood party. Listen to Conan when you want to laugh at something truly original.

✨ The Verdict

For most people: Start with SmartLess. The celebrity guests are bigger, the format is instantly accessible, and you'll be laughing within five minutes.

For comedy nerds and longtime Conan fans: Go straight to Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend. Conan's comedic craft is something you won't find anywhere else in podcasting.

For the full experience: Listen to both. They're both short enough to fit one of each into a single commuting week. You won't regret it.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is SmartLess better than Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend?

It depends on what you want. SmartLess is funnier and more chaotic with three A-list hosts surprising each other with guests. Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend is more consistent and conversational, with Conan's unique brand of absurdist wit. Both are excellent — many listeners enjoy both.

How long are SmartLess episodes?

SmartLess episodes typically run 50–70 minutes. They're longer than most comedy podcasts due to the three-host chemistry and extended guest banter.

How long are Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend episodes?

Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend episodes run 45–75 minutes. There's usually a main guest interview plus shorter Conan bits with his producer Sona and sidekick Matt Gourley.

Which comedy podcast is better for long drives?

Both are excellent road trip companions. SmartLess episodes feel like eavesdropping on a celebrity party. Conan rewards attention with callback jokes and running gags. Either will make the miles disappear.
