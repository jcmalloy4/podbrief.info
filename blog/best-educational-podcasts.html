Best Educational Podcasts (2026)

Turn your commute, workout, or chores into a learning opportunity. These podcasts will make you smarter, more curious, and better informed—all while doing other things.

🧪 Science & Technology

Huberman Lab

Topic: Neuroscience, health, performance
Why it's great: Dr. Andrew Huberman breaks down complex brain science into actionable protocols. Learn how your body actually works.

Best for: Anyone who wants to optimize health and performance

Radiolab

Topic: Science, philosophy, human experience
Why it's great: Mind-bending stories that explore big questions through science. Beautifully produced, endlessly fascinating.

Best for: Curious minds who love "wait, what?" moments

Ologies

Host: Alie Ward
Topic: Every -ology you can imagine
Why it's great: Interviews with experts in obscure fields. Learn about everything from volcanology to forensic anthropology.

Best for: People who want to explore niche topics

StarTalk

Host: Neil deGrasse Tyson
Topic: Astrophysics, space, science
Why it's great: Neil makes space science accessible and fun. Combines education with humor.

Best for: Space nerds and casual science fans alike

The Ezra Klein Show

Topic: Politics, philosophy, technology, ideas
Why it's great: Deep conversations about how the world works. Ezra asks the questions you wish you'd thought of.

Best for: Big-picture thinkers

📚 History

Hardcore History

Host: Dan Carlin
Why it's great: Epic 3-6 hour deep dives into historical events. World War I, Genghis Khan, the fall of Rome—told like thriller novels.

Best for: History buffs who want immersive storytelling

Revolutions

Host: Mike Duncan
Why it's great: Complete chronological histories of major revolutions (American, French, Russian, etc.). Ridiculously well-researched.

Best for: People who want to understand how societies change

The History of Rome

Host: Mike Duncan
Why it's great: 179 episodes covering the entire history of Rome. Completed series you can binge.

Best for: Rome enthusiasts and ancient history lovers

Throughline

Host: NPR
Why it's great: Connects current events to their historical roots. "Why is X happening?" answered through history.

Best for: Understanding the present through the past

💰 Economics & Business

Planet Money

Host: NPR
Why it's great: Economics made fun. Complex topics explained through real-world stories.

Best for: Anyone who thought economics was boring

Freakonomics Radio

Host: Stephen Dubner
Why it's great: "The hidden side of everything." Economics applied to unexpected topics.

Best for: People who love counterintuitive thinking

How I Built This

Host: Guy Raz
Why it's great: Learn business principles through founder stories. Airbnb, Instagram, Patagonia—how iconic companies were built.

Best for: Entrepreneurs and business students

The Indicator

Host: NPR
Length: 10 minutes
Why it's great: Daily economics explainer. Short, digestible, relevant.

Best for: Staying economically literate in 10 minutes

🧠 Philosophy & Ideas

The Knowledge Project

Host: Shane Parrish
Why it's great: Mental models for better thinking. Learn how to think, not just what to think.

Best for: Decision-makers and lifelong learners

Philosophize This!

Host: Stephen West
Why it's great: Entire history of philosophy made accessible. From Socrates to postmodernism.

Best for: Philosophy beginners and veterans

Very Bad Wizards

Hosts: A psychologist and a philosopher
Why it's great: Psychology meets philosophy. Deep topics with humor.

Best for: People who like debates and nuance

🌍 Culture & Society

99% Invisible

Host: Roman Mars
Topic: Design, architecture, hidden aspects of everyday life
Why it's great: Makes you notice things you never noticed before.

Best for: Curious observers of the built world

Hidden Brain

Host: Shankar Vedantam
Topic: Psychology, human behavior
Why it's great: Why we do what we do, explained through science and stories.

Best for: Understanding yourself and others

Stuff You Should Know

Hosts: Josh & Chuck
Topic: Everything
Why it's great: "How X works" on every topic imaginable. Relaxed, conversational, endlessly informative.

Best for: Generalists who love trivia

🗣️ Language & Communication

Lexicon Valley

Topic: Language, linguistics, etymology
Why it's great: For word nerds. How language evolves and why we talk the way we do.

The Allusionist

Host: Helen Zaltzman
Topic: Words, language, etymology
Why it's great: Short episodes on the history and quirks of language.

📖 Literature & Storytelling

The New Yorker: Fiction

Why it's great: Authors read and discuss short stories from The New Yorker archives.

LeVar Burton Reads

Host: LeVar Burton
Why it's great: Short fiction read by the most soothing voice in existence. Educational and relaxing.

🎓 Multi-Topic Learning

TED Talks Daily

Length: 5-20 minutes
Why it's great: Bite-sized ideas from brilliant people. Quick learning hits.

The Tim Ferriss Show

Why it's great: World-class performers across all fields. Learn from the best at everything.

Lex Fridman Podcast

Topic: AI, science, philosophy, life
Why it's great: Deep conversations with the smartest people alive.

🚀 How to Learn from Podcasts Effectively

Active Listening

Take notes during episodes. Write down ideas, facts, or follow-up research.

Spaced Repetition

Re-listen to favorite episodes months later. Reinforces learning.

Apply What You Learn

Listening alone isn't learning. Use the information, share it, teach it.

Curate Your Queue

Don't subscribe to everything. Pick 3-5 educational shows and go deep.

Adjust Speed

Educational podcasts work great at 1.5x-2x speed. Absorb more in less time.

💡 Learning Hacks from Educational Podcasts

The Feynman Technique (Huberman Lab)

If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it. Test yourself by teaching.

Spaced Learning (Various)

Don't binge 10 episodes in a day. Space them out for better retention.

Cross-Domain Learning (Tim Ferriss)

Lessons from one field apply to others. Connect ideas across domains.

Question Everything (Radiolab)

The best learning starts with curiosity. Ask "why?" and "how?" constantly.
