Best History Podcasts 2026

Published March 11, 2026

History podcasts bring the past to life through storytelling, expert analysis, and deep research. Whether you're fascinated by ancient Rome, World War II, or forgotten historical moments, these podcasts make history engaging and accessible.

Why Listen to History Podcasts?

  • Context for Today: Understand current events through historical patterns
  • Engaging Storytelling: History told as compelling narratives, not dry facts
  • Expert Insights: Learn from historians, professors, and researchers
  • Forgotten Stories: Discover lesser-known events that shaped our world

Top History Podcasts

Hardcore History

Dan Carlin's legendary podcast delivers epic, multi-hour deep dives into historical events. From the fall of the Roman Republic to the Eastern Front of WWII, Dan's theatrical storytelling and meticulous research create audiobook-length masterpieces. Episodes can run 4-6 hours but are absolutely gripping.

Revolutions

Mike Duncan chronicles major revolutions throughout history—from the English Civil War to the Russian Revolution. Each multi-episode series builds context, explores key figures, and analyzes what drives revolutionary change. Essential for understanding how societies transform.

The History of Rome

Mike Duncan's completed 179-episode series covers Roman history from the founding of the city to the fall of the Western Empire. The definitive audio history of Rome, beloved by history buffs worldwide.

Throughline

NPR's history podcast connects past events to present-day issues. Each episode traces the historical roots of current topics like voting rights, immigration, or social movements. Perfect for understanding the "why" behind today's news.

The Rest Is History

Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook discuss everything from ancient empires to modern politics with wit and scholarship. Engaging conversations that make history fun and accessible, covering listener questions and current events through a historical lens.

Fall of Civilizations

Paul Cooper explores the collapse of great civilizations—from the Maya to Easter Island, Bronze Age societies to African kingdoms. Beautifully produced with immersive sound design, these episodes examine why powerful societies fall.

Our Fake History

Sebastian Major investigates historical myths, legends, and misconceptions. Did Vikings really have horned helmets? Was Robin Hood real? Entertaining deep dives into what we think we know about history versus what actually happened.

The History Unplugged Podcast

Scott Rank interviews authors and historians about their latest research. Covers all eras and regions, from ancient Egypt to Cold War espionage, with expert guests sharing new discoveries and interpretations.

In Our Time: History

BBC Radio 4's Melvyn Bragg hosts discussions with academics on historical topics. 45-minute episodes covering specific events, movements, or figures with scholarly depth made accessible.

You Must Remember This

Karina Longworth uncovers the secret and forgotten history of Hollywood's first century. Deep dives into old Hollywood scandals, the blacklist era, and how the film industry shaped American culture.

History Podcast Categories

Ancient History

The Ancient World and The History of Ancient Greece cover classical civilizations. The History of Egypt Podcast explores pharaohs, pyramids, and ancient Egyptian society.

Military History

Hardcore History (WWI, WWII series), The History of WWII Podcast, and Lions Led By Donkeys examine wars, battles, and military strategy.

American History

BackStory, The History of American Slavery, and Presidential (Washington Post) explore U.S. history from colonization to modern day.

British History

The British History Podcast covers from ancient Britain to modern times. Rex Factor ranks English monarchs while The History of England tells the national story.

World History

The History of the World and The Oldest Stories explore global human history across continents and millennia.

Biographical

Noble Blood explores royal families and dynasties. History's Most covers famous and infamous historical figures.

What Makes a Great History Podcast?

  • Thorough Research: Based on primary sources, academic work, and expert consultation
  • Engaging Narrative: History told as story, not textbook
  • Context & Analysis: Not just what happened, but why it mattered
  • Accessibility: Complex events explained clearly for general audiences
  • Production Quality: Good sound, pacing, and sometimes dramatic elements

Popular Historical Eras

Ancient World (3000 BCE - 500 CE)

Egypt, Greece, Rome, Persia, China—the foundations of civilization, philosophy, and empire.

Medieval Period (500-1500 CE)

Knights, castles, the Crusades, the Black Death, and the rise of European kingdoms.

Early Modern (1500-1800)

Renaissance, Reformation, Age of Exploration, Enlightenment, and colonial empires.

Modern Era (1800-Present)

Industrial Revolution, world wars, Cold War, decolonization, and recent history.

How to Choose the Right History Podcast

  • Time Period: Ancient, medieval, modern, or specific eras?
  • Geographic Focus: European, American, Asian, African, or global?
  • Format: Epic multi-hour narratives or bite-sized episodes?
  • Depth: Academic rigor or entertaining overview?
  • Tone: Serious scholarship or entertaining conversation?

Learning from History Podcasts

  • Understand patterns that repeat across civilizations
  • See how past decisions shape present realities
  • Learn from humanity's successes and failures
  • Gain perspective on current events
  • Discover stories that inspire, warn, or fascinate

Best for Beginners

New to history podcasts? Start with these accessible shows:

  • Throughline: Short, relevant to current events
  • The Rest Is History: Conversational and entertaining
  • Our Fake History: Fun approach to common misconceptions
  • Fall of Civilizations: Beautifully produced, self-contained episodes
