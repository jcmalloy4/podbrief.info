Best History Podcasts 2026 - World History, American History & More

March 12, 2026 • 11 min read

History podcasts bring the past to life with expert storytelling, deep research, and engaging narratives. Whether you're fascinated by ancient Rome, World War II, or the American Revolution, there's a podcast for you.

Here are the best history podcasts in 2026, organized by era and style so you can find exactly what interests you.

Best Overall History Podcasts

1. Hardcore History

Host: Dan Carlin
Length: 3-6 hours per episode (sometimes longer)
Frequency: Sporadic (3-4 times per year)
Best for: Epic, immersive historical narratives

What makes it great: Dan Carlin is the gold standard of history podcasting. He spends months researching and produces cinematic audio experiences. Topics range from WWI ("Blueprint for Armageddon") to the Mongol invasions ("Wrath of the Khans"). Each series is a masterclass in storytelling.

Listen if: You want the absolute best history podcast, regardless of length. Prepare to be captivated for hours.

2. The Rest Is History

Hosts: Tom Holland, Dominic Sandbrook
Length: 45-60 minutes
Frequency: 3-4x per week
Best for: British historians discussing everything

What makes it great: Two acclaimed British historians banter about historical topics with wit and expertise. They cover everything from ancient Greece to modern politics, often in multi-part series. Accessible yet intellectual.

Listen if: You want regular, high-quality history content with British charm.

3. Revolutions

Host: Mike Duncan
Length: 30-45 minutes
Frequency: Completed series (listen to back catalog)
Best for: Understanding how revolutions unfold

What makes it great: Duncan covers major revolutions chronologically: English, American, French, Haitian, Latin American, and more. Each revolution gets 20-50 episodes. You'll understand the patterns, personalities, and consequences of revolutionary change.

Listen if: You want to deeply understand transformative historical moments.

Ancient & Classical History

4. The History of Rome

Host: Mike Duncan
Length: 20-30 minutes
Frequency: Completed series (179 episodes total)
Best for: Comprehensive Roman history

What makes it great: The podcast that launched Duncan's career. He covers Rome from its legendary founding through the fall of the Western Empire. Clear, engaging, and remarkably comprehensive.

Listen if: You want to understand Rome chronologically from start to finish.

5. The Ancient World

Host: Scott C.
Length: 30-45 minutes
Frequency: Irregular
Best for: Bronze Age civilizations

What makes it great: Deep dives into ancient Mesopotamia, Egypt, and the Bronze Age collapse. Scholarly but accessible.

Listen if: You're fascinated by the earliest civilizations.

World War II

6. WW2 Podcast by Ray Harris Jr.

Host: Ray Harris Jr.
Length: 45-60 minutes
Frequency: Weekly
Best for: Comprehensive WWII coverage

What makes it great: Harris has been covering WWII chronologically for over a decade. Incredibly detailed, covering military, political, and social history across all theaters of war.

Listen if: You want to understand WWII in exhaustive detail.

7. The History of WWII Podcast

Host: Various
Length: 30-45 minutes
Frequency: Weekly
Best for: Thematic WWII episodes

What makes it great: Covers specific battles, campaigns, and themes rather than strict chronology. Great for dipping in on topics that interest you.

Listen if: You want focused episodes on specific WWII topics.

American History

8. The Dollop

Hosts: Dave Anthony, Gareth Reynolds
Length: 60-90 minutes
Frequency: Weekly
Best for: Comedic American history

What makes it great: One comedian reads a story from American history to another who has no idea what it's about. Hilarious reactions to bizarre historical events. Educational and entertaining.

Listen if: You want to laugh while learning about weird American history.

9. American History Tellers

Host: Lindsay Graham
Length: 45-60 minutes
Frequency: Weekly
Best for: Narrative American history series

What makes it great: Each season covers a specific American event or era (Prohibition, Space Race, Wild West, etc.) in 4-8 episodes. Immersive storytelling with dramatized segments.

Listen if: You want engaging American history in digestible series.

10. Backstory

Hosts: Various historians
Length: 45 minutes
Frequency: Weekly
Best for: Connecting past to present

What makes it great: Historians explore the historical roots of current events and issues. Each episode takes a modern topic and traces it through American history.

Listen if: You want to understand how history shapes today.

British & European History

11. The British History Podcast

Host: Jamie Jeffers
Length: 30-45 minutes
Frequency: Weekly
Best for: Comprehensive British history

What makes it great: Covers British history chronologically from the Stone Age to modern times. Over 400 episodes and counting. Thorough, well-researched, and engaging.

Listen if: You want to deeply understand British history.

12. Rex Factor

Hosts: Graham Duke, Ali Hood
Length: 60-90 minutes
Frequency: Bi-weekly
Best for: British monarchs ranked and reviewed

What makes it great: Two friends review and rate every British monarch. Funny, informative, and surprisingly educational. They score each king or queen on various factors.

Listen if: You want to learn about British royalty while being entertained.

Specialty & Thematic History

13. Fall of Civilizations

Host: Paul Cooper
Length: 2-4 hours
Frequency: Every few months
Best for: How great civilizations collapsed

What makes it great: Beautifully produced episodes on civilizations that fell (Rome, Maya, Bronze Age, etc.). Cooper's narration is haunting and poetic. Each episode is a complete, cinematic experience.

Listen if: You're fascinated by why civilizations fail.

14. You're Dead to Me

Host: Greg Jenner
Length: 45 minutes
Frequency: Weekly
Best for: Comedy + history

What makes it great: Historian and comedian discuss historical topics with humor and expertise. BBC production values. Accessible and fun.

Listen if: You want history that makes you laugh.

15. Noble Blood

Host: Dana Schwartz
Length: 30-40 minutes
Frequency: Weekly
Best for: Royal scandals and intrigue

What makes it great: Each episode tells the dramatic story of a royal figure. Murders, betrayals, scandals—history's juiciest royal stories told with flair.

Listen if: You love royal drama and palace intrigue.

Short-Form History

16. Stuff You Missed in History Class

Hosts: Holly Frey, Tracy V. Wilson
Length: 30-45 minutes
Frequency: 2-3x per week
Best for: Diverse historical topics

What makes it great: Covers overlooked historical events, people, and topics from all eras and regions. Excellent research and clear presentation.

Listen if: You want variety and don't want to commit to long series.

17. 15 Minute History

Hosts: Various
Length: 15-20 minutes
Frequency: Weekly
Best for: Quick historical lessons

What makes it great: University of Texas professors discuss historical topics in bite-sized episodes. Perfect for commutes.

Listen if: You want quality history content but don't have much time.

How to Choose Your History Podcasts

If You're New to History Podcasts

Start with:

  • The Rest Is History — Entertaining and varied
  • Stuff You Missed in History Class — Accessible and diverse
  • You're Dead to Me — Fun and educational

If You Love Deep Dives

Go for:

  • Hardcore History — The deepest dives
  • Revolutions — Comprehensive series
  • Fall of Civilizations — Epic narratives

If You Want Chronological Coverage

Choose:

  • The History of Rome — Rome from start to finish
  • The British History Podcast — Britain from Stone Age
  • WW2 Podcast — WWII chronologically

If You Want Comedy + Learning

Try:

  • The Dollop — Hilarious American history
  • Rex Factor — British monarchs ranked
  • You're Dead to Me — BBC comedy history

Building Your History Podcast Routine

The Commute Stack

  • Short commute (15-30 min): Stuff You Missed in History Class, 15 Minute History
  • Medium commute (30-60 min): The Rest Is History, American History Tellers
  • Long commute (60+ min): Hardcore History, Fall of Civilizations

The Deep Learning Plan

  1. Pick an era or topic you're curious about
  2. Find a podcast that covers it comprehensively
  3. Commit to listening through the whole series
  4. You'll emerge with genuine expertise

The Variety Approach

  • Subscribe to 3-4 podcasts covering different eras
  • Pick episodes based on topics that interest you
  • Don't feel pressure to finish every series

Tips for History Podcast Listeners

Take Notes

Keep a notes app for:

  • Key dates and names
  • Books or topics to explore further
  • Connections between different episodes

Follow Up with Reading

Most history podcasts include:

  • Show notes with book recommendations
  • Primary sources and further reading
  • Maps and images on websites

Start with What Interests You

Don't force yourself through topics you find boring. History is vast—find what excites you:

  • Love battles? Focus on military history
  • Fascinated by culture? Try civilization podcasts
  • Into politics? Revolutions and rulers
  • Like drama? Royal scandals and intrigue

The Best Long-Form Series to Binge

Blueprint for Armageddon (Hardcore History)

6 episodes, ~24 hours total — WWI from start to finish. The definitive podcast on the Great War.

The French Revolution (Revolutions)

55 episodes — The most important revolution in history, covered in meticulous detail.

The History of Rome (Complete Series)

179 episodes — The entire Roman story. Perfect for a year-long educational journey.

Why Listen to History Podcasts?

  • Context for current events: History doesn't repeat, but it rhymes
  • Better decision-making: Learn from humanity's mistakes and successes
  • Cultural literacy: Understand references and allusions
  • Perspective: Your problems feel smaller when you understand human struggle across millennia
  • Entertainment: Real history is often more dramatic than fiction
