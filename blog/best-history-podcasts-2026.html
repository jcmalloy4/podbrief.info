Best History Podcasts 2026 - Top Historical Shows

Published March 6, 2026 | 8 min read

History comes alive through audio storytelling. Whether you're fascinated by ancient Rome, World War II, or American history, these podcasts deliver engaging narratives backed by expert research.

Why Listen to History Podcasts?

History podcasts offer:

  • Deep dives: Go beyond textbook summaries
  • Expert analysis: Learn from historians and researchers
  • Engaging storytelling: History as narrative, not just facts
  • Diverse perspectives: Explore untold stories and overlooked events
  • Context for today: Understand how the past shapes the present

Top History Podcasts of 2026

1. Hardcore History (Dan Carlin)

Why it's great: Epic multi-part series on major historical events. Carlin's theatrical style and exhaustive research create immersive experiences.

Best for: Deep dives into wars, empires, and pivotal moments

Episode length: 2-6 hours (seriously)

Popular episodes: Blueprint for Armageddon (WWI), Wrath of the Khans (Mongol Empire), King of Kings (Persian Empire)

2. The Rest Is History (Tom Holland & Dominic Sandbrook)

Why it's great: Two British historians discuss everything from ancient civilizations to modern pop culture with wit and depth.

Best for: Accessible history with humor and insight

Episode length: 45-60 minutes

Topics covered: Ancient Rome, Cold War, British royalty, historical figures

3. Revolutions (Mike Duncan)

Why it's great: Comprehensive series covering major world revolutions from the English Civil War to the Russian Revolution.

Best for: Understanding how societies transform

Episode length: 30-45 minutes

Completed series: English, American, French, Haitian, Latin American, and Russian revolutions

4. The History of Rome (Mike Duncan)

Why it's great: Complete 179-episode journey through Roman history from founding to fall. The gold standard for historical podcasting.

Best for: Rome enthusiasts and ancient history fans

Episode length: 20-30 minutes

Coverage: 753 BC to 476 AD

5. Fall of Civilizations (Paul Cooper)

Why it's great: Beautiful production exploring how and why great civilizations collapsed. Haunting music and storytelling.

Best for: Ancient and medieval history lovers

Episode length: 2-3 hours

Civilizations covered: Maya, Easter Island, Aztecs, Byzantine Empire, Sumerians

6. In Our Time: History (BBC Radio 4)

Why it's great: Melvyn Bragg and expert guests discuss historical topics in depth. Academic yet accessible.

Best for: Broad historical coverage with expert analysis

Episode length: 45 minutes

Topics: Everything from Vikings to the Silk Road

7. Throughline (NPR)

Why it's great: Connects past to present, showing how history explains current events. Excellent production values.

Best for: Understanding historical context of modern issues

Episode length: 30-40 minutes

Approach: Investigative journalism meets history

8. The British History Podcast (Jamie Jeffers)

Why it's great: Comprehensive chronological history of Britain from ancient times. Over 400 episodes and counting.

Best for: British history enthusiasts

Episode length: 30-45 minutes

Coverage: Pre-Roman Britain to modern era (ongoing)

9. You're Dead to Me (BBC)

Why it's great: Comedian Greg Jenner brings historians and comedians together for funny, educational discussions.

Best for: History with humor

Episode length: 30-40 minutes

Style: Educational comedy about historical figures and events

10. American History Tellers (Wondery)

Why it's great: Narrative storytelling covering American history events in multi-part series. Immersive and dramatic.

Best for: American history focused listening

Episode length: 30-40 minutes

Series covered: Prohibition, Space Race, Wild West, Civil War

  • The Ancient World - Egypt, Greece, Mesopotamia

  • Cold War Conversations - Post-WWII to 1991

Tips for Getting Started

1. Start with Your Interest

Don't feel obligated to start with ancient history. Jump into whatever period fascinates you most.

2. Try Different Styles

  • Academic: In Our Time
  • Narrative: Fall of Civilizations
  • Conversational: The Rest Is History
  • Comedy: You're Dead to Me

3. Listen at Your Own Pace

Many history podcasts have long episodes or extensive back catalogs. It's not a race—enjoy the journey.

4. Take Notes

Consider keeping a history journal to remember key facts, figures, and connections you discover.

Why These Podcasts Stand Out

Expert Hosts

Many history podcast hosts are trained historians (Mike Duncan, Tom Holland, Dominic Sandbrook) or serious researchers (Dan Carlin) who bring academic rigor to accessible formats.

Production Quality

Top history podcasts invest in sound design, music, and editing that enhance storytelling without overwhelming content.

Primary Sources

The best shows cite sources, quote historical documents, and distinguish between fact and interpretation.

Narrative Arc

Great history podcasting isn't just reciting facts—it's telling stories with characters, conflicts, and consequences.

Learn History Through Podcasts

History podcasts make the past accessible, entertaining, and relevant. Whether you're commuting, exercising, or relaxing at home, these shows transform how you understand human civilization.

From Dan Carlin's epic narratives to the BBC's academic discussions, there's a history podcast for every interest and learning style. Start with one episode, and you might find yourself hooked on historical storytelling.
