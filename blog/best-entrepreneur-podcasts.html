Best Podcasts for Entrepreneurs: 15 Must-Listen Shows (2026)

Building a business? These podcasts share real strategies from founders who've actually done it—no motivational fluff, just practical insights.

What Makes a Great Entrepreneur Podcast?

Most "entrepreneur" podcasts fall into two traps:

  • Too motivational: "Hustle harder!" without actionable advice
  • Too tactical: Deep dives into growth hacks that don't scale

The best entrepreneur podcasts balance inspiration with execution—real founders sharing what actually worked (and what failed).

Best Overall Entrepreneur Podcasts

1. My First Million

Hosts: Shaan Puri, Sam Parr

Format: 45-60 min, 3x per week

Why it's great: Two founders brainstorm business ideas and share real revenue numbers. No BS, just "here's what's working right now."

Best for: Solopreneurs, bootstrappers, idea generators

Key topics: Business ideas, content, marketing, acquisitions

Browse My First Million summaries →

2. How I Built This

Host: Guy Raz

Format: 45-60 min, weekly

Why it's great: Deep-dive interviews with founders of Airbnb, Spanx, Instagram, Patagonia, and more. Stories of how iconic companies started.

Best for: Inspiration, long-term thinking, understanding founder journeys

Key topics: Origin stories, pivots, fundraising, scaling

Browse How I Built This summaries →

3. All-In Podcast

Hosts: Chamath Palihapitiya, Jason Calacanis, David Sacks, David Friedberg

Format: 90-120 min, weekly

Why it's great: Four Silicon Valley insiders debate tech, markets, startups, and the future. Brutally honest takes.

Best for: Tech founders, VCs, understanding macro trends

Key topics: Tech, investing, markets, AI, regulation

Browse All-In summaries →

4. Acquired

Hosts: Ben Gilbert, David Rosenthal

Format: 2-4 hours, every 3-4 weeks

Why it's great: Deep business history lessons. Episodes on Nike, NVIDIA, Costco, Microsoft—understanding how companies actually built moats.

Best for: Strategy, long-form learning, business model analysis

Key topics: Company histories, acquisitions, business strategy

Browse Acquired summaries →

5. The Tim Ferriss Show

Host: Tim Ferriss

Format: 2-3 hours, 2-3x per week

Why it's great: Tim deconstructs world-class performers—founders, investors, athletes, artists—to find patterns in success.

Best for: Systems thinking, productivity, mental models

Key topics: Habits, investing, health, decision-making

Browse Tim Ferriss summaries →

Best for Early-Stage Founders

6. Lenny's Podcast

Host: Lenny Rachitsky

Format: 60-90 min, weekly

Why it's great: Product, growth, and career advice from top PMs and founders. Extremely tactical.

Best for: Product managers, startup founders, growth leads

Key topics: Product-market fit, growth loops, hiring, metrics

7. The Knowledge Project

Host: Shane Parrish

Format: 60-90 min, weekly

Why it's great: Mental models and decision-making frameworks from top thinkers.

Best for: First principles thinking, avoiding mistakes

Key topics: Decision-making, learning, cognitive biases

8. StartUp Podcast

Hosts: Various (Gimlet)

Format: 25-40 min, seasonal

Why it's great: Documentary-style storytelling about what it's really like to start a company—including the hard parts.

Best for: Reality check, emotional preparation

Key topics: Fundraising, failure, co-founder dynamics

Best for SaaS Founders

9. SaaStr Podcast

Host: Harry Stebbings, Jason Lemkin

Format: 20-30 min, daily

Why it's great: Quick, tactical SaaS insights from CEOs and VCs.

Best for: B2B SaaS, scaling, sales

Key topics: ARR growth, sales, customer success

10. Nathan Latka Show

Host: Nathan Latka

Format: 15-20 min, daily

Why it's great: Nathan asks founders to share exact revenue, churn, and CAC numbers. No fluff.

Best for: Benchmarking your startup's metrics

Key topics: Revenue, metrics, funding, exits

Best for Content & Marketing

11. Marketing Against the Grain

Hosts: Kipp Bodnar, Kieran Flanagan

Format: 30-40 min, weekly

Why it's great: HubSpot CMOs discuss what's actually working in marketing today (not 5 years ago).

Best for: Marketers, content creators, growth hackers

Key topics: SEO, content, distribution, trends

12. The Colin and Samir Show

Hosts: Colin Rosenblum, Samir Chaudry

Format: 40-60 min, weekly

Why it's great: Inside look at the creator economy—how YouTubers, podcasters, and influencers build businesses.

Best for: Creator entrepreneurs, personal brands

Key topics: YouTube, monetization, audience building

Best for Bootstrappers

13. Indie Hackers

Host: Courtland Allen

Format: 60-90 min, weekly

Why it's great: Bootstrapped founders share revenue, lessons, and growth strategies. No VC talk.

Best for: Solopreneurs, indie makers, bootstrappers

Key topics: MRR, profitable businesses, side projects

14. The Bootstrapped Founder

Host: Arvid Kahl

Format: 20-40 min, weekly

Why it's great: Practical advice for building profitable businesses without VC funding.

Best for: Solo founders, micropreneurs

Key topics: Audience building, selling, exit strategies

Best for Investment Thinking

15. Invest Like the Best

Host: Patrick O'Shaughnessy

Format: 60-90 min, weekly

Why it's great: Conversations with top investors about frameworks, markets, and long-term thinking.

Best for: Understanding capital allocation, market trends

Key topics: Investing, business models, frameworks

How to Listen Effectively

Don't try to listen to everything. Pick 3-5 shows that match your current stage:

  • Pre-launch: How I Built This, StartUp, Indie Hackers
  • 0-$10k MRR: My First Million, Lenny's Podcast, The Bootstrapped Founder
  • $10k-$100k MRR: SaaStr, Nathan Latka, All-In
  • $100k+ MRR: Acquired, Invest Like the Best, All-In

Read summaries first: Not every episode is relevant. Browse AI-powered summaries to find episodes worth your time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Listening for motivation instead of execution: Action beats inspiration.
  • Following advice from people who haven't done it: Learn from founders who've built what you want to build.
  • Trying to implement everything: Pick 1-2 tactics per month max.
  • Not taking notes: Great insights are useless if you forget them.
