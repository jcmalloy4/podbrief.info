Best Science Podcasts of 2026

Published February 17, 2026 · 12 min read

Science podcasts have evolved far beyond dry lectures and academic jargon. Today's best science shows are riveting audio experiences — mixing original research, vivid storytelling, expert interviews, and genuine curiosity to make everything from quantum mechanics to evolutionary biology feel urgent, relevant, and endlessly fascinating. Whether you're a professional scientist, a lifelong learner, or someone who just wants to understand the world a little better, there has never been a richer landscape of science podcasting to explore.

Here are the 10 best science podcasts of 2026 — shows that will genuinely change how you think about the universe, your brain, and everything in between. You can find episode summaries for all of these shows at PodBrief.

The 10 Best Science Podcasts of 2026

1. Radiolab

Produced by WNYC and hosted by Lulu Miller and Latif Nasser, Radiolab is simply the finest science and philosophy podcast ever made. Since its founding by Jad Abumrad in 2002, the show has set the standard for how audio can make complex scientific ideas visceral, emotional, and profound. Episodes tackle questions that sit at the intersection of science, philosophy, and human experience — from the nature of time and consciousness to the ethics of gene editing and the biology of color. The sound design is extraordinary: Radiolab uses music, tape, and layered audio to create immersive sonic essays that you feel as much as hear. Every episode is a masterpiece of its form.

Why listen: The gold standard of science storytelling — deeply researched, beautifully produced, and capable of making you see the world in an entirely new way.

2. Science Vs

Hosted by Wendy Zukerman for Spotify/Gimlet, Science Vs takes on the internet's most contentious health and science claims and puts them through the rigorous filter of actual peer-reviewed research. Is intermittent fasting actually effective? Do gun control laws save lives? Can you really "boost" your immune system? Zukerman's forensic approach — talking to researchers on multiple sides of each issue, reading the primary literature, and following the data wherever it leads — is both refreshing and genuinely useful. Science Vs is an antidote to misinformation and a model for evidence-based thinking about everyday life.

Why listen: Rigorous fact-checking of popular health and science claims — the show that tells you what the evidence actually says, not what you want to hear.

3. Ologies

Hosted by comedian and science communicator Alie Ward, Ologies has a delightfully simple premise: every episode interviews a scientist about their specific "ology" — from volcanology to thanatology to hippology (the study of horses). What makes Ologies exceptional is Alie's genuine curiosity, warmth, and ability to make even the most esoteric scientific disciplines feel exciting and accessible. The format means you can dip in and out based on whatever topic catches your eye, and the sheer breadth of disciplines covered — well over 400 episodes — means there's literally something for everyone. It's also genuinely funny, which helps.

Why listen: Endlessly varied, genuinely funny, and extraordinarily good at making you care about scientific fields you'd never thought about before.

Find Ologies episode summaries on PodBrief →

4. The Infinite Monkey Cage

BBC Radio 4's The Infinite Monkey Cage, hosted by physicist Brian Cox and comedian Robin Ince, is the rare science show that achieves genuine comedy without sacrificing intellectual rigor. Recorded live in front of an audience with rotating celebrity and expert guests, the show tackles everything from dark matter and parallel universes to the science of music and the origins of life. Cox's deep expertise combined with Ince's philosophical bent and sharp wit creates a chemistry that makes even the most daunting scientific concepts feel approachable and entertaining. A perennial favorite of British science communication and an international hit.

Why listen: Science comedy at its finest — Brian Cox and Robin Ince make physics and philosophy genuinely laugh-out-loud funny without dumbing anything down.

5. Huberman Lab

Stanford neuroscientist Andrew Huberman's Huberman Lab podcast has become one of the most influential health and science shows of the decade — a sprawling, deeply evidence-based resource for understanding how your brain and body actually work. Episodes run 2–3 hours on focused topics: sleep optimization, the neuroscience of dopamine, protocols for building strength, the biology of stress, and much more. What sets Huberman apart from typical wellness content is his commitment to citing actual research and explaining the mechanisms behind every recommendation. Whether you follow all his protocols or just want to understand the science, Huberman Lab is essential listening.

Why listen: The most comprehensive and evidence-based health and neuroscience podcast available — actionable protocols backed by rigorous science.

6. StarTalk

Astrophysicist Neil deGrasse Tyson brings the cosmos down to earth on StarTalk, his long-running science podcast that blends astronomy, physics, and cosmology with pop culture, comedy, and celebrity guests. Tyson's gift is his ability to make the incomprehensibly large — galaxy formation, black holes, the Big Bang — feel immediate and relevant to everyday life. Co-hosted with various comedians who ask the questions a layperson would ask, StarTalk keeps the conversation grounded even when the topics are literally universal. It's a perfect gateway show for people who want to understand the universe but find traditional science content intimidating.

Why listen: Neil deGrasse Tyson's infectious enthusiasm makes astrophysics and cosmology genuinely accessible and exciting for any listener.

Find StarTalk episode summaries on PodBrief →

7. Hidden Brain

NPR's Hidden Brain, hosted by Shankar Vedantam, explores the unconscious patterns that drive human behavior — the invisible psychological forces shaping our decisions, biases, relationships, and perceptions. Drawing on behavioral economics, social psychology, neuroscience, and cognitive science, Vedantam crafts episodes that are simultaneously intellectually rigorous and personally revelatory. Why do we make irrational decisions? How does our environment shape our identity? What does science tell us about grief, love, creativity, and purpose? Hidden Brain answers these questions in ways that feel immediately applicable to your own life. One of the most consistently excellent podcasts in any genre.

Why listen: Mind-expanding psychological science delivered with extraordinary storytelling — the show that reveals the hidden forces shaping every decision you make.

Find Hidden Brain episode summaries on PodBrief →

8. Skeptoid

Brian Dunning's Skeptoid is the most rigorous debunking podcast in existence — a 15-minute weekly show that has been systematically dismantling pseudoscience, conspiracy theories, urban legends, and misinformation since 2006. With over 900 episodes in its archive, Skeptoid has covered everything from homeopathy and chemtrails to ancient alien theories and haunted houses, applying the same calm, evidence-based methodology to each topic. Dunning cites his sources extensively, acknowledges uncertainty where it exists, and never talks down to his audience. In an era of rampant misinformation, Skeptoid is an invaluable public health resource for critical thinking.

Why listen: The definitive skeptical podcast — short, thoroughly researched, and an essential tool for thinking clearly in a world full of misinformation.

9. Freakonomics Radio

Stephen Dubner's Freakonomics Radio applies economic and social science thinking to questions nobody thought to ask — exploring the hidden side of everything from the economics of sleep deprivation to why cities are healthier than suburbs to what actually works in education reform. Based on the wildly popular book series co-authored with Steven Levitt, the podcast has evolved well beyond its origins to become one of the most intellectually adventurous and entertaining shows in any category. Every episode challenges assumptions, surfaces counterintuitive data, and makes the social sciences feel as exciting as any thriller.

Why listen: Economics and social science applied to the most unexpected questions — the show that makes data fascinating and assumptions crumble.

Find Freakonomics Radio episode summaries on PodBrief →

10. Daniel and Jorge Explain the Universe

Physicist Daniel Whiteson and cartoonist Jorge Cham have created something genuinely rare: a physics podcast that's funny, accessible, and scientifically uncompromising all at once. Daniel and Jorge Explain the Universe tackles the genuinely hard questions in physics — dark matter, dark energy, quantum entanglement, the multiverse, the Higgs boson — and explains them with the patience and clarity that most physicists can't quite manage. Jorge's outsider perspective as a cartoonist rather than a scientist means he asks the questions a layperson would ask, and Daniel's answers are models of clear science communication. For anyone who wants to understand the universe without a physics degree, this is the show.

Why listen: The best physics podcast for non-physicists — genuinely funny, genuinely rigorous, and capable of making quantum mechanics feel like it makes sense.

Choosing the Right Science Podcast for You

The beauty of science podcasting in 2026 is that there's something for every level of prior knowledge and every learning style. Here's a quick guide:

  • Complete beginners: Start with StarTalk or Ologies — accessible, entertaining, and won't overwhelm you with jargon.
  • Health and self-improvement: Huberman Lab and Hidden Brain offer science directly applicable to your daily life.
  • Critical thinkers: Science Vs and Skeptoid are your weapons against misinformation.
  • Deep explorers: Radiolab and The Infinite Monkey Cage reward listeners who want to sit with big ideas.
  • Physics and cosmos: Daniel and Jorge Explain the Universe and StarTalk make the largest questions feel manageable.
  • Social science: Freakonomics Radio and Hidden Brain reveal the science behind human behavior and society.
