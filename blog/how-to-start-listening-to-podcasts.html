How to Start Listening to Podcasts - Complete Beginner's Guide

Podcasts are everywhere, but getting started can feel overwhelming. Here's everything you need to know to begin your podcast journey—from choosing an app to finding shows you'll actually love.

🎧 Step 1: Choose a Podcast App

iPhone/iPad: Apple Podcasts (Free, Built-in)

Pros: Already on your phone, easy to use, huge catalog
Cons: Basic features, no advanced customization
Best for: Complete beginners who want simple and free

Android: Google Podcasts or Spotify (Free)

Pros: Free, integrated with your existing apps
Cons: Google Podcasts is basic; Spotify mixes music and podcasts
Best for: Android users who want something free and familiar

Spotify (Free/Premium, All Platforms)

Pros: You probably already have it, combines music and podcasts, great recommendations
Cons: Some exclusive shows, ads unless you have Premium
Best for: People who already use Spotify for music

Overcast (Free, iOS)

Pros: Smart Speed (removes silence), Voice Boost, great queue management
Cons: iOS only, more advanced features
Best for: Power users who want control

Pocket Casts (Paid, All Platforms)

Pros: Beautiful design, cross-platform sync, advanced playback features
Cons: Costs money (worth it!)
Best for: Serious podcast listeners who want the best experience

Our Recommendation for Beginners: Start with whatever's already on your phone (Apple Podcasts or Spotify). Upgrade to Overcast or Pocket Casts later if you get serious.

🔍 Step 2: Find Shows You'll Love

Start with Popular Shows

These are popular for a reason—they're well-produced and broadly appealing:

  • The Daily (NYT) - News in 20 minutes
  • SmartLess - Celebrity interviews that are actually funny
  • Huberman Lab - Science of health and performance
  • How I Built This - Entrepreneur origin stories
  • Serial - True crime storytelling masterpiece

Browse by Topic

Think about what you're interested in:

  • News: The Daily, Up First, NPR News Now
  • True Crime: Serial, Criminal, My Favorite Murder
  • Business: How I Built This, Planet Money, The All-In Podcast
  • Comedy: SmartLess, Conan O'Brien Needs a Friend
  • Science: Huberman Lab, Radiolab, Ologies
  • Interviews: The Joe Rogan Experience, Lex Fridman Podcast, The Tim Ferriss Show

Use PodBrief to Preview Shows

Don't commit blindly. PodBrief gives you daily summaries of episodes so you can sample before subscribing.

▶️ Step 3: Start Listening

Don't Start with Episode 1

Most podcasts don't require you to start from the beginning. Start with:

  • The most recent episode
  • A "best of" or fan-favorite episode
  • An episode on a topic that interests you

Exception: Serialized shows like Serial or S-Town—these you should start from the beginning.

Give It 2-3 Episodes

One episode might be off. If you're not feeling it after 2-3 episodes, move on. There are millions of shows out there.

Use Playback Speed

Most podcast apps let you speed up playback:

  • 1.0x: Normal speed (start here)
  • 1.2x: Slightly faster, barely noticeable
  • 1.5x: Fast but still comfortable (most people end up here)
  • 2.0x+: For speed demons (try it!)

📱 Step 4: Subscribe to Shows You Like

What "Subscribe" Means

Subscribing is free. It just means new episodes automatically appear in your app. You're not signing up for anything or paying money.

How Many Shows Should You Subscribe To?

Start with 3-5 shows. You can always add more. Better to have a few you actually listen to than 50 you ignore.

Organize Your Subscriptions

  • Daily news: The Daily, Up First
  • Commute entertainment: SmartLess, Conan
  • Workout motivation: Jocko Podcast, Huberman Lab
  • Weekend deep dives: Joe Rogan, Lex Fridman

⏰ Step 5: Build a Listening Routine

When to Listen

  • Morning routine: News podcasts while making coffee
  • Commute: Interviews or entertainment
  • Workout: Motivational or educational content
  • Cooking/Cleaning: Light entertainment or learning
  • Before bed: Calming stories (see our sleep podcast guide)

Download Before You Go

Download episodes on WiFi before commuting or traveling. Saves data and prevents buffering.

💡 Pro Tips for Beginners

Skip Episodes That Don't Interest You

Just because you subscribe to a show doesn't mean you have to listen to every episode. Skip freely.

Use Episode Descriptions

Read the description before listening. Don't waste time on topics you're not interested in.

Create a Listening Queue

Most apps let you queue up episodes. Build a playlist for your commute or workout.

Don't Feel Obligated to Finish

If an episode isn't grabbing you, skip it. Life's too short for boring podcasts.

Explore Podcast Charts

Check "Top Charts" in your app to see what's trending. Often a great way to discover new shows.

❓ Common Beginner Questions

Are podcasts free?

Yes, 99% of podcasts are completely free. Some shows have premium bonus content, but the vast majority are free.

Do I need headphones?

Nope! You can listen on phone speakers, car speakers, smart speakers, or headphones—whatever works for you.

How long are podcast episodes?

It varies wildly:

  • News podcasts: 10-30 minutes
  • Interview shows: 45-90 minutes
  • Long-form interviews: 2-4 hours (Joe Rogan, Lex Fridman)

Can I listen offline?

Yes! Download episodes to your phone and listen anytime, anywhere—no internet required.

How often do new episodes come out?

Depends on the show:

  • Daily: The Daily, Up First
  • Weekly: Most interview shows (SmartLess, Huberman Lab)
  • Sporadic: Some shows release whenever they're ready

What if I don't like any popular podcasts?

That's fine! Popular doesn't mean good for you. Keep exploring until you find your niche.

🎯 Your First Week of Podcasts

Day 1: Download a podcast app (start with what's on your phone)
Day 2: Subscribe to 3 shows from different categories
Day 3: Listen to one episode during your commute or workout
Day 4: Try playback speed at 1.2x
Day 5: Download episodes for offline listening
Day 6: Explore a new show based on recommendations
Day 7: Build a queue of your favorite episodes

Podcasts are one of the best free resources for learning, entertainment, and staying informed. The hardest part is starting—after that, you'll wonder how you ever lived without them.
