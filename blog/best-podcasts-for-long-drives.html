Best Podcasts for Long Drives: 10 Shows for Road Trips (2026)

Planning a road trip? These podcasts will make the hours fly by—gripping stories, fascinating conversations, and binge-worthy series that keep you engaged mile after mile.

What Makes a Great Road Trip Podcast?

The best podcasts for long drives need:

  • Engaging storytelling: Keeps you hooked without needing to look at your phone
  • Right length: 45+ minutes (short podcasts require too much episode-switching)
  • Binge-ability: Multiple episodes so you don't run out mid-trip
  • Easy to follow: You might zone out occasionally—story should be forgiving
  • Good audio quality: Clear even with road noise

Best True Crime Podcasts for Road Trips

1. Serial

Host: Sarah Koenig

Format: Serialized investigative journalism

Episode Length: 40-60 minutes

Why it's perfect: The OG binge-able true crime podcast. Each season tells one story across multiple episodes. You'll want to keep driving to find out what happens next.

Best seasons for road trips:

  • Season 1: Adnan Syed murder case (12 episodes)
  • Season 2: Bowe Bergdahl story (11 episodes)
  • Season 3: Cleveland courthouse cases (8 episodes)

2. Dr. Death

Host: Laura Beil (Wondery)

Format: Narrative true crime series

Episode Length: 40-50 minutes

Why it's gripping: Tells the story of Dr. Christopher Duntsch, a neurosurgeon who maimed and killed patients. Each season is a complete story—perfect for a 5-8 hour drive.

Seasons:

  • Season 1: Dr. Christopher Duntsch (9 episodes)
  • Season 2: Dr. Farid Fata (8 episodes)
  • Season 3: Dr. Paolo Macchiarini (6 episodes)

3. Casefile True Crime

Host: Anonymous Australian narrator

Format: Case-by-case true crime

Episode Length: 40-90 minutes

Why it's great: Hundreds of episodes. The host's voice is calm and clear. Each case is self-contained but you'll want to binge them all.

Notable episodes for road trips:

  • Case 53: The Silk Road (3-part series)
  • Case 91: Amy Allwine
  • Case 100: Mark and John

Best Storytelling Podcasts for Long Drives

4. S-Town

Host: Brian Reed

Format: 7-episode limited series

Total Length: ~6.5 hours

Why it's perfect: A complete story that unfolds over exactly one long drive. Starts as a murder mystery in rural Alabama, becomes something much more complex and human.

Best for: 6-8 hour drives (you'll finish the whole series)

5. This American Life

Host: Ira Glass

Format: Weekly storytelling

Episode Length: 60 minutes

Why it's great: 25+ years of episodes. Each episode has 3 acts—natural breaks for gas station stops. Stories range from funny to heartbreaking.

Classic episodes for road trips:

  • #129: Cars
  • #220: Testosterone
  • #355: The Giant Pool of Money
  • #513: 129 Cars

6. Radiolab

Hosts: Jad Abumrad, Lulu Miller, Latif Nasser

Format: Science and philosophy storytelling

Episode Length: 30-60 minutes

Why it's perfect: Amazing sound design keeps you engaged. Episodes make you think without being exhausting. Great for conversations with passengers afterward.

Best episodes for road trips:

  • The Worst Thing
  • Darkode

  • 60 Words

How to Build the Perfect Road Trip Playlist

For a 3-4 hour drive:

  • 2-3 episodes of This American Life
  • OR: 1-2 long Joe Rogan/Lex Fridman episodes
  • OR: Start S-Town (finish on the way back)

For a 6-8 hour drive:

  • Full S-Town series (7 episodes)
  • OR: Dr. Death Season 1 (9 episodes)
  • OR: Serial Season 1 (12 episodes)
  • OR: 3-4 long interviews (Rogan, Lex, Tim Ferriss)

For a multi-day road trip:

  • Mix of genres: true crime, storytelling, interviews, comedy
  • Download 20-30 hours of content (you'll listen more than you think)
  • Include some "filler" shows you can skip if you're not feeling them

Pro Tips for Road Trip Podcast Listening

  • Pre-download everything: Don't rely on cell service in rural areas
  • Test audio quality at home: Some podcasts sound bad at highway speeds
  • Start with an attention-grabber: Save mellow podcasts for later
  • Bring aux cable or Bluetooth adapter: Don't rely on rental car tech
  • Download twice as much as you think: Better to have too much than too little
  • Sync across devices: If switching drivers, sync playback position

Podcasts to Avoid on Road Trips

  • Meditation/sleep podcasts: Obviously
  • Podcasts with visual components: "As you can see in the chart..."
  • Super dense technical content: Highway driving isn't the time to learn calculus
  • Anything requiring note-taking: Save educational content for desk time
  • Very short episodes (<20 min): Too much episode-switching

Match Podcast to Drive Type

Solo drive: Long interviews, true crime, anything you want

Drive with partner: Storytelling podcasts (This American Life, S-Town), comedy

Drive with kids: Family-friendly storytelling (avoid true crime)

Scenic drive: Save intense stories for boring highways—enjoy the views
