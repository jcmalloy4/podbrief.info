Best Music Podcasts 2026: For Fans, Musicians & Industry Professionals

March 4, 2026 10 min read

Music podcasts offer unique access to artists, deep dives into music history, industry insights, and discovery of new sounds. Whether you're a casual listener, aspiring musician, or industry professional, 2026's best music podcasts provide entertainment, education, and inspiration across every genre.

Why Music Podcasts Matter

Music podcasts serve multiple purposes:

  • Discovery: Find new artists and genres beyond algorithm recommendations
  • Context: Learn the stories behind your favorite songs and albums
  • Access: Hear directly from artists about their creative process
  • Education: Understand music theory, production, and industry dynamics
  • Community: Connect with fellow music lovers and professionals

Best Music Interview Podcasts

1. Song Exploder

Why it's great: Artists break down how they created specific songs, track by track. Intimate insights into the creative process from everyone from Radiohead to Taylor Swift.

Best for: Musicians, producers, and anyone curious about how songs are made

Episode length: 15-30 minutes

Notable episodes: "The National - Bloodbuzz Ohio," "Billie Eilish - when the party's over"

View Song Exploder summaries →

2. Broken Record

Why it's great: Rick Rubin, Malcolm Gladwell, and Bruce Headlam interview legendary musicians in deep, thoughtful conversations about creativity and music.

Best for: Fans of long-form music conversations and industry insights

Notable guests: Paul McCartney, Andre 3000, Questlove, Flea

View Broken Record summaries →

3. WTF with Marc Maron

Why it's great: While not exclusively music-focused, Maron's interviews with musicians are intimate, revealing, and emotionally honest. Often gets artists to open up like nowhere else.

Best for: Deep dives into musicians' personal lives and creative struggles

Notable episodes: Bruce Springsteen, Dave Grohl, Jack White

View WTF summaries →

Best Music History & Storytelling Podcasts

4. Disgraceland

Why it's great: True crime meets music history. Jake Brennan explores the wild, criminal, and outrageous stories behind music's greatest artists.

Best for: Music fans who love rock n' roll mythology and scandal

Notable episodes: "Jerry Lee Lewis," "Sam Cooke," "The Rolling Stones"

View Disgraceland summaries →

5. Lost Notes

Why it's great: KCRW's narrative series explores music history through forgotten stories, lost recordings, and overlooked moments. Beautifully produced.

Best for: Music history buffs who want lesser-known stories

Format: Seasonal narrative series

View Lost Notes summaries →

6. Dissect

Why it's great: Cole Cuchna takes one album per season and analyzes it track-by-track, exploring themes, influences, and cultural context. Academic-level analysis made accessible.

Best for: Fans who want to understand albums on a deeper level

Notable seasons: Kendrick Lamar's "To Pimp a Butterfly," Kanye West's "My Beautiful Dark Twisted Fantasy"

View Dissect summaries →

Best Music Industry & Business Podcasts

7. The Business of Fashion Podcast (Music Episodes)

Why it's great: While fashion-focused, many episodes explore the intersection of music and fashion with major artists and industry figures.

Best for: Understanding music as cultural and commercial force

View BoF summaries →

8. Mogul: The Life and Death of Chris Lighty

Why it's great: The Ringer's documentary series about the influential hip-hop manager. Essential listening for understanding modern music business.

Best for: Hip-hop fans and aspiring music industry professionals

View Mogul summaries →

Best Music Discovery & Recommendation Podcasts

9. All Songs Considered

Why it's great: NPR Music's flagship show features new music discoveries, interviews, and themed playlists. Hosts Bob Boilen and Robin Hilton have impeccable taste.

Best for: Discovering new indie, alternative, and emerging artists

Format: Weekly new music roundup + interviews

View All Songs Considered summaries →

10. Sound Opinions

Why it's great: "The world's only rock n' roll talk show." Hosts Jim DeRogatis and Greg Kot review new releases, interview artists, and debate music topics.

Best for: Music nerds who love critical discussion

Format: Reviews, interviews, and the "Desert Island Jukebox" segment

View Sound Opinions summaries →

Best Genre-Specific Music Podcasts

11. Switched on Pop

Why it's great: Musicologist Nate Sloan and songwriter Charlie Harding break down pop music with academic rigor and genuine enthusiasm. Makes you hear pop differently.

Best for: Pop music fans who want to understand what makes hits work

Topics: Song structure, production, trends, cultural impact

View Switched on Pop summaries →

12. Questlove Supreme

Why it's great: The Roots drummer Questlove interviews musicians, actors, and cultural figures with encyclopedic music knowledge and infectious enthusiasm.

Best for: Hip-hop, R&B, and soul fans

Notable guests: Chris Rock, Dave Chappelle, Dua Lipa, Common

View Questlove Supreme summaries →

13. The Trap Set

Why it's great: Drummer Joe Wong interviews other drummers about their lives, careers, and craft. Essential for drummers and rhythm lovers.

Best for: Drummers, musicians, and percussion enthusiasts

View The Trap Set summaries →

Best Music Production & Technical Podcasts

14. The Mastering Show

Why it's great: Mastering engineers discuss the final stage of music production with technical depth and industry insights.

Best for: Producers, engineers, and serious audiophiles

View The Mastering Show summaries →

Best Music Culture & Commentary Podcasts

15. Popcast

Why it's great: New York Times' music critics Jon Caramanica and team discuss the latest in pop music, hip-hop, and music culture with sharp analysis.

Best for: Keeping up with music news and cultural debates

Format: Weekly roundtable discussions

View Popcast summaries →

How to Choose Music Podcasts by Interest

For Musicians & Producers

  • Creative process: Song Exploder, Broken Record
  • Technical skills: The Mastering Show, The Trap Set
  • Industry knowledge: Mogul, Questlove Supreme

For Music Fans

  • Discovery: All Songs Considered, Sound Opinions
  • History: Disgraceland, Lost Notes
  • Analysis: Dissect, Switched on Pop
  • Interviews: Broken Record, WTF with Marc Maron

By Genre Preference

  • Pop: Switched on Pop, Popcast
  • Hip-hop/R&B: Questlove Supreme, Mogul, Dissect
  • Indie/Alternative: All Songs Considered, Sound Opinions
  • Rock: Sound Opinions, Disgraceland
  • All genres: Song Exploder, Broken Record

What You'll Learn from Music Podcasts

Creative Insights

  • How songs are written and produced
  • Artists' influences and inspirations
  • Overcoming creative blocks
  • Collaboration processes

Technical Knowledge

  • Recording techniques
  • Mixing and mastering fundamentals
  • Instrument-specific skills
  • Studio gear and software

Industry Understanding

  • Music business models
  • Streaming economics
  • Artist management and promotion
  • Career paths in music

Cultural Context

  • Music's role in social movements
  • Genre evolution and influences
  • Industry trends and changes
  • Global music scenes

Building Your Music Education

For Beginners

  1. Start with: Song Exploder (accessible, fascinating)
  2. Then: All Songs Considered (discovery)
  3. Deep dive: Dissect (album analysis)

For Aspiring Musicians

  1. Creative: Song Exploder, Broken Record
  2. Technical: The Mastering Show, instrument-specific podcasts
  3. Business: Mogul, industry interviews

For Music Professionals

  • Stay current with Popcast, Sound Opinions
  • Deepen technical skills with production podcasts
  • Network through podcast communities
