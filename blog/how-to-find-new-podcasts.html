How to Find New Podcasts: 10 Proven Discovery Methods (2026)

Stuck listening to the same 3 podcasts on repeat? Here's how to discover shows you'll actually love—without wasting hours on bad episodes.

The Problem with Podcast Discovery

There are over 3 million podcasts and 70+ million episodes available today. The challenge isn't finding podcasts—it's finding good podcasts worth your time.

Most discovery methods fall into two traps:

  • Too broad: "Best podcasts" lists with shows you've already heard of
  • Too risky: Committing 45+ minutes to a random episode that might be terrible

Here are 10 proven methods to find podcasts you'll actually enjoy.

1. Start with AI-Powered Summaries

Why it works: Read a 2-minute summary before committing 45 minutes of listening time.

Instead of gambling on a random episode, read AI-generated summaries to understand:

  • What topics are covered
  • Who the guest is (and why they're interesting)
  • Key takeaways and insights
  • Whether it matches your interests

How to use it: Browse PodBrief's 10,000+ podcast summaries by topic, podcast, or keyword. Find shows that match your interests, then listen to the full episode if it sounds good.

2. Use Podcast App Recommendations

Why it works: Apps track what you listen to and suggest similar shows.

Where to find recommendations:

  • Spotify: "You Might Like" section based on your listening history
  • Apple Podcasts: "Top Shows" and personalized "For You" section
  • Pocket Casts: Trending and featured shows
  • YouTube Music: Algorithm-driven suggestions

Pro tip: The more you listen (and finish episodes), the better the recommendations get.

3. Follow Your Favorite Guests

Why it works: Interesting people appear on multiple podcasts.

Loved a guest on Joe Rogan? Search for their name + "podcast" to find:

  • Other interviews they've done
  • Shows they host themselves
  • Different conversations that explore other aspects of their work

Example: If you enjoyed Naval Ravikant on Joe Rogan, you'll also find him on Tim Ferriss, Lex Fridman, The Knowledge Project, and more.

4. Check "Listeners Also Subscribed To"

Why it works: People with similar taste have already done the curation for you.

On Apple Podcasts and Spotify, scroll to the bottom of any podcast page to see "Listeners Also Subscribed To." This reveals shows with overlapping audiences.

How to use it:

  1. Find a podcast you love
  2. Check "Listeners Also Subscribed To"
  3. Try 1-2 episodes from similar shows
  4. Repeat the process with new favorites

5. Browse by Topic or Category

Why it works: Narrow down 3 million podcasts to a manageable list.

Popular categories:

  • Business
  • True Crime
  • Comedy
  • Technology
  • Education
  • Interviews
  • History
  • Browse all topics →

Where to browse:

  • Apple Podcasts → Browse → Categories
  • Spotify → Search → Browse All
  • PodBrief Topics (with summaries)

6. Ask Friends and Social Media

Why it works: Personal recommendations from people you trust.

Where to ask:

  • Twitter/X: "What's your favorite podcast right now?"
  • Reddit: r/podcasts, r/truecrimepodcasts, r/businesspodcasts, etc.
  • Discord/Slack communities in your niche
  • Instagram stories poll

Pro tip: Be specific. "What's your favorite podcast?" gets generic answers. "What's the best podcast for learning about AI?" gets gold.

7. Check Chart Rankings

Why it works: Popular shows are popular for a reason.

Where to find charts:

  • Chartable - Industry-standard rankings
  • Apple Podcasts → Browse → Top Charts
  • Spotify → Search → Charts
  • Podchaser → Trending

Caveat: Charts show what's popular, not necessarily what's good. Use as a starting point, not the final word.

8. Start with "Best Episodes" Lists

Why it works: Skip the mediocre episodes and jump straight to the best.

Instead of starting at episode 1 or the latest episode, find curated "best episodes" lists for popular shows:

  • Best Lex Fridman Episodes
  • Best Joe Rogan Episodes
  • Best Tim Ferriss Episodes
  • Best Huberman Lab Episodes
  • Best All-In Episodes

This lets you sample a show's best work before subscribing.

9. Follow Podcast Networks

Why it works: Networks curate multiple shows under one umbrella.

Top networks to follow:

  • NPR: This American Life, Planet Money, Throughline, How I Built This
  • Wondery: Business Wars, Dr. Death, The Shrink Next Door
  • Radiotopia: 99% Invisible, Criminal, The Memory Palace
  • The Ringer: The Bill Simmons Podcast, The Rewatchables
  • Gimlet: Reply All, StartUp, Science Vs

If you like one show from a network, explore their other offerings.

10. Use Google Search Strategically

Why it works: Find niche podcasts that don't show up in app recommendations.

Search formulas that work:

  • "Best [topic] podcasts" → e.g., "best venture capital podcasts"
  • "[Person] podcast appearances" → e.g., "Sam Altman podcast appearances"
  • "Podcasts like [show name]" → e.g., "podcasts like How I Built This"
  • "Best podcast episodes about [specific topic]" → e.g., "best podcast episodes about stoicism"

This surfaces curated lists, blog posts, and Reddit threads with hidden gems.

How to Decide What to Listen To

You've found 20 interesting podcasts. Now what?

The 3-Episode Rule:

  1. Pick an episode with an interesting guest or topic
  2. Listen to 15-20 minutes
  3. If you're not engaged, skip to the next show
  4. Give each show 3 episodes before deciding to subscribe or move on

Quality over quantity: It's better to follow 5 shows you love than 50 shows you never listen to.

Avoid These Common Mistakes

  • Starting with episode 1: Many shows take time to find their voice. Start with recent, highly-rated episodes.
  • Forcing yourself through bad episodes: Life's too short. Skip or unsubscribe.
  • Only listening to popular shows: The best podcasts for you might be niche shows with 5,000 listeners.
  • Subscribing to everything: A bloated podcast feed is overwhelming. Be selective.
