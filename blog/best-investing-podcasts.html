Best Investing Podcasts (2026)

Whether you're putting your first $100 into an index fund or managing a seven-figure portfolio, these investing podcasts will sharpen your thinking, keep you up on markets, and help you build lasting wealth. No hype, no get-rich-quick noise — just the shows that serious investors actually listen to.

💰 The Must-Listen Investing Shows

Planet Money

Host: NPR team (Stacey Vanek Smith, Kenny Malone, and others)
Why it's great: Planet Money makes complex economic and financial concepts feel approachable and entertaining. Each episode traces a single idea — inflation, supply chains, the Fed — through a compelling story. It's the rare investing show that's genuinely fun to listen to, even for non-investors.

Best episodes: "The Gold Standard," "How the IMF Works," "The Invisible Plumber," "The Student Loan Pause"

Perfect for: Beginners who want macro context before picking individual investments, curious minds who like narrative-driven finance

We Study Billionaires

Host: Preston Pysh & Stig Brodersen (The Investors Podcast Network)
Why it's great: Deep-dive analysis of Warren Buffett, Charlie Munger, and value investing principles. The team reads Berkshire Hathaway letters, interviews world-class investors, and breaks down exactly how the ultra-wealthy think about money. The depth here is unmatched in free media.

Best episodes: "Warren Buffett's Annual Letters Breakdown," "Peter Lynch's Investment Principles," "Ray Dalio on Debt Cycles," "The Psychology of Money with Morgan Housel"

Perfect for: Value investors, Buffett disciples, anyone who wants to understand how billionaires think

Invest Like the Best

Host: Patrick O'Shaughnessy
Why it's great: O'Shaughnessy interviews the world's top investors, fund managers, and capital allocators with intellectual rigor rare in financial media. Conversations go deep on mental models, portfolio construction, and how great investors actually find edge. One of the highest-signal podcasts in finance, period.

Best episodes: "Nick Sleep on Long-Term Investing," "Cliff Asness on Factor Investing," "Howard Marks on Market Cycles," "Bill Gurley on Technology Disruption"

Perfect for: Serious investors, fund managers, and anyone who wants to understand capital allocation at the highest level

The Motley Fool Money

Host: Chris Hill and rotating Motley Fool analysts
Why it's great: Weekly market commentary and stock analysis delivered in an accessible, conversational format. The Motley Fool team covers earnings, macro events, and individual companies without the jargon that drowns out most financial media. Great for staying current on what's happening in markets each week.

Best episodes: Annual "Stocks to Watch" roundtables, earnings season breakdowns, "5 Stocks We're Watching" episodes

Perfect for: Individual stock pickers who want weekly analysis without paying for Wall Street research

Afford Anything

Host: Paula Pant
Why it's great: Paula's thesis: you can afford anything, but not everything. The show bridges personal finance and investing with a FIRE (Financial Independence, Retire Early) lens. Interviews with financial planners, economists, and real-estate investors make it both practical and thought-provoking. Paula is one of the sharpest interviewers in personal finance media.

Best episodes: "The 4% Rule with Bill Bengen," "Everything You Know About Real Estate Is Wrong," "JL Collins on Simple Path to Wealth," "Ramit Sethi on Conscious Spending"

Perfect for: People pursuing financial independence, real estate investors, anyone building a long-term wealth strategy

Animal Spirits

Hosts: Michael Batnick & Ben Carlson (Ritholtz Wealth Management)
Why it's great: Two of the most respected financial writers on the internet co-host this weekly show covering markets, behavioral finance, and the human side of investing. Their writing — A Wealth of Common Sense and The Irrelevant Investor — is some of the best in the business, and the podcast captures that same voice. Honest, humble, evidence-based.

Best episodes: "Why Investors Underperform Their Own Funds," "The History of Market Crashes," "Passive vs. Active Investing," "What Retail Investors Get Wrong"

Perfect for: Evidence-based investors, behavioral finance fans, people who want a more grounded take on markets

📈 Best Investing Podcasts by Goal

For Beginners: Planet Money + Afford Anything

Start with Planet Money to understand how economies and markets work, then move to Afford Anything for a practical framework for building wealth. Together they give you the "why" and the "how."

For Stock Pickers: Motley Fool Money + Invest Like the Best

Motley Fool covers individual companies week to week; Invest Like the Best teaches you the mental models to evaluate them properly. A powerful combination.

For Long-Term/Passive Investors: Animal Spirits + Afford Anything

If you're an index fund investor, these shows will reinforce your conviction, keep behavioral biases in check, and help you stay the course during volatile markets.

For Deep Value/Billionaire Thinking: We Study Billionaires + Invest Like the Best

These two cover the highest-level investment thinking available in podcast form. The quality of guests on both shows is exceptional.

💡 Key Investing Lessons from the Shows

Time in the Market > Timing the Market (Animal Spirits)

Every episode of Animal Spirits hammers this home with data. The investors who win are those who stay invested through volatility, not those who try to predict it.

Read the Business, Not the Stock (We Study Billionaires)

Buffett's philosophy: understand what the company actually does, its competitive moat, and its long-term earning power. The stock price is a trailing indicator.

Understand Your Actual Risk Tolerance (Afford Anything)

Paula Pant's recurring theme: most people think they're more risk-tolerant than they actually are until their portfolio drops 40%. Know yourself before you allocate.

Seek the Highest-Quality Information (Invest Like the Best)

Patrick O'Shaughnessy's guests consistently emphasize primary research over financial media noise. Go to the source: filings, academic research, actual conversations with operators.

Macro Context Matters (Planet Money)

Individual stock picking happens inside a macro environment you can't control. Planet Money will make you fluent in the forces — interest rates, inflation, policy — that shape everything.

⚠️ Investing Podcast Traps to Avoid

Don't Confuse Entertainment with Research

Some investing podcasts are great for mood and motivation but shouldn't drive actual portfolio decisions. Know which type you're listening to.

Beware of Short-Termism

Any show that focuses primarily on what the market will do this week is entertainment, not investing education. The best investors think in years, not days.

Hot Takes ≠ Edge

If a podcast tells you exactly which stocks to buy right now, it's probably not the show that made its hosts wealthy. The edge is in frameworks and thinking, not tips.

Serious investor: Invest Like the Best + We Study Billionaires
FIRE / financial independence: Afford Anything + Animal Spirits
