Best Business Podcasts 2026 - Strategy, Leadership & Growth

Published March 6, 2026 | 9 min read

Business podcasts deliver insights from successful entrepreneurs, CEOs, and industry leaders. Whether you're building a startup, managing a team, or growing your career, these shows provide actionable strategies and real-world lessons.

Why Listen to Business Podcasts?

Business podcasts offer:

  • Learn from success: Hear how top companies and leaders built their businesses
  • Actionable strategies: Practical tactics you can implement immediately
  • Market insights: Stay current on industry trends and opportunities
  • Leadership skills: Develop management and decision-making abilities
  • Networking knowledge: Learn what successful people are thinking about

Top Business Podcasts of 2026

1. How I Built This (Guy Raz)

Why it's great: Founder origin stories from household-name companies. Guy Raz is a master interviewer who uncovers the real struggles and breakthroughs behind success.

Best for: Entrepreneurs and anyone curious about business building

Episode length: 45-60 minutes

Notable episodes: Airbnb, Instagram, Spanx, Warby Parker, Bumble

2. Masters of Scale (Reid Hoffman)

Why it's great: LinkedIn co-founder Reid Hoffman explores how companies grow from startup to scale. High-production value with actionable frameworks.

Best for: Scaling startups and growth strategy

Episode length: 30-45 minutes

Guests: Mark Zuckerberg, Brian Chesky, Reed Hastings, Sheryl Sandberg

3. The Tim Ferriss Show

Why it's great: Deep-dive interviews with world-class performers across business, investing, sports, and arts. Ferriss deconstructs excellence and extracts learnable strategies.

Best for: Self-optimization and learning from top performers

Episode length: 90-180 minutes

Guests: Naval Ravikant, Peter Thiel, Tony Robbins, Ray Dalio

4. Acquired (Ben Gilbert & David Rosenthal)

Why it's great: Deep dives into company histories and acquisitions. Thorough research (100+ hours per episode) reveals business strategy lessons.

Best for: Understanding how great businesses are built over time

Episode length: 2-4 hours (seriously deep)

Notable episodes: NVIDIA, Costco, LVMH, Berkshire Hathaway

5. All-In Podcast

Why it's great: Four successful tech investors discuss markets, politics, tech, and business. Unfiltered debates and insider perspectives.

Best for: Tech industry insights and venture capital thinking

Episode length: 90-120 minutes

Hosts: Chamath Palihapitiya, Jason Calacanis, David Sacks, David Friedberg

6. Planet Money (NPR)

Why it's great: Economics and business explained through entertaining stories. Makes complex economic concepts accessible and fun.

Best for: Understanding how the economy and markets actually work

Episode length: 20-30 minutes

Style: Narrative storytelling with economic analysis

7. The Prof G Pod (Scott Galloway)

Why it's great: NYU marketing professor offers sharp analysis of business, tech, and markets. No-BS takes on strategy and trends.

Best for: Business strategy and market analysis

Episode length: 45-60 minutes

Format: Solo commentary + guest interviews

8. My First Million (Sam Parr & Shaan Puri)

Why it's great: Entrepreneurs brainstorm business ideas, analyze trends, and share strategies. Energetic, idea-dense conversations.

Best for: Finding business opportunities and entrepreneurial inspiration

Episode length: 45-60 minutes

Vibe: Two friends riffing on business ideas

9. The Twenty Minute VC (Harry Stebbings)

Why it's great: Venture capital insights from top VCs and founders. Quick, focused interviews on startup fundraising and growth.

Best for: Startup founders seeking VC funding

Episode length: 20-30 minutes

Guests: Leading VCs from Sequoia, a16z, Benchmark, etc.

10. StartUp Podcast (Gimlet Media)

Why it's great: Documentary-style series following real startup journeys, including the host's own company. Raw, honest look at entrepreneurship.

Best for: Understanding the reality of starting a company

Episode length: 30-45 minutes

Style: Season-long narratives following specific companies

Tips for Learning from Business Podcasts

1. Take Notes on Frameworks

Many hosts share repeatable frameworks (Reid Hoffman's "blitzscaling," Tim Ferriss's "fear-setting"). Capture these for your own use.

2. Focus on Your Stage

Pre-revenue startup? Listen to early-stage content. Scaling? Prioritize growth-focused shows. Match content to your current needs.

3. Listen Actively

Business podcasts reward engaged listening. Pause to research companies mentioned or write down ideas sparked by discussions.

4. Follow Up on Recommendations

Guests often mention books, tools, or other resources. Keep a list and explore them.

5. Apply, Don't Just Consume

Pick one actionable insight per episode and implement it. Knowledge without action is entertainment.
