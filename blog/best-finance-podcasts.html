Best Finance Podcasts in 2026

March 3, 2026

Whether you're building wealth, learning to invest, or just trying to understand money better, these finance podcasts deliver actionable insights from top investors, economists, and financial experts.

📈 Best Investing Podcasts

The Compound and Friends

Why it's great: Josh Brown (The Reformed Broker) and Michael Batnick discuss markets, investing, and financial planning with humor and real-world experience. Weekly market commentary plus deep-dive interviews.

Best for: Market analysis, portfolio construction, financial advisor perspectives

Format: 2 shows per week - "What Are Your Thoughts?" (market recap) + "The Compound Show" (interviews)

Frequency: Twice weekly

Invest Like the Best

Why it's great: Patrick O'Shaughnessy interviews top investors, founders, and thinkers. Deep conversations about investing frameworks, mental models, and building businesses.

Best for: Investment philosophy, venture capital, long-term thinking

Notable guests: Howard Marks, Bill Gurley, Naval Ravikant, Chamath Palihapitiya

Frequency: Weekly

We Study Billionaires

Why it's great: Two shows in one: "Billionaire Study" (deep dives into investor strategies) and "Bitcoin Fundamentals" (crypto investing). Learn from Buffett, Munger, Dalio, and more.

Best for: Value investing, Bitcoin, wealth-building strategies

Hosts: Preston Pysh and Stig Brodersen

Frequency: 2-3 episodes per week

Animal Spirits

Why it's great: Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson discuss market behavior, investor psychology, and financial news. Conversational, accessible, and honest about what they don't know.

Best for: Behavioral finance, market psychology, portfolio management

Frequency: Weekly

💰 Best Personal Finance Podcasts

The Money Guy Show

Why it's great: Brian Preston and Bo Hanson cover personal finance fundamentals: budgeting, saving, investing, retirement planning. Clear frameworks like the Financial Order of Operations.

Best for: Building wealth systematically, retirement planning, financial milestones

Frequency: Twice weekly (Monday & Friday)

ChooseFI

Why it's great: Focused on financial independence and early retirement. Practical strategies to save more, invest wisely, and achieve FI faster.

Best for: FIRE movement, tax optimization, increasing savings rate

Hosts: Jonathan Mendonsa and Brad Barrett

Frequency: 3 episodes per week

Afford Anything

Why it's great: Paula Pant explores financial independence with a focus on real estate investing, side hustles, and lifestyle design. "You can afford anything, but not everything."

Best for: Real estate investing, side income, life optimization

Frequency: Weekly

So Money with Farnoosh Torabi

Why it's great: Daily show covering all aspects of personal finance. Mix of interviews, listener questions, and "So Money" moments sharing financial strategies.

Best for: Daily financial motivation, career and money, financial psychology

Frequency: Weekdaily

Best for: Quick daily economic updates, bite-sized learning

Odd Lots

Why it's great: Bloomberg's Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway dig into finance, markets, and economics with expert guests. From crypto to commodities to complex derivatives.

EconTalk

Best for: Economic thinking, philosophical depth, intellectual curiosity

Frequency: Weekly

💡 How to Learn Finance from Podcasts

  1. Focus on frameworks, not predictions - Nobody can predict the market. Learn timeless principles instead.
  2. Browse summaries for specific topics - Use PodBrief to find episodes about specific concepts (index funds, tax optimization, etc.)
  3. Take action on what you learn - Podcasts are entertainment without execution. Implement strategies as you learn them.
  4. Balance optimism with skepticism - Most finance podcasts have bias (bullish on stocks, crypto, real estate). Get multiple perspectives.
  5. Don't chase trends - If everyone's talking about it on podcasts, you're probably late. Focus on fundamentals.

⚠️ Finance Podcast Red Flags

Be cautious when a podcast:

  • Promises specific returns ("10% guaranteed!")
  • Focuses on get-rich-quick schemes
  • Pushes specific products or services constantly
  • Never admits mistakes or uncertainty
  • Treats investing like gambling

Trust podcasts that:

  • Admit when they don't know something
  • Emphasize risk management and diversification
  • Share mistakes and lessons learned
  • Explain concepts clearly without jargon
  • Prioritize education over entertainment
