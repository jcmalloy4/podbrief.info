Best Podcasts for Learning: 20 Educational Shows to Expand Your Mind (2026)

Published February 28, 2026 • 15 min read

Turn your commute, workout, or chores into learning opportunities. These educational podcasts deliver expert knowledge across science, history, technology, psychology, and more—making continuous learning accessible and engaging.

Why Learn Through Podcasts?

  • Multitask learning: Listen while commuting, exercising, cooking
  • Expert access: Learn from leading researchers and practitioners
  • Depth + accessibility: Complex topics explained clearly
  • Free education: University-level content at no cost
  • Consistent habit: Build a daily learning routine

🧬 Best Science & Health Podcasts

1. Huberman Lab

Host: Dr. Andrew Huberman (Stanford neuroscientist)

Format: 90-120 min deep dives

Science-based protocols for optimizing brain and body. Topics include sleep, focus, fitness, mental health, and performance.

Learn: Neuroscience, health optimization, behavioral protocols

2. Radiolab

Hosts: Jad Abumrad, Lulu Miller

Format: 30-60 min narrative science stories

Explores big questions through sound-rich storytelling. Philosophy meets science meets humanity.

Learn: Scientific thinking, ethics, wonder

3. Short Wave (NPR)

Format: 10-15 min daily

Quick science news and discoveries. Accessible, fun, and current.

Learn: Latest research across all sciences

4. Science Vs

Host: Wendy Zukerman

Format: 30-45 min investigative episodes

Pits facts vs. popular fads. Examines claims about health, nutrition, psychology with actual research.

Learn: Critical thinking, research literacy

📖 How to Build a Learning Habit with Podcasts

The 30-Day Learning Challenge

Week 1: Start with daily 10-15 min shows (Short Wave, TED Talks Daily)

Week 2: Add 30-45 min episodes during commute (Planet Money, Hidden Brain)

Week 3: Incorporate longer weekend listens (Lex Fridman, Huberman Lab)

Week 4: Mix formats—daily shorts, weekly deep dives, narrative series

Learning Retention Tips

  • Take notes: Voice memos while listening, written notes after
  • Apply immediately: Use one insight from each episode
  • Discuss with others: Share episodes, debate ideas
  • Slow down for dense content: 0.8-1.0x speed for complex topics
  • Re-listen to key episodes: Repetition aids retention
  • Follow up with reading: Books/articles mentioned in episodes

Building Your Learning Queue

Morning commute (20-30 min): News + quick learning (Short Wave, The Indicator)

Lunch break (30-45 min): Medium-length education (Planet Money, Science Vs)

Exercise (45-60 min): Engaging narratives (Radiolab, Revisionist History)

Weekend deep dive (2+ hours): Long-form mastery (Lex Fridman, Hardcore History, Acquired)

Learning Paths by Interest

🧬 Science Track: Short Wave → Radiolab → Huberman Lab → Lex Fridman (science guests)

💼 Business Track: The Indicator → Planet Money → How I Built This → Acquired

🧠 Psychology Track: The Happiness Lab → Hidden Brain → Huberman Lab → Making Sense

💻 Tech Track: Hard Fork → Darknet Diaries → Acquired (tech companies) → Lex Fridman (AI guests)

📚 Liberal Arts Track: 99% Invisible → Revisionist History → Radiolab → The Ezra Klein Show

Conclusion

The average American commutes 200+ hours per year. That's enough time to complete several university courses through podcasts alone. Add exercise, chores, and other "background time," and you could log 400+ hours of learning annually.

Start with one daily podcast this week. Add a weekly deep-dive next week. Within a month, you'll have transformed dead time into continuous education.

Recommended starter combo:

  • Daily: Short Wave (10 min science news)
  • Weekly: Hidden Brain (behavioral science)
  • Weekend: Lex Fridman or Huberman Lab (deep learning)
