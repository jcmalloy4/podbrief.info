SmartLess vs Armchair Expert: Which Celebrity Interview Podcast Wins?

SmartLess and Armchair Expert are two of the biggest celebrity interview podcasts ever made—but they take wildly different approaches to the genre. One is a 45-minute comedy ambush with A-list guests. The other is a 2-hour deep-dive with a host who treats every conversation like therapy. Here's how they stack up.

🎭 SmartLess

What It Is

Jason Bateman, Sean Hayes, and Will Arnett started SmartLess in 2020 with a simple gimmick: each episode, one host secretly books a guest and surprises the other two. The genuine reactions—from screaming to disbelief to immediate ribbing—became the show's signature. Guests have included Paul McCartney, Barack Obama, Tom Hanks, Conan O'Brien, and virtually every A-lister in Hollywood. SmartLess became one of the most downloaded podcasts in the world within months of launching.

Strengths

  • The surprise format: Nothing else in podcasting works quite like it. Hearing three celebrities react to an unexpected guest is genuinely delightful.
  • Host chemistry: Bateman, Hayes, and Arnett have known each other for decades. Their friendship is real, and it shows. They're hilarious together.
  • A-list access: The hosts' Hollywood relationships get them guests nobody else can book. McCartney, Obama, Harrison Ford—SmartLess gets them all.
  • Efficient runtime: At 45–60 minutes, SmartLess never outstays its welcome. Tight, fun, easy to finish.
  • Genuinely funny: All three hosts are comedic performers. The banter is effortless and consistently funny.

Weaknesses

  • Shallow interviews: The comedic energy means conversations rarely go deep. You'll learn little about a guest's actual inner life.
  • Hosts dominate: Bateman, Hayes, and Arnett are funny enough to make the guest almost secondary. Some listeners wish the guest got more airtime.
  • Limited variety: If you're not interested in Hollywood celebrities, SmartLess has a narrow range.
  • The joke can run thin: The surprise format is great at first. After 100+ episodes, some listeners find it formulaic.

Best Episodes to Start

  • Paul McCartney: Hearing three grown men lose their minds when Paul McCartney walks in is pure joy.
  • Barack Obama: Obama is genuinely funny here. One of the best political celebrity interviews in podcast history.
  • Conan O'Brien: Two comedy worlds colliding. Chaotic, hilarious, and deeply entertaining.
  • Tom Hanks: The nicest man in Hollywood, with three hosts who clearly adore him. Warm and funny.

Who Should Listen

✅ Fans of celebrity culture and Hollywood gossip
✅ People who want to laugh for an hour
✅ Listeners who enjoy three-way banter and chemistry
✅ Anyone who loves the golden age of TV/film comedy

🛋️ Armchair Expert

What It Is

Dax Shepard and Monica Padman launched Armchair Expert in 2018. Dax—actor, writer, recovering addict—interviews guests with unusual emotional honesty. He reads extensively before each episode and asks the questions most hosts avoid: about shame, addiction, family dysfunction, childhood trauma, and failure. Monica co-hosts and later runs the "Fact Check" segment that closes each episode, where she and Dax laugh about the things they got wrong. The show consistently tops podcast charts globally.

Strengths

  • Real vulnerability: Dax's own openness about addiction and mental health creates space for guests to be equally honest. You get confessions you won't hear anywhere else.
  • Preparation: Dax reads guests' books, watches their films, studies their work. Questions are specific, not generic.
  • Range of guests: Beyond celebrities, Dax regularly hosts scientists, psychologists, and authors. Episodes with experts are often the best ones.
  • The Fact Check: Monica and Dax's post-episode segment is funny and charming. It humanizes both hosts and adds a second act to every episode.
  • Monica's evolution: Monica went from co-host sidekick to essential partner over the show's run. Watching her grow into the role is its own arc.

Weaknesses

  • Very long: Two-hour-plus episodes require commitment. Not for casual listeners.
  • Dax can make it about Dax: His tendency to share his own parallel experiences can occasionally overshadow the guest's story.
  • Uneven guest selection: Not every guest is equally compelling. Some episodes drag.
  • Heavy topics: If you want light entertainment, Armchair Expert is not it. This show goes to dark places.

Best Episodes to Start

  • Barack Obama: One of the most human portrayals of Obama in any media format. Dax asks questions no journalist would.
  • Brené Brown: A master class in vulnerability. Two people who've done the work talking honestly about shame and courage.
  • Bill Gates: Surprisingly funny and revealing. Gates is much more personable than his public image suggests.
  • Malcolm Gladwell: Two deep thinkers going deep on ideas. One of the show's great intellectual episodes.

Who Should Listen

✅ People who like emotional depth and real conversations
✅ Fans of psychology and self-improvement content
✅ Listeners who want expert guests alongside celebrities
✅ Anyone who appreciates a host who's done their homework

🥊 Head-to-Head

Entertainment Value

Winner: SmartLess
SmartLess is consistently funnier. Three comedic performers riffing together beats Dax's earnest interview style for pure entertainment. If you want to laugh, SmartLess wins.

Interview Depth

Winner: Armchair Expert
Dax goes places other interviewers won't. The vulnerability he extracts from guests—from Obama to Ashton Kutcher—is genuinely remarkable. SmartLess skims the surface by comparison.

Guest Quality

Winner: Tie
Both shows attract the biggest names in the world. SmartLess edges it for pure Hollywood star power. Armchair Expert wins for intellectual and expert diversity.

Efficiency

Winner: SmartLess
45–60 minutes versus 90–150 minutes. SmartLess delivers the goods faster. Armchair Expert demands a much larger time investment.

Originality

Winner: SmartLess
The surprise reveal format is genuinely original. No one else in podcasting does it this way. Armchair Expert is a great long-form interview show, but the long-form interview is a well-established format.

Replay Value

Winner: Armchair Expert
Deep, vulnerable conversations reward re-listening. SmartLess episodes are fun once but rarely cry out for a second playthrough.

🎯 Which One Should You Choose?

Choose SmartLess if you want:

  • To laugh consistently for an hour
  • Access to the biggest celebrities on earth
  • Something to listen to while driving or doing chores
  • Warm, effortless host chemistry
  • Episodes that don't overstay their welcome

Choose Armchair Expert if you want:

  • Real emotional depth and vulnerability
  • A host who prepares obsessively and asks hard questions
  • Scientists and experts alongside Hollywood names
  • Long, winding conversations you can get truly lost in
  • A Fact Check segment that adds a charming second act

Why Not Both?

They serve completely different moods:

  • SmartLess: Commute, gym, cooking—easy background listening that makes you laugh
  • Armchair Expert: Long walks, focused listening time, when you want to feel something

✨ The Verdict

For casual listeners who want to laugh: SmartLess. The surprise format, the three-way chemistry, and the A-list guests make it consistently delightful.

For deep-dive listeners who want real conversations: Armchair Expert. Dax Shepard extracts honesty from celebrities in a way few interviewers can match.

For the complete celebrity interview experience: Listen to both. SmartLess for the laughs. Armchair Expert for the substance.
