Best History Podcasts: 16 Shows to Explore the Past (2026)

Published February 28, 2026 • 13 min read

History doesn't have to be dry textbooks. These podcasts bring the past to life with gripping storytelling, expert analysis, and fresh perspectives on events that shaped our world.

⚔️ Best Epic History Podcasts

1. Hardcore History

Host: Dan Carlin

Format: 3-6 hour epic deep dives

The gold standard of history podcasts. Carlin makes ancient battles, world wars, and empire collapses feel immediate and visceral. His "Supernova in the East" series on Japan in WWII is legendary.

Best for: Immersive, cinematic history experiences

Start with: "Blueprint for Armageddon" (WWI), "Wrath of the Khans" (Mongol Empire)

2. The History of Rome

Host: Mike Duncan

Format: 179 episodes covering Roman history (COMPLETE)

The complete story of Rome from founding to fall. Legendary series that launched the history podcast genre.

Best for: Comprehensive understanding of Rome

Note: Complete series—binge-worthy deep dive

3. Revolutions

Host: Mike Duncan

Format: Multi-episode series on major revolutions

From the English Civil War to the Russian Revolution, Duncan examines how societies overthrow their governments. Essential for understanding modern politics.

Best for: Understanding political upheaval patterns

Covers: English, American, French, Haitian, Latin American, Russian revolutions

📖 Best Storytelling History Podcasts

4. Revisionist History

Host: Malcolm Gladwell

Format: 30-45 min investigative episodes

Gladwell reexamines overlooked and misunderstood moments in history. Challenges conventional wisdom with trademark curiosity.

Best for: Fresh perspectives on familiar stories

Style: Investigative journalism meets history

5. You're Dead to Me

Host: Greg Jenner (BBC)

Format: 45 min comedy history episodes

Historian + comedian discuss historical topics. Educational and genuinely funny. British charm meets serious scholarship.

Best for: Learning history while laughing

Topics: Everything from ancient Greece to space race

6. Slow Burn

Network: Slate

Format: Serialized seasons on recent history

Deep dives into major recent events: Watergate, Clinton impeachment, Tupac/Biggie, Iraq War. Makes you feel like you're experiencing history as it unfolds.

Best for: Understanding events you may have lived through

🏛️ Best Ancient History Podcasts

7. The Ancient World

Host: Scott C.

Format: Multi-episode series on ancient civilizations

Covers Mesopotamia, Egypt, Bronze Age collapse, and more. Academic depth made accessible.

Best for: Deep dives into ancient civilizations

8. The History of Ancient Greece

Host: Ryan Stitt

Format: Chronological Greek history

From Bronze Age to Roman conquest. Comprehensive and engaging.

Best for: Understanding classical Greek civilization

🌍 Best World History Podcasts

9. Fall of Civilizations

Host: Paul Cooper

Format: 2-4 hour episodes on fallen civilizations

Beautifully produced deep dives into civilizations that collapsed: Maya, Rome, Easter Island, Aztecs. Atmospheric and haunting.

Best for: Understanding why civilizations fail

Production quality: Documentary-level sound design

10. Empire

Hosts: William Dalrymple, Anita Anand

Format: British Empire history

British historians examine the British Empire—the good, bad, and ugly. Nuanced, not hagiographic.

Best for: Understanding colonialism and its legacy

11. The Rest is History

Hosts: Tom Holland, Dominic Sandbrook

Format: 45-60 min episodes on diverse topics

Two acclaimed British historians discuss everything from gladiators to the Beatles. Witty, accessible, wide-ranging.

Best for: Variety and British wit

🇺🸠Best American History Podcasts

12. BackStory

Hosts: American historians

Format: 45 min episodes connecting past to present

Uses current events as jumping-off points to explore American history. Makes history feel relevant.

Best for: Understanding modern America through history

13. American History Tellers

Network: Wondery

Format: Serialized American history stories

Dramatic retellings of American events: Prohibition, Space Race, Civil Rights Movement. Narrative-driven with high production.

Best for: Dramatic American history

⏰ Best Recent History Podcasts

14. Cold War Conversations

Format: Cold War deep dives

Everything from Cuban Missile Crisis to Reagan-Gorbachev meetings. Essential for understanding post-WWII world.

Best for: Understanding 20th century geopolitics

15. The 80s and 90s Uncensored

Format: Recent pop culture + political history

Revisits the decades many listeners actually lived through. Nostalgia meets analysis.

Best for: Millennials/Gen X wanting context on their youth

🎓 Best Academic History Podcasts

16. Tides of History

Host: Patrick Wyman (historian)

Format: Deep dives + interviews

Academic rigor with accessible storytelling. Covers medieval Europe, ancient Rome, and more.

Best for: Serious history buffs who want scholarly depth

💡 How to Choose Your History Podcast

By Time Period

Ancient (Pre-500 AD):

  • The History of Rome (Rome)
  • The Ancient World (Mesopotamia, Egypt)
  • The History of Ancient Greece (Greece)

Medieval (500-1500):

  • Tides of History
  • The Rest is History (selected episodes)

Early Modern (1500-1800):

  • Revolutions (English, American, French)
  • Empire (British colonialism)

Modern (1800-1945):

  • Hardcore History (WWI, WWII)
  • Revolutions (Russian Revolution)

Contemporary (1945-Present):

  • Slow Burn (Watergate, Clinton, Iraq)
  • Cold War Conversations
  • BackStory

By Style

Epic, immersive narratives: Hardcore History, Fall of Civilizations

Educational + funny: You're Dead to Me, The Rest is History

Academic depth: Tides of History, The Ancient World

Dramatic storytelling: American History Tellers, Slow Burn

Revisionist/fresh takes: Revisionist History, BackStory

By Length

Short (30-45 min): Revisionist History, You're Dead to Me, The Rest is History

Medium (60-90 min): Most history podcasts

Epic (2-6 hours): Hardcore History, Fall of Civilizations

📚 Learning History Through Podcasts

Build a Chronological Understanding

1. Ancient World Foundation:

  1. The Ancient World (Mesopotamia)
  2. The History of Ancient Greece
  3. The History of Rome

2. Add Medieval + Early Modern:

  1. Tides of History (medieval Europe)
  2. Revolutions (political transformations)

3. Modern Context:

  1. Hardcore History (WWI, WWII)
  2. Slow Burn (recent American history)

Thematic Approach

Instead of chronological, follow themes:

Rise and fall of empires:

  • The History of Rome
  • Fall of Civilizations
  • Empire

Revolution and change:

  • Revolutions
  • Slow Burn
  • BackStory

War and conflict:

  • Hardcore History
  • Cold War Conversations

🎧 Building Your History Podcast Routine

Weekly Listening Plan

Commute (30-45 min episodes):

  • Monday: Revisionist History
  • Wednesday: The Rest is History
  • Friday: You're Dead to Me

Weekend deep dive (2+ hours):

  • Saturday morning walk: Hardcore History or Fall of Civilizations

Before bed (60 min):

  • Tides of History, BackStory, American History Tellers

Beginner's Path

Week 1: Start with Revisionist History (accessible, short episodes)

Week 2: Try You're Dead to Me (funny + educational)

Week 3: Graduate to The Rest is History (more depth, still accessible)

Week 4: Take on Hardcore History (commit to one epic episode)

💎 Hidden Gems & Honorable Mentions

  • History That Doesn't Suck: American history told chronologically, no fluff
  • The British History Podcast: Complete British history from the beginning
  • Noble Blood: Stories of royals throughout history
  • Stuff You Missed in History Class: Overlooked historical stories
  • History Extra: BBC History Magazine's official podcast

📖 Using Podcasts to Supplement Reading

Podcasts work great alongside books:

  1. Listen to podcast episode first: Get overview and context
  2. Read recommended books: Many hosts mention sources
  3. Return to podcast: Deepen understanding with context from reading

Example: Listen to Hardcore History's "Blueprint for Armageddon" (WWI), then read "The Guns of August," then re-listen with new understanding.

Conclusion

History podcasts prove that the past is anything but boring. Whether you want epic narratives, academic depth, or comedic takes, there's a history podcast for every interest and schedule.

Start with these three:

  1. Revisionist History - Accessible, fresh perspectives (30-45 min)
  2. The Rest is History - Broad topics, British wit (45-60 min)
  3. Hardcore History - Epic deep dives when ready for commitment (3-6 hours)

From ancient Rome to recent events, these podcasts make history more engaging than any textbook ever could.
