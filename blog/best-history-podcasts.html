Best History Podcasts in 2026

March 3, 2026

History podcasts bring the past to life with expert storytelling, deep research, and engaging narratives. Whether you're into ancient Rome, World War II, or lesser-known historical events, these shows deliver.

⚔️ Best Epic History Podcasts

Hardcore History

Why it's great: Dan Carlin creates 4-6 hour epic narratives about major historical events. Blueprint for Armageddon (WWI), Wrath of the Khans (Mongol Empire), Death Throes of the Republic (Fall of Rome).

Best for: Deep dives, dramatic storytelling, military history, epic narratives

Format: Multi-part series, 3-6 hours per episode

Frequency: Sporadic (few per year, worth the wait)

The History of Rome

Why it's great: Mike Duncan tells the complete story of Rome from founding to fall over 179 episodes. The gold standard for narrative history podcasts.

Best for: Ancient Rome, chronological storytelling, comprehensive coverage

Status: Complete series (finished 2012, but timeless)

Revolutions

Why it's great: Mike Duncan (History of Rome) covers major revolutions: English, American, French, Haitian, Latin American, 1848, Paris Commune, Russian, and more.

Best for: Political revolutions, social change, understanding modern politics

Frequency: Weekly (currently on hiatus after Russian Revolution series)

📚 Best Academic History Podcasts

The Rest Is History

Why it's great: Historians Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook discuss everything from ancient Egypt to the Beatles. Academic rigor with engaging delivery.

Best for: Wide-ranging topics, historian banter, British humor

Frequency: Multiple episodes per week

History That Doesn't Suck

Why it's great: Professor Greg Jackson tells American history chronologically with academic accuracy and storytelling flair. "Academic rigor with the narrative flare."

Best for: American history, chronological understanding, educational

Frequency: Bi-weekly

The British History Podcast

Why it's great: Jamie Jeffers tells British history from the very beginning—starting with Roman Britain and working chronologically forward.

Best for: British history, Anglo-Saxon period, comprehensive coverage

Frequency: Weekly

🎙️ Best Storytelling History Podcasts

Revisionist History

Why it's great: Malcolm Gladwell re-examines overlooked or misunderstood moments in history. Not traditional history, but thought-provoking historical analysis.

Best for: Unconventional perspectives, challenging assumptions, social history

Frequency: Seasonal (10 episodes per season)

You're Dead To Me

Why it's great: BBC podcast where historian Greg Jenner, a comedian, and an expert discuss historical topics with humor and accuracy.

Best for: Fun history, British humor, accessible learning

Frequency: Weekly

The Memory Palace

Why it's great: Nate DiMeo tells short, beautifully crafted stories from history. Poetic, emotional, and memorable. Each episode is 5-15 minutes.

Best for: Short-form history, emotional storytelling, lesser-known stories

Frequency: Monthly

⚔️ Best Military History Podcasts

The History of World War II Podcast

Why it's great: Ray Harris Jr. provides detailed, chronological coverage of WWII from the Treaty of Versailles through the end of the war.

Best for: WWII deep dives, chronological understanding, comprehensive coverage

Frequency: Weekly

The Civil War (1861-1865): A History Podcast

Why it's great: Rich and Tracy tell the complete story of the American Civil War chronologically with academic rigor.

Best for: Civil War history, military tactics, social context

Frequency: Weekly

Lions Led By Donkeys

Why it's great: Military history told with dark humor, focusing on military disasters, incompetent leadership, and absurd moments in war.

Best for: Military history with humor, war stories, criticism of military leadership

Frequency: Weekly

🌍 Best World History Podcasts

Fall of Civilizations

Why it's great: Paul Cooper explores the rise and fall of great civilizations: Rome, Maya, Sumerians, Easter Island, and more. Beautifully produced 2-3 hour episodes.

Best for: Ancient civilizations, archaeological mysteries, long-form narratives

Frequency: Sporadic (few per year)

The History of Byzantium

Why it's great: Robin Pierson continues where Mike Duncan's History of Rome left off—covering the Byzantine Empire from 476 to 1453 CE.

Best for: Byzantine history, continuation of Roman Empire, medieval history

Frequency: Weekly

The Ancient World

Why it's great: Scott C. explores ancient civilizations from Sumeria to Rome. Academic but accessible.

Best for: Ancient history, early civilizations, archaeology

Status: Complete series

  • Start with: Revisionist History (bite-sized, thought-provoking)
  • Then try: History That Doesn't Suck (American history fundamentals)
  • Build to: The Rest Is History (wide-ranging topics)

For Ancient History Fans

For Military History Buffs

For Quick Listening

  • Seasonal: Revisionist History

💡 How to Learn History from Podcasts

  1. Start chronologically - For major topics (Rome, WWII, Revolutions), chronological podcasts build better understanding than random episodes.
  2. Browse by topic, not just show - Use PodBrief to find episodes about specific events or periods across all history podcasts.
  3. Mix narrative with analysis - Balance storytelling podcasts (Hardcore History) with analytical ones (Revisionist History).
  4. Follow up with reading - History podcasts often mention books. Make a list and read the ones that interest you most.
  5. Listen at your own pace - History podcasts aren't time-sensitive. Take breaks, re-listen, and let information sink in.

⚠️ History Podcast Quality Markers

Good history podcasts:

  • Cite sources and research
  • Acknowledge uncertainty and debate
  • Present multiple perspectives
  • Distinguish fact from interpretation
  • Update when new research emerges

Red flags:

  • Present opinions as facts
  • Cherry-pick evidence
  • Push political agenda onto past events
  • Ignore scholarly consensus without explanation
  • Treat myths as history

📚 From Podcast to Deeper Learning

Effective history podcast routine:

  1. Pick a period you're curious about
  2. Find a chronological podcast on that topic
  3. Listen to 3-5 episodes to build foundation
  4. Branch out to related topics
  5. Read one book on your favorite discovery
