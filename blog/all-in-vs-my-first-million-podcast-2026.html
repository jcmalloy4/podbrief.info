All-In vs My First Million Podcast: Which Business Podcast Is Better? (2026)

March 12, 2026 • 8 min read

When it comes to business and entrepreneurship podcasts, two shows dominate the conversation: All-In Podcast and My First Million. Both offer insider perspectives on business, tech, and investing—but they're completely different experiences.

If you're trying to decide which one deserves a spot in your podcast rotation, this comparison breaks down everything you need to know.

The Hosts: Who's Behind Each Show?

All-In Podcast: The "Besties"

All-In features four highly successful tech investors and entrepreneurs:

  • Chamath Palihapitiya: Former Facebook exec, founder of Social Capital
  • Jason Calacanis: Angel investor (Uber, Robinhood), founder of LAUNCH
  • David Sacks: PayPal mafia member, founded Yammer, invested in SpaceX
  • David Friedberg: Founder of The Climate Corporation, investor

These four have been friends for years, which creates natural chemistry—and occasional tension when they disagree on politics or policy.

My First Million: The Operators

  • Shaan Puri: Former CEO of Bebo (acquired by Twitch), angel investor, serial entrepreneur
  • Sam Parr: Founder of The Hustle (acquired by HubSpot for $27M), entrepreneur

Shaan and Sam are both builders who've started, scaled, and exited companies. They bring operator experience rather than just investor perspective.

Content Style & Topics

All-In: Macro Analysis & Debate

All-In tackles big topics:

  • Federal Reserve policy and inflation
  • Tech regulation and antitrust
  • Geopolitics and foreign policy
  • AI and emerging technologies
  • Venture capital and startup trends
  • Presidential elections and political strategy

The format: Each host picks a topic, presents their take, and the group debates. Expect 2-3 hour episodes with deep dives into complex subjects.

My First Million: Business Ideas & Stories

MFM is all about entrepreneurship:

  • Business ideas they'd start tomorrow
  • How specific companies grew (with numbers)
  • Interview successful founders
  • Breakdown trends and opportunities
  • Personal stories from building companies
  • Mental models for decision-making

The format: Fast-paced brainstorming sessions. Shaan and Sam riff on ideas, share stories, and occasionally bring on guests. Episodes are typically 45-90 minutes.

Who Should Listen to Each?

Listen to All-In if you:

  • Want to understand macro economic and political trends
  • Enjoy intellectual debate and contrarian viewpoints
  • Work in venture capital, tech policy, or finance
  • Don't mind long-form content (2+ hours)
  • Like hearing multiple perspectives on complex issues
  • Care about topics like Fed policy, geopolitics, and regulation

Listen to My First Million if you:

  • Want actionable business ideas you could actually execute
  • Prefer entrepreneurship tactics over theory
  • Enjoy brainstorming and creative thinking
  • Like learning from specific company case studies
  • Want shorter, more frequent episodes
  • Care more about building than investing

Strengths & Weaknesses

All-In Strengths:

  • ✅ Unfiltered takes from people with real skin in the game
  • ✅ Deep analysis of complex topics
  • ✅ Multiple perspectives in one episode
  • ✅ Insider knowledge of Silicon Valley and tech
  • ✅ Willingness to be politically incorrect

All-In Weaknesses:

  • ❌ Very long episodes can feel repetitive
  • ❌ Sometimes veers too far into politics
  • ❌ Can feel out of touch (billionaires discussing policy)
  • ❌ Less actionable for everyday entrepreneurs

My First Million Strengths:

  • ✅ Highly actionable business ideas
  • ✅ Energetic, fun conversations
  • ✅ More relatable hosts (built businesses themselves)
  • ✅ Specific tactics and frameworks
  • ✅ Regular episodes (more content)

My First Million Weaknesses:

  • ❌ Sometimes surface-level analysis
  • ❌ Idea overload (hard to execute on everything)
  • ❌ Less focus on big-picture trends
  • ❌ Can feel overly optimistic about business ideas

Audience & Popularity

All-In: Massive reach in tech and finance circles. Episodes regularly hit #1 on Apple Podcasts business charts. Strong following among VCs, founders, and policy folks.

My First Million: Huge audience of entrepreneurs and side hustlers. Known for spawning actual businesses (listeners execute on ideas discussed). Strong community engagement.

Both shows have millions of listeners, but different demographics. All-In skews older and wealthier; MFM attracts younger entrepreneurs.

Episode Examples to Start With

All-In Episodes:

  • "E1: The COVID-19 Pandemic" — The pilot episode that started it all
  • "E50: Inflation, Midterms, Trump's Return?" — Classic debate format
  • "E100: The Future of AI" — Tech-focused analysis

My First Million Episodes:

  • "Hampton's $100M Course Business" — Business breakdown
  • "Shaan's $1.5M Angel Portfolio Review" — Investing insights
  • "How Andrew Wilkinson Built a $1B Empire" — Founder interview

Can You Listen to Both?

Absolutely! Many people do. They serve different purposes:

  • All-In on Fridays: Your weekly dose of macro analysis
  • MFM 2-3x per week: Ongoing business inspiration

Think of All-In as your "understanding the world" podcast and MFM as your "building in the world" podcast.

The Verdict: Which One Should You Choose?

Choose All-In if: You want to understand macro trends, enjoy debate-style content, and don't mind long episodes. Best for investors, policy folks, and those who think about systems and trends.

Choose My First Million if: You want actionable business ideas, prefer entrepreneurial tactics over theory, and like faster-paced content. Best for builders, founders, and aspiring entrepreneurs.

Or choose both: They complement each other perfectly. All-In helps you understand the landscape; MFM helps you build in it.
