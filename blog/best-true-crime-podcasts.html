Best True Crime Podcasts of 2026

Published February 17, 2026 · 12 min read

True crime podcasts have completely transformed how we consume crime stories — moving from sensational tabloid coverage to nuanced, deeply-reported journalism that reopens cold cases, holds systems accountable, and shines a light on the very human stories behind the headlines. Whether you're a seasoned true crime fan or just dipping your toe in for the first time, 2026 has never been a better year to dive in.

We've compiled the 10 best true crime podcasts right now — shows with compelling storytelling, rigorous reporting, and the kind of depth that keeps you up at night (in the best way possible). Find summaries and episode guides for all of these shows at PodBrief.

The 10 Best True Crime Podcasts of 2026

1. Serial

The podcast that launched a thousand true crime obsessions, Serial from Sarah Koenig and This American Life remains the gold standard of the genre. Season 1, which investigated the 1999 murder of Hae Min Lee and the conviction of Adnan Syed, became a cultural phenomenon that redefined what podcasts could be. Each season takes on a single story — from wrongful convictions to the inner workings of the U.S. criminal justice system — and unpacks it with meticulous journalistic rigour and storytelling that's impossible to stop listening to. Over a decade later, Serial's episodes still stand up as masterclasses in narrative nonfiction.

Why listen: The originator of the modern true crime podcast genre — cinematic storytelling, careful journalism, and cases that genuinely matter.

2. Crime Junkie

Hosted by Ashley Flowers and Brit Prawat, Crime Junkie is the most-downloaded true crime podcast in the world — and for good reason. Every Monday, the duo delivers a tight, well-researched 30–45 minute episode covering a single case: unsolved murders, missing persons, cold cases, and serial killers. What sets Crime Junkie apart is its consistency and accessibility. Ashley's clear, direct narration strips away any exploitation and keeps the focus on the victims and facts. The "#MotivationMonday" format has made it easy to consume for true crime fans who want quality without the fluff.

Why listen: Consistently excellent, victim-centered reporting in a digestible weekly format — perfect for true crime newcomers and veterans alike.

Find Crime Junkie episode summaries on PodBrief →

3. My Favorite Murder

Karen Kilgariff and Georgia Hardstark turned true crime into a comedy and community with My Favorite Murder — a podcast that somehow manages to be both hilarious and genuinely informative. The hosts take turns sharing their "favorite murders" (cases they find particularly fascinating), mixing in personal anecdotes, dark humor, and genuine empathy for victims. The show spawned the "Murderino" fandom, a global community of listeners who find catharsis in laughing through dark material. It's not for everyone, but for those who connect with its tone, MFM is one of the most entertaining listens in the genre.

Why listen: True crime meets comedy — two friends who make dark stories approachable, funny, and strangely comforting.

4. Casefile

Hosted by the mysteriously anonymous "The Host," Australia's Casefile is atmospheric, haunting, and deeply serious. With a distinctive voice and no-frills format, Casefile covers cases from around the world — many of which get little coverage in mainstream English-language media. Episodes are meticulously researched, often running 90 minutes or more on complex cases, and the somber tone is a deliberate choice to honor victims rather than entertain at their expense. Casefile has built one of the most dedicated listener bases in true crime by simply doing the work with integrity.

Why listen: International cases, serious tone, exceptional research — ideal for listeners who want substance over showmanship.

5. Murdaugh Murders: A Southern Scandal

The Murdaugh family saga is one of the most extraordinary true crime stories in recent American history — involving murder, financial fraud, decades of privilege, and a legal dynasty brought crashing down in rural South Carolina. This podcast from journalist Mandy Matney traces the full arc of the Murdaugh empire from its roots to Alex Murdaugh's conviction in 2023, providing unparalleled access and context that no other outlet matched. Matney broke many of the original stories, and her insider reporting gives the podcast an authenticity that documentaries and books simply can't replicate.

Why listen: The definitive deep-dive into America's most sensational recent true crime story, told by the journalist who broke it wide open.

6. Scam Goddess

Hosted by comedian Laci Mosley, Scam Goddess tackles the world of cons, fraudsters, and financial crimes with energy, outrage, and genuine comedic flair. Each episode focuses on a famous or little-known scam — from Ponzi schemes to catfishing rings to corporate fraud — and Laci brings in a rotating cast of comedian guests who react in real time to increasingly wild revelations. It's part true crime, part comedy, and completely addictive. Scam Goddess occupies a unique lane by focusing on financial and confidence crimes rather than violence, making it more accessible to listeners who find traditional true crime too intense.

Why listen: Hilarious, righteous, and surprisingly educational — true crime for people who love a good con story and have a sense of humor about it.

7. Dr. Death

Dr. Death from Wondery is peak true crime storytelling — an investigative series that uncovered the horrifying story of Dr. Christopher Duntsch, a neurosurgeon who maimed and killed patients in Texas operating rooms while the medical establishment looked the other way. Journalist Laura Beil's reporting is impeccably sourced and the production quality is cinematic. The show asks hard questions about medical oversight, patient safety, and how someone this dangerous operated for so long. The success of Dr. Death sparked a TV adaptation and cemented Wondery's position as the premier true crime podcast network.

Why listen: Gripping medical true crime with systemic implications — a terrifying story about the worst doctor in modern American history.

8. Dateline NBC

The original investigative crime franchise has translated remarkably well to podcast form. Dateline NBC brings the full weight of NBC News' reporting resources to each episode, with Keith Morrison's unmistakable voice guiding listeners through cases that span decades of American crime history. The podcast format lets Dateline go deeper than a 42-minute TV episode allows, and the archive is enormous — hundreds of cases covering murders, missing persons, cults, and corporate crime. For listeners who want the legitimacy of a major news organization behind their true crime, Dateline is the gold standard.

Why listen: Decades of investigative journalism from a trusted news brand — authoritative, well-produced, and endlessly bingeable.

9. Bear Brook

New Hampshire Public Radio's Bear Brook is a stunning piece of investigative journalism that shows what's possible when a local reporter refuses to give up on an unsolved case. In 1985, four bodies were found in barrels in the woods of Bear Brook State Park — and for decades no one knew who they were. Reporter Jason Moon followed the case over years as DNA technology and genealogical databases finally provided answers, unraveling one of the most disturbing serial killer cases in New England history. It's a model of patient, public-interest journalism and one of the finest true crime podcasts ever made.

Why listen: Exceptional local journalism, groundbreaking use of forensic genealogy, and a mystery that took decades to solve — essential listening.

10. Cold

Cold from Dave Cawley is the definitive investigation into the 2009 disappearance of Susan Powell in Utah — a case that became one of the most heartbreaking and infuriating true crime stories in modern American history. Cawley spent years obtaining thousands of documents, recordings, and evidence files through public records requests, and the podcast presents this mountain of material in a way that is both comprehensive and compelling. The story involves an uncooperative husband, a conspiracy-minded father-in-law, and a legal system that failed Susan and her two sons at every turn. Cold is the standard for long-form true crime investigative podcasting.

Why listen: The most thoroughly documented true crime investigation in podcast history — heartbreaking, infuriating, and impossible to stop listening to.

How to Get Started with True Crime Podcasts

If you're new to true crime podcasts, Serial Season 1 is the universal starting point — it's accessible, brilliantly produced, and gives you a clear picture of what the genre is capable of. From there, branch into Crime Junkie for weekly case-of-the-week format, or Casefile if you want something more immersive and serious.

For listeners who prefer their true crime with some levity, My Favorite Murder and Scam Goddess offer completely different — but equally compelling — takes on the genre. And for those who want investigative depth on a specific case or system, Dr. Death, Bear Brook, and Cold are among the best serialized investigations ever produced.
