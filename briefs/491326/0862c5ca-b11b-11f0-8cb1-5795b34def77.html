What Xi’s New Playbook for Trump Means for U.S.-China Trade

Key Takeaways

  • Chinese leader Xi Jinping has adopted a mixed diplomatic strategy towards President Trump, balancing concessions and assertive actions.
  • Intel reported an unexpected third-quarter profit, but its long-term competitive standing in chip manufacturing remains challenged.
  • Major U.S. stock indexes reached record highs, driven by consumer price data that showed a 3% annual increase below economists' expectations.
  • OpenAI CEO Sam Altman is strategically leveraging competition among tech giants to secure essential computing power for the company.

Deep Dive

  • WSJ Chief China Correspondent Lingling Wei detailed Xi Jinping's evolving diplomatic strategy toward President Trump.
  • China, reactive in Trump's first term, now uses a playbook of concessions and assertive responses, including actions concerning rare earth materials.
  • The U.S.-China relationship remains fragile despite a current trade ceasefire, with an upcoming meeting expected to extend the truce.

  • Major U.S. stock indexes, including the Dow, S&P 500, and NASDAQ, reached record highs.
  • This performance was driven by consumer price data indicating a 3% annual increase.
  • The reported 3% increase was below economists' expectations, contributing to the market rally.

  • Investors are showing renewed confidence in Intel, which reported an unexpected third-quarter profit after six consecutive losses.
  • WSJ writer Asa Fitch noted that despite investments from NVIDIA, the U.S. government, and SoftBank, Intel's fundamental challenges persist against rivals like TSMC and Samsung.
  • Fitch explained Intel's competitive process is unproven, and profitability from new chips is uncertain due to high production costs.
  • Intel's upcoming year will be a crucial test, requiring it to attract external customers like NVIDIA or Apple.

  • OpenAI CEO Sam Altman is pursuing a deal-making strategy to secure vital computing power.
  • This strategy involves playing Silicon Valley giants against each other, leading to investments from major semiconductor and cloud companies.
  • WSJ reporter Berber Jin attributes Altman's success to his vision and leveraging the fear of missing out among these companies.
  • OpenAI is currently unprofitable and faces challenges despite these investments.
