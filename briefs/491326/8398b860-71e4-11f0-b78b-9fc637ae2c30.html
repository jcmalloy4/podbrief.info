WSJ What’s News

Trump Turns Up the Heat on Wall Street

Key Takeaways

  • Trump administration pressures banks, raising investor fears of political interference on Wall Street.
  • Justice Department re-investigates 2016 Russia election findings, citing claims of political motives.
  • Government data integrity concerns surface, as a new visa bond program targets overstays.

Deep Dives

Banking Pressure

  • A draft executive order threatens fines for lenders who drop clients for perceived political reasons, specifically targeting alleged discrimination against conservatives and crypto companies.
  • WSJ Finance Editor Alex Frangos notes investors worry about political interference on Wall Street, despite banks citing regulatory compliance and risk management for their client decisions.

Russia Reinvestigation

  • The Justice Department is re-examining the intelligence community's findings on Russian interference in the 2016 election, prompted by claims the original assessment was false and designed to undermine Trump's victory.
  • Attorney General Pam Bondi authorized a U.S. attorney to present evidence to a Washington, D.C. grand jury, with authority extending beyond the prosecutor's usual jurisdiction.

Data & Visas

  • Wall Street expresses concern over government data integrity following the firing of the Bureau of Labor Statistics Commissioner, fearing erosion of investor confidence in U.S. markets.
  • The State Department is piloting a program requiring travelers from certain countries with high visa overstay rates to post a bond of up to $15,000 to deter non-compliance.
