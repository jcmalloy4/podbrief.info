U.S. Stocks Tumble as Trump Threatens Higher Tariffs on China

Key Takeaways

  • President Trump's new tariff threats against China sent U.S. markets falling.
  • The White House initiated mass federal worker layoffs amid the government shutdown.
  • MIT rejected a Trump administration proposal linking federal funding to campus reforms.
  • A ceasefire took effect in Gaza, with efforts underway for hostage and prisoner exchanges.
  • Russia launched drone and missile attacks, targeting Ukraine's energy infrastructure.

Deep Dive

  • President Trump threatened higher tariffs and export controls on China in response to recent actions.
  • China had previously announced restrictions on rare earth materials and launched an anti-monopoly investigation into Qualcomm.
  • WSJ reporter Jack Pitcher explained that these escalations sparked investor fears similar to those seen in April.

  • The White House began mass layoffs of federal workers due to the ongoing government shutdown.
  • Thousands of employees across multiple departments received layoff notices.
  • Democrats blamed President Trump for the shutdown, while Senate Majority Leader John Thune suggested a stopgap funding bill could end it.

  • A ceasefire in Gaza took effect at noon local time, with Israeli troops withdrawing from parts of the Strip.
  • Hamas began deploying security forces within Gaza following the ceasefire.
  • Mediators are working on final details for hostage releases, prisoner exchanges, and border control.

  • MIT became the first university to reject the Trump administration's 'Compact for Academic Excellence' proposal.
  • WSJ reporter Doug Belkin explained MIT rejected the compact because it believes federal grants should be based solely on scientific merit.
  • The administration asserts universities must adhere to new standards for federal funding, challenging their independence.
