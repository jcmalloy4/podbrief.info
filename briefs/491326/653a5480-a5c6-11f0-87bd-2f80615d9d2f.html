WSJ What’s News

The Art of the Peace Deal

Key Takeaways

  • President Trump's diplomatic pressure secured an Israel-Hamas ceasefire and hostage release agreement.
  • The multi-stage Gaza agreement faces significant challenges, including demilitarization and international governance.
  • Federal judges halted National Guard deployments in Chicago and Memphis amid ongoing government shutdown negotiations.
  • Venezuelan opposition leader María Corina Machado received the Nobel Peace Prize for her democracy advocacy.

Deep Dive

  • President Trump's diplomatic efforts brokered a foreign policy victory between Israel and Hamas, leading to a ceasefire.
  • Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu confirmed the agreement to free hostages held by Hamas and establish a ceasefire in Gaza.
  • Netanyahu thanked the U.S. president and his team, including Steve Whitcoff and Jared Kushner, for their involvement.

  • WSJ reporter Anat Peled explained next steps: Israeli forces will withdraw from Gaza, followed by a ceasefire and an exchange of Palestinian prisoners for Israeli hostages, with approximately 20 believed alive.
  • Negotiations were rushed, deferring complex issues like Gaza's demilitarization and international governance for a later stage.
  • U.S. Central Command is slated to lead a stabilization force to monitor the ceasefire and humanitarian aid.
  • The deal's second stage involves a potential board of peace, possibly chaired by Tony Blair, which remains to be worked out.

  • A federal judge halted President Trump's deployment of National Guard troops to Chicago for two weeks, a move praised by Illinois Governor J.B. Pritzker; a similar halt occurred in Memphis.
  • Senate negotiations continue to end a government shutdown, focusing on potential temporary extensions of Affordable Care Act subsidies with new income-based restrictions, likely extending the shutdown into a second weekend.
  • The Justice Department indicted New York Attorney General Letitia James on bank fraud charges, viewed as an escalation against officials who pursued the president.

  • The Bureau of Labor Statistics is recalling staff to publish the delayed September Consumer Price Index, a key economic data point.
  • Americans, particularly lower-income consumers, are increasingly falling behind on car payments, with subprime auto loan delinquencies reaching record highs, signaling economic strain.
  • Venezuelan opposition leader María Corina Machado was awarded this year's Nobel Peace Prize for her efforts in promoting democracy and combating dictatorship in Venezuela.
