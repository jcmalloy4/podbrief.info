What the Fed’s interest-rate cut means for you and the economy

Key Takeaways

  • The Federal Reserve cut its benchmark interest rate by 0.25% for the first time this year, with dissent.
  • Former CDC Director Susan Monarez testified about pressure to alter vaccine schedules and her subsequent dismissal.
  • The Israel-Gaza conflict escalates amid a ground assault and stalled ceasefire negotiations.
  • Concerns are rising over potential changes to the national childhood hepatitis B vaccine schedule.

Deep Dive

  • The Federal Reserve cut its benchmark interest rate by a quarter percentage point, its first reduction this year.
  • Reuters reporter Ann Saphir explained that lower borrowing rates are intended to encourage hiring and economic expansion.
  • The decision was not unanimous, with new board member Stephen Myron advocating for a larger rate cut, sparking concerns about political influence.
  • Analysts generally believe the Fed maintains its independence despite internal disagreements.

  • Former CDC Director Susan Monarez testified that Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. pressured her to alter the childhood vaccination schedule and fire officials.
  • Monarez stated she refused the directives, leading to her dismissal after 29 days in the role; Kennedy disputes her account.
  • Deborah Howry, a former CDC official, testified that COVID vaccine guidance changes were announced via social media without scientific data.
  • The CDC's vaccine advisory panel is now composed of Kennedy appointees, some holding anti-vaccine stances.

  • Senator Bill Cassidy, a physician, highlighted the success of the current hepatitis B vaccine schedule, preventing infections in fewer than 20 babies annually.
  • Cassidy raised concerns that altering the existing schedule could impact insurance coverage for essential vaccinations.
  • A national vaccine panel is preparing to meet to discuss potentially delaying hepatitis B vaccines for newborns.
  • Several states, including California, Oregon, Washington, and Hawaii, have issued independent vaccine recommendations, advising COVID shots for pregnant women and children aged six months to two years.

  • Israel's ground assault on Gaza City has resulted in nearly 100 deaths and 400 wounded over two days.
  • Satellite data indicates 78% of buildings in Gaza City are destroyed or partially damaged.
  • A UN-backed report suggests famine in the area, a finding contested by Israel; military officials state approximately 40% of the population has left.
  • Diplomatic efforts for a ceasefire between Israel and Hamas have stalled, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio expressing pessimism.
  • Wall Street Journal reporter Alex Ward stated Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu favors military action over diplomacy to resolve the conflict.
