Apple News Today

Trump didn’t take military action against Venezuela in his first term. Here’s what changed.

Key Takeaways

  • The U.S. military operation in Venezuela targets the Maduro regime, driven by various administration priorities.
  • U.S. justifications for action, including drug interdiction, face scrutiny due to a lack of evidence.
  • The Trump administration expresses interest in Venezuela's oil and asserts American dominance in the Western Hemisphere.
  • Conflicting statements from U.S. officials complicate the narrative surrounding the Venezuela operation.
  • Venezuelans face intensified repression and uncertainty regarding their future and legal status in the U.S.

Deep Dive

  • Venezuela has been a long-standing target for the Trump administration due to interconnected priorities.
  • Key figures, including Marco Rubio, have advocated for regime change, viewing Venezuela as supporting socialist dictatorships in Cuba and Nicaragua.
  • Donald Trump's stance evolved, pressuring the Venezuelan military while being reluctant to commit full U.S. intervention resources.
  • Immigration concerns, particularly Stephen Miller's nativist perspective, linked Venezuela to an influx of migrants, framing Maduro as a destabilizing force.

  • The administration's 'war on drugs' includes U.S. strikes on boats, yet the guest noted a lack of verified information on cargo or individuals.
  • The U.S. government cited national self-defense against drug smuggling for bombing small vessels in Venezuela.
  • The guest questions this justification, noting the majority of U.S. overdoses stem from fentanyl, not originating from Venezuela.
  • The U.S. Coast Guard has not seized fentanyl in the Venezuelan region, further challenging the stated grounds for action.

  • The U.S. president has expressed a desire for Venezuela to supply oil to the U.S.
  • There is also an interest for American companies to invest in Venezuela's oil infrastructure.
  • The feasibility of U.S. investment is uncertain due to significant capital requirements and the speculative nature of these aspirations.
  • The desire for American dominance and oil symbolism may outweigh actual U.S. oil needs, as the U.S. is not currently in need of external supply.

  • The administration has increased rhetoric regarding American dominance in the Western Hemisphere, linking it to discussions about Greenland.
  • Stephen Miller's comments on CNN revealed the administration's thinking, describing 'civilizational' explanations for U.S. global involvement post-WWII.
  • The administration believes the U.S. is justified in asserting its interests globally.
  • The guest found it difficult to pinpoint a geopolitical explanation for this rhetoric, suggesting it stems from idiosyncratic interpretations of history and a feeling of grievance.

  • Trump asserted the U.S. would 'run' Venezuela, which contradicts Marco Rubio's framing of the operation.
  • Rubio described the operation as a law enforcement action to effectuate an existing indictment against Nicolas Maduro, who has a $50 million reward.
  • Venezuela's Vice President, Delcy Rodriguez, a Maduro loyalist, has been appointed to a new position, creating a contradiction with the U.S. stance on Maduro's illegitimacy.
  • Trump issued a threat against Delcy Rodriguez despite her elevated role challenging the Venezuelan opposition's popular mandate.

  • Venezuelans are experiencing a chaotic situation with intensified repression by the government and paramilitary groups called colectivos.
  • The crackdown followed an emergency declaration by Maduro before his perceived ouster.
  • The future for Venezuelans is uncertain, with concerns about ongoing government repression and human fallout.
  • Tens of thousands of Venezuelans with provisional legal status in the U.S. face apprehension, detention, and deportation due to disregard for immigration law.
