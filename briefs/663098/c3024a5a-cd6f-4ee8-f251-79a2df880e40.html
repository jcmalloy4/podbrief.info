How ICE entered its most aggressive era — and what comes next

Key Takeaways

  • Federal immigration enforcement expanded significantly under President Trump, notably in Minneapolis where officers killed two citizens.
  • ICE's post-9/11 counterterrorism mission has primarily shifted to arresting and deporting immigrants lacking legal status.
  • ICE expanded its agent force by approximately 12,000, with reduced vetting and a directive to deport as many people as possible.
  • Deportations from within the U.S. interior substantially increased, and immigration detention numbers have more than doubled.
  • Accountability for serious harm caused by immigration agents has diminished due to eliminated oversight mechanisms.
  • ICE utilizes over 1,000 "287G agreements" to deputize local police for immigration enforcement, often for minor infractions.
  • A leaked ICE memo suggests officers may no longer require a judicial warrant to enter homes, a policy likely to face court challenges.
  • Growing public criticism, even among Trump supporters, suggests a potential, albeit uncertain, shift in the administration's immigration policy.

Deep Dive

  • Federal immigration enforcement expanded under President Trump, increasing budgets and agent deployments nationwide.
  • In Minneapolis, federal officers killed two American citizens, Renee Goode and Alex Predi, in separate incidents.
  • President Trump later called Predi's death "terrible," leading to leadership changes and agents involved being placed on administrative leave.
  • Caitlin Dickerson suggests Trump's shift in rhetoric was driven by optics and public reaction to video evidence of harsh enforcement actions.

  • ICE hired approximately 12,000 new officers under the Trump administration, offering significant pay bumps and bonuses.
  • Vetting and training for these new officers have been significantly reduced, raising questions about qualifications and backgrounds.
  • The directive for ICE agents is to "deport as many people as possible, no matter the cost"; approximately 70% in custody have no criminal record.
  • ICE agents have been observed stopping individuals based on factors like accent and skin color, conducting mass arrests in locations such as Home Depot.

  • The Trump administration reported over 600,000 deportations, with more than 300,000 occurring from within the country's interior.
  • The number of individuals in immigration detention has doubled since the end of the Biden administration, reaching over 70,000.
  • This increase is partly attributed to a $45 billion expansion of detention facilities.
  • The administration explores expedited removal without judicial review due to courts slowing deportations, described as a "spaghetti to the wall" strategy.

  • ICE relies on local law enforcement cooperation, described as a "force multiplier," through expanded "287G agreements," now over a thousand.
  • These agreements allow local police to deputize officers for immigration enforcement or provide access to records and jails, often ensnaring individuals with minor infractions.
  • Accountability for serious harm caused by immigration enforcement agents has diminished due to the elimination of various investigative layers.
  • Recent appropriations acts provided significant funding to ICE without implementing added accountability measures.

  • A leaked ICE memo suggests officers may no longer need a judicial warrant to enter a home, a significant shift from previous advice.
  • The memo's legal standing is uncertain due to its limited distribution and lack of official copies, suggesting potential administrative hesitations.
  • The legal process for challenging this new interpretation of ICE's authority is expected to involve court challenges, potentially reaching the Supreme Court.
  • Congress could implement changes by adding stipulations to new appropriations bills or by updating immigration law to redefine ICE's enforcement priorities.

  • The administration's focus on high-profile cases overshadows widespread, less-publicized deportations of individuals with long-term community ties.
  • Focusing enforcement on specific cities like Minneapolis creates a myopic view, as similar actions likely occur nationwide without public attention.
  • Even some of President Trump's supporters are criticizing ICE's work, drawing parallels to past family separations.
  • A policy shift from the Trump administration is anticipated due to the President's attachment to his immigration agenda, though its magnitude and duration are uncertain.
