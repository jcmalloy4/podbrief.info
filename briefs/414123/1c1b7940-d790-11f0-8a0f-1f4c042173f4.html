The Matt Walsh Show

Ep. 1706 - Why I Don’t Buy The Charlie Kirk Conspiracy Theories

Key Takeaways

  • The host refutes conspiracy theories surrounding Charlie Kirk's murder, presenting factual evidence.
  • Tyler Robinson is identified as the killer, supported by DNA evidence and multiple confessions.
  • Conspiracy theories are critiqued for their lack of evidence and logical inconsistencies.
  • Unanswered questions remain regarding potential foreknowledge of the attack by other individuals.

Deep Dive

  • The host announced a focus on facts regarding the public and gruesome murder of Charlie Kirk, which was broadcast globally.
  • The shock of Kirk's public execution led to trauma and the proliferation of conspiracy theories.
  • Conspiracy theories, akin to those around Sandy Hook, emerged from a public need to comprehend the horrific event.

  • The host asserted Tyler Robinson's guilt, citing his demeanor in court, described as familiar to sociopaths and narcissistic murderers.
  • Key evidence includes Robinson's DNA found on a screwdriver, a towel, and the rifle used in the shooting, mathematically excluding other suspects.
  • Additional evidence comprises a text message from Robinson expressing a desire to 'take out Charlie Kirk' and a note stating the opportunity to do so, photographed by the FBI.
  • Robinson made three confessions after the killing, including wondering about suicide and apologizing via Discord, turning himself in to local sheriffs.

  • The host contrasted the UVU police officer's direct investigation, which identified the shooting location and captured the shooter on camera, with circulating conspiracy theories.
  • Evidence against Tyler Robinson includes his clenched fists visible in security footage and his unresponsiveness after the shooting, noted by a former coworker.
  • The host argues the evidence against Robinson is overwhelming, derived from multiple independent sources, while conspiracy theories are based on non-existent proof and hearsay.

  • The host directly questioned the logic of conspiracy theories, specifically claims about powerful conspirators leaving witnesses alive.
  • A particular claim regarding a bullet from Tyler Robinson's rifle causing decapitation was critiqued.
  • A demonstration video concerning bullet trajectory was argued to be flawed, failing to account for crucial variables like the shooter's elevated position and shot angle, referencing a Phoenix Ammunition diagram.

  • Reasons for the popularity of conspiracy theories include ignorance of firearms, frustration with organizations like Turning Point USA, and a desire for catharsis.
  • The host notes the intoxicating nature of fictional narratives in real-world events, comparing it to the appeal of true crime shows.
  • While acknowledging understandable distrust in government, the host states it does not constitute evidence for alternative theories, asserting Robinson's guilt is clear.

  • The host raised several unanswered questions, including social media posts predicting the attack and subsequent posts celebrating the event, linked to the username 'Tally Hall Album' and the shooter's associates.
  • Further questions concern Tyler Robinson's text messages about engraving bullets, his roommate Lance Twiggs's potential foreknowledge and subsequent disappearance, and the scrubbing of social media by 'Armed Queers Salt Lake City'.
  • The host posited a potential conspiracy involving the 'militant left,' citing their predictions, history of political violence, and perceived benefit from Kirk's death.
  • The host defended Erica Kirk, Charlie Kirk's widow, highlighting her strength and dignity, and asking that she be left alone to mourn.
