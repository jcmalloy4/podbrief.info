An Immigration Shutdown Is President Trump's Big Winning Issue

Key Takeaways

  • President Trump's proposed migration moratorium from nearly 20 countries holds popular support.
  • Polling indicates a significant portion of self-identified Democrats align with socialist ideologies.
  • An expert argues that the U.S. faces an "under-incarceration problem" prioritizing criminals over public safety.
  • The societal costs of crime extend to economic impact, public disorder, and psychic tolls.
  • Immigration policy debates increasingly involve concerns about cultural preservation beyond crime rates.

Deep Dive

  • President Trump's announced moratorium on migration from nearly 20 countries shows 57% support among likely voters, a +24 net positive.
  • The policy significantly energizes the MAGA base, with Republican voter enthusiasm increasing by eight points.
  • Guest Rich Barris suggests 10% of 'unsure' voters may be hesitant to express true opinions to pollsters.
  • The strategy aims to exclude 'welfare charges' from the third world, which is presented as a politically popular stance.

  • Polling data indicates approximately 60% of self-identified Democrats now align with socialist ideologies.
  • This trend signifies a notable shift from traditional liberalism within the Democratic Party.
  • The identification with socialism is observed to be higher among foreign-born individuals, with 60% identifying as democratic socialist and 12% as socialist.

  • The discussion critiques the historical outcomes of socialist policies, citing examples from Argentina and Mexico.
  • It contrasts the 'village morality' appeal of socialism with the discipline required for effective economic policies.
  • Polling data indicates that while policies like rent control are unpopular with economists, they can gain political traction, especially among certain demographic groups.

  • Expert Heather Mac Donald asserts an "under-incarceration problem" exists, driven by recent violent crimes from individuals with extensive arrest records.
  • Mac Donald argues the criminal justice system prioritizes the freedom of criminals over public safety, leading to predictable violent outcomes.
  • She states that focusing on avoiding a "disparate impact" on Black criminals has led to under-incarceration and decriminalization, making public officials complicit.

  • Crime's broader societal costs include increased disorder, minor offenses like turnstile jumping, and disruptive behavior on public transportation.
  • It impacts housing affordability and job location choices, contributing to friction in American life.
  • The discussion emphasizes the psychic tolls of crime and suggests increased capacity in mental institutions and prisons for violent individuals with combined mental illness and drug addiction.
  • Reduced prison capacity and a focus on rehabilitation over incarceration are criticized for contributing to increased crime.

  • Data quality for illegal immigrants regarding crime rates is acknowledged as poor.
  • Crime rates are noted to vary by immigrant origin country; Asians have lower rates, while Hispanics have higher rates than whites but lower than blacks.
  • Mac Donald suggests crime rates tend to increase with subsequent generations of immigrants.
  • The more significant issue in immigration policy is argued to be cultural change and a society's right to preserve its cultural heritage.
