TIP774: Being Greedy While Others are Fearful w/ Shawn O'Malley

Key Takeaways

  • Active, deep-dive research into individual stocks can outperform passive ETF investing when done carefully.
  • A concentrated portfolio of high-conviction, long-term investments in compounding businesses drives significant returns.
  • Well-known consumer-facing tech and strong retail brands can offer attractive opportunities when purchased at a discount.
  • Adobe demonstrates resilience against AI disruption fears due to its enterprise focus, recurring revenue, and high switching costs.
  • Rigorous critical thinking, particularly through writing investment theses, is key to developing investor skill and identifying core business drivers.

Deep Dive

  • The guest trimmed Reddit from over 5% to 2% of the portfolio after its price increased significantly, deeming implied growth at $250 per share unrealistic.
  • Uber is viewed bullishly due to market concerns about autonomous vehicles, presenting an opportunity for Uber to integrate this technology while its core business grows.
  • Ferrari, admired for its strong brand power and pricing ability, remains on the watchlist due to the conservative investment approach requiring a larger margin of safety.
  • Ulta was also trimmed from the portfolio due to valuation concerns.

  • The guest shifted from passive learning via reading to active application through deep-dive research and investing, managing significant net worth portions.
  • Overcoming imposter syndrome, the guest moved from defaulting to ETFs to actively investing in individual stocks.
  • Investment philosophy now emphasizes few, high-conviction investments with compounding intrinsic value, aiming for 20% annual growth.
  • The approach prioritizes identifying companies with a minimum five-year holding potential, rejecting short-term trading strategies.

  • The Intrinsic Value Portfolio includes large-cap tech companies like Alphabet, Uber, and Reddit, with Adobe as a recent consideration.
  • Preference is given to consumer-facing tech like Alphabet over complex businesses such as NVIDIA due to easier understanding of brand and network effects.
  • Companies like Reddit are viewed as advertising businesses leveraging network effects, while Uber is a logistics company tied to the real world.
  • Even mega-cap stocks like Alphabet, which fluctuated between $146 and $290 within a year, offer substantial gains through patient investing.

  • Uber's business model is framed as a logistics company enhanced by subscriptions, network effects, and advertising, with Uber One boosting engagement.
  • Unlike hardware-focused tech, Uber leverages technology for scale and diversification, creating a sticky consumer ecosystem.
  • Investment decisions are guided by consumer insights, recognizing the power of deeply integrated services like Spotify, Reddit, and Adobe.

  • The Intrinsic Value Portfolio includes retail companies such as Nike, Lululemon, and Ulta Beauty, focusing on brand loyalty, quality, and pricing control.
  • Lululemon is considered a favorable risk-return profile despite a 50% stock decline and low PE ratio, citing consistent sales and international growth.
  • Strong brands like Lululemon and Nike are believed to endure for decades, offering long-term dominance and consumer loyalty despite retail challenges.

  • Nike's inclusion in the portfolio is a turnaround situation, noting its flat stock and earnings per share over the past decade.
  • The investment strategy uses a reverse discounted cash flow model, expressing confidence in Nike's long-term viability.
  • Despite recent struggles in the US, Nike's global brand, scale, and ability to invest significantly in R&D suggest competitive advantages, with a 25% average return on invested capital over five years.
  • The guest believes Nike, down over 50% since 2020, is undervalued due to market focus on past strategic errors.

  • Despite being labeled an 'AI loser,' Adobe has shown continuous net income and revenue growth of nearly 50% since 2021, maintaining double-digit annual growth.
  • 95% of Adobe's revenue comes from recurring subscriptions, primarily from corporate customers who find products like Photoshop and Acrobat integral to workflows.
  • Adobe's Firefly AI is trained on legally licensed data, offering a commercially viable solution for major clients facing copyright risks with other generative AI tools.
  • High switching costs for enterprise customers, combined with the reliability and familiarity of Adobe's professional suite, create a significant barrier for competitors.
  • Adobe, like Microsoft, is well-positioned to integrate new technologies into existing products, with significant switching costs providing time to adapt.

  • The guest presents an intrinsic value estimate for Adobe north of $500 per share, with a 20% margin of safety implying a $400 target price.
  • The stock is considered undervalued at $330 per share, leading to increased position despite community controversy over AI disruption fears.
  • Acknowledging risks like AI image generation, the guest believes fears are overblown and that creative control for clients like Pepsi and Coca-Cola justifies Adobe's value.
  • The best investment returns often come from contrarian bets on companies facing market skepticism, buying great businesses when out of favor.

  • The research process begins by downloading company filings and using Notebook LM to parse information, ensuring reliance solely on provided source material.
  • Publicly available research, including podcasts, substacks, Twitter threads, and Value Investors Club forums, is reviewed.
  • The core process involves connecting gathered information to clearly explain the business model and investment thesis, even to a fifth grader.
  • Significant time is spent writing, editing, and rewriting thoughts to gain deep appreciation for a company's history and decision-making.
  • Critical thinking through writing investment theses differentiates investors, building analytical skills and identifying key business drivers beyond numerical data.
