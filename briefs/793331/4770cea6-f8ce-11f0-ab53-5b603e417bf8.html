RWH065: Joyful Excellence w/ Brad Stulberg

Key Takeaways

  • Excellence is an ongoing path of deep satisfaction, not a fixed destination.
  • Pursuing excellence requires combining strong commitment with personal vulnerability.
  • Algorithmic distraction and pseudo-excellence hinder genuine mastery and satisfaction.
  • Top performers often live intentionally minimalist, focused, and seemingly mundane lives.
  • Consistency, rest, and "fierce self-kindness" are integral to sustainable high performance.
  • Aligning life and career with core values is crucial for true success and resilience.

Deep Dive

  • Brad Stulberg's new book, "The Way of Excellence," draws inspiration from Robert Pirsig's "Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance."
  • Pirsig's concept of 'quality' is defined as a deep caring between an actor and their act, leading to a merging of the doer and the deed.
  • This philosophy influenced investors like Nick Sleep and Zach at Nomad Fund, who applied it to their focus on long-term quality in companies.

  • Excellence is defined as involved engagement, requiring deep care for something worthwhile that aligns with personal values and goals.
  • The pursuit of excellence demands both commitment and vulnerability, a process that shapes the individual.
  • High performers, including mountain biker Kate Courtney, violinist Hilary Hahn, and climber Alex Honnold, exemplify this involved engagement.
  • Peak experiences are described by high performers not intellectually, but through a 'feeling tone' of deep satisfaction.

  • Ubiquitous digital distraction, fueled by algorithmically engineered content, prevents individuals from living excellent lives by offering existential validation.
  • Constant access to notifications and social media makes it tempting to disengage from more meaningful pursuits, leading to alienation.
  • A market of quick fixes and fads, termed "pseudo-excellence," promises unrealistic results, further challenging individuals seeking genuine mastery.

  • Excellence is linked to "love," signifying deep care, consistent practice, commitment, and perseverance, contrasting with alienated, ego-driven approaches.
  • Cultivating excellence involves practical methods such as identifying 2 to 5 core values to maintain meaningful focus and navigate trade-offs.
  • Dr. Avedis Donabedian initiated a quality movement in healthcare, crediting him with developing sophisticated tracking systems and dashboards in modern hospitals.

  • The popular ideal of work-life balance is an illusion that often leads to mediocrity, as individuals struggle to divide attention equally.
  • Instead, individuals should intensely focus on a few value-aligned priorities at different times in their lives, akin to different seasons.
  • The "identity house" metaphor suggests diversifying roles (e.g., parent, athlete, coffee lover) to build resilience during setbacks in one area.
  • Minimum Effective Doses (MEDs) are crucial for maintaining important relationships and personal well-being during intense focus on a primary pursuit.

  • A distinction exists between "reckless obsession," which is compulsive and addiction-like, and "healthy obsession," involving deep care and control over one's pursuits.
  • Reckless obsession leads to degraded performance, burnout, anxiety, depression, and unethical behavior, with Elizabeth Holmes cited as a cautionary example.
  • Truly successful individuals manage their obsessions to avoid constant 24/7 work, highlighting that rest and renewal are integral to high performance.

  • Sustainable excellence stems from relentless consistency and breaking down large goals into small, regular steps, mirroring the law of compounding in investing.
  • Building wealth from $1 million to $10 million is more effectively achieved through small, smart investments over time that compound, rather than large, high-risk bets.
  • Consistent daily 'deposits' and actively managing 'bad days' prevent them from escalating, a principle likened to minimizing losses in a volatile market.
  • Tom Gaynor of Markel Corporation exemplified consistency by showing up daily during the COVID-19 pandemic, embodying a philosophy of 'one foot in front of the other.'

  • "Fierce self-kindness" is a crucial element for achieving excellence, complementing self-discipline by providing the resilience needed to persist through difficulties.
  • This approach contrasts with traditional, often harsh, self-discipline methods, advocating instead for a balanced perspective.
  • The guest reflects on navigating a period of severe depression, attributing their survival to valuing life over suffering and trusting the process of therapy and incremental adjustments.

  • Routines should serve individual objectives, such as being a better writer or family member, rather than becoming a burden or elaborate schedule.
  • Daily practices include 1-1.5 hours of distraction-free deep work, at least 45 minutes of movement (formal training or walking), and prioritizing sleep.
  • Weekly practices involve a "digital Sabbath" (12-24 hours without phone/computer for rest), over an hour of walking outside for creativity, and planned social interaction.

  • Finding joy in intense, values-aligned work is crucial, as a 'joyless grind' can signal an overscheduled or misaligned approach.
  • Sustained greatness is characterized by genuine joy and reverence for the craft, even amidst intensity, rather than constant anger or suffering, critiquing approaches like David Goggins'.
  • Completion rituals, such as NBA coach Gregg Popovich's practice of organizing team dinners after wins and losses, provide necessary points of completion and reflection.
  • Intentionally stepping back to reflect and celebrate milestones is crucial for long-term success and well-being in the continuous pursuit of excellence.
