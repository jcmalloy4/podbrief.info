TIP770: Mastering the Markets w/ Andrew Brenton

Key Takeaways

  • Markets are becoming less efficient due to structural shifts like passive investing and short-term trading.
  • Cognitive biases, such as loss aversion, contribute to market inefficiencies and extreme reactions.
  • Value investors require significant patience, as market recognition of undervalued assets can take years.
  • Intrinsic value should be calculated based on future free cash flows, not short-term price movements.
  • Adhering to a fundamental investment strategy is crucial, even during periods of underperformance.

Deep Dive

  • Andrew Brenton states that markets have become less efficient over his 27-year career, contrary to the efficient market hypothesis.
  • Factors contributing to this inefficiency include passive investing, low interest rates, and technology-driven herding behavior.
  • A decline in fundamental investors and a shift towards short-term trading at quantitative firms represent a structural market change.
  • Opportunities for value investors are now greater than 10-20 years ago, though recognizing value may require waiting longer than five years.

  • Andrew Brenton discusses the importance of avoiding 'resulting,' where investors judge decisions solely by short-term outcomes.
  • He references Cliff Asness's definition of a bubble as many stocks trading at prices unjustifiable by reasonable models.
  • Current market conditions feature struggling industries alongside a rising broader market, primarily driven by tech and AI winners.
  • Brenton suggests the current environment shares similarities in popular sentiment and market multiples with the 1999 tech bubble.

  • Turtle Creek Asset Management considers Floor & Decor a mispriced stock in their portfolio.
  • The firm's approach involves forecasting 10-20 years out, identifying companies with long-term opportunities.
  • Floor & Decor democratizes hard surface flooring by sourcing directly from manufacturers, creating a structural cost advantage.
  • The company became attractive enough for Turtle Creek's portfolio three years prior due to prevailing recession fears.

  • Turtle Creek Asset Management employs a 'buy and optimize' strategy, distinct from a static 'buy and hold' approach.
  • This strategy uses stock price volatility as a lever to enhance returns within a core buy-and-hold framework, stemming from private equity experience.
  • For Floor & Decor, despite revenue and store count doubling, cyclical downturns impacted earnings, leading to significant stock price volatility.
  • The optimization involves adjusting position sizes based on valuation changes, reducing exposure as the stock price increases and the margin of safety potentially decreases.

  • Floor & Decor, a company with no debt, reinvests all capital into geographic expansion, aiming for long-term growth.
  • The company is actively taking market share from competitors, even amidst a weak North American economy.
  • The soft economy has made some companies, like Floor & Decor, cheap enough for long-term investors to consider.
  • A weak economy can sometimes benefit strong companies like Floor & Decor by driving weaker competitors out of business.

  • Andrew Brenton defines intrinsic value as the present value of all future free cash flows, discounted at a consistent 10% rate.
  • Turtle Creek Asset Management seeks a significant gap between a company's intrinsic value and its current share price, adjusted for specific risks.
  • While Floor & Decor currently has a P/E ratio of approximately 30, Brenton's portfolio typically holds stocks with a P/E around 11.
  • Accounting rules can necessitate material adjustments to reported earnings, but Floor & Decor's intrinsic value remains attractive despite depressed earnings.

  • Kinsale Capital is a specialty insurer with a 1.5% market share in the excess and surplus market, a figure that doubled over five years.
  • Brenton describes Kinsale as a technology-driven company with a significant cost advantage and strong underwriting capabilities.
  • The investment thesis for Kinsale Capital has developed as expected, demonstrating consistent intelligence and a thoughtful underwriting approach.
  • The company focuses on small and medium-sized businesses, adapting to market conditions and achieving high marks across evaluation criteria.

  • Kinsale Capital strategically reduces policy writing during periods of intense competition to focus on profitable business.
  • The company's first share buyback authorization indicates management believes the stock trades at a discount to intrinsic value.
  • Brenton views jagged share price movements as beneficial for their investment strategy, which favors low intrinsic value volatility.
  • Kinsale management's deep understanding of the company's intrinsic value makes share repurchases a positive indicator, likened to slow-motion management buyouts.

  • Turtle Creek Asset Management has experienced periods of both outperformance and underperformance, including recent lags attributed partly to AI developments.
  • Andrew Brenton affirms his commitment to their established investment strategy despite market fluctuations, emphasizing conviction in high-quality, shareholder-focused companies.
  • The firm measures performance based on changes in intrinsic value on a company-by-company basis, rather than solely on mark-to-market prices.
  • With a high active share of 97.5%, Turtle Creek's portfolio significantly deviates from market indices like the S&P 500.
