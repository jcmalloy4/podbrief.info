The Agonies of Brett Kavanaugh

Key Takeaways

  • Supreme Court extended a SNAP benefits stay; Justice Jackson dissented from the full court's action.
  • Jeffrey Epstein emails detail his conduct and ties to a powerful network aware of his abuse.
  • DOJ challenged California's new congressional map, alleging it constitutes a racial gerrymander.
  • Supreme Court arguments covered military contractor liability and religious rights under RLUIPA.
  • Court denied Kim Davis's petition to overrule Obergefell; granted review of Mississippi ballot rules.
  • Justices Thomas and Gorsuch questioned Congress's power over territories in a cert denial.

Deep Dive

  • Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson initially issued an administrative stay in a SNAP benefits lawsuit.
  • The full Supreme Court later extended this stay, with Justice Jackson noting she would have denied the extension.
  • The order's wording suggested the full court acted without Justice Jackson's referral of the matter.

  • Leaked emails reveal Epstein advised prominent figures like Ken Starr on sexual misconduct issues.
  • Epstein also advised Steve Bannon on discrediting a Kavanaugh accuser, highlighting his broad influence.
  • Emails detail interactions with Donald Trump, including discussions about women and potential awareness of misdeeds.
  • The discussion of Epstein's ties led to a presidential investigation announcement, while also being labeled a hoax.

  • The Department of Justice challenged California's new congressional map, alleging it constitutes a racial gerrymander.
  • The challenge is based on claims that Democrats feared a Latino voice would be undervalued compared to a white voice.
  • The DOJ also noted concerns that Texas might revert to Jim Crow-era discrimination.

  • The Supreme Court heard Hensley v. Flora Corporation, concerning lawsuits against military contractors.
  • Justice Brett Kavanaugh expressed strong opinions during argument, focusing on potential contractor liability and interrupting parties.
  • An exchange saw Justice Gorsuch reportedly tell Kavanaugh he was being rude, described as signaling "end times" at the court.
  • Kavanaugh and Alito appeared sympathetic to contractor immunity, while Gorsuch leaned against it.

  • RICO v. United States questioned whether "fugitive tolling" applies to supervised release cases without statutory inclusion.
  • Coney Island Auto Parts focused on Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 60, regarding relief from a judgment.
  • Justices considered if seven years was too long for motions to vacate void judgments under a reasonable time standard.

  • The case Lander v. Louisiana Department of Corrections concerned damages under the Religious Land Use and Institutionalized Persons Act (RLUIPA).
  • A Rastafarian inmate with long hair was forcibly shaved after presenting prison officials with a Fifth Circuit opinion.
  • Justice Kavanaugh questioned whether RLUIPA contained a "clear statement" for allowing monetary damages.
  • Extensive and ideologically diverse amicus support for the plaintiff, including from the Trump Justice Department, was noted.

  • Supreme Court arguments questioned whether federal spending clause legislation binds individuals or only states.
  • Justices expressed concern that a ruling against enforceability could undermine numerous federal statutes and programs.
  • This issue impacts religious inmate rights and other federal spending-powered initiatives.
  • Justices Gorsuch and Alito focused on circuit splits regarding a request for a ruling that contradicted numerous appellate court decisions.

  • GeoGroup v. Menocal involves ICE detainees suing a government contractor for alleged forced labor, which led to a detainee's death.
  • The contractor claims immunity under the "yearsly defense," arguing ICE directed the labor, but lost in district court.
  • The Supreme Court is now considering whether the contractor can immediately appeal that order.

  • The Supreme Court denied Kim Davis's cert petition, which sought to overrule Obergefell v. Hodges.
  • The Court granted cert in a case challenging Mississippi's rule for counting late-arriving mail-in ballots.
  • The Fifth Circuit's ruling against Mississippi's rule, allowing ballots postmarked by election day to be counted within five days, was noted.
  • A cert denial in United States v. Veneno included a statement from Justices Thomas and Gorsuch questioning Congress's power over territories.
