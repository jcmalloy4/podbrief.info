192: A Conversation with Ken Burns and Sarah Botstein: The American Revolution and WWII

Key Takeaways

  • Ken Burns' new 12-hour film series, "The American Revolution," premieres November 16th on PBS.
  • The American Revolution is portrayed as a complex, multifaceted civil and world war, often obscured by myth.
  • Filmmakers combine traditional storytelling with advanced techniques, including custom maps, CGI, and extensive archival research.
  • The U.S. Constitution's amendability and the Declaration's evolving concept of "all" are highlighted as foundational for national growth.
  • The 2007 documentary "The War" used a unique four-town approach to illustrate World War II's global interconnectedness and immense human cost.

Deep Dive

  • Filmmaker Ken Burns labels the American Revolution as the "most important historical event since the birth of Christ."
  • This assertion is intended to provoke thoughtful discussion about world history and the unique formation of the U.S. as a nation of citizens.
  • The conflict, which took nearly a decade to produce into a film, is characterized as a long, bloody civil and world war, often obscured by myth.

  • The American Revolution documentary series emphasizes the war's complex origins, including internal divisions and a civil war dynamic among colonists.
  • Key catalysts included the Proclamation of 1763, which restricted westward expansion, and other grievances such as taxes and representation.
  • Multiple potential starting points are considered, including the Boston Tea Party (December 1773), the Boston Massacre (March 1770), or the presence of standing troops.

  • Thomas Paine's "These are the times that try men's souls" (December 1776) reflects a critical low point for the Continental Army.
  • Despite severe hardships, George Washington's army achieved a pivotal victory at Trenton on December 26, 1776.
  • The new documentary depicts Washington's strategic turnaround with animated maps and sweeping visuals, garnering praise from reviewers.

  • Filmmakers discuss the evolution of map depiction in Ken Burns' documentaries, from the Civil War to 'The American Revolution.'
  • Custom "Molly maps," topographical maps created by a cartographer over two years, integrate archival maps and CGI for historical accuracy.
  • CGI and 3D animation are used to depict specific battle locations like Yorktown, Long Island, and Trenton, making complex military history accessible.

  • Filmmakers integrate modern technology like CGI and reenactments with traditional "old-fashioned" storytelling techniques to create authentic narratives.
  • They explicitly avoid over-reliance on CGI and have ruled out the use of AI in their documentary work.
  • Scriptwriting is an iterative process, with drafts completed during shooting and evolving with new research, prioritizing factual accuracy.

  • The documentary explores how changing the Declaration of Independence's core phrase from "property" to "pursuit of happiness" broadened the Revolution's promise.
  • The American Revolution was fought by a diverse group beyond elite property owners, including those without property, teenagers, and immigrants.
  • The significance of the word "all" in the Declaration of Independence is examined for its eventual implications for extending rights to marginalized groups, despite slow progress.

  • The U.S. Constitution's instructions and permission for amendment were crucial for its ratification, as the public anticipated changes to enshrine their rights.
  • The amendments provide specific freedoms, such as no establishment of religion and freedom of the press, seen as essential lessons from history.
  • The discussion transitions to World War II, described as the "greatest cataclysm in human history" and the subject of their 2007 documentary, 'The War.'

  • The 2007 documentary 'The War' focused on four American towns—Waterbury, Connecticut; Mobile, Alabama; Sacramento, California; and Luverne, Minnesota—to capture a wide range of experiences.
  • Filmmakers integrated European and Pacific theater events simultaneously to show interconnectedness, such as D-Day and the Battle of Saipan.
  • The conflict resulted in 60 million deaths and fundamentally changed global dynamics, as noted by Annette Gordon-Reed.

  • Franklin D. Roosevelt leveraged major events like Pearl Harbor and Germany's declaration of war to unite the country during WWII, preserving American democracy.
  • The victory in WWII depended on American manufacturing, Soviet sacrifice, and collective Allied efforts.
  • Ken Burns connects personal sacrifices, like Rebecca Tanner losing five sons in the Revolutionary War, to themes in films like 'Saving Private Ryan,' emphasizing the need to remember individual soldiers.
