SYSK's 12 Days of Christmas… Toys: How Play-Doh Works

Key Takeaways

  • Play-Doh originated as a wallpaper cleaner before its reinvention as a children's toy.
  • Early success was significantly propelled by strategic television promotion on 'Captain Kangaroo'.
  • Over 950 million pounds of Play-Doh have been sold since 1956, across more than 2 billion cans.
  • The toy's unique texture is achieved through a precise chemical balance of starch, water, and additives.
  • Play-Doh has expanded into various product lines, including licensed sets and 3D printing devices.
  • Hasbro celebrated Play-Doh's 50th anniversary in 2006 with a limited-edition scented perfume.

Deep Dive

  • Play-Doh was invented by Joe McVickers as Kutol, a wallpaper cleaner effective against coal soot and kitchen grease.
  • His sister-in-law, Kay Zufall, observed children using the cleaner as a modeling compound for Christmas ornaments.
  • In 1950, McVickers created a non-toxic, almond-scented, off-white version, distributing samples to Cincinnati schools.
  • Captain Kangaroo (Bob Keeshan) significantly boosted Play-Doh's popularity, mentioning it weekly in exchange for 2% of gross sales.

  • Since 1956, over 950 million pounds of Play-Doh have been sold, packaged in more than 2 billion cans.
  • Current annual production stands at approximately 100 million cans.
  • The product line expanded to include the 'Fun Factory' extruder in 1960 and various licensed sets like Disney Princesses.
  • Newer formulations, such as softer versions for frosting, cater to trends like the cupcake craze.

  • Play-Doh's primary ingredients include water, a starch-based binder, salt, and various additives for texture and preservation.
  • Its base relies on the interaction between starch and water, involving amylose and amylopectin molecules.
  • Adding a lubricant, potentially mineral oil derived from petroleum, prevents stickiness and maintains pliability.
  • The balance of amylose and amylopectin is crucial; too much amylose leads to brittleness, while high amylopectin (75%) creates stickiness.

  • Play-Doh dries out due to evaporation, which can be counteracted by rehydrating with a damp paper towel in its container.
  • Salt is incorporated into the mixture, functioning as an anti-microbial preservative.
  • The manufacturing process requires precise measurements of ingredients, heat, and mixing to ensure shelf stability and consistent texture.

  • Online recipes allow for homemade Play-Doh, offering an educational opportunity to teach children basic chemistry.
  • Modern innovations include Play-Doh 3D printers, some connecting to iPads, blending technology with traditional play.
  • One notable product is the Play-Doh DoVinci 3D printer, showcasing new creative applications for the compound.
