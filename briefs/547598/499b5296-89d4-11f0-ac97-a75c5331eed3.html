Private Equity Took Over a Hospital. Then It Shuttered.

Key Takeaways

  • A private equity firm acquired a Chester, Pennsylvania hospital, leading to its closure.
  • The hospital's closure left the community without vital local healthcare services.
  • Emergency services in Chester are strained due to increased demand and uncompensated care.
  • Pennsylvania is pursuing legislation to increase scrutiny on private equity hospital acquisitions.

Deep Dive

  • Chester, Pennsylvania, a city with a high poverty rate, experienced a healthcare crisis following the closure of its last local hospital in May.
  • The hospital was acquired by Prospect Medical Holdings, a private equity firm, which had a strategy to make hospitals profitable.

  • Private equity firm Leonard Green and Partners raised $654 million from Prospect Medical Holdings between 2012 and 2018 through dividends and share sales.
  • In 2019, Prospect sold the Chester hospital's land to a real estate company, creating significant annual rent obligations under a decade-long lease.
  • Nurses reported deteriorating conditions, including mold and halted payments for supplies, at the Chester hospital starting around 2018.
  • Leonard Green sold its stake in Prospect Medical in 2021, attributing financial distress to the COVID-19 pandemic, despite prior ongoing struggles.

  • Prospect Medical Holdings filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in January after failing to make rent payments and could not secure a buyer.
  • The bankruptcy left $20 million in unpaid taxes owed to Chester county and local school districts, resulting in property tax increases.
  • The closure of Chester's hospital in May led to nearby emergency departments facing an influx of patients and affiliated doctors' offices shutting down.

  • Shane Wheeler's nonprofit emergency medical service, VSMC, took over servicing the Chester community, but resources are strained by non-emergency calls.
  • VSMC experiences over 45% of responses as uncompensated care, leading to financial strain and delayed emergency responses.
  • Pennsylvania Governor Shapiro is advocating for a law to increase scrutiny on hospital acquisitions, requiring consideration of community impact and patient care.
