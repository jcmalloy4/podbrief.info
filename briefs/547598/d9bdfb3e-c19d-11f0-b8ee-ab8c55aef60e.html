The Repo Man is Busier Than Ever

Key Takeaways

  • The repossession business is booming due to increasing car payment defaults.
  • Lower-income consumers face significant financial struggles, seen in rising car loan delinquencies.
  • Advanced technology and nocturnal operations are crucial for difficult vehicle repossessions.
  • Repo agents face considerable danger, prioritizing de-escalation and personal safety.

Deep Dive

  • Repossession activity is surging, driven by more Americans failing to make car payments.
  • This trend is linked to high car prices, extended loan terms, and stagnant wages, increasing subprime loan delinquencies.
  • Vehicle repossessions reached 1.73 million in the past year, approaching 2009 Great Recession levels.
  • Repo companies like Speed Kings Recovery earn an average of $275 per repossessed car, with individual repo men making $75-$90 per vehicle.

  • Repo tow trucks act as mobile command centers, equipped with laptops and smartphones displaying apps for car location.
  • These trucks feature cameras that scan up to 21,000 license plates nightly, cross-referencing them with a database of target vehicles.
  • The camera system can detect vehicles like a 2023 Camry on the highway, though effectiveness is limited for fast-moving targets.

  • Despite an increase in cars marked for repossession, locating them is challenging, often compared to finding 'Waldo'.
  • Repo man George attempted to locate a Toyota Corolla at an apartment complex but did not find it.
  • Jay, a 33-year-old repo man, works 12-13 hour shifts, emphasizing consistent effort and the necessity of camera technology for successful recovery.
  • The effectiveness of camera systems is limited for moving vehicles, as detected cars are often gone before they can be secured.

  • Repo men often operate at night to mitigate confrontations with vehicle owners.
  • Encounters can turn dangerous, as exemplified by George's experience in rural Virginia where he was threatened with a gun and trapped on property.
  • At least 10 repo agents nationwide have been killed in recent years, underscoring the severe risks involved.
  • Agents prioritize personal safety and de-escalation, reminding individuals that possessions are replaceable, unlike life or time.

  • The surge in vehicle repossessions indicates significant economic challenges, especially for lower-income consumers.
  • An increase in Americans falling behind on car payments serves as a broader signal of economic struggle.
  • The booming repo industry, while unpalatable, plays a necessary role in the economy and provides livelihoods for its workers.
