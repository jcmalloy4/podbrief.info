The $55 Billion Deal to Take EA Private

Key Takeaways

  • Electronic Arts is being acquired for $55 billion in the largest leveraged buyout ever.
  • Investors include Jared Kushner's firm and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund.
  • EA, known for Madden and The Sims, has faced gamer backlash over business practices.
  • The deal places significant debt on EA, raising gamer concerns about future monetization.
  • Saudi Arabia aims to diversify its economy and build a global gaming presence.

Deep Dive

  • Investors, including Jared Kushner's firm Affinity Partners and Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, are acquiring Electronic Arts.
  • The $55 billion deal is the largest leveraged buyout in history.
  • The acquisition occurs amidst pressure and layoffs across the video game sector.

  • Founded in the 1980s by Trip Hawkins, EA developed hit franchises like Madden and FIFA.
  • EA's reputation declined due to shutting down acquired studios and implementing in-game purchases.
  • The 2017 Star Wars Battlefront 2 controversy over pay-to-unlock mechanics led to massive player backlash.
  • Consumerist readers twice voted EA "Worst Company in America," citing bugs and microtransactions.

  • Private equity firm Silverlake, led by Egon Durbin, long viewed EA as a 'white whale' target.
  • Durbin lacked sufficient capital until partnering with Jared Kushner's firm, Affinity Partners.
  • EA's value significantly increased since 2013, despite a 17% stock drop in January.

  • Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners connected Silverlake to Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF).
  • The PIF, a sovereign wealth fund, had existing investments in EA and aims to diversify its oil wealth.
  • Saudi Arabia seeks to build a global gaming empire and enhance its soft power through these investments.

  • The leveraged buyout structure means EA will assume approximately $20 billion in debt.
  • Going private allows EA to operate with less public scrutiny but shifts the debt burden onto the company.
  • Gamers on social media fear increased prices and further monetization pressures due to the debt.
  • EA must balance cost-cutting and investor demands with its core gaming community's concerns.
