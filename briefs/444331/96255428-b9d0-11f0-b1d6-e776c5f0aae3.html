This Past Weekend w/ Theo Von

#622 - Miles Teller

Key Takeaways

  • Miles Teller shared experiences from his Florida childhood, including unique local events and cultural aspects.
  • The actor discussed his new movie "Eternity," exploring themes of love, afterlife choices, and consciousness.
  • Teller reflected on navigating fame, the importance of support systems, and his collaborative filmmaking philosophy.
  • He emphasized the complexities of veteran reintegration, mental health support, and military family sacrifices.
  • Teller detailed his acting approach, linking his empathy and curiosity to his childhood experiences and family trauma.

Deep Dive

  • Miles Teller's new movie, "Eternity," praised for its original script and cinematography, is set to release on November 26th.
  • The film explores an afterlife where characters choose their eternal experience, featuring afterlife coordinators in a purgatory resembling a mall.
  • Themes include love, loss, the afterlife, and the impact of war on widows, with a focus on a character's dilemma between first love and current partner.

  • Miles Teller recounted his childhood in central Florida, recalling local events like the Cooter Festival and the town of Inglis, known for banning the devil.
  • The origin of "Cooter Turtle" was explored, linked to the African word "Kuta" from Bambara and Malian languages.
  • Humid Florida air was described as "thick" and "infected," sparking childhood mischief and nostalgia during sunset hours.

  • Miles Teller's mother, Mary, previously worked in real estate, while his father, born in 1954, recently retired around age 70.
  • The guest discussed the adjustment for couples post-retirement, noting his father enjoys outdoor activities.
  • His stepdad engages in building numerous birdhouses and doghouses, suggesting a need for individual hobbies after retirement.

  • While filming in London, Miles Teller's home suffered a fire, leading to a feeling of violation and his wife's subsequent desire for a safer neighborhood.
  • TMZ first reported the fire, initially highlighting celebrity homes, though many neighbors had lived in the Palisades community for decades.
  • Teller's brother-in-law advised him to save memorabilia, like a Kobe jersey, during the evacuation, but he struggled to grasp the situation's reality.

  • Miles Teller stated that success and fame can become a "nightmare" without personal maturity and a strong support system.
  • His career progression aligned with his personal growth, preventing isolation often associated with celebrity.
  • Friends who moved to Los Angeles with him formed a crucial support network, offering stability during his rise to fame.

  • Miles Teller recalled his high school band, 'The Mutes,' formed after a chaotic homecoming parade where power failed.
  • He recounted participating in a 'Students Working Against Tobacco' (SWAT) float, where a student hidden inside a tree was smoking marijuana.
  • The D.A.R.E. program, led by Officer Bob, was present in his high school, including a controversial tactic with a dog and hidden marijuana.

  • A sophisticated heist at the Louvre's Apollo gallery saw four individuals use scooters and power tools to steal eight precious items, including a tiara.
  • The 1911 theft of the Mona Lisa involved the thief hiding overnight and escaping as an employee.
  • Miles Teller praised Tom Cruise's meticulous approach on "Top Gun: Maverick," noting Cruise's dedication to script discussions and filming on an active aircraft carrier.

  • Miles Teller discussed the complexities of veteran reintegration after service and the challenges of mental health support.
  • He highlighted limitations of the VA and the importance of specialized training and funding for veterans.
  • Teller emphasized empathy for those struggling with trauma and the value of support systems among fellow soldiers.

  • Miles Teller reflected on his acting career evolution, initially feeling typecast as a comedic actor before prioritizing authentic roles.
  • He underscored empathy and curiosity as crucial for developing as an actor and connecting with the human experience.
  • Teller traced these traits to his childhood, marked by family trauma, frequent moves, and adapting to environments like nursing homes due to a quadriplegic uncle.

  • Miles Teller discussed an encounter with children whose father returned from a year-long deployment in Qatar, personalizing military family sacrifices.
  • Reflecting on his role in "Thank You for Your Service," he noted the public's disconnect from those serving and the importance of acknowledging their sacrifice.
  • Teller observed that military service now seems concentrated in specific socio-economic pockets, unlike ancient Rome where politicians were also soldiers.
