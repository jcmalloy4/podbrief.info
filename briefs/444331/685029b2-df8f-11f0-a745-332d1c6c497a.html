This Past Weekend w/ Theo Von

#630 - Stephen Wilson Jr.

Key Takeaways

  • Musician Stephen Wilson Jr. received a CMA 'New Artist of the Year' nomination despite an unconventional background.
  • Wilson Jr.'s father, a boxer, died at 59 from a pulmonary embolism, catalyzing his music career and new album 'Son of Dad'.
  • His songwriting process involves applying scientific rigor to personal grief and emotional experiences, treating them as data.
  • Childhood memories of southern Indiana's Midwest/South cultural blend, including unique religious and food traditions, were shared.
  • The conversation delved into the evolving complexities of parent-child relationships from childhood idealization to adult humanization.
  • Meta allegedly suppressed internal research indicating Facebook's negative impact on users' mental health.
  • Australia banned social media for users under 16, effective December 10th, citing extensive evidence of harm to youth.

Deep Dive

  • Wilson Jr.'s father was a boxer and single father who raised his children; he performed traditionally feminine tasks, like curling his daughter's hair, alongside providing for the family.
  • Wilson Jr. noted living out some of his father's unfulfilled dreams while acknowledging his father's creation of him.
  • His father's name, Stephen Wilson, connected to the biblical story of Stephen, the first Christian martyr.
  • The guest recounted his father's intense training regimen under a coach named Champ Cheney at a gym in Indianapolis, fighting at Police Athletic League (PAL) clubs.

  • Wilson Jr. described growing up in a religious household in southern Indiana, attending an Apostolic Christian church, where he witnessed perceived 'demon-possessed' individuals and exorcisms.
  • He recounted observing other homes, sometimes using a step stool, as a way to escape his own unhappy home environment.
  • This childhood curiosity for how others lived shaped his ability to develop narratives from observation, a skill useful for songwriting.

  • Wilson Jr.'s father, a boxer, died suddenly at 59 from a pulmonary embolism, a blood clot blocking an artery in the lungs.
  • During their final phone call, his father was conscious, expressed love four times, and asked him to write a good song.
  • This conversation catalyzed Wilson Jr.'s music career, driving him to honor his father's memory and process grief through songwriting.
  • He views his music as keeping his father's spirit alive and providing a 'home' for the grief and love of others who have experienced loss.

  • Wilson Jr. reflected on his father's pride in his musical ability, perceiving it as magical, contrasting with his own quiet and nerdy childhood.
  • The conversation explored the complex emotional journey of parental relationships, from childhood idolization to teenage demonization and eventual adult humanization and acceptance.
  • This psychological process was noted as potentially biological, linked to a need for independence and genetic diversity in early adulthood.

  • Wilson Jr. and Theo Von reminisced about growing up in a small Midwestern town named Wyoming, Illinois.
  • Childhood memories included summer days, hot tar on roads causing 'NAACP feet', and playing in a park.
  • Nostalgic activities such as collecting baseball cards featuring players like Mark Grace and Sean Dunstan, and chewing stale gum were discussed, relating to his song '1994'.
  • The guest recalled lying on his mother's cowhide rug, inhaling its scent, associating it with escape and imagination.

  • Wilson Jr. stated his advantage in songwriting comes from authenticating real emotions, avoiding 'fantasy music' or portraying characters like Superman.
  • He reflected on being called 'different' as a child, noting this perceived difference became a career advantage, akin to a 'rare foot'.
  • Songwriting served as a survival tool and therapy after his father's death, complementing his science degree and work as a food scientist for Mars.
  • He recounted colleagues consuming pet food, including wet cat food with a spoon, as a 'power move' among high-level employees at Mars.

  • Wilson Jr.'s songwriting aims to evoke personal memories in listeners rather than dictate a story, an approach inspired by an emotional reaction to the song 'Don't Take the Girl' in childhood.
  • His mother, Kathy Lynn, had a difficult upbringing, including abusive relationships and becoming pregnant at 17, marrying young due to religious reasons.
  • She recently survived a near-fatal ATV accident, resulting in a two-month ICU stay; ATVs cause hundreds of annual deaths in the U.S.
  • His father gained custody of him and his siblings, which was uncommon at the time, allowing him to channel emotions about his mother into songs.

  • Wilson Jr. discussed the emotional impact of childhood awareness of parental struggles, including fear of loss, which led him to grow up quickly.
  • The host recounted his father bringing him to funerals, directly confronting death at a young age, possibly due to a lack of childcare.
  • Wilson Jr. shared anecdotes of his father's unconventional parenting, including leaving him at Burger King for hours and letting him drive without a license, leading to multiple car accidents.
  • These experiences in odd environments shaped his perspective and a childhood tendency to maintain internal balance.

  • The guest expressed fascination with the 'first time' of experiences and how specific sensory details can unlock powerful, long-lost memories for individuals.
  • He described nostalgia as a tool that, when combined with sensory input, can unlock deeply buried memories, allowing people to relive the excitement of past experiences, like rediscovering a childhood toy.
  • Listening to Nirvana's 'Heart-Shaped Box' from the album 'In Utero' was described as life-changing.
  • Soundgarden was identified as a significant influence, teaching him to play guitar, with Chris Cornell's vocal performance on 'Blow Up the Outside World' highlighted as a clinic in musicianship.

  • A news segment reported on allegations that Meta buried internal research showing Facebook's causal harm to users' mental health.
  • Meta's 2020 'Project Mercury' reportedly found improved mental health metrics when users deactivated Facebook.
  • Court filings allege Meta ceased further research, dismissing negative findings as tainted by media narrative despite internal insistence on its validity.
  • A legal filing by Motley Rice includes similar allegations against Meta, Google, TikTok, and Snapchat on behalf of U.S. school districts.

  • The discussion delved into the complexities of love, contempt, and resentment in relationships, suggesting that understanding negative emotions is key to overcoming fear, which often stems from a lack of understanding.
  • Wilson Jr. recounted childhood deer hunting experiences with his father, highlighting how the initial fear of the dark, unknown woods dissipated with the rising sun and increased understanding, serving as a metaphor for confronting and understanding fears.
  • The conversation shifted to past rumors and the nature of truth in the pre-social media era, referencing a specific rumor about Richard Gere and gerbils.
  • The speakers noted the impact of social media in debunking rumors quickly, contrasting it with the past where misinformation spread more easily.

  • Wilson Jr. discussed the therapeutic nature of his music, noting how sharing it connects him with others, and performed his song 'Gary'.
  • The song 'Gary' describes a declining archetype of men and touches on themes of aging and changing times, mentioning characters like Gary and Debbie.
  • Following the performance, the conversation humorously turned to the idea of a museum dedicated to the name Gary, drawing a parallel to actor Gary Busey and his unique on-screen presence.
