We Can Do Hard Things

Katie Gavin: How to Know What You Want

Key Takeaways

  • Katie Gavin's debut solo album, "What A Relief" (October 2024), explores desire and connection.
  • The episode discusses identifying inner desires and the courage required to pursue them for personal agency.
  • Queer representation and self-expression have evolved significantly since MUNA's formation in 2014.
  • Glennon Doyle adopted the "femme dom" label after Katie Gavin identified her with it, finding it resonant.
  • The conversation covers insights into long-term recovery, sobriety, and navigating codependency.
  • The episode examines intergenerational legacies, including spiritual independence and tender-hearted listening.
  • Katie Gavin advocates for community interdependence and direct activism, such as connecting with Palestinian families.

Deep Dive

  • MUNA is the most played artist in the host's home, indicating Katie Gavin's significant influence.
  • Gavin is highly respected by "old lesbians" within the host family, fostering discussions on generational respect in lesbian culture.
  • The hosts expressed mutual excitement and nervousness, highlighting Gavin's importance as an artist, activist, and role model.

  • Katie Gavin notes an exponential shift in queer representation since MUNA's formation in 2014.
  • The discussion highlights differing bravery required for public self-expression between femme and masc-presenting LGBTQ+ individuals.
  • Gavin emphasizes the increasing safety and openness for queer individuals to assert their desired self-representation against conventional industry norms.

  • The hosts discuss the fundamental importance of identifying and acting on desires, exemplified by Glennon Doyle creating her life.
  • They explore the difficulty some individuals face in recognizing even simple desires, such as dinner choices, questioning the origins of this inability.
  • Katie Gavin describes identifying desire as a process of elimination, akin to a sculptor revealing a statue by removing excess rock.

  • Katie Gavin found co-writing songs challenging, describing difficulty in clear thinking with others present.
  • She experienced physical exhaustion and illness as indicators that a collaborative situation was not right for her.
  • This led to introspection about her own creative capabilities and the personal exploration of desire in her solo album, "What a Relief."

  • Katie Gavin's solo album project began unexpectedly after Phoebe, who signed MUNA, heard her initial recordings.
  • Phoebe encouraged a more developed production with professional producer Tony Berg, shifting Gavin's perspective toward a formal release.
  • The project led to discussions about timing its release after MUNA's album, with Gavin's bandmates providing understanding and support.

  • Katie Gavin developed a 'relay race' metaphor for her song, which crystallized during a co-writing session in Nashville.
  • Inspired by meeting young women and a full moon ceremony, she reframed generational contributions not as falling short, but as fulfilling a destined part.
  • This realization came to her around the age of 29, highlighting a broader generational understanding that parents do their best.

  • Speakers reflect on the slow progress experienced during the first year of sobriety, often underestimating long-term changes.
  • The discussion highlights the significant transformations possible over an eight-year recovery period, emphasizing sustained effort.
  • One speaker identified 'people' as a significant addiction, becoming lost in fantasy and focusing on external validation.

  • A speaker noted their partner's observation of excessive gym and dance class attendance.
  • This behavior was initially justified as preparation for an album cycle, revealing a pattern of external validation.
  • The discussion highlights the value of partners who can gently point out codependent patterns and behaviors.

  • The guest, Katie Gavin, reflects on desired intergenerational legacies from her family history.
  • She aims to preserve the 'independence of spirit' inherited from women in her family.
  • Gavin also seeks to carry on the 'tender heart' and listening skills demonstrated by her lawyer father.

  • The discussion highlights the difficulty of unlearning individualism to embrace community interdependence.
  • Hosts note historical links between personal safety and self-reliance, contrasting it with the benefits of community.
  • Community connection is presented as a more secure and preferred alternative to traditional individualistic approaches.
