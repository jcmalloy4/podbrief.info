Maybe We CAN Be Loved w/o Being Known: Ashley C. Ford

Key Takeaways

  • The definition of love extends beyond "being known" to include pursuit, accountability, service, and loyalty.
  • Navigating relationship dynamics requires understanding communication styles, past trauma, and emotional labor.
  • Internal conflicts and a fear of misunderstanding can drive behaviors and create distance in relationships.
  • Aligning career choices with personal values and community impact leads to significant satisfaction.
  • Advocating for marginalized voices is crucial, especially in conservative or less diverse environments.

Deep Dive

  • The host's youngest daughter was surprised upon meeting Ashley C. Ford's husband, Kelly, prompting discussion on diverse relationships.
  • Ashley C. Ford is frequently mistaken for a lesbian despite her marriage to a man, Kelly.
  • Ford notes that some perceive her marriage as 'queer,' and she and the host often feel a similar sense of mild disappointment from others.

  • A speaker describes a deep-seated fear of being misunderstood and misinterpreted, which drives a desire for absolute clarity.
  • The struggle between intellectual understanding and genuine belief is noted as a survival mechanism and potential source of conflict.
  • Anxious energy, when disguised as curious questions, can lead to a gaslighting dynamic in conversations.
  • Communication differences in relationships are attributed to backgrounds and culture, necessitating mutual effort to match energy.

  • Ashley C. Ford discusses her childhood experience of being gaslit by her mother, leading to a pattern of internalizing blame and fear of conflict.
  • Any misunderstanding in relationships felt like a profound betrayal and personal failing due to this past trauma.
  • Abby Wambach posits that misunderstandings may arise when another person observes a truth about oneself that has been intellectualized or remains unseen.
  • Kelly Stacy confirmed this dynamic in his marriage, noting that his expressions, though potentially harsh, were not fundamentally incorrect.

  • The conversation delves into forgiveness and acceptance, specifically discussing Kelly Stacy's unique ability to clearly see and understand situations and people.
  • This insight, though sometimes difficult to receive, is acknowledged as valuable for personal growth and making better decisions.
  • The speaker notes that trusting Kelly's perspective, even when it's about herself, is important for growth.
  • Kelly's role is likened to a 'seer' in a Dungeons & Dragons campaign, contrasting with her role as a 'speaker'.

  • The discussion questions the definition of love, proposing it may be more than just 'being known,' potentially including 'being tended to' or 'being nourished.'
  • Ashley C. Ford defines love as including the pursuit of knowing a person, accountability, service, and loyalty.
  • Understanding different perspectives and how personal definitions shape reactions is compared to a spectrum rather than black-and-white choices.
  • One speaker realized personal codependency issues after reading Elizabeth Gilbert's book, prompting self-discovery.

  • One speaker describes how their hyper-independence contributes to a partner's fear of distance, heightened by concerns about their return.
  • A speaker reflects on not recognizing her husband's pain during moments of distance, attributing it to her lack of assumption he was experiencing similar distress.
  • The guest expresses resentment that certain emotional labor, like understanding difficult feelings, was a 'demanded skill' she had to develop.
  • This leads to loneliness when a partner cannot fully meet her in difficult emotional spaces, framed as a necessary solo work.

  • The host reflects on her desire to 'fix' her partner's sadness and the frustration that she cannot always provide peace.
  • She realizes her own sadness is a sign of her capacity for care, not a flaw to be eliminated.
  • The host accepts that her partner may not always be able to join her in difficult emotional spaces but can offer support from the outside.
  • This external support is likened to being a 'light' to guide her out of difficult emotions, rather than fixing them.

  • Ashley C. Ford found a job posting for a Women and Girls Reporter with Free Press Indiana, initially hesitant to apply due to her New York Times bestselling author status.
  • She feared displacing local talent but was contacted by the CEO of Free Press Indiana about her interest.
  • A lengthy conversation and interview process followed, during which Ford shared her ideas for the role.
  • Learning that five men had also applied motivated her to promptly submit her application for this 'unicorn job'.

  • Ashley C. Ford's new job focuses on reporting on the challenges and resilience of women and girls across Indiana.
  • She cites Indiana's poor public relations, conservative culture, and lack of exposure to diversity as factors policing confidence.
  • Ford highlights her commitment to telling the stories of those in the most marginalized positions in the state.
  • Her decision to move back to Indiana was driven by 'unfinished business' and a desire for the state to acknowledge her history and family's connection.
