We Can Do Hard Things

Have We Ruined Fun? (And the Future of the Pod!)

Key Takeaways

  • The hosts' daughter sustained a broken collarbone during an aggressive high school soccer game.
  • The incident spurred a discussion on youth sports' systemic issues and the influence of parental ambition.
  • The podcast is transitioning to an independent, 'less-is-more' model for 2026, featuring new content segments.
  • Hosts reflect on the podcast's five-year journey, its healing impact, and future personal and professional goals.
  • The show emphasizes authentic discussion of difficult topics to foster collective healing and reduce isolation.

Deep Dive

  • The hosts recount a recent incident where their daughter, Amma, broke her collarbone during a high school soccer game, an injury sustained two weeks prior to the episode.
  • Amma was targeted by aggressive defense, a recognized strategy by opposing coaches, leading to her injury.
  • Upon assessment, Abby confirmed a broken collarbone, which was later verified by X-ray, sidelining Amma for eight weeks.

  • The discussion highlights a general feeling of chaos and pressure in youth sports, noting parents and coaches often appear stressed rather than enjoying the activity.
  • One speaker suggests parents project their own ambitions, using their children to gain social capital and validation, enabled by the current system.
  • Proposed radical solutions include 'watch parties' for parents only, with referees for parental conduct, or 'silent Saturdays' where parents are banned from the sidelines.

  • The podcast expresses pride in its independent production, stating a commitment to curating content and featuring diverse voices over more mainstream ones.
  • The creators intentionally made career-impacting decisions, including leaving a network, due to consistently speaking out on political issues like Palestine.
  • They report no regrets over their choices and are proud of the in-house production developed over the past year.

  • Producing the podcast authentically, from an internal perspective, has been personally transformative for a host, enabling the healing of underdeveloped parts of herself.
  • This process has allowed a host to re-engage with the sports world, particularly women's sports, with a more integrated sense of self, contrasting with previous intense focus as a player.
  • The hosts suggest that healing involves building self-trust, allowing individuals to remain whole within challenging situations and re-engage with previously overwhelming systems.

  • The podcast is shifting to a 'less-is-more' approach for 2026, moving from multiple episodes per week to one weekly podcast, aiming for higher quality and reduced listener overwhelm.
  • The new format includes one monthly personal update, two curated past episodes relevant to current themes, and one new episode dissecting societal norms and their origins.
  • A new weekly segment, 'You Won't Believe This Bullshit,' will explore hidden histories and societal implications, covering topics like the creation of billionaires and the origins of terms like 'carbon footprint.'

  • The podcast plans future monthly shows that will explore cultural or historical moments and their impact on daily life.
  • Amanda Doyle's Thanksgiving video, connecting current genocides to historical events and emphasizing interconnectedness, is highlighted as a model for these monthly shows.
  • One host expresses excitement and a sense of vulnerability about these upcoming monthly show concepts.

  • A host, approaching her 50th year, reflects on a desire to shift focus from work-centric relationships to more authentic, in-person connections, moving away from hustle culture.
  • The podcast celebrates significant milestones over its five-year run, including over half a billion plays, multiple major award wins, debuting as the number one show on Apple Podcasts, and raising $56 million for global aid.
  • Future projects include 'Come See Me in the Good Light' and a musical, with Abby Wambach also collaborating with Billie Jean King.
