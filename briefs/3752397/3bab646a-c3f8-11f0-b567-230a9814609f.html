We Can Do Hard Things

Cynthia Erivo — Wicked, Wild & Wise — is here!!!

Key Takeaways

  • Cynthia Erivo emphasizes self-care and setting personal boundaries through daily rituals.
  • Her book, "Simply More," offers raw insights into personal growth and overcoming past trauma.
  • A difficult relationship with her father deeply impacted her trust, addressed through therapy.
  • Erivo advocates for authenticity in acting, rejecting "method acting" for personal experience.
  • She embraces her "too muchness" and uses questioning to foster genuine connections and clarity.
  • Erivo views the actor's role as a vessel, emphasizing the importance of maintaining one's true self.

Deep Dive

  • Cynthia Erivo begins her day by dressing for bed as a form of self-care.
  • This practice creates a clear boundary between day and night, regardless of observers.
  • It helps her feel centered and good within herself before the day's demands.

  • Cynthia Erivo's book "Simply More," released November 21st, is described as honest, raw, and profound.
  • The book details her personal discoveries and difficult journey, particularly with her father.
  • Erivo expressed excitement mixed with apprehension about sharing her experiences in the book.

  • Cynthia Erivo determines trustworthiness based on intuitive feelings and people revealing themselves.
  • She was fortunate to build a strong foundation of supportive friends early in her career.
  • Erivo notes that discerning genuine support helps her decide when to keep distance or engage easily.

  • A strained relationship with her father, including an incident at a train station, triggered a lifelong pattern of self-reliance.
  • This past trauma led to a lack of trust and difficulty accepting help, impacting her role in "The Color Purple."
  • Erivo sought therapy in her late twenties to unlearn these patterns, becoming more open to support and understanding departures.

  • Cynthia Erivo's commitment to authenticity stems from a drama school experience where she felt betrayed and misused her gift.
  • She ensures authenticity by showing up as her true self, including "rough parts," and not wearing makeup in public.
  • Erivo builds genuine connections by deeply knowing people, remembering personal details like family names.
  • She identifies her tendency to question with "why" as a challenging aspect for others, driven by high expectations.

  • Cynthia Erivo defines "too muchness" as embracing all aspects of her identity.
  • This includes her physical presentation, such as elaborate nails and clothing choices.
  • She views these as forms of creativity and storytelling that initiate conversations and connections.

  • Cynthia Erivo defines true acting as telling the truth, rejecting "method acting" as dangerous and potentially self-harming.
  • She draws on her own experiences and emotions, rather than replicating a character's difficult circumstances, citing her role as sexually abused Tessa.
  • Erivo accepted the role of Jesus in 'Jesus Christ Superstar' to explore masculinity and femininity, and connect with her faith's message of love.
  • Her portrayal aimed for a balance between masculine and feminine energies, with love as the central tenet, despite polarized reactions.

  • Cynthia Erivo views the actor's role as being a vessel for characters, sometimes requiring setting aside one's own identity.
  • This involves embodying the role and then consciously stepping back once the performance concludes.
  • She believes maintaining 'self-ness' and authenticity, even in acting, creates a boundary between professional and personal life, ensuring personal well-being.
