The Real Reason Blue Cities Are Choosing Decline | Mayor Francis Suarez

Key Takeaways

  • Miami's success is attributed to low taxes, public safety, and innovation.
  • Mayor Suarez highlights Miami's strong private sector and community resilience.
  • Debate surrounds policy effectiveness versus gun ownership in crime statistics.
  • Media scrutiny and character assassinations pose challenges for political engagement.
  • Housing affordability and traffic remain key concerns amid Miami's growth.

Deep Dive

  • U.S. cities rank high in overall gun deaths, but top five to ten 'blue' cities significantly influence national data.
  • Miami, despite widespread gun ownership, reports one of the lowest homicide rates in U.S. history, achieving a record low in 2024.
  • The discussion suggests policy choices, rather than gun ownership, are the primary factor in reducing violence and improving societal health.

  • Miami's 16-year success story is founded on three core principles: low taxes, public safety, and innovation.
  • The city experienced 150% economic growth in a decade, alongside a significant reduction in homelessness.
  • Miami's commitment to law and order, rejecting 'defund the police' and 'no cash bail' policies, resulted in one of its lowest homicide rates ever.

  • Proposed property tax changes in Florida raise concerns, as property tax revenue constitutes 45% of Miami's budget.
  • A reduction in property tax revenue could necessitate layoffs of essential city personnel like police officers and firefighters.
  • Miami achieved fiscal soundness from near bankruptcy in 2009, establishing a surplus and its lowest property tax rate through budget cuts and union negotiations.

  • High-tax policies in places like New York are described as creating an 'economic death spiral' that encourages residents to leave.
  • The influx of residents fleeing 'blue states' has contributed to Florida becoming a 'solid red state' with a voter registration advantage of one million.
  • This shift is partly attributed to Governor Ron DeSantis's focus on voter registration and Miami's pro-business, low-tax, and law-and-order policies.

  • Housing affordability is a primary concern in Miami, with rising prices impacting long-time residents and essential workers like teachers and police officers.
  • Miami is implementing initiatives to combat housing issues, including building affordable housing through public-private partnerships.
  • Other solutions include educational pathways via a tech charter school, privately funded STEM scholarships, coding boot camps, and crypto initiatives like giving children Bitcoin.

  • A significant majority, 31 out of 34, of mayors in Miami-Dade County hold outside jobs, often in business or law.
  • Mayor Suarez reflected on the financial challenges of public service and his family's extensive political history, with 24 of his 48 years involved in politics.
  • He suggested that negative media character assassinations contribute to a reluctance among individuals, particularly younger ones, to pursue public office.

  • Mayor Suarez utilized social media, including a viral tweet to 'Zelian Astrov' during the COVID-19 pandemic, to attract tech industry professionals to Miami, bypassing traditional media.
  • He attributed intense criticism from his local newspaper to his growing national attention and ability to communicate directly with the public.
  • Suarez expressed his belief that the media should reflect the values of the community it covers, finding the Miami press surprisingly left-leaning given the city's political leanings.

  • Mayor Suarez won his mayoral elections with 85% and 80% of the vote, though an attempt to implement a strong mayor system failed.
  • A city council effort to change the election year from odd to even years, aiming to increase voter turnout from 15% to 70% and save over $1 million annually, was overturned by a lawsuit.
  • Significant accomplishments during his tenure include passing the deal to bring Inter Miami and Lionel Messi to the city.
