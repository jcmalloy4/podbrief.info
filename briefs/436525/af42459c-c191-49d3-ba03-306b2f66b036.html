ElevenLabs CEO: Why Voice is the Next AI Interface

Key Takeaways

  • ElevenLabs rapidly ships research-grade AI for voice, expanding from text-to-speech to full AI agents.
  • Small, autonomous teams and a global hiring strategy enable ElevenLabs' fast product development.
  • The Voice Marketplace has paid creators over $10M, as ElevenLabs shifts to enterprise solutions.
  • Strategic industry collaboration and specialized hiring are key for navigating AI market expansion.

Deep Dive

  • ElevenLabs develops research-level AI for voice, expanding from text-to-speech to voice agents, licensed music, and sound effects.
  • The Voice Marketplace features 10,000 voices and has paid creators over $10 million.
  • The company operates with approximately 20 small, autonomous product teams, each comprising 5 to 10 individuals.
  • Research initiatives expected to exceed three months allow product teams to develop extensions or integrate other models, maintaining rapid delivery.

  • ElevenLabs employs a global hiring strategy, combining distributed work with hubs in London, Warsaw, and San Francisco.
  • The company actively seeks untraditional talent, exemplified by hiring a call center worker with an open-source text-to-speech model.
  • ElevenLabs implemented a flat organizational structure and removed-title policy to encourage impact based on skill rather than tenure.
  • Small teams, managed by leads for subdivisions like research and sales, support immediate impact for new members.

  • ElevenLabs observed increasing AI adoption in creative industries like art, music, and advertising, moving from initial resistance to welcoming.
  • Collaboration involved understanding industry priorities, like adapting AI tools for voice acting production processes.
  • The Voice Marketplace has paid creators $10 million, featuring nearly 10,000 voices.
  • The company partnered with music labels like Merlin and Cobalt to license music for AI generation, a process that took 18 months.

  • ElevenLabs acquires talent for new territories by hiring individuals with prior field experience and supplementing with external consultants.
  • In the music industry, music lawyers served as consultants to bridge communication gaps and facilitate understanding.
  • The company prioritizes hiring risk-tolerant individuals who understand commercial opportunities, particularly for legal roles.
  • A legal counsel experienced in risk assessment and enterprise practices was hired to navigate complex decisions and industry standards.

  • ElevenLabs transitioned from a creator-focused brand to serving enterprise clients, expanding into AI agent platforms.
  • The company revised its sales strategy, shifting from engineers handling sales to an 80% sales, 20% engineering focus.
  • Enterprise needs, such as Hippocratic in healthcare for appointment scheduling, drove the focus on orchestrating speech-to-text, language models, and text-to-speech.
  • Underpinning the enterprise strategy is a focus on security, compliance, and reliability, aiming for high uptime.
  • The company balances rapid product iteration with enterprise reliability by delineating alpha products from stable releases.

  • ElevenLabs delineates products as pre-product market fit, allowing a six-month period before potential cancellation, and post-product market fit.
  • The CEO identified evolving incentive structures for the growing go-to-market team as the hardest transition, emphasizing aligning commissions with strategy.
  • A policy was implemented prohibiting sales to foundational model competitors, regardless of commission incentives.
