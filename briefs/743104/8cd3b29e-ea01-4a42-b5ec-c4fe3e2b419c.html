Hidden Toxins In Your Home Exposed: Mold, Air Quality, EMF's & The Simple Fixes For A Healthier Home Ft. Ryan Blaser

Key Takeaways

  • Environmental toxins in homes significantly impact physical and mental well-being.
  • Addressing past traumas can unlock subconscious issues affecting present health and behavior.
  • Key home health pillars include air quality, water quality, lighting, and EMF exposure.
  • Simple swaps like wired headphones and natural fibers can reduce daily toxin exposure.
  • Prioritizing a clean home environment supports the body's natural detoxification processes.

Deep Dive

  • Guest Ryan Blaser underwent ketamine therapy, which helped him recall and process a blocked childhood memory at age four involving the accidental death of a pet cat.
  • The therapy revealed profound feelings of pain, shame, and guilt associated with the incident, which he believes impacted his life.
  • Forgiving his four-year-old self was crucial for healing and resolved a subconscious fear of pets.
  • Early childhood traumas (ages 1-7) can manifest as subconscious issues, potentially leading to self-sabotaging behaviors and addictions.

  • Test My Home assesses air quality, chemical exposure from products and off-gassing, and the presence of mold and other microbes.
  • Paint off-gassing can persist for two to three years, detectable through air samples in new homes.
  • Three pillars for home health improvement include fresh air circulation, air filtration systems (like Jasper), and reduced electronic and chemical exposure.
  • Environmental toxins stress the body, potentially leading to illnesses based on individual genetics and predispositions.

  • EMF exposure from cell phones is a concern, especially for infants whose skulls and brains are more sensitive; the precautionary principle advises caution with modern technology.
  • Practical swaps to reduce exposure include using wired headphones instead of AirPods and employing laptop heat shields.
  • Avoid synthetic textiles like polyester due to microplastics and chemical treatments.
  • Opt for natural fibers such as organic wool, cotton, silk, and bamboo for healthier bedding and blankets.

  • Air fresheners and artificial scents in homes are critiqued, as a truly healthy environment should have a neutral smell.
  • Overpowering chemical or artificial scents may mask underlying biological or chemical issues.
  • Modern environmental stressors, including EMFs, artificial light, microplastics, and seed oils, can negatively impact gut and immune health.
  • Reducing synthetic fragrances leads to a neutral-smelling home, making strong artificial scents noticeably bothersome and potentially headache-inducing.

  • Conventional candles can release chemical particles, with phthalates identified as endocrine disruptors that affect hormone balance.
  • Natural cleaning alternatives include vinegar, water, and lemon essential oil for surfaces.
  • Specific laundry detergents like Zum and Truly Free are recommended, with vinegar or baking soda suggested for added cleaning power.
  • After detoxifying an environment, increased sensitivity to smells is common, reflecting heightened health awareness.

  • Discussion includes hairspray and makeup toxicity, suggesting compromises like using exhaust fans or applying products outdoors to reduce exposure.
  • Applying hairspray outdoors or utilizing a powerful exhaust system could resolve a significant portion of home toxicity issues.
  • Significant lifestyle changes like using organic materials, improving air filtration, and reducing toxic cleaning supplies have a greater impact than minor adjustments.
  • The concept of hermetic stress is introduced, where occasional exposure to minor stressors can strengthen detoxification pathways, emphasizing the need for an optimal home environment for recovery.

  • A family experienced health issues in a new home, including a 12-year-old daughter with night terrors and bedwetting.
  • Analysis revealed the daughter's bed proximity to the refrigerator's motor emitted electromagnetic waves, disrupting her sleep and causing neurological symptoms.
  • Relocating the daughter's bed resolved her symptoms overnight, highlighting the impact of EMFs on brain electrical impulses.
  • Recommendations for managing phone EMF exposure include using airplane mode when not in use and keeping phones out of the bedroom during sleep.

  • Home safety considerations include setting up emergency bypass for communication and positioning sleeping areas away from potential electrical hazards.
  • It is advised to maintain a distance of at least a football field from high-voltage power lines and 100 feet from smaller neighborhood lines due to electromagnetic fields (EMFs).
  • A preference is expressed for natural sunlight and red light over artificial lighting in homes.
  • Artificial lights with flickering and incorrect color spectrums can cause stress and disrupt the body's natural rhythms.

  • Individuals may consume a credit card's worth of plastic weekly, prompting advice to eliminate plastic kitchenware, storage, and Ziploc bags.
  • Avoid microwaving food in plastic, especially when hot, to reduce microplastic exposure.
  • The bathroom is identified as the most common toxic room due to water and chemical usage, recommending constant ventilation.
  • The bedroom is considered the most impactful room for healing, rest, and intimacy, making its environment crucial for overall well-being.
