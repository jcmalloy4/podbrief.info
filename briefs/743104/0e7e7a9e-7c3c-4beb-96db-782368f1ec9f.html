Deep Dive

Introduction and Brazilian Lymphatic Massage Overview

The episode features Josie Rushing, a seven-time award-winning Brazilian lymphatic massage therapist who brings a unique perspective to lymphatic wellness. The conversation begins with establishing the key differences between Brazilian and traditional lymphatic massage:

  • Brazilian lymphatic massage focuses on both internal wellness and external cosmetic benefits, tailored to modern lifestyle concerns like post-surgery recovery and body contouring
  • Offers a more comprehensive approach compared to the original lymphatic massage developed 100 years ago
  • Can help reduce 4-5 pounds of waste/fluid in one hour, addressing fluid retention that might be mistaken for weight gain
The immediate benefits discussed include reducing fatigue, helping with acne, potentially alleviating emotional issues like depression and anxiety, and supporting overall wellness from "inside out." Host Lauren shares her personal positive experience with lymphatic massage, particularly during pregnancy.

Medical and Surgical Applications

The conversation deepens into the critical role of lymphatic drainage in medical contexts. Brazilian lymphatic massage is crucial before and after surgical procedures to prepare the body for surgery, boost the immune system, reduce trauma, aid recovery, and minimize scar tissue formation. Studies show it can reduce cellulite by up to 58% while providing comprehensive health benefits including lowering cortisol levels and reducing fluid retention.

Modern lifestyle challenges are highlighted as major factors affecting lymphatic health - sedentary work environments, lack of movement, prolonged sitting, and limited physical activity compared to past generations all contribute to lymphatic dysfunction.

Treatment Philosophy and Techniques

Josie outlines her specialized approach using pressure points, strokes, fascia release, and respiration techniques. The treatment always starts on the left leg to balance and optimize results, focusing on working with hard-to-reach organs like the spleen through pressure points. She emphasizes a non-painful, relaxing approach, rejecting the "beauty is pain" mentality and warning against excessive pressure or techniques that cause bruising.

The treatment philosophy positions lymphatic massage as ongoing, essential maintenance - comparable to regular activities like working out, hair care, or nail maintenance, recognizing the body's constant need to eliminate toxins and support immune system function.

Holistic Health Benefits and Emotional Aspects

The discussion reveals profound emotional and psychological dimensions of lymphatic massage. Benefits include activating the immune system, cleansing the body of pathogens, and reducing cortisol levels by up to 30% in one session. Clients can become emotionally vulnerable during treatment, with some experiencing emotional releases during massage, highlighting the deeply personal and transformative nature of the work.

Signs someone needs lymphatic drainage include visible indicators like acne, water retention, cellulite, significant eye bags, and extremity swelling, as well as internal signs including autoimmune disorders, anxiety, fatigue, and circulation issues.

Celebrity Practice and Cosmetic Procedures

Josie frequently works with celebrities, particularly for post-plastic surgery care and pre/post red carpet events. The conversation covers various surgical and aesthetic procedures including rib removal surgery, facelifts, abs sculpting, and tummy tucks. She emphasizes that lymphatic drainage after facelift requires careful approach due to potential lymph node damage, and discusses how weight loss can lead to excess skin requiring surgical intervention.

Non-invasive alternatives are explored, including energized devices, radiofrequency treatments, and collagen-boosting procedures, with recommendations to consider these options before surgery.

Timing and Recovery Protocols

Critical timing insights emerge regarding lymphatic drainage, especially after surgeries. In Brazil, lymphatic drainage can start immediately after surgery, differing from U.S. practices where waiting too long can lead to problematic scar tissue formation. Lymphatic drainage is considered "non-negotiable" for surgical recovery.

For postpartum recovery, lymphatic drainage helps with water retention, swelling, immune system recovery, and assists the body in returning to its natural shape. A comprehensive approach should address the whole body, with recommended combination of lymphatic drainage, belly binding, and targeted massage for optimal recovery.

Personal Journey and Business Development

Josie shares her immigrant success story, originally from a small Brazilian city of 30,000 people with limited financial resources. She worked while attending school, moved to São Paulo seeking opportunities, and was passionate about learning English. Starting her business in a spare bedroom charging $35 per session, she quickly expanded by hiring massage therapists as demand grew.

Her business evolution included developing a unique technique, training others as clients sought practitioners in other locations, and becoming concerned about improper lymphatic drainage techniques on social media. She now has certified professionals in 22 countries and 45 U.S. states.

Technical Training and Proper Techniques

The conversation provides detailed technical guidance for proper lymphatic massage and dry brushing:

Dry Brushing Guidelines:

  • Always work towards the heart
  • Divide body parts into three sections: proximal, medial, and distal
  • Start with the section closest to lymph nodes
  • For legs: start at proximal area (closest to groin), progress to medial then distal sections
Facial Lymphatic Drainage:
  • "Hula hula" technique to activate lymph nodes
  • Pump nodes behind ears, massage underneath chin
  • Work above and around clavicle
  • Facial massage should lift upward, especially under eyes
  • For neck: go up in the middle, down on the sides

Professional Practice and Energy Management

Josie discusses the professional aspects of her practice, including working barefoot to maintain ground connection and occasionally experiencing clients' negative energy, causing physical symptoms like headaches and nausea. She uses grounding techniques and breathing to reset after sessions.

With a majority female clientele who tend to be more vulnerable and open during sessions, she emphasizes the importance of being a licensed, trained professional who can handle sensitive situations while maintaining professional boundaries.
