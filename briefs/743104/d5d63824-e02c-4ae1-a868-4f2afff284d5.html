Bre Tiesi On The Truth Behind Selling Sunset, Motherhood, Personal Boundaries, & Finding Your Balance

Key Takeaways

  • Reality television filming demands significant time and emotional resilience, often involving unseen conflicts.
  • Establishing clear personal and professional boundaries is crucial for navigating public life and business dealings.
  • A career in real estate requires full-time commitment, strategic financial planning, and a strong professional team.
  • Postpartum recovery involves a minimum of two years for body and brain reset, with women often facing unfair societal and industry expectations.
  • Structured daily routines can effectively balance career aspirations with motherhood, enhancing family connection.
  • Owning one's narrative and protecting personal privacy are vital for mental well-being in the public eye.

Deep Dive

  • Filming 'Selling Sunset' involves long days, significant travel, and extensive 'glam time', which impacts the guest's ability to spend time with her son.
  • The constant conflict and 'cattiness' depicted on the show are emotionally exhausting for the guest, differing from physical fatigue.
  • Bre Tiesi indicates her 'masculine energy' aids in detaching from the reality TV drama, though persistent conflict remains challenging.

  • The guest's consistent boundary-setting was influenced by her mother's strict upbringing, where 'no' was definitive.
  • She advises understanding all terms and negotiable points in contracts, emphasizing the importance of being prepared to walk away from unfavorable deals.
  • Bre Tiesi considered leaving 'Selling Sunset' after negative experiences with narrative manipulation but returned after establishing clear contractual boundaries.

  • Bre Tiesi has held a real estate license since 2016, achieving $20 million in sales during her first year of serious effort.
  • She highlights the demanding nature of real estate, citing a $17 million home sale that took two and a half years, emphasizing it is a full-time career.
  • The Los Angeles real estate market has a 'sweet spot' for clients between $3.5 million to $5 million, with properties above $6 million considered expensive.

  • The guest advises buyers and sellers to be patient, thoroughly research properties, and understand whether their purpose is investment or personal use.
  • She expresses a preference for renting personally while owning investment properties, noting the ongoing costs and market risks of rental ownership.
  • Common mistakes include buying larger homes than necessary and falsely assuming all residential real estate consistently appreciates, overlooking market corrections.

  • Bre Tiesi maintains a strict wellness and beauty regimen, with her son recognizing her alarm as 'regiment time' for her nightly routine.
  • Her routine includes a family walk, skincare for herself and her son, and meticulous organization of products with specific treatments assigned to days.
  • Tiesi's Type-A personality and organizational skills allow her to prioritize her son while managing personal needs, fostering a strong attachment and flexibility.

  • Postpartum recovery involves at least two years for a woman's body and brain to reset, a process that restarts with each subsequent child.
  • The guest describes intense pressure filming a reality TV show six weeks postpartum as difficult and overwhelming, noting societal expectations.
  • The conversation highlights a lack of grace and education surrounding postpartum mental health, urging for mandatory three-month maternity leave for reality TV participants.

  • Bre Tiesi teases upcoming drama for the next season of 'Selling Sunset', revealing a 'trio' dynamic among cast members has dissolved.
  • This shift has led to some cast members growing closer, while others have become more distant, indicating a turbulent season.
  • Tiesi identifies Heather Rae El Moussa as her favorite cast member, followed by Emma Hernan and Mary Fitzgerald, with Sandra recently joining as a positive addition.
