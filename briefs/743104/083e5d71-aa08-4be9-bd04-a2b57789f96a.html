The Skinny Confidential Him & Her Podcast

Mel Robbins On The Tools, Habits, & Mindset Shifts That Actually Change Your Life

Key Takeaways

  • Mindset shifts, such as reframing anxiety, can directly impact emotional and physical states.
  • Intentional morning routines and micro-habits are crucial for personal and professional success.
  • Resistance is a normal signal of growth, not an obstacle to be avoided, in personal transformation.
  • Understanding and managing one's energy is a valuable currency in daily interactions.
  • Long-term personal and financial planning requires consistent audits and focused, incremental changes.

Deep Dive

  • Developed by Mel Robbins and her therapist, the "slithering technique" uses gravity to exit bed when motivation is low.
  • This method is applicable during challenging life periods such as grief, breakups, or job loss.
  • Robbins employed this technique after a difficult move from her childhood home after 26 years.
  • She also utilized it during a period of depression in Vermont at age 52, when building a new social circle felt overwhelming.

  • Anxiety is described as "separation anxiety"—specifically, a separation from oneself, as posited by neuroscientist Dr. Russell Kennedy.
  • It acts as an alarm system, triggered by uncertainty about future events one cares about, such as job interviews or the impact of AI.
  • Mental states can directly impact the body's biology and physiology, according to research from Stanford's Dr. Aaliyah Crum.
  • Managing uncertainty involves focusing on the present and one's capacity to cope, rather than dwelling on "what if" scenarios.

  • Research from Harvard Business School indicates that the physiological responses to anxiety and excitement are identical.
  • The key difference lies in cognitive labeling: excitement is associated with positive anticipation, while anxiety leads to self-doubt.
  • The body's physiological responses, such as a racing heart, prepare an individual for a significant event, not necessarily failure.
  • Mel Robbins advises reframing anxiety as excitement by using a positive "anchor thought" during high-stakes situations.

  • A new program specifically for menopause has been developed by health specialists to support women through this life stage.
  • The brand's signature points program has been updated with enhanced app features and virtual workshops for a flexible approach to healthy living.

  • Mel Robbins advocates for prioritizing sleep and implementing a consistent morning routine, which can include mouth tape.
  • Her routine involves the 5-4-3-2-1 method, exercise, making her bed, talking to her husband, and identifying one critical task before checking her phone.
  • She advises against checking phones first thing to avoid entering a reactive mode, potentially leading to work-related anxiety and resentment.
  • Michael Bosstick similarly prioritizes his own tasks before immediately addressing urgent external requests.

  • Procrastinating on a significant professional project by completing minor tasks instead indicates the project's importance and underlying resistance.
  • Mel Robbins emphasizes the necessity of a goal larger than daily struggles to provide direction, citing examples like writing a book or starting a podcast.
  • Resistance is presented not as an obstacle but as a normal signal of impending change, acting as a precursor to forging new thought patterns.
  • Robbins herself faced personal resistance while writing her latest book, citing dyslexia, a demanding podcast schedule, and underestimation of its complexity.

  • Mel Robbins stopped accepting speaking engagements due to overcommitment, adopting a rule to decline commitments if she wouldn't do them immediately.
  • She prioritizes resistance training and protein intake, noting women's distinct physiology differs from men's, and criticizing the marketing of collagen as protein.
  • Exercising on an empty stomach can increase cortisol and fat storage in women.
  • Her pre-workout nutrition includes protein options like eggs, kimchi, protein shakes, or a spoonful of honey with royal jelly.

  • The discussion questions the expectation of perfection in others, noting partners evolve over time and people can learn new skills.
  • A partner should serve as a "home base" where one can be oneself, allowing individuals to feel at ease.
  • Research from the Gottman Institute on relationship dynamics indicates that 69% of arguments stem from issues that will persist.
  • The ability to argue constructively, leading to resolution rather rather than escalating conflict, is presented as a sign of a healthy relationship.

  • The guest shares a personal experience of overcoming near bankruptcy and marital strain at age 41, reinforcing that it is never too late for financial stability.
  • Despite significant success, she prioritizes time with loved ones and meaningful work, recognizing the transient nature of material possessions.
  • Financial expert David Bach's work is highlighted for advising women on financial preparedness, addressing risks like early widowhood and medical bills.
  • Discussions include developing investing expertise beyond saving and debt, and considering annuities for guaranteed future income, as mentioned in Bill Perkins' "Die with Zero."
