The Great Fashion Reset: Can Designer Debuts Revive Luxury?

Key Takeaways

  • Creative resets address macro pressures and price inflation, not just consumer fatigue.
  • Luxury brands' price hikes demand improved quality or creative value to justify costs.
  • Creative director roles are constrained, balancing new customer excitement with existing client retention.
  • New creative leadership debuts offer strategy hints, not full overhauls for brands.
  • Sustaining post-runway excitement through ongoing content is critical for long-term momentum.

Deep Dive

  • The luxury fashion industry faces a significant demand slowdown after years of post-pandemic boom, leading to its 'worst creative and commercial funk in over a decade'.
  • Brands are implementing widespread creative director debuts as a response to re-engage consumers amidst macroeconomic factors like inflation and depressed markets, particularly China.
  • Creative resets aim to justify price increases through improved quality or design, with runway shows offering strategic hints toward craftsmanship or bolder fashion moments.
  • The market is bifurcated, with resilient high-end clients but struggling younger consumers, prompting brands to seek ways to reignite interest.

  • The role of a creative director is more constrained today due to brand size and market saturation, requiring a balance between exciting new customers and retaining existing ones.
  • Brands cannot necessarily count on gaining two new clients for every old one lost from a previous vision, with some brands being 'burned in recent years' by trying to scrap old business.
  • Higher luxury prices mean consumers are more hesitant to purchase trendy items, making new creative ventures riskier than in previous years.
  • New creative directors must excite customers without alienating existing ones and ensure economic viability alongside their creative vision in the current macroeconomic climate.

  • Gucci experienced a significant boom under Alessandro Michele's maximalist vision, drawing from archives and pop culture, but has since seen declining sales.
  • Customer fatigue with this aesthetic led to a sales decline of around 20% year-on-year for two consecutive years.
  • The market believes the lack of 'zeitgeisty' fashion is contributing to declining sales, despite a desire for a more heritage-driven approach.
  • The incoming designer at Gucci faces pressure to revitalize the brand's image and product urgently, given its large operational infrastructure.

  • Demna, with a proven track record at Balenciaga and Vetements, is seen as a potential candidate to bring the necessary 'zeitgeisty' spin to Gucci.
  • He is noted for his ability to create universally relatable yet niche fashion moments and understanding the evolving landscape of luxury fashion and young consumers.
  • Balenciaga, under Demna, maintained stable sales despite backlash from a controversial ad campaign depicting children, impacting the organization.
  • Balenciaga's stability was bolstered by accessible items like sneakers and hoodies, alongside a growing high-end ready-to-wear business.

  • Chanel is facing scrutiny for a potential mismatch between its product quality and pricing, fueled by online complaints from consumers.
  • There is anticipation around Matthieu Blazy's mandate as creative director, with significant industry interest in his vision and creative freedom.
  • Speculation suggests Blazy's focus at Chanel will be on deep product development, emphasizing craftsmanship, material innovation, and close collaboration with ateliers.
  • This strategic shift aims to highlight tangible quality and craftsmanship, similar to Blazy's approach at Bottega Veneta, to justify higher prices.

  • Jonathan Anderson's debut women's collection for Dior is previewed, with early looks from Venice suggesting a less conservative and more challenging vision than his predecessor, Maria Grazia Chiuri.
  • These initial designs feature draped silhouettes and distinct historical elements, such as bustles.
  • The collection aims to capture attention in the current digital media landscape, indicating a strategic shift for the brand.

  • The sustained narrative and content strategy post-runway are deemed more critical for long-term brand momentum than initial show excitement or pre-runway teasers.
  • Brands like Dior utilize pre-runway teasers, including red carpet looks, to build anticipation, but follow-through is crucial.
  • The discussion highlights Celine, where Michael Ryder's first show received a warm reception, but a recent campaign maintained continuity with Eddie Sliman's aesthetic, prompting subtle commentary on intellectual property.

  • Early signals of success for new creative directions will be a brand's ability to sustain post-show excitement and demonstrate incremental progress over weeks and months, rather than a single 'hot day' event.
  • A significant debut mistake would be an overly ambitious collection that exceeds the brand's delivery capabilities, potentially leading to a crash.
  • A more measured approach, hinting at future developments and building desirability, might be more effective than a radical but poorly executed debut.
  • Maison Margiela's ready-to-wear debut and its future evolution are highlighted as a brand likely to surprise, with its team radically rethinking brand identity and communication.
