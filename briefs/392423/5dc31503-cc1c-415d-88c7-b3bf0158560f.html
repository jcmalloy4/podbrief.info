Happier with Gretchen Rubin

Move Happier: How to "Get It Done" and Finally Complete Your Dream Project [Bonus]

Key Takeaways

  • Project completion prioritizes clarity, specific steps, and monitoring over mere motivation.
  • Define goals specifically and measurably, breaking them into realistic, controllable daily actions.
  • Understand your project's 'why' and align actions with core values for sustained effort.
  • Prioritize physical well-being, like adequate sleep, to boost energy and improve project focus.

Deep Dive

  • The audiobook offers practical, science-backed tips for completing large projects and includes a downloadable workbook for personalized planning.
  • It is available exclusively on Audible and included in their Plus Catalog for $14.95 per month after a 30-day trial.
  • The host, Gretchen Rubin, shares her personal difficulty in learning watercolor painting despite past attempts.

  • The host states that clarity on project specifics and actionable steps is more vital than waiting for motivation.
  • A 2009 study showed voters who planned their process were 9% more likely to vote.
  • Vague goals like 'learn Italian' lead to discouragement as they lack clear definition and progress measurement, providing an excuse for inaction.

  • Goals should be specific, measurable, and controllable, such as becoming conversational in Italian by a specific date or dedicating 30 minutes daily to marketing a novel.
  • A clear finish line, like 'a year of watercolor' with a postcard mailed monthly, helps define specific and manageable actions.
  • Realistic actions, such as practicing Italian for 20 minutes daily or decluttering for 40 minutes daily, are crucial to prevent feeling overwhelmed and ensure progress.

  • Benjamin Franklin's 'virtue chart' demonstrated the power of self-monitoring for self-improvement.
  • Research indicates that tracking behavior, such as with pedometers or food journals, increases effort and goal alignment.
  • Implementing monitoring for measurable actions, like applying to jobs for 30 minutes daily, helps prevent distractions and ensures task completion.

  • Gaining clarity on a project's purpose is crucial, exemplified by Michelangelo's deep faith motivating his Sistine Chapel work.
  • A 2007 study by Adam Grant found call center employees interacting with scholarship recipients raised nearly three times more funds.
  • Identifying and reconciling conflicting values, such as wanting focused work time versus being responsive to others, is essential to avoid project paralysis.

  • Exhaustion hinders project progress, as illustrated by a woman who completed a photo album after changing her late-night TV routine to reading, enabling more sleep.
  • Adequate sleep, at least seven hours, and regular exercise, even a 20-minute walk, are critical for physical well-being and project completion.
  • Research indicates that short sleep negatively impacts health and cognitive function.

  • The host outlines a five-step process: (1) Define the specific, completable, and controllable project, like cooking 10 healthy meals.
  • (2) Define what a completed project looks like, such as a garden with cleared underbrush and an irrigation system.
  • (3) Outline specific, measurable actions, including daily practice or museum sketches, while advising against buying new supplies.
  • (4) Monitor progress through methods like sticky notes or marking a calendar, planning daily practice and monthly postcard mailing.
  • (5) Articulate the project's 'why' by connecting with personal values, such as gaining a lifelong occupation or experiencing personal growth.

  • The host advises listeners to start projects immediately, emphasizing that learning occurs through action and insights gained during the process are more valuable than extensive upfront planning.
  • She congratulates listeners on completing initial planning steps, stressing that perfection is not the goal.
  • Her motivation for learning watercolor includes gaining a lifelong occupation, seeing the world differently, and experiencing personal growth.
