More Happier: Introducing “Since You Asked with Gretchen Rubin and Lori Gottlieb”

Key Takeaways

  • Gretchen Rubin launched a new podcast, "Since You Asked," with therapist Lori Gottlieb.
  • The podcast tackles daily dilemmas, from family responsibilities to relationship advice.
  • Segments include listener questions, "Wisdom of Crowds" polls, and "advice gone wrong" stories.
  • The hosts emphasize communication, understanding perspectives, and trusting one's instincts.

Deep Dive

  • Gretchen Rubin announced her new podcast, "Since You Asked," co-hosted with therapist and author Lori Gottlieb.
  • The advice-focused show addresses everyday problems, pet peeves, and dilemmas, combining Rubin's happiness research and Gottlieb's therapeutic expertise.
  • The first episode included a question about family dynamics and listener responses to a dating dilemma.
  • The format includes listener questions, advice segments, and rotating topics like 'advice that went terribly wrong' and 'unsolicited advice.'

  • A listener named Laura expressed guilt about her husband's lack of initiative in fostering relationships with his family.
  • The hosts discussed this as a common issue involving emotional labor and the importance of relationships for happiness.
  • They suggested a collaborative approach for couples to manage family connections rather than individual responsibility.
  • The discussion began with a listener query about primary responsibility for fostering family relationships.

  • The "Wisdom of Crowds" segment addressed whether to comment on a friend's perceived bad romantic match.
  • A poll indicated 56% would speak up only if asked, 33% would not intervene, and 10% believed in honesty.
  • Listeners shared varied perspectives, citing instances where advice negatively impacted friendships or proved incorrect.
  • The hosts emphasized understanding another's perspective before offering relationship advice.

  • The hosts addressed a listener's question about recurring arguments stemming from differing preferences, such as planning versus spontaneity.
  • They advised focusing on communication and understanding each other's values instead of attempting to change minds.
  • Premarital therapy was suggested as a proactive method to address potential differences before marriage.
  • The discussion drew on personal anecdotes and therapeutic principles to highlight the importance of working through disagreements.

  • The podcast introduced a segment soliciting stories about "advice gone wrong," noting that learning from others' mistakes can be effective.
  • Gretchen Rubin shared an anecdote from her legal career about a colleague facing significant professional consequences for misusing firm letterhead.
  • Listener Jane, a GP, admitted giving bad parenting advice before having children, and Shelly moved to a larger apartment against advice.
  • Sarah bought a home before financial readiness due to market pressure, illustrating poor advice outcomes.

  • Rose kept her child in a "mean environment" believing it would build resilience, but it backfired.
  • Jess was advised by her aunt to change her English major, ultimately preventing her desired writing career.
  • Christy regretted not taking a Wall Street Journal internship in Brussels in 1988, adhering instead to an advisor's insistence on a prior commitment.
  • The hosts highlighted that advice often reflects the giver's risk tolerance, emphasizing the importance of trusting one's own instincts.

  • The "Unsolicited Advice" segment explored the complexities of offering counsel that might not be welcomed.
  • A host shared a personal dilemma about a friend's husband having bad breath, while listeners submitted diverse situations.
  • Listeners wished to advise on toxic relationships, negative attitudes, wardrobe choices, and accepting compliments by simply saying "thank you."
  • The discussion also touched on the difficulty of intervening in serious personal matters, like a neighbor's cheating husband.

  • The hosts introduced Rebecca's listener question concerning a family text thread with judgmental comments from a sister.
  • The sister expressed frustration regarding unequal caregiving responsibilities for elderly parents within the text thread.
  • Rebecca sought advice on effectively setting boundaries in this challenging interpersonal communication scenario.
  • The hosts invited listeners to share their perspectives and advice on how to navigate such family dynamics.
