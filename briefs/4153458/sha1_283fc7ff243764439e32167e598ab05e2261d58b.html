The Skeptics' Guide to the Universe

The Skeptics Guide #1063 - Nov 22 2025

Key Takeaways

  • Skepticism is vital when assessing new technologies like AI and unsubstantiated claims.
  • AI is now designing functional viruses, opening doors for combating antibiotic resistance and raising biosecurity concerns.
  • High-resolution Earth simulations leverage advanced computing to enhance climate and weather modeling.
  • NASA's X-59 jet aims to overcome the sonic boom, potentially allowing commercial supersonic flight over land.
  • Initial findings on cognitive supplements and complex disease diagnostics require cautious, rigorous scientific validation.
  • The criteria for defining animal "tool use" are actively debated in scientific circles.
  • Innovations in synthetic biology enable immortal cell lines and advanced material development.

Deep Dive

  • Dubai positions itself as a "city of the future" through tech investment, where critical thinking about claims is essential.
  • Discussions were dominated by AI's potential impact; hosts debated if current enthusiasm mirrors an internet bubble.
  • Prominent investors selling NVIDIA stock are noted as a potential indicator of an impending market downturn.
  • Skepticism is crucial to distinguish legitimate progress from pseudoscience in technological advancements.

  • Two California research teams successfully used AI to design and create functional viruses that infect and kill bacteria.
  • AI models, trained on DNA datasets, designed complete viral genomes synthesized in the lab to produce bacteriophages.
  • These bacteriophages target antibiotic-resistant bacteria, a significant global health concern.
  • Research is currently preliminary, tested only in controlled lab environments on safe organisms, raising concerns about potential misuse and regulatory lag.

  • A new Earth simulation, published in Physics, achieves 1.25-kilometer resolution for weather phenomena.
  • The model, based on the German Weather Service's ICON model, involves 672 million cells and a trillion degrees of freedom.
  • It is powered by 20,480 NVIDIA GH200 'Grace Hopper' superchips, enabling 145.7 Earth days to be modeled in a single day.

  • Creatine, known for bodybuilding, is being explored for potential benefits in cognitive function and brain health.
  • A 2024 systematic review of 16 trials found mixed results; memory, attention, and processing speed showed improvement, but overall and executive function did not.
  • Effects were more significant in specific subgroups, including those with diseases, individuals aged 18-60, and females.
  • The evidence had low to moderate confidence, with authors calling for larger, more robust clinical trials.

  • NASA and Lockheed Martin's X-59 jet is designed to mitigate sonic booms, producing only 'sonic thumps'.
  • Commercial jet speeds have not significantly increased since the 1950s due to engineering challenges related to fuel efficiency, drag, and cost.
  • The 2000 Concorde crash, caused by tire debris puncturing a fuel tank, significantly reduced public confidence and profitability.
  • The X-59's long, chisel-shaped nose is key to reshaping continuous shock waves into a quieter thump.

  • The X-59's initial test flight confirmed airworthiness at 230 mph.
  • Future tests will aim for Mach 1.4 at 55,000 feet, followed by flights over communities.
  • These community flights will gather public perception data on the reduced sonic thump.
  • The viability of commercial supersonic flight hinges on regulatory acceptance of this reduced noise level to change aviation laws.

  • Chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS), or myalgic encephalomyelitis, is now recognized as a distinct physiological disorder involving immune, neurological, and metabolic dysfunctions.
  • A Science Daily headline claimed a breakthrough blood test could confirm CFS with 96% accuracy using epigenetic profiling.
  • Podcast hosts expressed skepticism, noting a key flaw: the comparison group only included healthy individuals, not those with other chronic illnesses.

  • A listener queried a Science paper claiming wolves use tools to retrieve submerged crabs.
  • The hosts debated the definition of tool use, specifically the requirement for an unattached object.
  • One host argued the wolf's action was instinctual behavior rather than true tool use, comparing it to squirrels accessing bird feeders.
  • Discussion questioned the wolf's cognitive processes and potential for hasty generalization from a single observation.

  • Scientists successfully created immortal cow cells without genetic modification.
  • The mechanism involves spontaneous telomerase activation and PGC1-alpha expression.
  • This advancement could potentially aid lab-grown meat production by bypassing GMO regulations and reducing costs.
