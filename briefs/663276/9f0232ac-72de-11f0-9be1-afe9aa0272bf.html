Today, Explained

Millennials are getting old

Key Takeaways

  • The internet is experiencing a resurgence of 2016 nostalgia, notably on TikTok.
  • Millennials are grappling with anxieties about aging and a perceived less secure societal future.
  • A study identifies age 44 as an "aging cliff" due to accelerated cellular degeneration.
  • The episode discusses whether accepting aging and mortality is healthier than actively fighting it.

Deep Dive

  • Internet culture reporter Deja Tolentino notes a viral TikTok trend romanticizing 2016, featuring songs like Drake's "One Dance" and "King Kylie" content.
  • This nostalgia is linked to millennials entering their 30s and 40s, facing anxieties about aging and a less secure society.
  • The '2016 mood board' highlighted maximalist makeup, neon wigs, and the start of influencer culture, contrasting with pre-algorithm feeds.
  • The perceived optimism of the 2010s, particularly post-2012, offers an escape from current challenges, often overlooking significant political events like Brexit.

  • Analysis suggests 2016 represents a period of cultural monoculture and shared experiences.
  • This era is viewed as preceding a decade of political and social turmoil.
  • The nostalgia stems from a perceived loss of national and political identity in subsequent years.

  • The conversation raises concerns about younger generations focusing on past cultural moments, akin to a 'remakes and sequels' mentality.
  • This trend is interpreted as a form of escapism from current challenges and a potential loss of hope for the future.
  • A recent online shift suggests a move away from constant internet-driven escapism towards creating new cultural content.

  • Host Astead Herndon introduces Emily Gould of New York Magazine to discuss a study on "aging cliffs."
  • The phenomenon is particularly noted around age 44, when cellular degeneration accelerates.
  • Gould explains that this milestone resonates with many millennials as they approach this specific age.

  • Emily Gould shares personal accounts of individuals experiencing sudden health and appearance changes around age 44.
  • She emphasizes open-mindedness and a willingness to change one's mind as key to maintaining a youthful spirit, beyond creams or pills.
  • Gould challenges the cultural narrative that aging is a problem to be solved, advocating for psychological acceptance.
  • She highlights the value of connecting with older individuals and embracing shared life traumas that come with age.

  • The conversation considers whether millennials might age differently due to economic insecurity and delayed traditional life markers.
  • Personal choices, such as living in an expensive city, have fostered resilience from a lack of stability.
  • The discussion contrasts millennial optimism with a history of societal structures collapsing around their generation.
  • The host and guests discuss accepting aging and mortality, viewing the opportunity to grow old as a privilege rather than an anxiety.
