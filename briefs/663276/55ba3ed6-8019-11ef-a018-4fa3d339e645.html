AI Video Killed the Video Star

Key Takeaways

  • AI video production is rapidly evolving but still complex - While tools like Google's VO3, Runway, and MidJourney can create impressive AI-generated films, the process remains technically challenging and time-consuming, requiring sophisticated coordination across multiple platforms.
  • Hollywood is quietly embracing AI despite public hesitation - The industry's "dirty little secret" is that many professionals are already using AI tools like Metaphysic's deepfake technology (used in Tom Hanks' "Here" to age him from 18 to 80), but few openly admit it due to concerns about public perception.
  • AI is democratizing filmmaking while disrupting traditional roles - These tools are making visual effects cheaper and faster, potentially giving more creators access to professional-quality production while forcing traditional departments like makeup and VFX to adapt their business models.
  • Public reception remains polarized - Audiences are split between being impressed by AI video realism and finding it "soulless," reflecting broader societal tensions about synthetic content's authenticity and the growing difficulty in distinguishing real from AI-generated media.
  • Industry experts maintain nuanced optimism - Those most familiar with AI technology recognize that while it's transforming production capabilities, human creativity remains indispensable in the creative process, suggesting collaboration rather than complete replacement.

Deep Dive

Project Genesis and Technological Evolution

  • Joanna Stern, Wall Street Journal technology columnist, embarked on an ambitious AI video project in March, collaborating with a colleague to create a short film using cutting-edge AI video tools
  • The project evolved rapidly due to constant technological updates in the AI video space, with new developments emerging throughout production:
- Google's VO3 generative AI video model became available - New AI video editing tool called Flow was introduced - VO3 expanded to include audio generation capabilities alongside video prompts

  • The film's conceptual framework centered on exploring efficiency through a humanoid robot character, following Stern's character living with an AI robot (OptiMax 5000) designed to maximize productivity, featuring humorous human-robot interactions

Production Process and Technical Challenges

  • Multi-tool AI production pipeline required sophisticated coordination:
- MidJourney for generating robot character images - Runway for video generation - Google Vio for motion and scene creation - Google Gemini for crafting detailed prompts

  • Significant technical constraints emerged during production:
- Robot character lacked mouth movements - Narrator's character also had no mouth animation - Complex production process requiring precise AI tool instructions - Creating coherent narrative proved possible but difficult and time-consuming

  • The rapid pace of AI advancement created ongoing challenges, with new tools and updates emerging frequently throughout the production timeline

Audience Reception and Industry Context

  • Mixed audience reactions to the completed AI-generated video:
- Positive responses: Viewers impressed by realism and enjoyed behind-the-scenes bloopers - Negative responses: Some called it "soulless" and "garbage," expressing strong emotional reactions

  • Broader implications for video production include:
- Democratization of filmmaking tools - Potential integration into Hollywood productions - Concerns about deepfakes and misinformation - Challenges in distinguishing synthetic from real video content

AI's Current Hollywood Integration

  • Industry adoption reality: Despite public narratives of either job destruction or creative magic, there's a "dirty little secret" that many in Hollywood are already using AI but hesitate to admit it
  • Metaphysic, a specialized AI visual effects company, demonstrates advanced deepfake technology:
- Can digitally recreate and manipulate actors' faces - Working on the film "Here" starring Tom Hanks and Robin Wright - Used AI to allow Tom Hanks to play the same character from age 18 to 80, eliminating need for traditional makeup or prosthetics

Industry Transformation and Future Implications

  • AI is making visual effects cheaper and faster, potentially giving directors more creative control while disrupting traditional film industry departments (makeup, VFX, etc.)
  • Compensation models may shift, with professionals like makeup artists potentially paid for initial design rather than entire production cycles
  • Tom Hanks' surprisingly cavalier attitude toward AI potentially using his likeness posthumously may reflect a "class divide" in AI concerns, raising future ethical questions about posthumous likeness usage
  • Potential positive outcomes include:
- Lower barriers to film production - More films possible with smaller budgets - Parallels to how digital technology democratized filmmaking in the 1990s

  • Industry experts most familiar with AI tend to be most nuanced about its capabilities, recognizing that human creativity remains indispensable in AI-assisted creative processes
  • The overarching tension remains between concerns about AI's capacity to replace human workers and uncertainty about the extent of potential job displacement, creating mixed emotions of fear and relief regarding AI's expanding capabilities
*The completed short film is available at wsj.com and YouTube under the title "We Tested Google Vio and Runway to Create This AI Film."*
