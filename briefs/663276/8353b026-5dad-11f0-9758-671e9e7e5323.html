Today, Explained

Talk turkey to me

Key Takeaways

  • Thanksgiving meal preparation requires strategic planning and specific cooking techniques to ensure a tender turkey.
  • Common turkey cooking problems, particularly thawing, can be resolved with proper temperature checks and resources like the Butterball Talkline.
  • The turkey's status as a Thanksgiving staple is rooted in its historical abundance and affordability, formalized by Sarah Hale's advocacy in 1863.
  • Modern Thanksgiving traditions were shaped by industrialization and commercial promotion, with one-fifth of all turkeys consumed on this holiday.
  • Avoiding post-meal discomfort involves mindful eating practices, managing alcohol intake, and incorporating light physical activity.

Deep Dive

  • Gastroenterologist Dr. Tricia Pesricha reports increased cases of food stuck in the esophagus and heart attacks on Thanksgiving night.
  • Exercising, like a 'turkey trot,' prepares the digestive system and releases endorphins.
  • Experts advise against starving all day, as this leads to faster eating and bloating due to a 20-minute fullness delay.
  • Prioritize vegetables and protein to fill at least half your plate for satiety and blood sugar control.
  • Consider a 'grazing' approach with appetizers to prevent extreme hunger before the main meal.

  • Chef Hamil Whaley recommends a detailed game plan for cooking, outlining all necessary items and times.
  • He suggests a 'low and slow' cooking method initially, then increasing oven temperature for crispy skin.
  • The Butterball Turkey Talkline, staffed by Gwen Carlisle, provides solutions for unusual cooking mishaps, like turkeys roasted with sink stoppers.
  • Common issues include thawing, which requires approximately four hours per pound in the refrigerator.
  • Always check the internal temperature of the turkey with a meat thermometer to ensure it is fully cooked.

  • Historian Troy Bickham states the traditional first Thanksgiving story is inaccurate, with eyewitness accounts confirming venison, a luxury food.
  • Turkey became a Thanksgiving staple due to its abundance and affordability, especially during the fall harvest season.
  • Thanksgiving evolved into a national holiday during the Civil War era, established by Abraham Lincoln in 1863, following advocacy from Sarah Hale.
  • Post-1863, Thanksgiving, alongside Christmas and July Fourth, solidified its place in American customs, with turkey recipes becoming widely published.

  • Early 20th-century industrialization saw companies like Butterball promote turkeys, while Libby's sold condensed milk via pumpkin pie recipes.
  • Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, starting in the 1920s, helped kick off the holiday shopping season.
  • Approximately one-fifth of all turkeys are consumed on Thanksgiving, reinforcing its image as a grand feast.
  • The holiday's adaptability and lack of strong religious connotations contribute to its enduring popularity.

  • The host debunks the myth that turkey's tryptophan causes post-meal sleepiness, attributing it instead to simple carbohydrates like pasta and biscuits.
  • Alcohol consumption before or during the meal can slow stomach emptying, potentially leading to discomfort.
  • A 10-15 minute post-meal walk is recommended to aid digestion, reduce bloating, and manage blood sugar.
  • Maintain an upright posture after eating if a walk is not immediately possible.
  • The host offers a pro-tip to spatchcock turkeys for better cooking and suggests creative leftover ideas like turkey fajitas.
