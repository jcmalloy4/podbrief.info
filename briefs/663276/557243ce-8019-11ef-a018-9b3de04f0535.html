Today, Explained

Black schools, red states

Key Takeaways

  • Political interference in HBCU leadership is escalating, exemplified by Florida's controversial appointment of Marva Johnson at FAMU—a politically connected lobbyist with minimal university experience who wasn't initially a top candidate, raising concerns about merit-based selection processes.
  • HBCUs face severe financial vulnerabilities due to heavy reliance on federal funding, with 90% of students receiving federal aid; even small policy changes like 2011 parent loan modifications caused immediate 3-4% enrollment drops, making these institutions extremely susceptible to political budget decisions.
  • Institutional erosion threatens HBCU missions, as demonstrated by FAMU's previous losses of its hospital and law school transfer to FSU, with current fears about potential elimination of critical programs like pharmacy and nursing that serve historically underrepresented communities.
  • Strategic positioning around economic impact rather than political identity may be HBCUs' best defense, as leaders like Tuskegee's Dr. Mark Brown advocate emphasizing these institutions' role as economic engines producing essential professionals and creating social mobility.
  • Systemic funding disparities remain stark, with total HBCU endowments collectively smaller than single predominantly white institutions, highlighting the need for sustained support to maintain these institutions' critical role in American higher education.

Deep Dive

Political Intervention in Florida Higher Education

The conversation begins with the context of Governor Ron DeSantis's increasing control over university presidential appointments in Florida, with particular focus on the political intervention at historically black colleges like Florida A&M University (FAMU). This sets the stage for examining a controversial leadership appointment that has raised significant concerns within the HBCU community.

The Marva Johnson Appointment Controversy

Background and Qualifications:

  • Marva Johnson, a public sector lobbyist previously with Charter Communications, was appointed as FAMU's president despite lacking significant university administration experience
  • She has strong ties to the Florida Republican Party, previously served on the State Board of Education, and was a Donald Trump elector
  • Johnson was not initially among the top three presidential candidates but was added as a last-minute finalist
Contentious Selection Process:
  • Her interview process proved controversial, with Johnson struggling to demonstrate knowledge about FAMU
  • She made statements that were directly challenged by the university's board chairwoman
  • The appointment faced significant skepticism from FAMU alumni and stakeholders who questioned both her qualifications and motivations
  • Many perceived this as a politically motivated appointment rather than one based on educational leadership merit

Institutional Concerns and Historical Context

Immediate Fears About Johnson's Leadership:

  • Significant concerns exist about potential elimination of long-standing academic programs, particularly pharmacy and nursing programs that are historically important to FAMU and HBCUs generally
  • These fears are grounded in FAMU's previous institutional losses
Historical Challenges:
  • FAMU has previously lost its hospital, representing a significant institutional setback
  • The university's College of Law was dismantled and transferred to Florida State University, demonstrating a pattern of institutional diminishment
Johnson's Leadership Rationale:
  • Her argument for the presidency centers on being an ally who can maintain state funding and raise money
  • She prioritizes students as the primary "customer and constituent"
  • The university currently faces fundraising challenges, with only 6% of alumni contributing donations

Broader HBCU Financial Vulnerabilities

Federal Funding Dependencies:

  • HBCUs face critical financial pressures, including potential loss of federal funding and changes in federal student aid allocation
  • Public HBCUs are particularly vulnerable due to heavy reliance on public financing
  • 9 out of 10 HBCU students receive some form of federal financial aid, making them especially susceptible to policy changes
Historical Impact of Policy Changes:
  • Changes in federal assistance programs, specifically parent plus loans in 2011, led to an immediate 3-4% reduction in HBCU enrollment
  • Most HBCU students rely on Pell Grants and federal aid, so reduced access directly impacts student enrollment
  • Concerns exist about potential budget reductions affecting grad plus loans, Pell Grant eligibility, and student loan default policies

HBCU Leadership Perspective: Dr. Mark Brown's Approach

Institutional Philosophy:

  • Dr. Mark Brown, President of Tuskegee University (founded in 1881 by Booker T. Washington and ranked #3 among HBCUs), advocates for viewing education as politically neutral
  • He emphasizes economic impact and capability over political or demographic considerations
  • His approach focuses on student outcomes like internships, experiences, and graduation rates
Strategic Positioning:
  • Brown promotes approaching administrations by highlighting economic contributions rather than political considerations
  • He views HBCUs as economic engines creating social and economic mobility
  • HBCUs produce critical professionals including chemical engineers, electrical engineers, and aviators, contributing to national workforce needs
Addressing Systemic Disparities:
  • Total HBCU endowments are less than a single university like Brown University, highlighting significant wealth disparities between HBCUs and predominantly white institutions
  • Brown emphasizes that the core issue may be poverty rather than HBCU status specifically
  • He advocates for promoting the success of all HBCUs as beneficial to the entire country, emphasizing capability, performance, and tangible outcomes

Trump Administration Context

The discussion acknowledges that the Trump administration signed an executive order to support HBCUs, aimed at increasing federal opportunities, grants, and contracts, with claims of providing unprecedented funding to HBCUs. However, this occurs within the broader context of ongoing concerns about federal support and the complex relationship between political administrations and HBCU funding stability.
