Today, Explained

Your pop music is influenced by God

Key Takeaways

  • Christian music experienced significant mainstream growth in 2023, boosted by TikTok and Billboard charts.
  • Streaming and social media platforms are democratizing music access, enabling genre crossovers like Christian music.
  • Gospel music profoundly influences secular pop, evident in vocal styles and song structures.
  • The "slow build" song structure, with roots in gospel, is a pervasive element in contemporary secular hits.
  • Streaming data now reveals Christian music's substantial, previously under-recognized listener base.

Deep Dive

  • Christian music saw significant growth in 2023, with artists gaining traction on TikTok and climbing Billboard charts.
  • Wall Street Journal reporter Elias Light noted two Christian artists simultaneously on the Hot 100, supported by Luminate data.
  • The genre is expanding rapidly, approaching the popularity of electronic dance music (EDM) in the U.S.

  • Streaming and social media, particularly TikTok, have made genres like K-pop, Latin, and country more accessible.
  • This increased accessibility now benefits Christian music, allowing artists to reach wider audiences beyond traditional gatekeepers.
  • Artists can achieve popularity without relying on established channels, leading to new negotiation dynamics in the music industry.

  • Gospel music, defined broadly to include call-and-response structures, vocal ornamentation, and harmonic progressions, deeply influences secular music.
  • Examples include Beyoncé's "Single Ladies" for call-and-response and Whitney Houston's vocal style.
  • Thomas Dorsey, born in 1899, blended secular blues and jazz into gospel, earning the title "godfather of gospel music."
  • Modern artists like Justin Bieber's 2025 album "Swag" (specifically "Devotion") incorporate gospel choruses and vocal ornamentations.

  • The "slow build" song structure, a characteristic often associated with gospel music, begins quietly and gradually increases in intensity.
  • This structure mirrors the emotional arc of a religious experience and the rhythm of a church service.
  • Coldplay's "Fix You" exemplifies this, influencing contemporary worship music from groups like Hillsong and Maverick City Music.
  • Lil Wayne's "Let the Beat Build" also successfully employs this gospel-influenced musical structure.

  • Streaming has changed how music consumption is tracked, moving beyond radio play and record sales to individual plays, revealing diverse listening habits.
  • This enables a broader view of popular music, as seen in varied Billboard charts featuring artists like Sizza, Kendrick Lamar, and K-pop's Rose.
  • Christian music's substantial listener base, previously overlooked due to a lack of mainstream media attention, is now visible through streaming data.
  • The trend suggests a growing desire for music offering connection or respite from challenging news.
