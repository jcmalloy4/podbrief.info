Key Takeaways

  • John Register's mission focuses on helping organizations overcome adversity by "amputating fear."
  • His father's activism in the 1960s civil rights movement profoundly influenced his early life.
  • A severe leg injury in 1994 led to amputation, shifting his athletic focus to the Paralympics.

Deep Dive

  • John Register, a two-time Paralympian and Persian Gulf veteran, works with organizations and executives.
  • His professional mission involves helping them overcome adversity by "amputating fear."
  • He advocates for embracing a "new normal mindset" to achieve success in challenging environments.

  • As a freshman, the guest won the collegiate high hurdles at the Dallas Morning News Invitational.
  • He was invited to compete against elite athletes that evening but finished last in the race.
  • Jeanette Bolden approached him, advising him to use his admiration for competitors as motivation to train harder and win the following year.

  • Post-graduation, the guest bypassed a job offer to enlist in the Army, motivated by the World Class Athlete Program and financial support.
  • His Army experience, including exposure to struggles for equality and service like the Tuskegee Airmen, shaped his character.
  • Reflecting on service, his focus shifted towards building a meaningful life founded on faith and purpose by 17:00.

  • On May 17, 1994, a misstep on the third hurdle resulted in a severe left leg injury, leading to the guest choosing amputation over lifelong mobility aid use.
  • Despite choosing amputation to alleviate pain, he experienced intense phantom pains afterward, which worsened as the limb healed.
  • On June 17, 1994, while in severe pain, he watched a news report about the O.J. Simpson white Bronco chase, leading to a moment of spiritual clarity.

  • The guest initially felt shame regarding his amputation and worried about losing his wife, realizing the question was whether he was still desirable.
  • His son's simple validation of him as 'dad' prompted him to question his own doubts, aiding his transition from an athlete to a disabled individual.
  • He outlines a resilience model beginning with 'reckoning,' accepting the past and focusing on the present and future rather than hypothetical changes.

  • The guest unexpectedly qualified for the Paralympic Games as a swimmer, despite initially aiming to be a hurdler, and later won a silver medal in the long jump in Sydney.
  • While preparing for a TEDx talk, he had an 'aha' moment, realizing his true strength came from faith, family, and friendships, providing freedom.
  • This realization marked an internal shift, helping him recognize himself as more than just an athlete or veteran, especially after losing that identity by 35:58.

  • The guest believes investing in others and 'breathing life' into them opened new opportunities, comparing this to planting seeds that yield an abundant harvest.
  • He states that negative actions yield negative outcomes, while positive intentions lead to good results and growth.
  • This philosophy became his mission: to inspire life in others, viewing inspiration as the catalyst for motivation, action, and transformation.

  • During Operation Desert Shield/Storm, the guest unexpectedly connected with a Saudi prince by learning basic Arabic.
  • This interaction led to a shift in his relationship with Saudi guards, who invited him to share meals, teaching him the value of "breaking bread" and learning from every engagement.
  • He highlights wisdom from 'Uncle Gloucester,' emphasizing the importance of understanding and arguing for differing perspectives, a trait he observed and modeled in Saudi Arabia.
