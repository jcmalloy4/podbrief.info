Stocks Drop In Major Reversal… And Opportunities In A Red-Hot Biotech Sector 11/20/25

Key Takeaways

  • An early rally in stocks gave way to a broad market sell-off, with NVIDIA's reversal as a key factor.
  • Walmart delivered strong earnings, signaling robust consumer health and reduced inflationary pressures.
  • Gap Inc. posted its best comparable sales growth since 2018, raising its full-year financial guidance.
  • The biotech sector significantly outperformed the broader market, fueled by substantial M&A activity.
  • OpenAI partnered with Foxconn to expand U.S. AI data center hardware production amidst efforts to diversify chip suppliers.

Deep Dive

  • An early market rally reversed, with NVIDIA's stock falling after initially rising, causing major indexes like the NASDAQ, Dow, and S&P 500 to end the day lower.
  • Market internals revealed that while the S&P 500 reached all-time highs, the average stock within the index was already down significantly from its peak.
  • Selling was noted as indiscriminate, and tech companies like Oracle experienced significant losses, with competitor AMD closing down 8%.
  • The VIX (volatility index) and only 150 S&P stocks advancing suggested the sell-off was not widespread panic, but potential for further downside remains.

  • Walmart's stock closed up over 6% after exceeding sales and profit estimates, driven by strong e-commerce growth.
  • The company raised its full-year guidance for the second consecutive time, indicating strong consumer health and no negative impact from a recent SNAP benefits shutdown.
  • Former Walmart U.S. CEO Bill Simon noted only 1.2% inflation in the report, which is seen as positive for the broader economy and suggests subsiding tariff fears.
  • Walmart's leadership transition, with an insider CEO, is viewed positively for maintaining momentum, contrasting with challenges faced by competitor Target, which is at a 52-week low.

  • Gap Inc. reported its best comparable sales growth since early 2018, with Gap, Old Navy, and Banana Republic brands exceeding expectations.
  • The company's earnings of 62 cents per share and revenue surpassed forecasts, leading to a raised full-year net sales outlook and operating margin guidance.
  • CEO Richard Dixon expressed confidence in Athleta's resurgence under new leadership despite its underperformance.
  • Gap's marketing strategy includes celebrity partnerships and campaigns aimed at reconnecting with consumers and attracting Gen Z, blending music and fashion.

  • The delayed September jobs report was analyzed as bullish due to increased labor force participation and a rise in long-term unemployed.
  • Ben Emmons of Fed Watch Advisors suggested the report supports the Federal Reserve's stance on holding interest rates steady, with a 40% market probability for a December rate cut.
  • Despite comments from Beth Hammack about elevated inflation, the guest believes the Fed will remain on hold through December given a stable labor market.
  • The bond market exhibits low volatility with yields around 4.1%, influenced by hedging against the equity market and anticipation of future Fed policy.

  • Concerns were raised about the rapid growth of private credit and emerging stresses, exemplified by a terminated merger.
  • Federal Reserve officials, including Lisa Cook, are closely monitoring private credit markets, indicating potential regulatory scrutiny from the SEC and FSOC.
  • Interest rates have remained stagnant around 4% since autumn 2022, fluctuating between 3.2% and 5%, supporting a 'Goldilocks' market narrative.
  • Volatility in specific stocks, such as Oracle's 6.5% fall after a 10% intraday reversal, is linked to increased debt issuance and complex financing deals in opaque private markets.

  • The biotech sector outperformed the broader market, rising 22% year-to-date, driven by increased M&A activity.
  • John Flavin, CEO of Portal Innovation, noted 18 deals worth over $1 billion in 2025, replenishing big pharma pipelines and benefiting early-stage startups.
  • Total M&A activity reached $100 billion for the year, doubling last year's figures, which allows venture investors to recoup and reinvest in new companies.
  • This M&A surge is primarily attributed to transformative therapeutics addressing diseases like cancer and neurological disorders.

  • A significant breakthrough in gene editing, exemplified by the cure of a fatal disease using CRISPR technology, is expected to drive future biotech innovation and business models.
  • Finding quality deals at desired valuations remains competitive, with acquiring companies focusing on late-stage assets after a cleansing of the public market.
  • Traditional biotech innovation hubs like Boston and San Francisco continue to be central, with emerging centers like Texas gaining prominence.
  • Texas is highlighted for its state funding initiatives, including a $6 billion cancer research bond and a new $3 billion neurodegenerative disease research program, attracting investment and faculty.

  • OpenAI is partnering with Foxconn to develop and manufacture AI data center hardware in the U.S., aiming to accelerate the deployment of its compute commitments.
  • Foxconn, now a major AI server maker and NVIDIA supplier, will produce AI server racks and key components at its American factories, bolstering U.S. AI infrastructure.
  • OpenAI CEO Sam Altman is seeking to diversify AI hardware, including signing deals with Broadcom for custom AI chips to rival NVIDIA GPUs.
  • NVIDIA's SEC filing indicated no assurance of definitive agreements with OpenAI for a potential $100 billion deal for its chips.
