CNBC's "Fast Money"

Fast Money 9/30/25

Key Takeaways

  • Consumer credit indicators suggest increasing stress for lower-income individuals, impacting banks and consumer finance.
  • Major pharmaceutical companies, including Pfizer, secured favorable drug pricing deals and tariff exemptions from the White House.
  • Instacart faces heightened competition but its robust financial position makes it a potential acquisition target.
  • Investors exhibit strong loyalty to megacap tech and AI stocks, even amid concerns about market overvaluation.
  • The U.S. government is strategically acquiring equity stakes in companies critical to mineral supply chains.

Deep Dive

  • Major banks like Citi, Wells Fargo, and Bank of America experienced significant drops, with the KBE Bank ETF falling nearly 1%.
  • Private equity firms KKR, Apollo, and Carlyle Group each declined at least 3%, indicating broader financial market strain.
  • The consumer finance sector, including Affirm, SoFi, Klarna, and Capital One, also showed notable downturns.
  • Bankruptcies of PartsMaker, First Brands, and subprime lender Tricolor Holdings signal stress on the lower-income consumer.
  • Ninety-day credit card delinquency rates have risen to over 12%, and credit scores have fallen since the financial crisis.

  • A JLL survey indicates consumers plan to spend less and shop later this holiday season.
  • Lower and middle-income households are pulling back, driving them to discount retailers like Walmart and Target over luxury options.
  • Consumer concerns are primarily focused on the soft job market, tariffs, gas prices, job security, and global conflicts.
  • Retailers' real estate decisions are based on long-term projections (two to three years out), with most remaining bullish on the consumer's future.
  • Eighty-four percent of consumers prefer brick-and-mortar stores, highlighting a historical shortage of new retail real estate.

  • Nike reported 1% revenue growth, exceeding analyst expectations, driven by progress in North America and over 20% growth in the running category.
  • Challenges persist, including a 10% decline in Greater China, drops in Nike Direct (primarily digital), and Converse (over 25%).
  • Nike's CEO acknowledges ongoing issues, with tariffs remaining a significant concern, potentially costing the company $1 billion.
  • Analysts noted that while progress is being made, the stock's valuation may not yet be justified by its modest revenue growth.
  • An inventory decline was viewed as a positive step towards margin improvement for the company.

  • The U.S. government, through the Energy Secretary, will acquire a 5% stake in Lithium Americas and its joint venture mine with General Motors.
  • This action, in exchange for government loans, indicates a broader strategy of the U.S. government taking equity in companies supporting critical mineral supply chains.
  • More such deals are expected as part of this initiative to secure vital resources.
  • A $65 million investment secured the 5% stake, reflecting a strategy of ownership over simple financial contributions.

  • Pfizer has agreed to lower drug prices and invest in the U.S. in exchange for a three-year tariff exemption.
  • The deal includes offering Medicaid prices closer to those in other developed countries and selling medicines directly to patients.
  • Analysts suggest the deal will not significantly impact Pfizer's sales as the discounted drugs are older and Medicaid already receives favorable pricing.
  • The White House is reportedly considering Eli Lilly next, a development seen as positive for pharmaceutical stocks.
  • Pfizer's stock also benefited from better-than-expected mid-stage data for a recently acquired long-acting GLP1 drug.

  • Instacart shares dropped 5% following a downgrade by BTIG analysts, citing increased competition from Amazon, DoorDash, and Uber.
  • Despite competitive pressures, Instacart boasts strong gross margins of 75%, projected earnings and revenue growth, and substantial cash reserves with no debt.
  • The company's logistics and delivery capabilities make it a potential strategic acquisition target for companies like Walmart or Uber.
  • Its recent price drop, coupled with high trading volume and a seven-times enterprise value to cash ratio, suggests a favorable risk-reward profile for long investors.

  • An Investopedia survey indicates investors are 'overly loyal' to their existing top holdings, which largely mirror the NASDAQ 100's largest companies.
  • Investors acknowledge a potential bubble in AI and megacap tech stocks but continue to hold these positions, often sitting on substantial gains.
  • The current market sentiment, characterized by returning IPOs, SPACs, and a strong crypto market, resembles the 2020-2021 period.
  • A 'risk-on' mentality persists among both global fund managers and individual investors, despite ongoing concerns about overvaluation.

  • Core Weave shares rose 12% following a $14 billion AI infrastructure deal with Meta Platforms, despite Meta's stock declining 1%.
  • Analysts expressed skepticism regarding Core Weave's profitability, capacity to build data centers, and the market's valuation of future contracts.
  • Concerns were raised about customer concentration, the cost of infrastructure, and the company's reliance on debt.
  • NVIDIA shares reached an all-time high, defying earlier expectations of a decline post-earnings, with a large volume day noted.
