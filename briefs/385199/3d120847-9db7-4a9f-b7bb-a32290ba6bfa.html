Tech, Software Sell Off… And The Impact On Private Credit 2/3/26

Key Takeaways

  • Tech and software stocks led a market sell-off, raising AI trade sustainability questions.
  • Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang dismissed OpenAI investment concerns as "complete nonsense."
  • AMD's stock declined despite exceeding revenue, partly due to significant China sales.
  • Novo Nordisk shares dropped 14.5% after a sales warning and increased market competition.
  • Bitcoin's slide continued, falling near $72,000 and impacting crypto-related stocks.
  • Chipotle shares fell 7% on declining same-store sales despite beating earnings expectations.
  • A market rotation into small-caps, industrials, and economically sensitive sectors is observed.
  • Private credit firms face concerns regarding their exposure to the AI-disrupted software industry.

Deep Dive

  • The NASDAQ led a market downturn, with tech and software stocks sinking.
  • Investors questioned the sustainability of the AI trade and explored rotation into other sectors.
  • Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang dismissed concerns about the company's investment in OpenAI as "complete nonsense."
  • Software valuations are high, with some trading over 30 times revenue, raising monetization questions.

  • Small-cap stocks and emerging markets showed strong performance in January.
  • A rotation from tech into industrials and economically sensitive areas is observed, creating a stock-picker's market.
  • Focus is shifting to quality and solid fundamentals, with a preference for the S&P 600 small-cap index over the Russell 2000.
  • A key risk identified is growth remaining flat rather than re-accelerating, impacting market dynamics.

  • AMD's stock dropped despite exceeding revenue expectations for the quarter.
  • Revenue beat was partly due to $390 million in China sales, making the non-China beat less significant.
  • Q1 guidance included $100 million for China sales, with potential US government tariffs posing a concern.
  • The company maintains a positive outlook on gross margins and a strong data center and GPU chip product ramp in the second half.

  • Investors are dumping software stocks, with the IGV ETF down nearly 5% for the day and 19% year-to-date.
  • AI advancements threaten to displace existing software across various segments, impacting major companies.
  • Specific companies like Vertex, Alchemy, Intuit, and Salesforce are identified as significant losers.
  • Some analysts suggest the market has overreacted, presenting a potential buying opportunity in oversold software.

  • Novo Nordisk shares dropped 14.5% midday, closing lower due to a sales warning forecasting a 5-13% decline this year.
  • The company faces increased competition in U.S. drug pricing and the loss of exclusivity for Ozempix in several countries.
  • The decline is attributed partly to disappointing Pfizer data and management's inability to provide comprehensive competitive information.
  • Concerns focus on flatlining US prescription volumes for the new oral GLP1 pill and overall pricing impact on future profitability.

  • Bitcoin's decline continued, hitting a low near $72,000, its lowest since November 2024, impacting crypto-related stocks.
  • PayPal's stock dropped over 20% following missed earnings estimates and a weak profit forecast for 2026.
  • PayPal shares have fallen over 86% since their 2021 peak, with the company also announcing a new CEO.
  • Charts indicate potential further weakness for Bitcoin, with both short and long-term trend lines breached.

  • Companies like Blue Owl, Ares, and TPG saw their stocks decline due to concerns about exposure to the AI-disrupted software industry.
  • An analyst argued these fears are overblown, citing low loan-to-value ratios (around 30%) and substantial equity buffers.
  • These firms operate on a fee basis, minimizing balance sheet exposure, and have historically underwritten selectively.
  • Despite potential redemption spirals, companies like Blue Owl show strong fundraising, with Q4 2025 being a record.

  • Chipotle's shares fell 7% despite beating earnings expectations for the quarter.
  • The company reported a 2.5% decline in same-store sales and decreased traffic for the fourth consecutive quarter.
  • Chipotle projects flat comparable sales for 2026, citing consumer focus on value and quality, anticipating margin pressure in H1.
  • Growth strategy includes menu innovation, international expansion, and leveraging AI for operational improvements.
