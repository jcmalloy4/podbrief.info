Oracle Drops Back Down To Earth… And State Of the Consumer Ahead Of Holidays 11/12/25

Key Takeaways

  • Oracle's stock experienced a significant post-earnings decline amid debt and AI business margin concerns.
  • The broader AI sector faces scrutiny regarding debt-funded growth and rapid depreciation of infrastructure.
  • Major banks achieved record highs, driven by deregulation and strong capital markets performance.
  • Consumer spending remains resilient, with value retailers gaining market share ahead of the holiday season.
  • Fed officials express caution on rate cuts, while Cisco sees accelerated AI infrastructure orders.

Deep Dive

  • Oracle's stock fell 4% and reached two-month lows, erasing a 35% post-earnings gain from September.
  • Analysts cited potential cash flow issues and a $38 billion increase in debt since September, funding further spending.
  • The stock's 35% sell-off is debated as a potential buying opportunity amidst market volatility.

  • The AI sector is shifting from asset-light to asset-heavy, raising concerns about rapid depreciation schedules, now potentially 8-12 months instead of 6-8 years.
  • Gil Lauria of D.A. Davidson criticized Oracle for borrowing to fund AI capacity for startups like OpenAI, unlike cash-flow-rich competitors.
  • Oracle's reported $300 billion backlog from OpenAI is considered a flexible arrangement with significantly lower margins than its core offerings.

  • Shares of IBM hit an all-time high after announcing a new experimental quantum computing chip.
  • The stock has risen over 43% year-to-date, with analysts noting steady growth.
  • Meta is also highlighted as a "Mag 7" member uniquely positioned to leverage AI for business enhancement.

  • Boston Fed President Susan Collins expressed caution against a December rate cut, favoring steady rates until inflation sustainably moves towards 2%.
  • Market expectations for a December rate cut have consequently lowered.
  • Discussions on CPI data coinciding with the Federal Reserve meeting created an "interesting setup."

  • Cisco's shares rose after reporting better-than-expected sales and profit and raising full-year guidance.
  • The company reported $1.3 billion in AI infrastructure orders from hyperscalers in Q1, an accelerated pace.
  • Cisco expects to recognize $3 billion in AI infrastructure revenue from hyperscalers in fiscal year 2026.

  • Flutter Entertainment, FanDuel's parent company, saw shares sink despite exceeding earnings expectations.
  • The company reported lower-than-expected revenues.
  • Flutter plans to offer event contracts in states without permitted sports betting.

  • Bank stocks like Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, and American Express reached record highs, with Bank of America trading at 2006 levels.
  • This rally coincides with bank CEOs dining at the White House with President Trump, an event lacking a formal agenda.
  • The favorable environment for large banks is attributed to deregulation, a stable economy, and strong capital markets performance.

  • Retail executive Jerry Storch offered a positive consumer outlook, citing strong year-over-year sales growth and credit card spending.
  • He described the economy as "tree-shaped" with wealthy consumers outperforming, anticipating a strong holiday season.
  • Analysts recommended focusing on value-based retailers like Walmart, Costco, and TJX, which are gaining market share.

  • Disney's stock underperformed the broader market in 2025, with stagnated performance despite streaming issues and service blackouts.
  • Technical analysis suggests Disney may be a buy, with charts indicating a potential triple bottom and a break from a long-term downtrend.
  • Analysts believe Disney's valuable intellectual property and strengthening streaming strategy make it a compelling investment.
