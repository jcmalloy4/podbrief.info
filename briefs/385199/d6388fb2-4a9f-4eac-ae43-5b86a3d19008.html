Broadcom, Oracle Lead Tech Lower, and Pot Stocks Light Up 12/12/25

Key Takeaways

  • Broadcom and Oracle shares dipped due to "AI Angst," raising debt and margin concerns.
  • "AI fatigue" has investors demanding tangible revenue and profitability from AI investments.
  • Marijuana stocks surged after President Trump's plan to reclassify the drug was announced.
  • The housing market is forecast to stabilize in 2026 with increased sales and affordability.
  • Analysts advise market diversification and hedging strategies amidst 2026 economic uncertainties.

Deep Dive

  • Oracle shares dropped 4.5% following a report of delayed OpenAI data center deployments, marking its worst week since 2018 after an earlier nearly 11% fall.
  • Oracle has disputed OpenAI delays, but concerns linger around debt financing, evidenced by increased leases and rising bond yields.
  • Analysts at D.A. Davidson suggest Oracle may be 'strapped for capital,' with credit default swaps showing increased costs.
  • Oracle's structural issues include a significant debt load and the challenge of maintaining its investment-grade rating, with dependence on OpenAI, which is not projected to be profitable for years.

  • The conversation shifts to broader AI market fatigue, questioning when investors will become more receptive to positive fundamental news.
  • One perspective suggests a significant pullback in AI stocks, potentially 10-20%, is needed to establish a bottom and reverse this fatigue.
  • Despite fatigue, a market outlook suggests a significant downturn in AI stocks is unlikely, with focus shifting to December and March earnings, expecting positive positioning.
  • Oracle is noted for likely falling short of 2026 growth targets due to capacity constraints, making it difficult to improve multiples.

  • As AI fundamentals prove themselves, stocks are expected to respond favorably in the first half of 2026, driven by December and March quarter earnings.
  • Discussion explores AI commoditization, with strong tokens anticipated to remain well-priced, indicating continued revenue growth.
  • Safest AI names are identified as those with strong cash flow to support current spending, with a potential AI pullback seen as a buying opportunity for the equal-weighted S&P 500 (RSP).
  • For a swing trade within AI, Broadcom is favored over Oracle due to recent price damage; Intel is also mentioned with an 88% year-to-date gain.

  • Lululemon's stock rose over 9% following the announcement of CEO Calvin McDonald's departure, despite remaining down over 46% year-to-date.
  • Analysts highlight Lululemon's challenges, including intense competition and the need for a significant strategic shift beyond brand innovation.
  • A federal judge certified a class-action lawsuit against Live Nation and Ticketmaster, alleging damages from over 400 million ticket purchases since 2010.

  • Cannabis stocks experienced a significant surge following news of President Trump's plan to reclassify marijuana as a Schedule III drug.
  • This administrative change could ease taxation and potentially facilitate exchange listings for institutional capital.
  • Despite the day's gains, many cannabis stocks remain significantly down from their all-time highs; the illicit market poses a primary challenge.
  • The discussion highlights potential for further upside if regulatory changes are confirmed, driven by short-covering and retail interest.

  • A market watcher questions consensus optimism for 2026, suggesting overlooked factors like tariffs could impact the economy and lead to fewer Fed rate cuts than anticipated.
  • Overvalued tech stocks contribute to market sensitivity and volatility, advising investors to hedge against uncertainty.
  • Hedging strategies include profit-taking, broadening market exposure to underrepresented sectors, considering international opportunities for better valuations, and tax-loss harvesting.
  • Portfolio diversification suggests reducing exposure to top-performing sectors like tech, industrials, financials, and energy, reallocating to healthcare, emerging markets, REITs, and dividend-paying stocks.

  • Chipotle's shares are down 40% since January, facing pressure from tariffs, inflation, and consumer financial strain despite opening its 4,000th store.
  • The 25-34 age demographic is identified as under financial pressure due to student loan payments, increased 'buy now, pay later' services, and limited job growth.
  • Despite a 40% drop, Chipotle stock still trades at nearly 30 times forward earnings, which is considered expensive given the current environment.
  • Analysts suggest Chipotle should focus more on international expansion for potentially more favorable labor issues and price points.

  • Homebuilder stocks are nearly flat for the year due to affordability issues, tight supply, and high borrowing costs.
  • Compass Chief Economist Mike Simonson predicts national home prices could be flat in 2026, with sales increasing as prices stabilize or decline, improving affordability.
  • Florida is highlighted as the most dynamic regional market, with pending sales up 10-15% year-over-year.
  • Lennar, D.R. Horton, Home Depot, and Lowe's are identified as key players to watch, benefiting from potential increases in large-ticket purchases if rates decline.
