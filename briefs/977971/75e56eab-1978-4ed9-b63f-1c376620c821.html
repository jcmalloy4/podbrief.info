Congress Has One Job, And It's Failing Miserably | The REAL Story with Dr. Phil

Key Takeaways

  • U.S. Congress is criticized for failing its primary duty to keep the government operational.
  • Government shutdowns inflict severe human and economic hardships on millions of Americans.
  • The U.S. recurrently faces shutdowns, a stark contrast to parliamentary systems with built-in safeguards.
  • Lawmakers are accused of leveraging constituent needs for political gain while remaining financially unaffected.

Deep Dive

  • Critics label the ongoing government shutdown as a disgrace, with implications that it should be criminal.
  • Millions of Americans are at risk of losing crucial food aid due to the shutdown's economic impact.
  • 1.4 million federal workers are affected, with approximately half furloughed and the other half working without pay.
  • Lawmakers, many of whom are millionaires with salaries up to $223,500, continue to receive their paychecks during the shutdown.
  • The shutdown causes significant operational delays, exemplified by personnel shortages at major airports like LAX.

  • Critical public health and economic data, including flu and COVID-19 hot spots, job reports, and food recall information, face delays exceeding three weeks.
  • Farmers are encountering financial difficulties and potential crop spoilage due to the lack of USDA support during the shutdown.
  • Food assistance programs like SNAP, Head Start, and WIC are projected to expire by November 1st, impacting over 40 million people and hundreds of thousands of children and mothers.
  • Mail service in rural areas may also experience interruptions as the shutdown progresses.

  • The U.S. has experienced 15 major government shutdowns since the 1980s, highlighting a recurring systemic issue.
  • Congress has failed to pass appropriations on time in 42 out of the last 45 years.
  • Parliamentary governments in Europe, Australia, New Zealand, and Canada often mandate new elections or resignations for lawmakers failing to pass budgets.
  • Countries like Belgium have statutory provisions to keep the previous budget in effect, preventing shutdowns and their use as political leverage.

  • Highly paid public servants, including members of Congress, are criticized for utilizing constituent needs as leverage in political power struggles.
  • The financial hardships faced by Americans are contrasted with the comfortable positions maintained by lawmakers during these periods.
  • Listeners are urged to contact their elected officials to demand action on pressing issues, with implicit threats of electoral consequences for inaction.
