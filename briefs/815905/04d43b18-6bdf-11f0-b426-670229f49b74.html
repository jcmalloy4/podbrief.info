CEOs Cut Workforces & World's Biggest Illegal Sports Streaming Network Shut Down

Key Takeaways

  • Tesco is piloting infrared scanners for avocados in UK stores to aid ripeness assessment and reduce food waste.
  • CEOs are increasingly attributing employee layoffs to efficiency gains from generative AI, impacting recent graduates' job prospects.
  • A major illegal sports streaming platform, Stream East, was shut down in a multi-agency sting operation, seizing over $6 million.
  • Pessimism about the American Dream is at a 15-year high, while lottery spending exceeded $103 billion in 2023.

Deep Dive

  • Tesco, a UK supermarket, is piloting avocado infrared scanners in five stores to classify ripeness for customers.
  • The technology aims to assist decision-making for buyers and potentially reduce food waste.
  • The hosts debated the scanners' utility, with both preferring the traditional squeeze method for assessing ripeness.

  • CEOs from major companies like Salesforce, Amazon, Wells Fargo, and Google are openly discussing generative AI's role in reducing workforce headcount.
  • A Stanford paper indicates a 16% drop in employment opportunities for recent college graduates due to AI adoption and automation.
  • Google eliminated over one-third of its managers overseeing small teams, aligning with a trend toward flatter organizational structures.
  • Meta's "Year of Efficiency" also targets reduced reporting layers, with the belief that AI empowers employees, potentially making managers superfluous.

  • The illegal sports streaming site Stream East, which recorded 1.6 billion visits last year, was shut down by a coalition including Amazon, Apple, and Netflix.
  • Egyptian authorities arrested two individuals and seized over $6 million in laundered money and cryptocurrency.
  • The platform was widely used to access sports like the NFL and Premier League without requiring paid subscriptions.
  • High costs for legal streaming options, estimated at $651 to $1,500 annually for all NFL games, contribute to the demand for such illegal services.

  • A survey indicates nearly 70% of Americans believe the American Dream of upward mobility is no longer attainable, marking a 15-year high in pessimism.
  • Americans spent $103 billion on lottery tickets in 2023, exceeding combined spending on entertainment like movies, books, and concerts.
  • Massachusetts leads the nation in lottery revenue despite its population size, potentially attracting buyers from neighboring states.
  • The odds of winning the current $1.7 billion Powerball jackpot are one in 292 million.
