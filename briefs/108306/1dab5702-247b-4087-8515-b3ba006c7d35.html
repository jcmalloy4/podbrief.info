Verdict with Ted Cruz

Venezuela looks to a New Horizon, Democrat Maduro Flip-Flop & Minnesota's very own Civil War Week In Review

Key Takeaways

  • A U.S. military-assisted operation in Venezuela aimed to arrest Nicolás Maduro, an indicted narco-terrorist.
  • Following Maduro's arrest, Delcy Rodríguez, a figure with a Marxist background, was sworn in as interim president.
  • The episode advocates for free and fair elections in Venezuela to foster economic recovery.
  • Minnesota faces a reported $9 billion fraud scandal involving government funds and Somali immigrant communities.
  • U.S. legal precedent supports presidential authority for extraterritorial arrests, even if challenging international law.
  • A congressional hearing examined welfare dependency statistics among Somali immigrant households in Minnesota.

Deep Dive

  • The U.S. operation in Venezuela was described as a short military-assisted arrest of an indicted narco-terrorist, Nicolás Maduro.
  • It was not intended as a regime change through force but rather a law enforcement action.
  • Following Maduro's arrest, Delcy Rodríguez, described as a communist with a Marxist background and corruption allegations, was sworn in as interim president.

  • Discussion highlighted Venezuela's potential for economic recovery through free and fair elections and a pro-America, pro-market leader.
  • The country possesses vast oil and gold reserves.
  • Energy industry executives require government stability, rule of law, and protection against asset seizure for investing billions in Venezuela.

  • Legal precedent from the 1989 Noriega case and an Office of Legal Counsel opinion by Bill Barr supports presidential authority for extraterritorial arrests.
  • This authority extends even if such actions potentially conflict with international law, such as the UN Charter.
  • Democrats were criticized for inconsistent reactions to actions against Nicolás Maduro, initially wanting him removed but later opposing his arrest.

  • The UN Charter's prohibition on the use of force does not prevent the U.S. executive from authorizing extraterritorial abductions under domestic law.
  • The Medellin v. Texas Supreme Court case affirmed U.S. law and the Constitution as supreme.
  • Legal arguments suggest a U.S. arrest abroad, even if violating foreign law, does not infringe on the Fourth Amendment, citing the Noriega case.
  • Nicolás Maduro's legitimacy as Venezuela's head of state has been denied by the U.S. and most of the world since 2019, providing legal justification for arrest.

  • Minnesota Governor Tim Walz is facing allegations of a massive fraud scandal involving $9 billion in misused government funds.
  • The fraud is primarily linked to Somali immigrant communities.
  • Minnesota Democrats are accused of corruption, vote-buying schemes, and inciting violence to distract from the scandal, with Governor Walz's rhetoric highlighted.

  • Senator Ted Cruz announced he will chair a hearing on the Minnesota fraud scandal.
  • Congressman Brandon Gill questioned a witness about welfare usage among Somali immigrant households.
  • Statistics were highlighted comparing food stamp, Medicaid, and general welfare reliance among Somali-headed households to native Minnesotans.
