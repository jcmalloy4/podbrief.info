White House Insider: You Won't Believe What's Coming to California This Year

Key Takeaways

  • Michael Gates, former DOJ official, is campaigning for California Attorney General to address the state's decline.
  • He advocates for enforcing existing laws and empowering local law enforcement to reduce crime and homelessness.
  • Gates criticizes Sacramento's one-party rule and alleged disregard for voter initiatives and constitutional principles.
  • His campaign emphasizes common-sense, nonpartisan issues like public safety and lower taxes, aiming for broad community outreach.
  • Gates highlights his legal experience and track record, including reducing crime and homelessness in Huntington Beach.

Deep Dive

  • Former DOJ Deputy Assistant AG Michael Gates left a prominent federal role to run for California Attorney General.
  • Motivated by the belief California has 'lost its way' due to political leaders in Sacramento being out of touch with everyday citizens.
  • Aims to address rampant injustice, squandered taxpayer money, rising costs, and increased crime in the state.

  • Michael Gates' background includes serving as a prosecutor and managing attorneys at the Department of Justice.
  • He initiated a prosecutor program in Huntington Beach in response to Propositions 47 and 57, leading to a double-digit crime reduction.
  • Successfully reduced the homeless population in Huntington Beach by over 60% through collaboration with local police.
  • Sued California and Orange County over election laws, advocating for clean voter registration systems and rolls.

  • California is experiencing an exodus of talent and resources, contrasting with its past era of innovation in aerospace and computing.
  • Michael Gates believes Californians across political affiliations are 'fed up' with issues like homelessness, crime, and high costs.
  • He criticizes the previous Attorney General for not investigating crimes related to the burning of Los Angeles.
  • Advocates for enforcing existing vagrancy laws to address homelessness and empowering local law enforcement.

  • Michael Gates states Sacramento has an 'agenda' promoting higher crime and homelessness, advocating for enforcing existing laws.
  • He supports district attorneys and police, noting a crime drop in Huntington Beach after implementing a criminal prosecutor program.
  • Listeners are encouraged to support his campaign by visiting gates4ag.com for information and donations.
  • The host and guest discuss the importance of supporting Gates, framing it as a choice between a righteous leader and a corrupt one.

  • Michael Gates introduces himself as a son of God, family man, patriot, and lawyer, prioritizing his passion for the law.
  • His key priorities include enforcing Proposition 36 from day one, which he states Governor Newsom and Attorney General Bonta have neglected due to funding issues.
  • He proposes the Attorney General's office provide resources for cities to defend their police departments and establish city-level prosecutor programs statewide.

  • Michael Gates criticizes Sacramento's political establishment for an alleged 'wanton disregard' for the will of the people and 'egregious legislation.'
  • He highlights the non-implementation of Proposition 36 due to lack of funding in Governor Newsom's budget.
  • Propositions 47 and 57 are cited as examples of voter initiatives allegedly misrepresented and leading to decreased safety and disrespect for Californians.
  • A 2024 law banning local voter ID, passed in response to Huntington Beach's policy, is described as a violation of the California Constitution.

  • Michael Gates plans a 'constitution-driven' Attorney General's office, focusing on enforcing existing state and federal laws, including Title IX.
  • He intends to issue opinions and legal arguments to uphold constitutional principles for local governance and election integrity.
  • The host introduces the 'Comeback California' theme, encouraging voter information and participation in elections.
  • Gates describes himself as 'stupidly optimistic,' maintaining hope and belief in not giving up on the state's future.

  • The host suggests issues like crime reduction, homelessness, and lower taxes are nonpartisan and resonate across the political spectrum, including with Democrats and Independents.
  • Michael Gates confirms his campaign will address these common-sense, nonpartisan issues.
  • He plans extensive outreach, including speaking to communities in Spanish, to connect directly with the Latino community and return control from Sacramento.
