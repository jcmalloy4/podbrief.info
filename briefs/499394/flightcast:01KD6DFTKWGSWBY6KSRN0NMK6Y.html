The Diary Of A CEO with Steven Bartlett

Episode

Key Takeaways

  • Reading offers profound wisdom, providing access to diverse historical experiences and life lessons.
  • Stoic principles advocate focusing energy on controllable factors to achieve practical wisdom and peace.
  • Engaging in daily physical challenges and embracing struggle builds resilience and a strong mind.
  • Meaning in life is derived from contributing to the common good and making a positive impact.
  • Confronting mortality can combat procrastination and foster a deeper appreciation for one's time.
  • Stoicism teaches understanding and processing emotions, rather than suppressing them, to prevent regret.
  • Accepting circumstances as they are, rather than wishing for change, is crucial for inner peace.

Deep Dive

  • The guest highlights that reading provides profound wisdom, likening it to living multiple lives and accessing countless individuals' experiences throughout history.
  • A core Stoic principle advises focusing energy solely on what is within one's control, citing examples like traffic and interpersonal conflicts.
  • Inefficient energy expenditure on uncontrollable factors is emphasized as a common pitfall in navigating daily life.

  • The guest underscores the importance of engaging in a physically challenging activity daily, from intense workouts to less demanding tasks, to push personal limits.
  • This practice, likened to ancient Greek and Roman training in gymnasia, cultivates a strong mind and body, contrasting with modern sedentary lifestyles.
  • The discussion links this to an innate human need for struggle, suggesting humans are designed to 'struggle forward,' inheriting a legacy of overcoming adversity.

  • The host contemplates Marcus Aurelius's wisdom on the finite nature of life, emphasizing the importance of playing one's role well.
  • The practice of 'memento mori,' remembering mortality, is presented as a tool to combat procrastination and gain perspective on life's fragility.
  • Increased lifespans and modern comforts have led to a greater detachment from mortality compared to ancient times, with ignoring it potentially leading to wasted lives.

  • The guest clarifies that Stoicism is not about suppressing emotions, but rather understanding and processing them without being enslaved by their influence.
  • Their personal practice involves identifying an emotion's cause and bodily sensations, then questioning the inclination to act upon it.
  • Acting out of frustration, such as losing one's temper, is noted to consistently lead to regret, underscoring the challenge of Stoic emotional control.

  • Frustration is often attributed to high expectations and the desire for things to conform to a specific ideal, leading to tension.
  • Drawing on Epictetus, the speaker introduces finding peace by accepting circumstances as they are, illustrating with an analogy to weather expectations.
  • Marquette basketball coach Shaka Smart's 'I'm a dress for the weather guy' response is cited as a practical example of Stoic acceptance.
