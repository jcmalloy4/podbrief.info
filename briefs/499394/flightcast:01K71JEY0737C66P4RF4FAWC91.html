The Diary Of A CEO with Steven Bartlett

Episode

Key Takeaways

  • Working-class roots and maternal encouragement were crucial for the guest's early resilience in Doncaster.
  • Fame with One Direction brought isolation and guilt, prompting a redefinition of personal and career success.
  • The profound, successive losses of his mother and sister deeply shaped his life and sense of purpose.
  • He navigated the band's end and solo challenges by prioritizing family and embracing authenticity in his music.
  • Fatherhood and a new relationship influenced his shift towards a more positive and fulfilling musical direction.

Deep Dive

  • Growing up in Doncaster, the guest lived in a crowded three-bedroom house with seven siblings, embracing an older brother role.
  • His mother, a primary caregiver for his first 4-5 years, instilled emotional expression and encouraged his three X Factor auditions.
  • After two rejections, his determination intensified, leading to success at 18 with his mother's balanced support.

  • Initially reluctant to join a boy band, the guest helped One Direction become 'cooler,' breaking pre-order records with their first single.
  • Joining at 18 led to a significant loss of personal independence, ending what he considered the best year of his life.
  • Despite band success, he felt isolation and 'working-class guilt' over earnings, preferring music focus over celebrity status.
  • A merchandise deal payday at 19 highlighted financial disparity, causing guilt when shared with friends.

  • Zayn Malik's 2015 departure to pursue a normal life angered the guest, who desired loyalty and prior discussion.
  • He learned of Zayn's unexpected exit after sharing a joint the previous night with no distress signals.
  • The end of One Direction felt devastating to the host, likened to grieving a friend and band member.
  • A somber band meeting decided the hiatus; the host sought clarity on its duration, which was not provided.

  • At 24, the guest realized his career trajectory was likely downward, a challenging perspective after reaching a pinnacle.
  • He achieved a number one record with his latest album, 'Faith in the Future,' a feat he never anticipated solo.
  • Performing a One Direction song to 1,200 people in a 5,000-capacity room contrasted sharply with Wembley Stadium.
  • He is redefining success internally, moving from numbers-based metrics to fulfillment, finding satisfaction with his latest record.

  • A year after One Direction ended, his mother passed away at 42 from leukemia, diagnosed during a wedding party.
  • Three days after her death, he performed on X Factor as she encouraged, a challenging 3.5-minute experience.
  • Years later, his younger sister Felicity tragically died at 18, profoundly impacting the family and his protector role.
  • He felt immense guilt and powerlessness, fearing he failed to protect his sisters, a role reinforced by his mother's dying wish.

  • The guest is reluctant to discuss past trauma publicly, fearing it will define him and be unfair to his family.
  • He felt pressured by a journalist on BBC Breakfast News to discuss sensitive topics despite prior agreements.
  • This perceived lack of empathy from media makes him guarded about discussing personal matters like grief.

  • The host heard about Liam Payne's passing from Niall Horan while in Los Angeles, shocking the guest, whom Liam saw as his best friend.
  • Liam was viewed as the most experienced and reliable in One Direction, often misunderstood, pure, and humble despite public perceptions.
  • The guest believes Liam's struggles were deeper than perceived, exacerbated by public development post-One Direction.
  • He felt inadequate in helping Liam, acknowledging Liam's likely surprise at the positive perception others held of him.

  • Becoming a father at 24 to his son Freddy, now 33, has positively impacted his ego and reinforced values of kindness.
  • He navigates explaining fame to Freddy, sometimes refusing photos in public to protect their limited time together.
  • His new music, influenced by a new relationship and personal fulfillment, aims for a positive, 'feel-good' experience.
  • He redefines success internally, focusing on fulfillment over competitive chart positions in the music industry.
