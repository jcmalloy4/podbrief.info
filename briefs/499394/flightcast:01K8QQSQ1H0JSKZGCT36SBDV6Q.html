The Diary Of A CEO with Steven Bartlett

Episode

Key Takeaways

  • The guest expressed deep disappointment post-election loss, comparing it to grief, and voiced strong concerns for the country's future.
  • A lifelong commitment to justice, freedom, and protecting people motivated the guest's career in law and public service.
  • The guest navigated imposter syndrome and external biases, advocating for self-belief against others' limiting perceptions.
  • Her 2019 presidential campaign ended due to funding issues; she later became Vice President despite past internal staff friction.
  • Political strategies increasingly exploit emotional and fear-based messaging, often overshadowing fact-based discourse.
  • Regrets surfaced over a missed Joe Rogan appearance, highlighting the strategic importance of diverse media engagement for broader reach.
  • The guest proposes a revised Democratic strategy emphasizing family policies and lowering the voting age to 16 for societal benefit.

Deep Dive

  • The guest grew up in an environment emphasizing justice, freedom, and equality, learning that everyone has a duty to participate.
  • Her career in law was driven by a desire to protect people and ensure dignity, influenced by being the eldest child and observing her mother's treatment, not by monetary gain.
  • Her husband noted her life's purpose was helping others on their first date.

  • The guest experienced imposter syndrome upon becoming District Attorney, a position unexpectedly won after starting at 6% in polls.
  • She views this not as inadequacy but as healthy humility recognizing the role's gravity and power vested by the people.
  • She advises mentees not to internalize others' limitations based on appearance, gender, or race, recalling being mistaken for an assistant.

  • The guest dropped out of her 2019 presidential campaign primarily due to running out of money, though she gained many lessons.
  • She later received a FaceTime call from Joe Biden during COVID-19, inviting her to be his running mate.
  • The vetting process for Vice President is extensive, taking weeks or months, followed by a decision based on chemistry for a strong partnership.

  • The guest experienced frustration and pain over a lack of promotion for her accomplishments, suggesting political figures deliberately dimmed her achievements.
  • President Biden's staff reportedly contributed to negative narratives about her, with the inner circle encouraging a 'zero-sum game mentality'.
  • She felt a constant need to prove loyalty to Biden, having run against him previously.

  • The guest observed Donald Trump's ability to capitalize on perceived weaknesses in opponents' articulation, noting his uncharacteristic moderation during one debate.
  • She recalled being informed just before a debate about a rumor spread by Trump's circle alleging Haitian immigrants ate pets.
  • This tactic of saying outrageous things is described as misdirection to distract from issues like plans for working Americans.

  • The guest discussed political strategies that prioritize emotional and fear-based appeals over factual arguments, citing Donald Trump's use of labels like 'Sleepy Joe'.
  • Neuroscience suggests the amygdala responds more to memorable, fear-based messaging than rational arguments, as seen with vaccine-autism claims.
  • The discussion addresses how the Democratic Party can counter such 'gaslighting' and disinformation by understanding differing information sets among voters.

  • The guest expresses a desire for both political parties to engage with opposing viewpoints, particularly in the upcoming 2028 election cycle.
  • She reflects on a past opportunity she regrets not pursuing, suggesting it could have led to a different outcome, and highlights the importance of transparency in modern leadership.
  • The guest expresses uncertainty about running for president again in 2028, despite polls showing her leading the Democratic field, acknowledging the personal toll.

  • The guest attributes current political outcomes to Democratic Party strategic failures and a loss in the 'game of chess' against conservative movements.
  • She criticizes the weaponization of government institutions and the capitulation of major industries impacting essential services like educational programs for special needs children.
  • A revised strategy is proposed, prioritizing family policies like the child tax credit and affordable childcare over infrastructure and semiconductor bills for immediate economic relief.

  • The guest's communication style has become bolder and more unfiltered during her book tour, contrasting with the pretense often found in other media.
  • She states her book aims to demystify the US presidential election process, making it accessible to a wide audience and highlighting individual power.
  • Despite dropping out of her past campaign due to insufficient time, she does not regret running and notes it inspired many individuals to pursue their own political aspirations or educational paths.
