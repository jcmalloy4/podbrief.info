The Diary Of A CEO with Steven Bartlett

Episode

Key Takeaways

  • Perseverance and commitment are essential for long-term success, as demonstrated by the guest's 13-year journey.
  • Acknowledging ignorance and asking questions is crucial for continuous learning and seizing opportunities.
  • An intense work ethic and focus on a single path can overcome financial struggles and doubt.
  • Leveraging one's brand and likeness is key to building diverse business ventures and strategic ownership.
  • Effective communication, setting boundaries, and prioritizing personal well-being are vital for managing relationships.

Deep Dive

  • The guest grew up in North Philadelphia, marked by his brother's involvement with drugs and his father's frequent jail time.
  • His strict mother, Nancy Hart, prioritized his success, granting him a set timeframe to succeed in comedy.
  • His primary motivation is ideation, deriving energy from bringing thoughts into reality.
  • He contrasts his driven adult self with an unmotivated youth who avoided school opportunities.

  • The guest's career began working at a sneaker store, leading to management and corporate roles.
  • Despite lacking formal education, he found success in sales, which eventually led to the suggestion of pursuing stand-up comedy.
  • His first amateur comedy night sparked an immediate love for performing and the positive energy of making people laugh.
  • This experience expanded into a vision of global impact through shared moments and laughter.

  • Between ages 18-22, the guest performed 25-28 comedy sets weekly, earning $400-500, often commuting from Philadelphia to New York.
  • After a $250,000 holding deal with ABC fell through, he moved to Los Angeles and began creating his own opportunities.
  • He successfully transitioned from selling out comedy clubs to theaters, then to films like 'Think Like a Man' (grossing over $90M) and 'Ride Along' ($140M).
  • Following film success, he established a production company and a venture capital firm, focusing on creating his own ventures.

  • In his early career around age 18, the guest faced a period of struggle with limited resources and widespread doubt.
  • He persevered due to a strong belief in his chosen path and passion for comedy.
  • He moved to Los Angeles to immerse himself in the industry, taking acting classes and performing stand-up.
  • Witnessing Cat Williams' impactful set at the BET Comedy Awards ignited a desire for a similar moment of audience connection and validation.

  • The guest likens his extensive 13-year training and work ethic to a professional athlete's consistent practice for high-stakes moments.
  • He introduces the concept of 'T-shaped people' possessing broad knowledge and deep expertise in one area, using his marketing skill as leverage.
  • This specialized skill allowed him to enter diverse fields, including media, stocks, and investment opportunities.
  • He emphasizes the importance of dedicating focus to a single endeavor until completion before pivoting to new ventures.

  • The guest emphasizes the importance of acknowledging ignorance and asking questions, particularly in complex areas like the stock market, investing, and brand partnerships.
  • He states people are often insecure about admitting what they don't know, but abundant online resources and experts are available.
  • He recounts discovering 'unknown unknowns' about how the wealthy operate in financial markets, stemming from a normal background.
  • This realization led to understanding how major investors collaborate to leverage collective resources for new opportunities.

  • The guest highlights the rapid pace of business success, citing investments in companies like Function Health (valued at $2.5 billion) and 11 Labs (valued at $3 billion).
  • He stresses navigating relationships, distinguishing between 'wrong' and 'right' people, and detaching emotions from business decisions for company success.
  • Strategic decisions often involve acquiring valuable assets and bringing in new, skilled personnel to break through 'ceilings.'
  • He believes strongly in talent, rewarding high performers, and the importance of delegating and allowing capable individuals to execute effectively.

  • The guest advises removing emotions from business decisions and conducting thorough review of all agreements to avoid future exploitation.
  • He notes young talent in industries like music are vulnerable to 'sharks' and predatory business practices.
  • However, many artists are now achieving greater success by establishing independent labels and taking control of their careers.
  • This trend involves mirroring successful models and creating one's own studios, labels, and product lines rather than being 'work for hire.'

  • The guest's show 'Acting My Age' explores the challenges of growing up and embracing adulthood.
  • The discussion highlights a 'masculinity crisis,' noting statistics like men having fewer friends, higher suicide rates, and a college degree gap.
  • He offers advice to young men on being a 'good man,' emphasizing the evolving definition of masculinity and the importance of personal change.
  • His parenting philosophy involves open communication with his children about his struggles while shielding them from unnecessary burdens, aiming to provide leadership, responsibility, and accountability.

  • The guest emphasizes straightforward communication in relationships, asserting that personal boundaries are acceptable.
  • He notes a societal trend towards conflict over differing viewpoints and the difficulty for men to express struggles due to fear of judgment.
  • He manages his workload by consciously limiting phone usage and calls, recognizing the unhealthy nature of constant work.
  • As he has gotten older, he has realized the need for silence and quiet reflection, such as driving alone without music, to maintain well-being.
