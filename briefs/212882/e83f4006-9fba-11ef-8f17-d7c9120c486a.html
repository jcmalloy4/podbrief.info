BÂAN HITS THEATERS | Trash Taste #275

Key Takeaways

  • Joey premiered his anime short film, Adios of Lee, at Grand Cinema Sunshine in Ikebukuro.
  • The premiere sparked discussions on creative anxieties, audience expectations, and constructive feedback.
  • The film explores universal themes of home and belonging, resonating with local and foreign audiences.
  • Connor directed the English dub of the short film, contributing to a vulnerable and intimate character portrayal.
  • A deep dive into the video game Soma highlighted its existential horror and profound impact on players.
  • Hosts recounted a challenging Mount Fuji climb, detailing physical demands, preparation needs, and practicalities.
  • The discussion extended to extreme sports, comparing the risks and motivations behind activities like Everest climbing and wingsuit flying.
  • The summit of Mount Fuji is revealed to be privately owned, raising questions about public access to national landmarks.
  • The importance of supportive friendships within the creative and personal lives of the hosts was emphasized.

Deep Dive

  • Joey's anime premiered at Grand Cinema Sunshine in Ikebukuro, a venue heavily decorated for "Demon Slayer," which occupied 80% of its showings.
  • Concessions caused anxiety for Joey's team, with Joey waiting 35 minutes for popcorn and the screening starting 10 minutes late.
  • Securing the venue required two months advance booking due to high demand, with one cinema reportedly dedicating 8 out of 10 rooms to "Demon Slayer."

  • The creator expressed anxiety about providing critical feedback to composer Kevin, fearing their own input might detract from the music's impact.
  • Hosts debated constructive feedback, emphasizing openness to others' ideas to avoid "mid" content, citing Stanley Kubrick as an example.
  • The discussion touched on the proverb "you catch more flies with honey than with vinegar," suggesting a softer approach to criticism.

  • Connor directed the English dub of the short film, leading to an intimate understanding of its characters and themes.
  • The characters Daiichi and Rin were identified as representing the creator and Sydney, respectively, exploring universal feelings of longing when leaving home.
  • Daiichi's communication style and journey were inspired by the creator's father's move from Thailand to England.

  • Translating anime presents challenges in adapting cultural nuances and idiomatic expressions to sound natural in English.
  • The team chose to treat English and Japanese character versions as separate entities, prioritizing authentic performances over strict faithfulness.
  • Connor's direction and Alex's performance exemplify this approach, allowing for natural interpretations for English-speaking audiences.

  • A director-added action scene depicting Rin's parents' death significantly increased audience retention by re-engaging viewers when attention dipped.
  • The scene's placement built lore, character, and world-building, with music by Kevin and extended shots enhancing visual storytelling.
  • Post-premiere Q&A featured relevant, insightful questions, signaling to the host the project's potential success and deep audience engagement.

  • The creator expressed satisfaction if viewers engaged with the film's message and scenes, noting an unexpected craving for curry after viewing became a significant discussion point.
  • While happy with the final product, the creator wished for more time to develop character storylines, such as Daichi and Rin's, and explore the film's world further.
  • There is a desire to continue the 'Barn' IP through various avenues, including manga or other media, to ensure the project's longevity.

  • "Soma" was highly praised by Garnt and Joey, with Joey calling it his favorite existential horror media, beginning with a car crash and experimental treatment.
  • The game is lauded as a masterpiece for its ability to subvert player expectations and prompt deep reflection on humanity and moral choices.
  • Despite its approximately nine-hour playtime, "Soma" offers suffocating tension without relying on jump scares, leveraging the video game medium for narrative impact.

  • Climbing Mount Fuji was described as a struggle, often likened to 'type two fun' where enjoyment is retrospective, with the group opting for a same-day ascent and descent.
  • Practicalities included reliance on bus transportation with limited hours, the need for pre-arranged taxis, and a 4:40 a.m. unexpected start for a 5 a.m. departure time.
  • The ascent via the Fujinomio route, the steepest and fastest, took 4.5 hours instead of the scheduled 5, and required carrying out all trash.

  • The latter half of the Mount Fuji climb was brutal, involving "double steps" on stairs and causing extreme foot fatigue, leading to significant leg soreness for days after the descent.
  • The summit view was described as "fine," with most people climbing for the sunset; an additional 20-minute hike to a higher point was skipped due to exhaustion.
  • The summit of Mount Fuji is privately owned, having been granted to a Shinto shrine in 1602, with toilet usage costing 300 yen.

  • Extreme mountain climbing was contrasted with cycling, with one speaker preferring cycling for its "better vibe" and lower danger.
  • Mount Everest has seen over 340 deaths, representing a 1% fatality rate, comparable to activities like wingsuit flying.
  • The necessity of supplemental oxygen at high altitudes on Everest was likened to "walking to space," leading to disbelief among participants.
