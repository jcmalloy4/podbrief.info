Lex Fridman Podcast

#478 – Scott Horton: The Case Against War and the Military Industrial Complex

Key Takeaways

  • U.S. military interventions post-Cold War resulted in significant human and financial costs, failing stated objectives.
  • The military-industrial complex and neoconservative ideologies consistently drive U.S. foreign policy toward conflict.
  • U.S. actions, including supporting certain groups and breaking diplomatic promises, have inadvertently empowered adversaries like Iran and radical groups.
  • The Israel-Palestine conflict is deeply rooted in historical displacement and strategic actions, with U.S. policy often aligning with Israeli interests.
  • Promises of NATO expansion to Russia were knowingly broken, contributing significantly to current geopolitical tensions and the Ukraine conflict.
  • Wars are often fueled by inflationary money, representing a net loss of societal wealth rather than economic benefit.
  • The U.S. government consistently manipulates public opinion through propaganda, leveraging fear and misinformation to justify interventions.
  • Diplomacy, particularly direct engagement with adversaries, is presented as the most viable path to de-escalation and preventing nuclear proliferation.

Deep Dive

  • The U.S. allowed Ayatollah Khomeini to return to Iran in 1979, then warned the new regime about Saddam Hussein's recent rise.
  • Saddam Hussein, reportedly groomed by the CIA since the 1950s, shifted alliances towards the Soviet Union in the 1970s.
  • Despite prior warnings to Iran, the U.S. later supported Saddam Hussein during the brutal 9-year Iran-Iraq War.

  • Guest Scott Horton recounted a debate where his denials of antisemitism were removed, while accusations remained, regarding criticisms of neoconservatives.
  • Horton argued that U.S. foreign policy and support for Israel are against American interests, citing the 1996 Qana incident.
  • The Qana Massacre, where Israeli artillery killed 106 people in a UN shelter, is linked to Osama bin Laden's first declaration of war against the U.S.

  • Neoconservative figures like Richard Perle and Douglas Feith orchestrated the Iraq War through manipulating intelligence to link Saddam Hussein to Al-Qaeda.
  • Zionism was a core motivation for war, with Saddam's payments to Palestinian families identified by Philip Zelikow as a key trigger.
  • The guest describes neoconservatism as a blend of the Israel lobby and the military-industrial complex, providing justification for arms purchases.

  • The guest described early exposure to AM radio as a vital "town square" for deep discussions on topics like the Texas Homesteading Act.
  • He recounted discovering KLBJ AM at age 16, hearing a Branch Davidian survivor, and being impressed by the interactive format and callers' detailed knowledge.
  • The guest views talk radio as essential for popular government, enabling broad public conversation over fragmented debates.

  • Barack Obama's support for Al-Qaeda in Syria stemmed from U.S. foreign policy establishment goals and the Israel lobby, not personal sympathies.
  • Neoconservatives and Zionists, including Jamie Rubin, advocated for U.S. intervention in Syria, with Rubin's memo to Hillary Clinton citing Israeli interests as the primary reason.
  • This strategy involved deploying Libyan jihadists, including those involved in the Benghazi attack, to Syria to destabilize Assad and cut Iran's link to the Mediterranean.

  • The guest delineates multiple Iraq Wars: I (1991 Desert Storm), I.5 (Clinton's bombings), II (W. Bush's 2003 invasion), and III (Obama's 2014 war against ISIS).
  • Current U.S. involvement with Shiite Popular Mobilization Units against Sunni insurgency is labeled "Iraq War III and a half."
  • The ongoing conflict with Iranian-linked militias risks a potential "Iraq War IV" if the U.S. aligns with Israel against these groups.

  • Many religious and reformed Jews initially opposed Zionism, fearing it would divide loyalties and cause Middle East disruptions.
  • The guest criticized the term "Israeli diaspora" for American Jews, noting Israel was created in 1948, while American Jews arrived in the 18th century.
  • The 1967 War saw Israel take control of the West Bank and Gaza Strip, displacing an additional 250,000 Palestinians and annexing East Jerusalem.

  • A British doctor reported Palestinian children exhibiting specific, repeated gunshot wounds to the same body parts, suggesting deliberate targeting for "fun" or "target practice."
  • The guest alleged further atrocities including burying, starving, and machine-gunning Palestinians, claiming Israelis deny their humanity.
  • Israel's prime minister's speech invoked the ancient biblical story of Amalek, which commands the total annihilation of a tribe, interpreted as orders to kill all Palestinians, including children.

  • The guest suggested non-military actions Putin could have taken, such as demanding UN peacekeepers for the Donbass ceasefire line or cutting off all gas supplies to Western Europe during winter 2021-2022.
  • President Biden's administration reportedly did not negotiate in good faith to prevent the war, despite earlier warnings about Russian intentions.
  • The U.S. refused to negotiate NATO expansion, potential missile deployments in Ukraine, or the implementation of the 2015 Minsk II deal, effectively provoking Russia.
