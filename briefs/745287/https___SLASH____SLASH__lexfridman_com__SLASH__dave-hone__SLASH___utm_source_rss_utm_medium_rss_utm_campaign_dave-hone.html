#480 – Dave Hone: T-Rex, Dinosaurs, Extinction, Evolution, and Jurassic Park

Key Takeaways

  • Tyrannosaurus Rex was a colossal predator, 12 meters long and 7 metric tons.
  • T-Rex hunted juvenile prey and scavenged, employing an endurance-based strategy.
  • Fossil discovery requires quarrying, active searching, and delicate preservation techniques.
  • High-value dinosaur fossils like "Stan" sell for millions, comparable to art.
  • T-Rex skull featured immense bone density for a powerful, bone-crushing bite.
  • Jurassic Park inaccurately depicted Velociraptors and other dinosaurs.
  • Spinosaurus was a distinct aquatic-associated dinosaur with unique anatomy.
  • Dinosaur extinction 66 million years ago was caused by an asteroid impact.
  • Modern birds are direct descendants of dinosaurs, with feathers evolving 165 million years ago.

Deep Dive

  • The T-Rex was a colossal bipedal carnivore, estimated at 12 meters long and weighing 7 metric tons, comparable to an orca on land.
  • Its robust build, including a short, bull-like neck and barrel-shaped chest, resulted in a mass greater than Giganotosaurus or Mapusaurus.
  • T-Rex moved like a power walker at an estimated top speed of 25 miles per hour (40 kph), with one foot always on the ground.
  • Its feet had a bird-like structure with three toes and vertically aligned metatarsals, an adaptation for faster walking and energy efficiency.

  • T-Rex primarily targeted younger, smaller herbivores like juvenile Triceratops and Edmontosaurus, to minimize injury risk.
  • Evidence from a hadrosaur skeleton from Mongolia suggests T-Rex also scavenged, indicated by erosion preceding bite marks on a humerus.
  • Its hunting strategy was likely endurance-based, similar to hyenas or wolves, supported by good senses of smell and potential nocturnal activity.
  • T-Rex possessed excellent vision, with eyes comparable in size to a baseball, contradicting the movie trope of it being blind to immobile objects.

  • Fossils are primarily discovered through quarrying sites with fine sedimentation, yielding incidental finds, and by active searching in geologically suitable areas.
  • A nearly complete Linheraptor skeleton in northern China was discovered after a small claw fragment was spotted, leading to extensive excavation.
  • Excavation involves mapping the skeleton's location, carefully chiseling rock, and strengthening fragile bone with consolidants like paraloid.
  • New technologies such as drones and photogrammetry are used for site mapping, but direct detection of bones via advanced scanning is limited.

  • The 'Stan' T-Rex fossil, discovered in 1987 in South Dakota, required several years and a team of six for its excavation and preparation.
  • 'Stan' sold for $31.8 million, a price comparable to the art market, with Abu Dhabi's Department of Culture and Tourism as the buyer.
  • The Boreal Pelta, an armored dinosaur from Alberta, Canada, is notable for its exceptional preservation, including skin and color patterns, enhancing marketability.
  • A proposed $10 billion paleontology plan suggests acquiring fossil-rich land for museums and incentivizing landowners to donate finds, preventing private sales.

  • The T-Rex skull is exceptionally robust, featuring significant bone density and fused nasal bones for rigidity, unlike the more open Giganotosaurus skull.
  • This powerful cranial structure evolved from smaller, feathered tyrannosaurs in the middle Jurassic, which already exhibited these traits.
  • Its immense bite force, allowing it to "chomp through almost anything," was generated by powerful muscles and specialized teeth, thicker than those of Carcarodontosaurus.
  • The T-Rex's slow, powerful bite contrasts with the faster, less forceful tearing methods of narrow-snouted dinosaurs like Carcharodontosaurs.

  • Spinosaurus featured a crocodile-like head with conical teeth, set-back nostrils, cranial crests, a long neck, a prominent sail, and a paddle-like tail, suggesting aquatic associations.
  • Its isotopic signatures indicate a diet primarily of fish, distinguishing it from other carnivorous dinosaurs.
  • Despite its aquatic features, the guest challenges the notion of Spinosaurus as a strong swimmer, citing weak evidence and new published research.
  • In a hypothetical confrontation, a T-Rex would likely win against a Spinosaurus due to the T-Rex's stronger neck and bite, despite the Spinosaurus's impressive length exceeding 15 meters.

  • Jurassic Park inaccurately portrayed Velociraptors as human-sized pack hunters; they were actually thigh-high, and scientific evidence for pack hunting is limited.
  • The T-Rex in the films is generally depicted with correct size, shape, and power-walking locomotion, a point of accuracy.
  • Jurassic World incorrectly depicted the Gallimimus with teeth, despite scientific consensus and supplementary materials showing it had a beak.
  • The guest suggests that greater scientific accuracy, even in fictional works, could enhance the narrative, citing the show "Chernobyl" as an example.

  • Dinosaurs became extinct approximately 66 million years ago due to an asteroid impact in the Yucatan Peninsula.
  • The impact triggered a near-instantaneous nuclear winter with massive ash clouds and widespread fires, leading to a global ecosystem collapse.
  • Large, terrestrial animals like most dinosaurs were poorly equipped to survive the extreme climate stress, suffering disproportionately due to higher resource needs and slower reproduction rates.
  • Without the dinosaurs' extinction, humans likely would not have evolved, as mammals, including primate ancestors, only gained dominance after the removal of major dinosaur predators.

  • The guest confirms that modern birds, estimated at 10,500 to 11,000 species, are direct evolutionary descendants of dinosaurs, similar to how humans are apes.
  • Feathers evolved at least 165 million years ago, predating bird origins, serving dual purposes for insulation in warm-blooded dinosaurs and for sexual selection and communication.
  • This direct lineage implies that for approximately 120-130 million years, any large animal on Earth was likely a dinosaur.
  • Paleontology reconstructs past behaviors through cumulative data and independent evidence like stomach contents, challenging and refining past interpretations.
