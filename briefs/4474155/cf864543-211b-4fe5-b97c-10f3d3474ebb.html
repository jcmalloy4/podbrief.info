Redistricting Showdown: Should California Hit Pause If Texas Is Blocked?

Key Takeaways

  • State redistricting efforts by Texas and California face legal and political scrutiny, with implications for electoral maps.
  • The podcast engages listeners through daily polls on current political and technological topics, such as smartphone voting.
  • Discussions cover various political strategies, including party platforms and international peace proposals.
  • The Smerconish.com newsletter provides a curated summary of diverse news headlines and in-depth discussions.

Deep Dive

  • The host questioned why a video was created that could instill doubt in military personnel following orders.
  • He suggested the situation surrounding military orders and potential disobedience is more complex than widely perceived.

  • New York City mayor-elect Eric Adams is scheduled for an upcoming White House meeting.
  • The host plans to cover this event and anticipates discussions around the 'Democratic Socialism' narrative.

  • Reporting on President Trump's 28-point plan to end the Russia-Ukraine war indicates a potential deal.
  • The plan suggests Putin could retain some territory in exchange for security guarantees for Ukraine.
  • These security guarantees would be similar to those provided by NATO.

  • The previous podcast poll indicated 53.48% of 34,185 voters supported using smartphones for municipal elections.
  • Anchorage, Alaska, was cited as an example, having conducted a pilot program for smartphone voting.

  • Texas's mid-decade reapportionment aims to gain Republican seats, drawing disapproval from the host.
  • A federal court has challenged Texas's reapportionment, citing racial gerrymandering, with the decision currently under appeal.
  • California is considering pausing its redistricting efforts if Texas's actions are halted, but the host expresses doubt about this reciprocity, despite Governor Newsom's prior letter to President Trump.
  • The current poll question asks if California should pause its redistricting if Texas's efforts are halted, with the host voting 'yes' and viewing California as the 'gold standard'.
