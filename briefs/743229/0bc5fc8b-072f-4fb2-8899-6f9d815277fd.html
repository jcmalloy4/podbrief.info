Mob Ties and Rigged Bets: Inside the N.B.A.’s Gambling Scandal

Key Takeaways

  • A federal investigation has indicted over 30 individuals, including NBA players and coaches, in illegal gambling schemes.
  • The schemes involved the Mafia rigging poker games and NBA insiders manipulating prop bets using confidential information.
  • The proliferation of online sports betting, particularly prop bets, has amplified opportunities for game manipulation.
  • Sports leagues benefit financially from betting partnerships but face growing concerns over compromised game integrity.
  • The scandal raises questions about the future integrity of professional sports and has prompted congressional scrutiny.

Deep Dive

  • A federal investigation has implicated over 30 individuals, including NBA players and coaches, in illegal gambling.
  • The FBI Director announced two criminal schemes: rigged illegal poker games involving the Mafia and NBA figures, and an NBA insider trading scheme related to betting.
  • Rigged poker games involved the Mafia, four New York families, and technology like shuffling machines to defraud victims.
  • One victim, John Doe number one, lost $1.8 million in a single rigged poker game.

  • Indictments detail illegal gambling operations across the U.S. involving millions of dollars, with NBA players allegedly used as draws.
  • NBA champion and coach Chauncey Phillips is named as a participant in these illegal games.
  • A second scheme involved an NBA player tipping co-conspirators about an early game exit due to injury.
  • This allowed associates to bet on his reduced performance, winning hundreds of thousands of dollars.

  • The integrity of NBA games is questioned due to schemes manipulating 'prop bets' on individual player performances.
  • 'Prop bets' allow gambling on specific player statistics or events within a game, not just final outcomes.
  • Damon Jones, an unofficial Lakers assistant coach, was implicated for sharing information about 'Player 3' (matching LeBron James) not playing.
  • This information allowed co-conspirators to bet on the Lakers losing.

  • The widespread normalization and accessibility of online sports gambling create conditions for criminal activity.
  • Following the 2018 Supreme Court decision, sports leagues capitalized on business opportunities, forming partnerships with sports books.
  • Leagues struck licensing deals and increased in-game betting promotions, rapidly embedding betting into sports consumption.
  • This rapid expansion has raised concerns among sports leagues and fans.

  • Increased fan engagement driven by betting boosts media rights value, a primary profit source for leagues.
  • Sports betting provides significant financial benefits to leagues, including hundreds of millions annually from betting operator partnerships.
  • The explosion of prop bets increases the potential for manipulating individual game occurrences, which are easier to fix than entire games.
  • While integrity monitors exist, the ease and volume of online betting create more opportunities for manipulation.

  • A broader concern suggests the NBA scandal may indicate a trend affecting multiple professional sports.
  • This raises questions about the integrity of games for fans.
  • Players face increased risk of suspicion and harassment from bettors, along with pressure from entourages for inside information.
  • A House Committee has requested a briefing from NBA Commissioner Adam Silver by October 31st regarding the gambling scandal.
