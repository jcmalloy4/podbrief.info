Trump Rants: ‘Let Them Go Back to Where They Came From’

Key Takeaways

  • President Trump delivered xenophobic remarks targeting Somali immigrants in Minnesota, labeling them "garbage."
  • A widespread fraud scheme in Minnesota, estimated at up to $1 billion, exploited state feeding programs since 2020.
  • Political sensitivities and accusations of racism hindered early intervention in the Minnesota fraud investigations.
  • The Trump administration's rhetoric and actions are openly questioning the legitimacy and belonging of immigrants in the U.S.

Deep Dive

  • President Trump, during a cabinet meeting, referred to Somali immigrants as "garbage" and stated they should "go back where they came from."
  • The estimated 80,000 Somali immigrants in Minnesota reacted with shock and fear to the President's comments.
  • Anxiety within the community was heightened by the deployment of 100 immigration agents to Minnesota, reportedly seeking Somalis for deportation.

  • A widespread fraud scheme, originating in 2020, exploited a state program designed to feed vulnerable children during the COVID-19 pandemic.
  • Up to $1 billion was stolen from various Minnesota safety net programs by networks of individuals.
  • State officials struggled to explain how such large-scale fraud went unnoticed, despite red flags emerging in 2020.
  • A defense lawyer noted that individuals involved perceived state officials as either tolerating or authorizing their actions due to ease of accessing funds.

  • The nonprofit Feeding Our Future, which organized meal providers, accused state officials of racism, creating a paralyzing effect that prevented timely intervention.
  • Democratic lawmakers were hesitant to intervene in fraud schemes due to concerns about alienating Somali American voters and donors, who are a key constituency.
  • A U.S. Attorney's office prosecutor argued that without stronger safeguards, Minnesota risked losing its ability to provide essential services, citing racism allegations as inhibiting a forceful response.

  • Minnesota is undergoing a reckoning over its lax enforcement of regulations, which allowed policies to be exploited and could turn public opinion against them.
  • Lawmakers and the governor have begun implementing stronger safeguards to detect and prevent fraud in response.
  • The broader debate over immigration and xenophobic rhetoric from the president has overshadowed the frank conversation needed to address fraud within the Somali community in Minnesota, making pragmatic change difficult.

  • President Trump intensified his focus on Somali immigrants in Minnesota following a conservative activist's report linking fraud money to Al-Shabaab, despite no prosecutorial evidence.
  • A New York Times article detailing Minnesota's fraud coincided with a shooting by an Afghan immigrant, providing the administration political cover for immigration crackdowns.
  • The administration is targeting immigrants from countries with instability and conflict, signaling that their cultures are not welcome in America.

  • President Trump declared the U.S. at a "tipping point," expressing frustration with immigrants from impoverished, conflict-ridden countries whom he accused of exploiting the welfare system.
  • Stephen Miller's social media posts and Trump's subsequent public remarks indicate a shift towards more open and direct xenophobic rhetoric.
  • Current actions and rhetoric go beyond immigration enforcement, aiming to delegitimize immigrants and naturalized citizens.
  • This rhetoric forces Somali immigrants, including U.S. citizens and long-term residents, to question their belonging and American identity.
