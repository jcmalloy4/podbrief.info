Family Separation 2.0: An Update

Key Takeaways

  • The Trump administration shifted its deportation strategy to target long-term undocumented immigrants over those with criminal records.
  • Fabricio Gomez, a Brazilian construction business owner, was detained by ICE after 12 years of consistent check-ins.
  • His deportation to Brazil separated his family and profoundly impacted his daughter, Isla, who now advocates against family separation.
  • Isla recounts the emotional toll of her father's detention and deportation, expressing determination to honor his legacy.

Deep Dive

  • President Trump's administration shifted its focus from deporting migrants with criminal records or recent border crossers.
  • The White House increasingly sought to deport those who came to the U.S. decades ago and established lives, careers, and families.
  • Fabricio had consistently checked in with ICE for 12 years prior to his detention, which occurred during a routine yearly check-in.

  • Isla received a distressing call from her mother stating that her father, Fabricio Gomez, was detained by ICE.
  • Fabricio, a 47-year-old construction business owner, came to Massachusetts from Brazil in 2001 due to violence and lack of opportunity.
  • He had no criminal record at the time of his detention.

  • Fabricio was detained on February 26 and moved to a correctional facility.
  • After two months of phone calls, Isla learned in April that her father faced an order of removal.
  • He was transferred to a detention center in Louisiana, which Isla described as a harsh prison environment known for alleged abuses.
  • Isla noted her father's faltering bravery and her own escalating anxiety as a result of the transfer.

  • Isla argues that separating families solely based on immigration status is a moral issue, emphasizing her parents' hard work.
  • She expressed her refusal to consider moving to Brazil, committed to continuing her parents' success in the U.S.
  • Isla hoped her story would encourage empathy from listeners, picturing their own daughters in a similar predicament.
  • Her dream of a business partnership with her father shifted to a focus on persevering through their current challenges.

  • Isla described an emotional reunion with her father at a Brazilian airport after his deportation, calling the experience surreal.
  • The reunion marked the realization of their new reality, jarringly experienced in a foreign environment.
  • She observed her father reconnecting with his own family in Brazil.
  • Isla remains determined to honor her father's legacy and build places, despite changed plans and his absence.
