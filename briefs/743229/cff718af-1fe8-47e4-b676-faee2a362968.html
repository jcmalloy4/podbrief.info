An Interview With the Man Behind Trump’s Current Immigration Crackdown

Key Takeaways

  • Trump administration escalated immigration enforcement, appointing Border Patrol Chief Greg Bovino to increase deportations.
  • Bovino implemented aggressive, militaristic tactics in interior U.S. cities, prompting legal challenges.
  • Biden administration's border policies, including 'throughput and decompression,' reportedly demoralized Border Patrol agents.
  • Ongoing lawsuits and a Supreme Court review address allegations of racial profiling and excessive force by Border Patrol.

Deep Dive

  • The Trump administration expressed frustration over the slow pace of deportations, despite aggressive ICE enforcement tactics.
  • The administration's daily arrest goal of 3,000 migrants was not consistently met, leading to changes in ICE leadership.
  • ICE's methodical surveillance and arrest operations were criticized by the administration as too slow, despite prioritizing efficiency.

  • Greg Bovino was inspired to join the Border Patrol in 1996 by stories of agents working alone in vast, violent territories.
  • He began his career in the El Centro sector, encountering frequent vehicle crossings with undocumented individuals and narcotics.
  • Returning to El Centro as chief in 2020, Bovino observed significant transformation, including a 30-foot border wall and improved technology.
  • Bovino viewed the Trump administration's Title 42 policy as aligned with Border Patrol's mission, enabling immediate deportation without asylum access.

  • In late 2020, Bovino intensified enforcement efforts in his sector, citing frustration with Biden administration policies.
  • In January, Bovino led 'Operation Return to Sender' in California's Central Valley, deploying over 60 agents.
  • The operation arrested 78 individuals accused of immigration violations, primarily farm workers commuting to jobs.
  • Immigrant advocates reported allegations of racial profiling and detentions of U.S. citizens during the interior operation.

  • The ACLU filed a lawsuit against Bovino and his agents, alleging random stops and arrests without reasonable suspicion.
  • A federal judge ruled that Border Patrol and Bovino violated the law during the Bakersfield operation.
  • Bovino considered 'Return to Sender' a successful test case for interior immigration enforcement.
  • In summer, Bovino was appointed tactical commander to lead mass deportation efforts, aligning with Trump's campaign promises.

  • June immigration enforcement in Los Angeles, overseen by Bovino, included ICE and Border Patrol agents chasing individuals, leading to mass protests.
  • President Trump deployed the National Guard during this period of intense enforcement.
  • Bovino explained operations use intelligence, targeting 'bad people and bad things,' and that collateral arrests help meet the 3,000 daily arrest goal.

  • Bovino explained Border Patrol stops are based on 'reasonable suspicion,' a lower standard than probable cause, using 'articulable facts.'
  • He stated ethnicity can be a factor but is assessed case-by-case, denying blanket racial profiling; he cited arrests of Europeans as evidence.
  • Legal challenges alleged racial profiling, particularly at Home Depot locations where Latino individuals were frequently stopped.
  • The Supreme Court temporarily blocked a lower court order limiting enforcement, with Justice Kavanaugh suggesting ethnicity could be a factor in stops.

  • The Trump administration transferred Bovino to Chicago, where he led an operation involving a helicopter with snipers and hundreds of agents.
  • Agents rappelled down buildings to surround an apartment complex, removing residents, including children, in a dramatic display.
  • Bovino expressed pride, calling it a success against gang members, despite reports of some arrested individuals having no criminal background.
  • This approach aligned with the administration's 'zero tolerance' policy for illegal immigration.

  • Bovino claims inner-city residents, unlike politicians, support his operations, despite polling suggesting voters dislike aggressive tactics.
  • Without changes from courts or elections, Bovino's aggressive style and influence in immigration enforcement are expected to continue growing.
  • Potential leadership changes within ICE, possibly involving former Border Patrol officials, could shift enforcement towards a more confrontational approach.
  • Border Patrol operations, previously in Chicago, are reportedly expanding to Charlotte and New Orleans.
