Trump’s D.O.J. Went After the Fed. It Backfired.

Key Takeaways

  • The Trump administration launched a criminal investigation into Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell.
  • The probe focused on a $700 million cost overrun for a Federal Reserve building renovation.
  • Chairman Powell publicly asserted the Federal Reserve's independence against political pressure.
  • Republican lawmakers and financial figures widely criticized the investigation's implications.
  • Trump's actions inadvertently strengthened Powell's position and the Fed's institutional independence.

Deep Dive

  • President Trump initiated a criminal investigation into Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell.
  • The probe stemmed from a conflict over interest rates, with Trump pushing for lower rates due to economic pressures.
  • The investigation centered on a $2.5 billion Federal Reserve building renovation that ran approximately $700 million over budget.

  • Trump ally Bill Pulte pushed for Powell's investigation, leveraging the costly renovation to exert political pressure.
  • U.S. Attorney Janine Pirow for Washington D.C., a Trump ally, began a preliminary inquiry after reading press accounts.
  • The Federal Reserve received two polite letters but did not respond, leading to increased pressure.
  • A criminal subpoena was issued after President Trump reportedly criticized U.S. attorneys for not aggressively pursuing his agenda.

  • Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell released an extraordinary video statement on a Sunday night responding to the investigation.
  • Powell stated the threats were a pretext to pressure the Fed into lowering interest rates.
  • He asserted the central bank would maintain its independence from political intimidation, marking a direct response to presidential attacks.

  • Republican lawmakers, including Senator Tom Tillis, voiced unprecedented criticism of the Trump administration's investigation.
  • Tillis threatened to block confirmation of any Powell replacement if the investigation continued.
  • The backlash was attributed to approaching midterm elections and the crucial importance of Federal Reserve independence for financial markets, the dollar, and U.S. treasuries.
  • Skepticism also emerged from Fox News and Larry Kudlow, who doubted Powell had intentionally lied to Congress.

  • President Trump continued criticizing Powell's performance and competence, despite claims of ignorance regarding the investigation.
  • Treasury Secretary Steven Mnuchin expressed frustration, warning the investigation could jeopardize the confirmation of a new Fed chair.
  • Senator Tillis stated he would not approve nominations, potentially leading to a deadlocked vote for Powell's replacement.
  • This situation could prompt Powell to remain a governor until 2028, blocking another vacancy for Trump's appointees.

  • President Trump's actions against Powell may have paradoxically reduced his own authority over the Federal Reserve.
  • The Federal Reserve's defensive response, after a period of quiet, appears to have bolstered its institutional independence.
  • This marks a potential shift towards more open criticism of presidential overreach, making the central bank stronger in the face of political pressure.
