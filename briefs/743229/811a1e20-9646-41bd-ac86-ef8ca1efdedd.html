Inside the U.S. Operation to Oust Venezuela’s President

Key Takeaways

  • U.S. forces captured Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro in a swift military operation.
  • 'Operation Absolute Resolve' involved months of intelligence gathering and military preparation.
  • Maduro was transported to New York to face criminal drug trafficking charges.
  • President Trump stated the U.S. would oversee Venezuela's governance and rebuild its oil industry.

Deep Dive

  • The host introduced a special Sunday episode detailing the capture of Venezuelan president Nicolás Maduro in a swift military operation.
  • National security correspondent Eric Schmitt characterized the capture as a historic and audacious exercise of American power, comparable to the 1989 Noriega operation.
  • Planning for 'Operation Absolute Resolve' commenced months prior, initially considering strikes on drug trafficking boats and later land strikes to remove Maduro.

  • CIA operatives infiltrated Venezuela to monitor Maduro's movements and routines, aided by an informant providing real-time location data.
  • Stealth drone flights also tracked Maduro in the months leading up to the operation.
  • Weeks prior to the capture, specialized military aircraft capable of jamming, surveillance, and troop transport began arriving.
  • Unseasonably bad weather delayed the operation in Venezuela by 10 days; it was approved by the president late Friday and began early Saturday.

  • President Trump announced the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro via social media early Saturday, stating he was flown out of the country.
  • General Kane detailed the operation, stating the president ordered the mission at 10:46 p.m. Eastern Time.
  • Over 150 aircraft launched from 20 bases, disabling Venezuelan air defenses before helicopters approached Maduro's compound, arriving at 1:01 a.m. Eastern Time.
  • Delta Force commandos, after extensive preparation including building a mock house, secured Maduro and his wife, who surrendered without significant resistance.

  • The military operation facilitated the transport of Nicolás Maduro via helicopter to the USS Iwo Jima.
  • The entire operation, from capture to arrival in New York, lasted two hours and 20 minutes.
  • The military's involvement in arresting Maduro was at the request of the Justice Department.
  • Law enforcement agents were present to serve an arrest warrant related to drug trafficking and other charges.

  • Following Maduro's capture, President Trump stated the U.S. would "run" Venezuela and rebuild its oil infrastructure.
  • Trump indicated American oil companies would be involved to benefit both Venezuela and U.S. businesses.
  • Advisors like "Heg Seth and Rubio" were mentioned as involved in governing Venezuela, alongside the current vice president.
  • Critics argue this intervention's rationale appeared to be primarily economic gain for the U.S., framed as 'America First'.

  • The Venezuelan diaspora in Florida celebrated Maduro's capture as the end of an authoritarian regime.
  • Critics expressed concern that President Trump lacked a clear plan for Venezuela, potentially leading to nation-building efforts he previously opposed.
  • Trump views military actions like this as successful 'surgical strikes' with potential for quick solutions.
  • This approach risks normalizing the use of military force, potentially leading to unforeseen international commitments.
