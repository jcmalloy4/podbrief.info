Trump Sent Them to a Notorious Prison. Torture Followed.

Key Takeaways

  • The Trump administration deported over 200 Venezuelan men to an El Salvadoran prison in March.
  • These men reported severe physical, mental, and sexual abuse, later corroborated by forensic experts as torture.
  • Deportations occurred despite legal challenges and a lack of evidence for alleged gang affiliation.
  • The U.S. government reportedly had prior knowledge of potential torture in El Salvador's prison system.
  • The prisoners' release was part of a negotiation brokered by Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro.

Deep Dive

  • In March, the U.S. deported 250 Venezuelan men to a maximum-security prison in El Salvador.
  • The deportation was framed as a deterrent against illegal immigration.
  • Deported men were reportedly denied access to lawyers and family; their names and criminal histories were withheld.

  • Luis Eric Chacón, a father of three, was arrested for a taillight violation while working for Uber Eats.
  • His past legal issues and tattoos were scrutinized, with the administration using tattoos as an indicator of gang membership.
  • There is no evidence Luis Chacón was a member of the gang Trender Agua, and little evidence many deported men were gang members.

  • Upon arrival in El Salvador, men were subjected to physical abuse, including being forced down stairs and onto buses.
  • Prisoners recounted being threatened with death by guards.
  • Luis Chacón was reportedly assured deportation to Venezuela but was sent to El Salvador instead.

  • Inmates described sharing cells with approximately 10 men and using open cistern water for drinking and bathing.
  • Reports indicated consistent denial of medical care, including a diabetic man receiving incorrect insulin.
  • Another severely ill man was reportedly told to 'resign himself to death' instead of receiving treatment.

  • In May, after a guard search led to a severe beating, inmates briefly opened cell doors using dislodged metal parts.
  • Guards retaliated by shooting inmates with rubber bullets at close range in their cells.
  • Following the rebellion, prisoners faced intensified punishment, including isolation in 'La Isla,' where sexual assault and attempted drowning were reported.

  • New York Times journalists provided testimony and evidence of injuries from the men to forensic experts.
  • These experts concluded that the testimony and evidence were consistent and credible.
  • The described abuse met the United Nations definition of torture.

  • The reported torture occurred despite the men never being tried or convicted of terrorism.
  • U.S. judges had attempted to halt the deportation of some of these men.
  • The Trump administration reportedly ignored a judge's order to stop flights carrying some men to El Salvador.

  • The release of 252 Venezuelan men from the El Salvadoran prison was part of a negotiation.
  • Venezuela secured the freedom of 10 U.S. citizens and residents detained in Venezuela.
  • Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, not President Trump, orchestrated the end of the men's ordeal.
