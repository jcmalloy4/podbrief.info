Why the Stock Market Just Keeps Going Up

Key Takeaways

  • U.S. stock markets are at record highs despite high tariffs and a weakening labor market.
  • Artificial intelligence enthusiasm, notably for "Magnificent Seven" companies, significantly drives market performance.
  • Federal Reserve interest rate cuts are seen as a response to economic signals, supporting market stability.
  • Rising gold prices and corporate defaults signal underlying market unease and consumer strain.
  • Markets have become desensitized to political actions, focusing instead on tangible corporate earnings.

Deep Dive

  • The U.S. stock market has shown perplexing strength, reaching all-time highs for the S&P and NASDAQ, despite the Trump administration's high tariff policies.
  • Markets initially reacted negatively to tariff announcements, prompting policy adjustments, but continued to climb as tariffs were gradually implemented.
  • Market stability is attributed to a better understanding of tariff rates, government revenue generation from tariffs, and contained inflation.
  • Uncertainty, particularly from unexpected tariff actions, had previously caused significant market disruption.

  • Factors like a more predictable economic environment, extended tax cuts, and the Federal Reserve's decision to lower interest rates have eased market anxiety.
  • Despite administration pressure, current Federal Reserve policy, including interest rate cuts, aligns with economic fundamentals.
  • Markets interpret the Fed's rate cuts as a response to economic signals, specifically a weakening labor market.
  • The U.S. labor market, while showing signs of slowing, remains historically strong with low unemployment.

  • Enthusiasm for artificial intelligence is identified as a primary driver of the stock market's record highs.
  • The "Magnificent Seven" companies, heavily invested in AI, now constitute over one-third of the S&P 500, significantly impacting market performance.
  • Concerns persist about the AI boom being a potential bubble, with companies like NVIDIA trading at historically unprecedented valuations.
  • NVIDIA's market cap grew from $500 billion in 2021 to over $4 trillion, highlighting speculative investor belief in AI's future returns.
  • The strong performance of a few large AI-centric companies can mask underlying issues, with many S&P 500 companies declining even as tech giants rise.

  • Rising gold prices, exceeding $4,000, indicate market unease and a flight to safe-haven assets by investors.
  • Central banks are increasing their gold reserves, signaling potential concerns about U.S. dollar assets and market stability.
  • Money is reportedly shifting out of the U.S. stock market and into European and emerging markets.
  • An increase in corporate defaults, particularly within the subprime auto lending sector, suggests growing strain on consumers and potential negative impacts on company revenues.

  • Despite volatile economic policies, financial markets have shown a disconnect from real-world economic impacts.
  • Markets have become desensitized to presidential actions and tweets, prioritizing tangible outcomes and strong corporate earnings growth.
  • Investors are advised to focus on underlying economic strengths, such as double-digit earnings growth, rather than political "noise" or potential outcomes.

  • Specific companies have seen significant gains, with seven, including Broadcom, Palantir, and Oracle, rising over 100% since April.
  • These substantial individual company performances contribute significantly to the overall positive trajectory of the U.S. stock market.
