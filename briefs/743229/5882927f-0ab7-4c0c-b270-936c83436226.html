The Push to Revise American History at the Smithsonian

Key Takeaways

  • Trump administration seeks to reshape cultural institutions, including the Smithsonian.
  • Campaign involves criticizing exhibits, replacing board members, and auditing content.
  • Focus is on "American greatness" over "divisive" historical aspects like slavery.
  • These actions challenge the long-standing independence of the Smithsonian and other institutions.
  • Efforts are seen as a reaction to the 2020 racial reckoning and diversity focus.

Deep Dive

  • President Trump initiated an overhaul of U.S. museums, specifically criticizing the Smithsonian for perceived negative portrayals of the country.
  • The administration plans to review Smithsonian exhibits to align them with a perspective focused on "American greatness."
  • Reporter Robin Pogrebin noted this is part of a broader attack on arts, with President Trump labeling museums as "woke" and "out of control."

  • The administration's interference began with the Kennedy Center, where President Trump accused it of "woke" programming and replaced board appointees.
  • An executive order in March criticized the Smithsonian for promoting "divisive, race-centered ideology."
  • The administration also called for the firing of National Portrait Gallery director Kim Sayet due to her interest in diversifying the museum's collection.

  • The Smithsonian faced controversy over artist Amy Sherold's "Transforming Liberty" exhibition, which depicted a transgender Statue of Liberty, leading to its cancellation.
  • On August 12th, the Trump administration announced an audit of Smithsonian exhibitions, demanding concrete changes within 120 days, including the replacement of "ideological or divisive" language.

  • The administration objected to specific exhibits, including art about the southern border, the pride flag, an animation about Anthony Fauci, and the Smithsonian's emphasis on slavery in an exhibition on Benjamin Franklin.
  • These actions are viewed as a reaction to the 2020 racial reckoning, which prompted cultural institutions to re-examine their collections for greater diversity and accuracy.

  • The current administration's direct attempt to influence Smithsonian exhibition content challenges the institution's independence.
  • The Smithsonian's governing Board of Regents, which includes political appointees and is overseen by Chief Justice John Roberts, has not yet issued an official response.
  • Future presidential appointments could further influence the board and the institution's narrative.

  • The guest expresses concern that the administration's actions could have lasting chilling effects on scholarly independence and artistic expression, drawing parallels to authoritarian regimes.
  • Administration control over the Smithsonian could result in a simplified version of American history, reflecting specific values and priorities rather than complex narratives.
