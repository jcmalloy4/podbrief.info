The Fallout From the National Guard Shooting

Key Takeaways

  • A shooting involving an Afghan immigrant led to sweeping U.S. immigration policy changes.
  • The suspect, Rahmanullah Lakanwal, served in a CIA-backed paramilitary unit in Afghanistan.
  • The Trump administration paused Afghan immigration applications and began reviewing past asylum grants.
  • New policies affect thousands of asylum seekers and green card holders, prompting legal challenges.

Deep Dive

  • President Trump responded to a shooting involving an Afghan immigrant by halting Afghan national entry to the U.S. and reviewing asylum decisions.
  • The Trump administration enacted far-reaching changes to the U.S. immigration system, blocking entry pathways and reviewing records of those with status.
  • The guest described the administration's response as a set of remarkable changes blocking pathways for entry and remaining in the country.

  • Rahmanullah Lakanwal served in the CIA-backed Zero Unit in Afghanistan, a paramilitary group accused of extrajudicial killings.
  • He was brought to the U.S. under Operation Allies Welcome in 2021 after the Taliban takeover, undergoing layers of vetting.
  • Lakanwal was granted asylum under the Trump administration in April, having arrived under the Biden administration's parole program.
  • He shot two National Guard members in Washington D.C., 20-year-old Sarah Beckstrom and 24-year-old Andrew Wolf; Beckstrom later died.

  • The guest suggested the shooting confirmed former President Trump's fears about immigrants, leading to a significant policy crackdown.
  • Trump administration officials, including Chad Wolf and Kash Patel, criticized the Biden administration's vetting of Afghan immigrants.
  • The White House did not provide specifics on vetting issues, and Trump deflected questions about granting Lakanwal asylum during his term.

  • The Trump administration paused all immigration applications from Afghan immigrants, impacting their ability to seek long-term residency.
  • A review of all asylum applications granted under the Biden administration expanded to potentially include tens of thousands of asylum seekers.
  • The U.S. will review green card holders from countries previously banned by Trump, impacting hundreds of thousands of records, and ceased issuing visas to Afghan nationals.

  • The host questioned the legal authority of the administration's sweeping and retrospective immigration changes.
  • The guest acknowledged that these actions, particularly reviewing individuals already vetted, will likely lead to lawsuits.
  • A Wall Street Journal editorial argued that abandoning allies undermines American credibility and future foreign policy.
  • Stephen Miller countered by focusing on an existential danger posed by migrants, describing it as an embrace of collective punishment.

  • Homeland Security Secretary Chris Wray stated the alleged shooter may have become radicalized after immigrating to the U.S.
  • The second guardsman shot in Washington remains in critical condition, while President Trump announced plans to send 500 additional National Guard members to the city.
  • President Trump took contradictory actions regarding drug cartels, closing airspace around Venezuela while pardoning a former Honduran president convicted of drug trafficking.
