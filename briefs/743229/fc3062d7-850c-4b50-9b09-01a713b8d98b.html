100 Years of ‘The Great Gatsby’

Key Takeaways

  • "The Great Gatsby" transformed from an initially overlooked novel into a foundational American text over a century, propelled by post-WWII academic interest and widespread distribution.
  • Fitzgerald's masterful prose captures the essence of American ambition, self-invention, and the elusive nature of the American Dream through Jay Gatsby's character.
  • The novel critically examines American societal flaws, including class conflict, the pursuit of wealth, and an ambivalent relationship with deceit and self-made personas.
  • "Gatsby" endures as a "Rorschach blot" for American identity, its open-ended nature allowing readers to project their own experiences with class, fraud, and the national narrative.

Jay Gatsby: The Embodiment of the American Dream and Its Flaws

  • Fitzgerald skillfully depicts Gatsby's self-creation as a "platonic conception" and a "son of God," embodying a blend of grand ambition and youthful idealism.
  • Gatsby's "American-ness" stems from his relentless self-invention and upward mobility, achieved through both military service and illicit activities, reflecting the nation's complex relationship with wealth and enterprise.
  • The novel highlights American ambivalence towards fraud and criminality, presenting Gatsby with an enduring innocence despite his association with figures like the 1919 World Series fixer.

Class Conflict and the Elusive American Ideal

  • The core dynamic between Gatsby's striving "new money" and Tom Buchanan's entitled "old money" highlights rigid class divisions and inherent injustices within American society.
  • While Tom ultimately wins Daisy, the narrative guides reader sympathy towards Gatsby's aspirational spirit, underscoring the enduring appeal of the self-made individual.
  • The novel suggests the American project itself is tragic, built on conflict and inequity, with idealized moments like Gatsby's pursuit of Daisy ultimately doomed from their inception, mirroring the nation's flawed beginnings.

"Gatsby" as an "Open Text" Reflecting America's Identity

  • The book functions as a "Rorschach blot" for American identity, its nuanced and often elusive meaning allowing readers to project their own understanding of the nation and themselves.
  • The characters' inscrutability is intentional, contributing to the book's mystique and spellbinding quality rather than making them mere allegorical figures.
  • The narrator Nick Carraway's task of interpreting unreliable accounts, and the lingering sense of an "elusive rhythm" or "fragment of lost words," symbolizes the difficulty of fully grasping America's meaning and a nostalgic longing for a lost national essence.
