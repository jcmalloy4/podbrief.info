The Happiness Lab with Dr. Laurie Santos

Make Vulnerability Your Superpower (with NBA star Kevin Love)

Key Takeaways

  • Male athletes face significant stigma regarding mental health, often encouraged to suppress emotions.
  • Unaddressed grief and generational trauma can manifest as severe physical and mental health issues.
  • Tying personal identity solely to athletic performance can lead to depression and anxiety.
  • Vulnerability, especially from prominent figures like Kevin Love, fosters crucial open dialogue and healing.
  • Advocacy and education, like through the Kevin Love Fund, are essential to destigmatize mental health.

Deep Dive

  • Dr. Laurie Santos introduced five-time NBA All-Star Kevin Love, highlighting the increasing mental well-being conversation and specific stigma faced by male athletes (1:55).
  • Kevin Love mentioned his dog's positive impact on his mental health (3:15).
  • Love learned to bury emotions and compartmentalize feelings, adhering to a stoic masculine ideal, leading him to become 'emotion phobic' (5:49).
  • He linked his anxiety and depression to identifying primarily as a basketball player rather than a person (6:49).

  • Love connected his struggles to hyper-masculine culture and generational trauma, referencing his father's role in caring for Brian Wilson of The Beach Boys, who also faced mental health issues (8:29).
  • He discussed a lineage of melancholy and dark spells spanning generations within his family.
  • His father, in hospice, confronted painful memories of his own father's physical abuse, finding peace through reconciliation and forgiveness (9:34).
  • Love's father redefined success beyond athletic achievement, emphasizing the presence of his children at his deathbed as ultimate success (10:33).

  • Despite early career success, Love faced struggles including unaddressed grief from his grandmother's 2013 death, a central family figure (13:33).
  • His inaction regarding this grief and not spending enough time with her led to regret, manifesting physically and mentally (14:34).
  • He spent six years with his team in Minnesota, using basketball to avoid processing his intense emotions (15:14).
  • Unaddressed pain resulted in panic attacks, rage fits, agoraphobia, and suicidal ideation, particularly after an injury limited him to 18 games in one season (15:39).

  • Kevin Love's mental health issues intensified during the 2017-18 NBA season, culminating in a public panic attack on the court (21:48).
  • Despite a championship in 2016 and other successes, Love was personally estranged from his parents and avoided therapy due to perceived failure and societal pressures (22:33).
  • His panic attack symptoms included breathlessness akin to cardiac arrest; tests at the Cleveland Clinic found no physical cause (23:55).
  • This public episode prompted Love to recognize the need for change, leading him to therapy and medication to address his mental health (24:54).

  • The host referenced Kevin Love's 2018 article as a pivotal moment in his mental health advocacy (25:34).
  • Love experienced another panic attack during his fifth All-Star season in Oklahoma City, intensified by a broken hand that removed his athletic outlet (26:01).
  • Facing team pressure and media mistrust, Love decided to write 'Everyone is Going Through Something' to share his story directly (27:03).
  • Initially fearing negative feedback, he received an overwhelmingly positive response with thousands of messages from people sharing their own struggles (27:55).

  • Love received supportive reactions from teammates LeBron James and Kyle Korver upon returning to the team after his article's publication, fostering deeper camaraderie (29:44).
  • His vulnerability now empowers other athletes to be more open about their own struggles (31:01).
  • Speaking openly provides Love with levity and reinforces his authenticity, aiming to help others (31:36).
  • He contrasts 'legacy virtues' with 'eulogy virtues,' emphasizing impact on others over athletic achievements as true success (31:49).

  • Love described an overwhelming public desire to change mental health stigma following his sharing (36:57).
  • He introduced the Kevin Love Fund, a nonprofit equipping the next generation with mental health management skills through education.
  • Dr. Santos noted how student vulnerability in programs, even discussing issues like food insecurity, fosters compassion and mutual support (38:41).
  • The ripple effect of vulnerability creates generational healing, as seen in a student's sign: 'everybody's going through something' (40:02).

  • Student athletes expressed gratitude for Love's public vulnerability, acknowledging it initiates crucial conversations others might fear (41:28).
  • Challenges for student athletes include hiding struggles due to scholarship loss fears or team selection impact (41:45).
  • The discussion highlighted the importance of coaches modeling vulnerability, with positive examples from the NBA, including Miami Heat's Coach Spoelstra (42:38).
  • Vulnerability is framed as a strength that improves short-term performance and long-term career longevity, akin to physical recovery practices (43:34).
