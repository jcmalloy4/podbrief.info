The DOJ Comes for Jerome Powell

Key Takeaways

  • The Justice Department opened a criminal investigation into Fed Chair Jerome Powell regarding building renovation claims, widely seen as politically motivated.
  • Experts characterized the investigation as unprecedented in US history, drawing comparisons to actions in nations with economic instability.
  • Despite serious implications for Federal Reserve independence, initial market reactions were mixed, with indices recovering to all-time highs.
  • Powell publicly addressed the subpoenas, asserting the administration lacked sufficient evidence and solidifying Senate opposition to a compliant Fed chair.
  • The events sparked concerns about the erosion of institutional credibility and the potential for political influence over monetary policy.
  • Powell's actions highlighted his perceived backbone in resisting political pressure, contrasting with other leaders.

Deep Dive

  • The Justice Department initiated a criminal investigation into Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell, examining claims he lied to Congress regarding Fed building renovations.
  • The investigation focuses on the Eccles building renovation, initially budgeted at $1.9 billion, now projected to cost $2.6 billion, a 35-36% increase under Powell's tenure.
  • Powell publicly stated the investigation is 'unprecedented' and serves as a pretext for the administration's attempts to lower interest rates.
  • Republicans, including Senators Murkowski and Tillis, criticized the probe, with some calling it an act of a 'banana republic,' indicating broad political condemnation.

  • Markets were initially rattled, with the S&P opening lower, but indices like the S&P and Dow later rallied to close higher, while the dollar weakened and gold and silver surged to record highs.
  • Liz Hoffman noted that despite the serious implications of potential Fed independence erosion, which historically leads to hyperinflation, the market reaction remained largely muted.
  • Guest Justin Wolfers described the situation as 'flabbergasting' and 'unprecedented,' comparing it to actions by 'tinpot dictators' that have led to economic instability and runaway inflation, as seen in Turkey (80% inflation).
  • The bond market, which historically acts as a check on presidential actions, showed minimal concern with only a slight increase in Treasury yields.

  • Fed Chair Jerome Powell made the threat of subpoenas public via a video statement to the American people, signaling a direct refusal to back down from attempts to undermine the Fed.
  • This unprecedented public address is interpreted by some as Powell 'winning' by asserting that the administration lacks sufficient evidence for the claims.
  • Powell's actions galvanized Senate opposition, with Republicans like Senators Tillis and Murkowski publicly stating they would not support a Fed chair perceived as a 'Trump toady.'
  • Markets did not react negatively as the threat had no immediate impact on monetary policy and inadvertently secured the Fed's independence by increasing Senate scrutiny.

  • The discussion addressed the future of the Fed chair, whose term ends in May, and whether recent events signal a change in the appointment of a potentially more compliant successor.
  • Liz Hoffman suggested Kevin Hassett or Kevin Warshu are likely candidates for the Fed chair position due to their perceived compliance with presidential preferences.
  • The Federal Reserve operates as a consensus-driven body where no single member has more power than others, with new voting members rotating and diverse viewpoints on interest rates.
  • Divergent views within the Fed on economic outlooks, including risks of a weakening labor market or rising inflation, lead to differing policy prescriptions and voting patterns.

  • The episode raised questions about the longevity of presidential actions on institutions like the Fed and the erosion of its credibility due to policy missteps and perceived political interference.
  • Increased transparency for the Fed, while allowing public scrutiny, may lead to the institution becoming more political and expected to answer for its decisions.
  • Economist Justin Wolfers argued that Trump's actions against Fed independence are politically damaging, as they lack a clear 'MAGA interest' and could ultimately raise long-term interest rates.
  • The investigation is framed as a political story unfavorable to Donald Trump, noting Powell's approval rating of 44% compared to Trump's 36% prior to these events.

  • Liz Hoffman discussed Jerome Powell's legacy, highlighting his 'textbook response' to the pandemic and a 'delayed response' to inflation, along with his backbone in resisting political pressure.
  • Powell's courage in resisting political pressure was celebrated, contrasting him with business and public leaders who have 'rolled over' in similar situations.
  • The host emphasized the upcoming week as an opportunity for private sector leaders, particularly CEOs of financial institutions, to demonstrate similar fortitude when questioned about the Fed investigation.
  • The investigation reportedly centers on the renovation of a Federal Reserve building, a project initially approved by Donald Trump during his first term.
