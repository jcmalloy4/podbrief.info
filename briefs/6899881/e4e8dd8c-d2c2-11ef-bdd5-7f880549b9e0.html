Tesla Profits Plunge 37% Despite Record Sales — Here’s Why

Key Takeaways

  • Tesla's Q3 profits dropped 37% due to lower EV prices despite increased deliveries.
  • Tesla faces challenges with aging models, Cybertruck uncertainty, and high valuation.
  • Beyond Meat experienced a significant, volatile meme stock surge driven by retail traders.
  • Meme stocks are characterized by low prices, high short interest, and community speculation.
  • The meme stock phenomenon has become institutionalized, with hedge funds tracking and ETFs emerging.

Deep Dive

  • Tesla's third-quarter net income fell 37% due to lower EV prices, despite a 12% revenue increase and 7% rise in deliveries.
  • Record deliveries were driven by customers utilizing expiring U.S. tax breaks, which helped flush inventory.
  • Profitability suffered partly from tariffs and a decrease in revenue from regulatory credits.
  • The market reacted negatively to the profitability figures and the company's product pipeline, with cheaper Model Y and Model 3 versions not fully offsetting lost tax credits.
  • Future challenges include an aging Model Y and Model 3 lineup, uncertain Cybertruck market reception, and an unclear long-term outlook for robo-taxis.

  • Discussion centered on Tesla's future as a robot company, acknowledging the significant investment required for AI development.
  • The company's substantial cash reserves are seen as a buffer against economic downturns and growing competition, particularly from China.
  • Uncertainty surrounds CEO Elon Musk's pay package, which faces an upcoming shareholder vote.
  • A comparison to GM's recent earnings noted GM's market optimism about overcoming EV transition challenges, despite Tesla's significantly higher valuation.

  • Beyond Meat shares surged significantly, emerging as a meme stock, partly driven by Walmart's expansion of its products into over 2,000 stores.
  • The stock later corrected sharply, leading to multiple trading halts on the Nasdaq.
  • Luke Kawa, Markets Editor at Sherwood News, noted that the reasons behind the surge remained unclear for both investors and the broader market.

  • Beyond Meat's dramatic stock price surge followed a debt-for-equity swap announcement.
  • Retail traders, notably one based in Dubai, promoted a thesis that the company's debt reduction and existing short interest would lead to a stock rebound, causing significant intraday gains.
  • The general life cycle of a meme stock typically includes a history of significant price drops, a low nominal stock price (below $5), and elevated short interest.
  • These characteristics foster a community dynamic and a perception of an 'us vs. them' battle among investors.

  • The current phase of a meme stock involves significant trading volumes and options activity, indicative of a 'buyer's binge' rather than a short squeeze.
  • Sustaining upward price momentum in this phase requires increasing demand, bearing resemblance to a Ponzi scheme structure.
  • The final stage typically involves a plateau and decline in trading volumes, necessitating operational improvements and proven performance for the underlying company.
  • GameStop serves as an example, using its meme stock boom to provide capital for cash burn or acquisitions, eventually achieving operational cash flow under new management.

  • Meme stock movements originated on platforms like Wall Street Bets, YouTube, and Discord, propagating through strong theses and online communities.
  • Specific tactics were employed, such as referring to Beyond Meat as 'fake meat' to bypass algorithmic detection.
  • The episode highlights that meme stocks, including the recent Beyond Meat phenomenon, are no longer novel but a commonplace and institutionalized feature of financial markets.
  • Hedge funds now widely track retail traders, hire meme stock experts, and meme stock ETFs have emerged, indicating the integration of these movements into the financial system.
