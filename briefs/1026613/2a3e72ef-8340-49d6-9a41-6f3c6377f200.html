Hard Fork

OpenAI Calls a ‘Code Red’ + Which Model Should I Use? + The Hard Fork Review of Slop

Key Takeaways

  • OpenAI has declared a "code red" to prioritize ChatGPT improvements amid rising competitive pressures.
  • New AI models from Google and Anthropic are significantly challenging OpenAI's market dominance and capabilities.
  • The AI industry is undergoing rapid advancements, shifting user expectations, and notable personnel changes.
  • AI-generated content, termed "slop," presents both risks like misinformation and benefits such as educational tools.
  • Anthropic is emerging as a significant enterprise competitor, characterized by a distinct, values-driven AI approach.

Deep Dive

  • OpenAI issued a "code red" memo, shifting resources to improve ChatGPT and delaying other projects like AI agents and Pulse.
  • The move follows a prior "code orange" declaration and is driven by competitive pressures from Google's Gemini 3 and Anthropic's Opus 4.5 models.
  • Competitor advancements threaten OpenAI's market dominance and potential user and revenue growth, impacting the heavily leveraged company.

  • OpenAI and Anthropic previously relied on advanced models as a competitive advantage in the market.
  • Increasing capabilities of rival AI models and low user switching costs now challenge OpenAI's market position.
  • OpenAI's large language model pre-training process may be hitting diminishing returns, a complex problem beyond post-training optimizations.
  • The company is reportedly developing new models, 'Garlic' and 'Shallot Pete,' to advance AI and regain its competitive edge.

  • Google's Gemini 3 is noted for its speed, making it a frequent choice for tasks like fact-checking, and for its ability to identify errors in user content.
  • Gemini 3 is described as a fast and powerful "workhorse" model, effective for research tasks such as organizing timelines and analyzing documents.
  • Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.5 has shown impressive results, generating text with style and structure comparable to a host's own writing.
  • One host uses Claude Opus 4.5 for personal research, family, and medical topics, citing its warm and humane responses as offering unique support.

  • Unlike Google and OpenAI, which focus on user engagement and ad monetization, Anthropic's Claude model prioritizes enterprise API sales.
  • This focus suggests Claude may face less risk of compromising user experience for commercial gains and can become more efficient for work use cases.
  • Claude 4.5 includes a unique "soul document" embedded in its weights, describing the company's philosophy and Claude's nature, signaling a long-term vision.

  • Anthropic is presented as a significant strategic threat to OpenAI in the enterprise market, with annualized revenue projected to reach $9 billion in 2024.
  • The success of ChatGPT has spurred competition, inadvertently helping Google overcome internal bureaucracy and allowing Anthropic to refine its AI strategy.
  • Significant personnel changes include Jan LaCun's departure from Meta to focus on world models and John Giannandrea stepping down as Apple's head of AI.

  • New AI models from Anthropic and Google have significantly improved capabilities, with advancements compared to a JPEG loading in higher resolution.
  • ChatGPT's initial release three years prior now seems rudimentary, highlighting a rapid increase in personal expectations for AI tools.
  • One host estimates AI has saved them a year of work on a book project.
  • A principle was established to prioritize opinions on AI from individuals who actively use the technology, as non-users discuss an outdated version of AI.

  • The "Hard Fork Review of Slop" segment features examples of AI-generated content.
  • AI-generated images of a Christmas market at Buckingham Palace led to tourists visiting a non-existent event, as reported by the BBC.
  • Such seemingly harmless AI content can cause real-world confusion and disappointment, raising concerns about more serious future consequences in an uncertain information environment.

  • AI-generated "slop recipes" are negatively impacting human food bloggers by reducing their website traffic, with some AI recipes proving nonsensical.
  • Conversely, AI-generated educational songs, such as those from the "Learning with Lyrics" Instagram account, are seen as acceptable and beneficial.
  • This technology could replace ad-heavy sites like WikiHow and aid auditory learners with complex topics.

  • A deepfake advertisement by Whirlpool, created by Omnicom subsidiary DM9, used an AI-generated voice and likeness of a North Carolina state senator without permission, winning and then losing Cannes Lions awards.
  • AI-generated videos of a non-existent 'Bird Game 3' have gone viral on TikTok, amassing over 13 million views and creating demand for a real game.
  • One host views 'Bird Game 3' as a satire of repetitive entertainment sequels, with speculation that a real version might eventually be created.
