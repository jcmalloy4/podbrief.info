Why Ukraine's drone attack on Russia changes war forever | Ian Bremmer

Key Takeaways

  • Ukraine achieved an unprecedented asymmetric victory with its "SpiderWeb" drone operation, using $300-400 drones to destroy over $7 billion worth of Russian strategic bombers—representing the most spectacular strike in the 3.5-year war and demonstrating how cheap technology can devastate expensive military assets.
  • Putin's strategic calculations are being forced into reassessment as Ukraine's ability to strike deep inside Russia challenges his assumptions about time favoring Russia, while his failure to effectively deter NATO makes him appear vulnerable to his own leadership.
  • Nuclear escalation risk has significantly increased from roughly 1% to potentially 5-20%, as Putin considers both negotiation and escalation options while being constrained by potential alienation of allies like China and uncertainty about international responses.
  • Drone technology is fundamentally democratizing military power, enabling weaker actors to inflict disproportionate damage on major powers and creating new national security implications that traditional defense establishments are slow to adapt to.
  • The China-Russia relationship remains complex and strained over Ukraine, with China seeking global stability while providing economic support but opposing Russia's territorial annexations and being excluded from key military decisions like North Korea's troop deployment.

Deep Dive

Ukraine's Unprecedented Drone Strike on Russia

The conversation begins with analysis of Ukraine's surprise drone attack on Russia, codenamed "SpiderWeb," which targeted strategic military aircraft. According to reports, Ukraine claimed to have destroyed 41 Russian planes in what The Economist described as Ukraine's most audacious operation on Russian territory to date.

Ian Bremmer's Assessment:

  • Actual plane destruction likely around half of Ukraine's claimed number
  • Represents the most spectacular strike in the 3.5-year war
  • Demonstrates an unprecedented "David versus Goliath" scenario
  • Drones costing $300-$400 were used to destroy strategic nuclear bombers worth over $100 million
  • Marks the first known asymmetric strike against a major nuclear power

Ukraine's Military Innovation and Capabilities

The discussion reveals how Ukraine developed sophisticated military capabilities through intense national commitment and leveraged its global diaspora of technologists and professionals. Ukraine has built world-class drone capabilities comparable to Israel's, driven by the existential threat of Russian invasion.

The Strategic Operation:

  • Drones were smuggled into Russia hidden in truck containers
  • Containers were strategically placed across Russia, including deep inside the country
  • Coordinated attacks launched on multiple Russian military bases
  • Strikes reached targets thousands of miles from Ukrainian front lines
  • Successfully destroyed advanced Russian strategic bombers
Impact on Russia's Military Assets:
  • Estimated loss of about one-third of Russia's strategic bombers
  • Over $7 billion in damage inflicted
  • Targeted Russia's nuclear triad bomber fleet - the oldest leg of nuclear deterrence composed of Soviet-era planes
  • Russia cannot easily replace these losses due to lack of ongoing production, limited manufacturing capacity, and resource constraints from the current war

Putin's Strategic Calculations and Reassessment

The conversation explores Putin's belief that time favors Russia due to the country's larger military capacity and his ability to conscript more soldiers without significant political pushback. He expects European support for Ukraine to wane and anticipates inconsistent American support over time.

However, Ukraine's demonstrated capabilities are forcing Putin to reassess his strategic position:

  • Ukraine has shown restraint by not targeting Russian civilians despite having the capability
  • Ukraine's ability to strike deep inside Russia challenges Putin's assumptions
  • The operation potentially weakens Russia's global military reputation and makes Putin appear vulnerable to his own leadership
NATO and International Dynamics:
  • NATO appears increasingly comfortable supporting Ukraine
  • Putin perceives he has failed to effectively deter NATO
  • Putin sees NATO viewing Russia as weak and unlikely to respond decisively

Escalation Risks and Nuclear Threat Assessment

Putin appears to be considering both negotiation and escalation options, with increased risk of attempting to show toughness against NATO. Potential escalation scenarios include increased attacks on Kyiv, more civilian damage, attempts to assassinate Ukrainian leaders, and potential spectacular attacks against Ukrainian officials.

Nuclear Considerations:

  • Likelihood of nuclear weapon use has increased from 1% to potentially 5-20%
  • Putin remains hesitant due to potential alienation of European allies, uncertainty about Trump's response, and strong opposition from China
  • The current geopolitical environment is considered more dangerous than during the Cold War
  • Putin is unlikely to step down peacefully if cornered, potentially risking extreme escalation

China-Russia Relationship Dynamics

The discussion reveals the complex Putin-Xi relationship built on mutual trust but characterized by asymmetrical dynamics, with China being more economically and technologically powerful. China seeks global stability while Russia acts as more of a "chaos actor."

Key Tensions:

  • China is unhappy with Russia's actions in Ukraine
  • China does not recognize Russia's annexation of Ukrainian territories
  • China provides economic and technological support but not direct military capabilities
  • Russia's invasion disrupted China's economic relations with Eastern European countries
  • North Korea's troop deployment to Ukraine was not pre-communicated to China

Peace Negotiations and Diplomatic Efforts

Recent peace talks ended inconclusively, with Ukraine strategically timing its attack considering ongoing diplomatic discussions. Two potential ceasefire options exist for Russia and Ukraine, but both remain currently unacceptable to the parties involved. While diplomatic relations show slight improvement, military tensions continue escalating.

Broader Implications of Drone Technology

The conversation highlights the rapid evolution of drone capabilities and their democratizing effect on military power:

  • Countries effectively using drone technology include Israel, Ukraine, India, and Gulf states
  • Cheap drones can cause significant damage to expensive military assets
  • Potential for micro drone swarms and ability to smuggle drones across borders undetected
  • Technology is empowering weaker actors, including potential terrorist organizations
  • Creates potential national security implications for the United States

Trump Administration's Approach

Trump appears reluctant to strongly pressure Putin and seems to be losing interest in the conflict. However, he's unlikely to completely cut off Ukrainian support and will likely continue allowing Ukraine to buy military weapons while maintaining NATO support.

Key Observations:

  • US defense establishment is slow to adapt to new technological needs
  • Existing leadership and congressional structures resist rapid military innovation
  • Current approach appears to be "wait for a crisis, then respond"
  • Ukraine is losing territory slowly in conventional warfare and faces challenges with troop numbers compared to Russia

Personal Impact and War's Human Dimension

The conversation concludes with discussion of Ukrainian architect Slava Balbek, who maintains his architectural practice in Kyiv while going to the front line monthly. His concept of "war work life balance" illustrates the challenging dual reality of Ukrainians living under Russian aggression, highlighting how the conflict permeates every aspect of professional and personal life.
