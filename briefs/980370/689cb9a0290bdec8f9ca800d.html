Sunday Pick: After the Glory Fades | Good Sport

Key Takeaways

  • Athletes confront an identity crisis when retiring, shifting from structured competition to an undefined future.
  • Channeling competitive drive into new endeavors fosters purpose and successful post-athletic transitions.
  • Reframing aging as unique evolution, not decline, fosters a fulfilling post-athletic life.

Deep Dives

Athlete Identity

  • Carli Lloyd viewed her Olympic reflection not as upset, but as contemplating her chosen retirement, highlighting the personal nature of such transitions.
  • The transition from professional sports mirrors universal aging, affecting identity and purpose after years of defining one's life through competition.
  • Host Jody Averger shared personal struggles with retirement from Ultimate Frisbee, where injuries forced an end to a life defined by the sport.

New Directions

  • Carli Lloyd embraced new ventures like business and media work, finding satisfaction in less intense activities like gardening after her career.
  • Olympian-turned-chef Dawn Burrell transitioned to culinary school at 34 after an injury, leveraging athletic discipline and problem-solving skills in her new career.
  • Burrell's participation in 'Top Chef' demonstrated her sustained competitive spirit, applying her athletic drive to culinary excellence.

Evolving Mindset

  • Gerontologist Tracey Gendron emphasizes viewing aging as growth and increased uniqueness, encouraging focus on who one is becoming rather than past losses.
  • Carly Lloyd embraced the concept of 'evolution' for retirement, likening it to continued growth and stepping into uncomfortable situations, just as she did in her sport.
  • A positive attitude towards aging is crucial for sustained growth, as studies show embracing aging can significantly extend lifespan and redefine success.
