Yancey Strickler and Jenny Du: How do you rethink how the world works? An entrepreneur and an engineer answer | TED Intersections

Key Takeaways

  • Rethinking old systems and embracing unconventional paths can lead to significant innovation and success.
  • Addressing complex global issues, like food waste, often benefits from multidisciplinary approaches and fresh perspectives.
  • Personal reflection and identifying shared structures across diverse fields can spark new ideas and ventures.
  • Creating new legal and cultural frameworks can bridge opposing concepts and unlock collective benefit.
  • Resilience, a focus on the collective good, and seeking truth are essential for navigating systemic challenges.

Deep Dive

  • Yancey Strickler, former CEO of Kickstarter, built a career on disrupting established systems and challenging conventional institutions.
  • His work includes developing a new artist corporation structure aimed at reimagining organizational models.
  • Strickler's rural upbringing and feeling of not belonging fostered self-awareness, driving him to find his own 'weird way' of operating.

  • A new venture was prompted by the 2012 UN Food and Agriculture Organization report, stating one-third of global food production is lost or wasted annually.
  • Witnessing food abundance in California agricultural regions underscored the profound impact of this statistic, compelling founders to act.
  • Founders, who are material scientists, chemists, and engineers, investigated fundamental reasons for food decay and spoilage.
  • Their lack of prior food or agriculture experience provided a fresh perspective to identify inefficiencies and apply technical skills to extend healthy food shelf life.

  • A guest observed a shared structural similarity between punk rock DIY labels and the historical Royal Society as platforms for innovation and community building.
  • This insight stemmed from personal reflection after feeling lonely despite professional success.
  • Feedback from five project leaders validated these observations, encouraging the development of tools to help cultural projects grow and generate wealth.

  • Referencing Joseph Campbell's 'The Hero with a Thousand Faces,' the guest discussed transcendence, often locked by opposing forces.
  • The concept proposes recognizing 'opposite' structures, such as artist and corporation, and finding the space between them.
  • This approach can open doors for collective benefit and navigate grand challenges involving potential opposition or indifference from the world.

  • Yancey Strickler introduced the 'artist corporation' concept, aiming to create a new legal structure beyond existing forms like public benefit corporations.
  • This idea arose from his personal experience and understanding of the technical processes involved in creating new legal frameworks.
  • Strickler recognized opportunities for innovation within legal structures by observing how entities like public benefit corporations were established.

  • Strickler emphasized navigating challenges and collaborating with knowledgeable individuals to implement ideas like the 'artist corporation.'
  • The speakers discussed the current 'doomy' global mood, suggesting that optimism can prevail as positive elements re-emerge in historical cycles.
  • They highlighted the importance of consciously seeking truth amidst societal 'noise' rather than being passively influenced by external factors.
