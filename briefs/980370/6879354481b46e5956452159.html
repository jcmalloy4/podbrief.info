Sunday Pick: How to value friendship (w/ Rhaina Cohen) | How to Be a Better Human

Key Takeaways

  • Prioritizing friendships alongside romantic partnerships can offload expectations, foster comprehensive support, and ultimately strengthen all relationships.
  • Friendships often operate without clear societal templates, allowing for unique definitions but also making navigation and expectations more challenging than romantic bonds.
  • Societal norms frequently undervalue platonic connections, leading to unrecognized grief for lost friendships and imposing undue pressure on romantic partners.
  • Nurturing deep friendships requires intentionality, proactive support, and a willingness to celebrate these vital bonds with the same seriousness as romantic relationships.

Deep Dives

Topic 1: The Expansive Potential of Platonic Relationships

  • Rhaina Cohen's research highlights profound platonic relationships, such as legally recognized co-parents or friends providing unwavering care through serious illness, challenging traditional expectations of friendship.
  • These 'other significant others' demonstrate that friendships can occupy a space typically reserved for romantic partners, serving as a vital source of support and pushing societal boundaries.
  • The conversation explores how some friendships become central to an individual's life, fulfilling roles that defy conventional relationship categories like 'just friends' or 'more than friends'.
  • The podcast emphasizes that these deep bonds showcase the expansive capacity of platonic connections to provide care, stability, and mutual growth comparable to family or romantic ties.

Topic 2: Navigating Societal Templates and Expectations

  • Unlike romantic relationships, which often follow clear societal blueprints, friendships lack prescribed templates, offering unique flexibility but also making navigation more challenging.
  • Over-relying on a romantic partner for all emotional needs can strain that relationship, whereas undervaluing friendships limits their potential to distribute emotional labor effectively.
  • The 'trellis' metaphor illustrates how societal expectations guide our relationships; recognizing alternative friendship models can empower individuals to pursue unconventional, fulfilling paths.
  • The episode suggests that questioning why platonic friendships aren't perceived as capable of fulfilling roles like co-living or co-parenting can reveal how societal norms influence our relational expectations.

Topic 3: Cultivating and Sustaining Deep Friendships

  • Defining 'friendship fidelity' not as exclusivity but as anticipating a friend's needs and being a consistent, supportive presence through both good times and difficult periods strengthens bonds.
  • The discussion highlights the significant, often disenfranchised grief associated with the loss or transition of friendships, underscoring society's tendency to invalidate this profound emotional pain.
  • Proactively offering support to friends, rather than waiting for them to ask, and actively noticing signs of distress, can significantly deepen connections and provide crucial assistance.
  • To nurture friendships amidst political divides or logistical challenges, the episode advises celebrating these bonds with rituals, prioritizing them in life decisions like living arrangements, and viewing differences as opportunities for growth.
