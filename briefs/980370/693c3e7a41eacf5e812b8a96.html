What I learned from cooking my way across a continent | Dieuveil Malonga

Key Takeaways

  • Chef Dieuveil Malonga promotes Pan-African cuisine through extensive travel and culinary innovation.
  • He established the Culinary Innovation Village in Rwanda to train African chefs and preserve traditions.
  • African culinary traditions, often oral, emphasize learning from local communities and grandmothers.
  • Food serves as a unifying cultural force, telling stories of people and the land.

Deep Dive

  • Chef Dieuveil Malonga traveled across over 48 African countries for two years, documenting traditional dishes and flavors.
  • He learned about distinct cuisines, such as Cameroon's 'Ndole,' Nigeria's over 200 tribal dishes, and Senegal's 'yesa.'
  • Malonga emphasizes the unifying power of food, contrasting it with a lack of personal connection in European fine dining.
  • He highlights specific ingredients like Penja pepper from Cameroon, noting its unique sweetness and lemongrass notes.

  • Chef Malonga established the Culinary Innovation Village in Rwanda, a food campus and training center for aspiring chefs from across Africa.
  • The village cultivates over 100 varieties of fruits and vegetables, teaching students farming methods alongside culinary skills.
  • Malonga integrates the "grandmother school" philosophy, emphasizing learning authentic African cuisine and food stories directly from villages.
  • The on-site restaurant, Mizamalonga (Meza Malanga), offers food experiences promoting African ingredients and their origins.

  • Chef Malonga grew up in a small village near Brazzaville, Congo, connected to food and farming traditions.
  • His grandmother ran a restaurant specializing in Saka Saka, a cassava leaf dish common across various African countries.
  • Moving to Germany at age 11-12, Malonga found the local food culture (primarily meat and potatoes) a shock.
  • This contrast inspired him to begin cooking for himself at 14-15, eventually solidifying his decision to become a chef.

  • Chef Malonga's travels focused on learning farming, fermentation, and conservation methods directly from African villages.
  • Fermentation is identified as crucial for food preservation in communities lacking electricity, yielding unique flavors.
  • These traditional techniques and tastes discovered in villages significantly influence Malonga's restaurant innovations.
  • Food is described as central to daily life and celebration in many African communities, fostering communal meals and storytelling.

  • Malonga's restaurant employs a 'menu-less' approach, focusing on seasonal ingredients and culinary transformation.
  • He envisions future cuisine based on locally available products and aims to connect food with the ecosystem, addressing climate change.
  • The "Chef's Exchange" program welcomes chefs globally to learn Malonga's innovative farming and cooking methods.
  • Malonga observes changing gender dynamics in the culinary world, noting increased recognition for female chefs and emphasizing learning from women.
