The surprisingly simple reason teams fail | Tessa West

Key Takeaways

  • Miscommunication, often from "hidden languages" and assumptions, sabotages even intelligent teams.
  • Teams frequently fail to share unique, critical information, leading to poor decisions.
  • Unawareness of others' knowledge gaps complicates smart team decision-making.
  • Repeatedly stating information and encouraging questions improves communication.

Deep Dive

  • In 1999, NASA's Mars Climate Orbiter disintegrated upon entering Mars' atmosphere.
  • The failure stemmed from a communication breakdown between the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and Lockheed Martin teams.
  • One team used the metric system (Newtons) for force, while the other used imperial (pounds) for calculations.
  • This unit discrepancy led to a significant error in the orbiter's flight path, causing its loss.

  • Subsequent to the initial error, warnings were overlooked due to a missed form submission.
  • A critical phone call failed to convey urgency, highlighting how nonverbal cues can hinder communication.
  • Psychology professor Tessa West asserts such communication failures are common and result from simple oversights.
  • These failures often occur in high-stakes situations, not just due to technical issues.

  • A social science experiment involved groups hiring a candidate using both shared and unique information.
  • Researchers at the University of Southern California studied this 'hidden profile task' for over 40 years.
  • Only 20% of teams unanimously selected the best candidate when unique, critical information was not shared.
  • Teams often focus on shared knowledge, neglecting unique data, leading to poor communication without obvious red flags.

  • Specialized terms, acronyms, or 'hidden languages' can hinder communication and cause inefficiency between groups.
  • The core issue is a lack of awareness regarding what others know, from unit understanding to critical data.
  • The guest advises against assuming information is obvious; stating the obvious can be beneficial in meetings.
  • People may withhold critical information due to fear of violating social norms or misinterpretation.
