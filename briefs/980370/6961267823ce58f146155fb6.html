Sunday Pick: Why your brain is an unreliable narrator (w/ Aparna Nancherla) | How to Be a Better Human

Key Takeaways

  • Impostor syndrome is defined as persistently feeling like a fraud despite success, attributing achievements to luck.
  • The term 'impostor syndrome' has become diluted in popular culture, often used as an umbrella for inadequacy.
  • Comedian Aparna Nancherla suggests creating a 'failure resume' to normalize rejections and reduce their impact.
  • Embracing non-traditional career paths and trusting intuition can be crucial when conventional systems fall short.
  • Feelings of impostor syndrome extend beyond professional life, influencing self-perception in health and personal identity.

Deep Dive

  • Host Chris Duffy opened by describing his own feelings of inadequacy, questioning his suitability to host and interview guests.
  • Aparna Nancherla defines impostor syndrome as the persistent feeling of being a fraud undeserving of success, attributing accomplishments to luck.
  • Nancherla notes that this term, along with other 'therapy speak,' has entered mainstream usage and is often used casually.

  • Nancherla shares a current joke about 'breaking the fifth wall,' framing her ambivalence about being on stage as a personal commentary.
  • She suggests talking about impostor syndrome with others to break isolation and gather external perspectives on self-doubt.
  • Nancherla also notes that impostor syndrome can be a systemic issue, extending beyond individual feelings.

  • The guest introduced the concept of a 'failure resume,' a list of rejections and failures designed to mitigate their impact.
  • While initially a personal exercise, the idea has gained traction, with its original creator benefitting from sharing failures publicly.
  • Nancherla created her failure resume to 'de-trigger' specific rejections by removing their status as secrets and acknowledging them.

  • Nancherla has shifted her focus to controllable actions, like applying for jobs, rather than uncontrollable outcomes, such as getting hired.
  • She proactively manages self-doubt by anticipating and cataloging rejections, incorporating them into her yearly goal-setting.
  • Nancherla critiques the societal emphasis on continuous success and the 'self-made' myth, arguing it overlooks randomness and complexity.

  • The rise of the internet and platforms like early Twitter provided alternative avenues for Aparna Nancherla's comedy career, allowing her to gain notice.
  • Nancherla advises embracing an alternative path when traditional structures don't work, emphasizing trusting intuition over conforming.
  • She discusses humorous coping mechanisms for impostor syndrome, including a party game where individuals introduce made-up words to gauge social reactions.

  • The discussion highlights that impostor syndrome's implications extend beyond professional settings to areas such as body image and illness.
  • Nancherla shared an experience of questioning her own depression diagnosis, feeling her internal struggles did not align with societal perceptions of the illness.
  • She discusses the paradox of trusting one's own thoughts while also needing to question them, particularly during periods of mental distress.
  • Nancherla views her impostor syndrome as better understood and a self-preserving mechanism, considering it a part of herself rather than her entirety.
