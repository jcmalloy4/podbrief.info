How do you turn hope into action? A doctor and a public health expert answer | David Fajgenbaum and Celina de Sola

Key Takeaways

  • Sustaining progress in challenging fields requires holding both hope and grief.
  • Leveraging existing solutions, from repurposed drugs to reformed social systems, can create significant impact.
  • AI accelerates discovery of overlooked treatments, but human judgment remains critical for implementation.
  • Achieving systemic change demands deep engagement with public sector, operational personnel, and communities.
  • Effective leadership is characterized by a clear vision, diverse teams, continuous communication, humility, and curiosity.

Deep Dive

  • Host Elise Hugh introduces immunology researcher David Fajgenbaum and public health expert Celina de Sola discussing how to maintain hope.
  • Fajgenbaum and de Sola emphasize the importance of simultaneously embracing hope and grief when navigating professional setbacks.
  • This approach is highlighted as crucial for maintaining motivation and enabling continued progress and collaboration in their work.

  • Celina de Sola's organization, founded in El Salvador, focuses on creating trauma-informed systems within public schools and other institutions.
  • One police officer, after receiving trauma-informed training, improved her self-regulation and peer interactions, leading to better community engagement.
  • This trauma-informed training was integrated into national police forces in three countries, beginning with their cadet programs.

  • Celina de Sola explains that Glasswing, her organization, began by volunteering in schools nearly 20 years ago.
  • The organization evolved by actively listening to students, teachers, and parents, identifying the impact of stress and trauma on communities.
  • Glasswing has developed psychoeducation and trauma-informed curricula now integrated into teachers' colleges, medical schools, and child protection systems.

  • Immunology researcher David Fajgenbaum describes his mission to repurpose existing drugs for new uses utilizing an AI platform.
  • AI can scan global knowledge of drugs and diseases, reducing computation time for 75 million scores from 100 days to 17 hours.
  • The AI identifies potential drug-disease matches, such as lidocaine for breast cancer, by analyzing overlooked research due to profitability or career considerations.
  • Human intervention is crucial after AI identifies potential treatments, for selecting which drugs to test in the lab, move to clinical trials, and ultimately reach patients.

  • The speakers agree on the necessity of working with the public sector to reach more people, viewing frontline workers as significant resources.
  • A key strategy involves engaging with operational-level personnel, such as teachers and administrators, who provide continuity regardless of political changes.
  • Effecting systems-level change requires understanding existing systems, building diverse teams, and aligning on common goals with public partners.

  • Effective leadership involves building diverse teams, understanding individual strengths and weaknesses, and incorporating varied skill sets.
  • Leaders emphasize setting and constantly reiterating a clear vision, such as "saving and improving lives," to motivate mission-driven teams.
  • Visualization, over-communication, and intentionality in leadership are highlighted as crucial for team alignment, especially across different locations.
  • Humility and curiosity are identified as essential ingredients for tackling complex challenges and fostering successful external partnerships, particularly with government entities.
