The Dan Le Batard Show with Stugotz

GOOD FOLLOW - Sandy Brondello Fired From New York, Zero Black Women Headcoaches In The W, A’ja’s 4th MVP & More!

Key Takeaways

  • The New York Liberty surprisingly released head coach Sandy Brondello, despite her leading the team to its first championship a year prior.
  • The WNBA currently has no Black women serving as head coaches, sparking concern about representation given 80% of players are Black women.
  • A'ja Wilson won her fourth WNBA MVP award, prompting discussion on audience 'greatness fatigue' amidst her sustained dominance.
  • The Liberty organization maintains a championship-or-bust standard, deeming a first-round playoff exit unacceptable.

Deep Dive

  • New York Liberty head coach Sandy Brondello, the winningest coach in team history, was surprisingly released despite leading the team to its first championship only one year prior.
  • The decision was questioned given the team's recent finals appearances and championship win.
  • Potential reasons for dismissal included observed effort and urgency issues, uncharacteristic losses, and critiques of limited player rotations during playoffs where starters struggled.

  • The New York Liberty organization considers a first-round playoff exit unacceptable, signaling a bold, highly competitive approach.
  • This decision reflects accountability to the fanbase and city, with a championship as the sole acceptable standard.
  • The team's actions are underscored by investments in a new practice facility and pressure from ticket prices.
  • The next Liberty coaching position is high-profile, potentially attracting a prominent figure from women's or men's basketball.

  • Following the New York Liberty's coaching change and the Seattle Storm's decision not to renew Noelle Quinn's contract, the WNBA has no Black women serving as head coaches.
  • This raises concerns about representation in a league where 80% of the players are Black women.
  • Angel McCoughtry questioned the disparity, noting the dismissal of Teresa Weatherspoon from the Chicago Sky after less than a year and significantly fewer wins than her successor.

  • Ros Gold-Onwude expressed an emotional reaction to Weatherspoon's situation, emphasizing the need for support, constructive feedback, and continuity for Black women in coaching roles.
  • The Chicago Sky franchise was cited for creating a turbulent environment regarding coaching tenure.
  • The discussion highlighted the importance of allies and consistent standards within the league for all coaches.

  • The conversation addressed a perceived lack of feedback and clarity regarding expectations for Black women coaches in the WNBA.
  • Speakers emphasized the need for specific reasons when hiring or firing, especially in a league with a high percentage of Black female players.
  • The importance of fair evaluation and support for Black women in coaching positions was highlighted.

  • A'ja Wilson won her fourth WNBA MVP award, marking her second consecutive win, and also earned co-Defensive Player of the Year honors.
  • Before age 30, Wilson has achieved multiple MVPs, Defensive Player of the Year awards, and championships.
  • Her statistical dominance includes near 50-40-90 shooting splits and significant impact during the Aces' challenging season.
  • The hosts debated the concept of 'greatness fatigue' among audiences and media, emphasizing the need to appreciate Wilson's historic achievements.
