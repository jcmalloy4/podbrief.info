The Dan Le Batard Show with Stugotz

Local Hour: The U Is Back (For Real This Time)

Key Takeaways

  • The University of Miami football team's resurgence and championship aspirations were a central topic.
  • The Miami Dolphins' organizational disarray and uncertain quarterback future were critically examined.
  • Michael Irvin's post-game simulation of child abuse by beating a cooler was discussed as a new college football tradition.
  • NFL playoff selection and team performances, including the Cleveland Browns' coaching change, drew criticism.

Deep Dive

  • Hosts acknowledged a two-week absence and audience dissatisfaction.
  • Discussion transitioned from a 'Cyclones' decision to the University of Miami's football team.
  • Miami football is perceived as championship-worthy, contrasting with struggles of the Miami Dolphins and New England Patriots.
  • The team's victory over Ohio State and capability to beat any opponent are highlighted.

  • The Miami Dolphins are described as being in disarray, lacking leadership and a clear quarterback solution.
  • Criticism was directed at Troy Aikman's consulting role, deemed of questionable value.
  • The team faces despair with no long-term quarterback solution and a significant cap hit.
  • Tua Tagovailoa's potential departure is discussed, alongside optimism for Ty Simpson's potential.

  • The University of Miami football team is noted for its potential to set a new standard, referencing NIL.
  • Their upcoming game against Mississippi is discussed following the Georgia-Mississippi game.
  • Mississippi's quarterback is highlighted for efficiency and lack of turnovers.
  • Playoff games, excluding Georgia-Mississippi, were considered poor entertainment.

  • Michael Irvin's post-game actions involve simulating child abuse by beating a cooler with an Ohio State jersey.
  • These actions are compared to college traditions like Clemson's 'running down the hill' and Iowa's 'waving at a children's hospital.'
  • Irvin's behavior is suggested to be a new notable tradition for the Miami team.

  • The hosts debate Miami's preferred playoff opponent, considering a team led by Trinidad Chambliss or Kirby Smart's Georgia.
  • Kirby Smart's coaching acumen and preparation time are highlighted as factors.
  • Ole Miss's coaching capabilities under Pete Golding are questioned due to significant staff turnover.
  • Ole Miss quarterback Trinidad Chambliss is compared to Vince Young and Cam Newton for his ability to extend plays.

  • Miami's quarterback is highlighted as the best opponent faced in two years by some teams.
  • Louisville's game plan against Miami involved drop blocking and quick passes to counter Ruben Bain.
  • Miami's success is attributed to a conservative offense that trusts its defense.
  • The team's ability to lead against top-10 opponents is noted, but performance when trailing remains a question.

  • A mid-season hiccup was suggested to be beneficial for Miami, teaching the team to adapt to different game paces.
  • Concerns were raised about Miami's conservative offensive style potentially struggling against fast-paced ACC teams.
  • The discussion touched on the introduction of tempo and potential defensive challenges for Miami.
  • Michael Irvin's unusual sideline noises were linked to a perceived 'Miami style' of aggressive play.

  • Recent Miami Hurricanes football games are deemed highly significant, surpassing previous ones.
  • A key pick-six play by Keontae Scott against Ohio State is placed into historical context of memorable Hurricanes moments.
  • Keontae Scott's remarkable performance is highlighted despite suffering from turf toe.
  • Mario Cristobal's role in representing Cuban Miami and building a program focused on physicality is noted.
