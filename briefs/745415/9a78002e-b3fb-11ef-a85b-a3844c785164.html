Local Hour: The Wemby Stopper

Key Takeaways

  • Greg Cody shared facts on human skin weight and intestine length.
  • Florida Panthers demonstrate strong depth despite key player injuries.
  • Aleksander Barkov's injury return timeline and salary cap implications are debated.
  • Kel'el Ware's NBA potential is a central discussion, with various player comparisons.
  • The Miami Heat's player development strategies were highlighted.

Deep Dive

  • Greg Cody stated human skin weighs approximately 20 pounds, which he likened to two bowling balls.
  • Discussion followed regarding whether skin weight varies by body size or fat content, and confirmed the small intestine as longer than the large intestine.
  • The conversation about skin weight began at approximately 1 minute and 32 seconds into the episode.

  • Debate arose over Aleksander Barkov's return timeline, with one speaker believing he is out for the season.
  • Video evidence showed Barkov weight-bearing and rehabbing without crutches, suggesting a possible return within three and a half months.
  • The discussion included 'reckless speculation' questioning the rapid progress of Barkov's recovery from a significant injury.

  • The Florida Panthers' salary cap situation implies they anticipate Aleksander Barkov's return, as full cap relief was not sought.
  • Skepticism was expressed regarding a video showing Barkov without a knee brace, arguing it lacked definitive information about his five-month return timeline.
  • The group debated who held more reliable information on Barkov's return: journalists or individuals close to the situation like coach Spolstra.
  • Coach Spolstra's opinion suggested Barkov would return sooner than anticipated, with hope for a quick recovery.

  • Udonis Haslem advised Kel'el Ware on rebounding, instructing him to study Mitchell Robinson's offensive rebounding technique.
  • Haslem also engaged in a public exchange with actor Ben Stiller regarding the Knicks and the Heat, referencing 'Something About Mary'.
  • Ware reportedly showed improved rebounding and performance, acting as the best player on the floor following Haslem's coaching.

  • Experts debated the potential of NBA player Kel'el Ware, assessing his current performance against Carl Anthony Towns.
  • Ware's potential was compared to historical players like Hassan Whiteside, noting his recent improvement in minutes played and crucial in-game contributions.
  • The discussion highlighted the importance of consistency for Ware and questioned his ability to maintain his current level of play.

  • The discussion explored the hypothetical combination of Draymond Green's basketball IQ with Kel'el Ware's physical attributes to create an elite player.
  • Ware's potential was compared to Victor Wembanyama due to physical attributes, and to Rudy Gobert and JaVale McGee, examining his speed, dribbling, and dunking ability.
  • The conversation emphasized the mental aspects of consistent performance, potential adaptation by opponents, and Ware's appetite for learning the game, noting his recent streak of double-doubles.

  • The concept of Kel'el Ware's "ceiling" was debated, with initial thoughts suggesting being a "pivotal part" of a championship team, even with limited minutes, represents high potential.
  • Comparisons were made to Hassan Whiteside and JaVale McGee, noting McGee's three NBA championships despite a history of inconsistent play.
  • The discussion highlighted the importance of mental preparation and continuous learning for Ware to achieve a high ceiling, contrasting him with players who wasted talent.

  • The Miami Heat's history of developing players was discussed, referencing figures like Ike Austin, Tim Hardaway, and Alonzo Mourning.
  • A debate ensued regarding whether these players were acquired due to attitude issues or physical limitations, with differing perspectives on how the Heat helped them succeed.
  • Former Heat player Kevin Willis and his 'alligator arms' were mentioned, discussing how his physical limitations might have impacted his game.
