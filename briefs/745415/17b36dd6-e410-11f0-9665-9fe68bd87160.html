The Dan Le Batard Show with Stugotz

Best of DLS: We Have to Wrap, Tony

Key Takeaways

  • Former NFL player Spice Adams debated pizza crust orientation and discussed his entertainment career.
  • His interview concluded abruptly with an unidentified individual, later confirmed as 'Tony,' demanding its end.
  • Hosts debated the interruption's identity, initially confusing the voice with David Samson's, and expressed frustration.
  • Later discussions analyzed NFL playoff scenarios, team performance, and the role of turnovers in game outcomes.
  • The episode also covered sports trivia, idiomatic expressions, and the repeatability of defensive performance.

Deep Dive

  • Former NFL player Spice Adams, presented as an expert by Red Baron Pizza, debated if a walking pizza's crust or pointy end would be at the bottom.
  • Adams stated the crust would be the bottom; hosts considered a poll for listener input on the topic.
  • Adams explained his transition to entertainment after football, encouraged by former teammates Earl Bennett and Chris Harris.

  • The guest discussed a viral meme of him rubbing his hands, which he recognized from a Red Baron pizza advertisement.
  • This led to a promotional segment for a 'humble pie' pizza with multiple spicy ingredients.
  • The special pizza is available on redbaron.com until December 15th.

  • Hosts debated the identity of the interrupting voice, initially mistaking it for David Samson due to vocal similarities.
  • The interrupting individual's name was revealed as Anthony, which Spice Adams confirmed was his actual first name, also known as Tony.
  • A host expressed frustration over the interruption's timing, the aggressive tone, and the guest's promotional activities.

  • Hosts reflected on the abrupt interview ending, noting an insistent voice stating, 'We have to rap.'
  • They questioned if the interrupting voice, compared to David Samson, sounded like an 'old woman.'
  • One host expressed anger at producers for the sudden conclusion, citing a missed discussion opportunity.

  • A discussion on the Clippers revealed their announcers were distracted during a game, engaging in a trivia debate.
  • A host posed a trivia question leading to debate about its fairness and whether it constituted an ambush.
  • Confusion over a 'duo' vs. 'trio' was resolved by clarifying three Berry brothers played for the same team.
  • Hosts debated the meaning and usage of idiomatic expressions like 'put a button on it' and 'put a bow on it.'

  • Hosts discussed the Dallas Cowboys' upcoming game against Detroit, identifying it as their last difficult opponent.
  • They debated potential playoff scenarios for the Cowboys and Detroit Lions, referencing betting lines.
  • The Philadelphia Eagles were mentioned as favorites against the Chargers, citing Justin Herbert's injury.

  • The disparity in strength between the AFC and NFC was highlighted, with the San Francisco 49ers' seeding discussed.
  • The Carolina Panthers' potential to win their division despite perceived weakness was analyzed, crediting turnovers for a win against the Rams.
  • Hosts debated whether turnovers are largely luck or a repeatable skill, unless supported by a strong pass rush.

  • Discussion questioned the repeatability of defensive performance, specifically forced turnovers, from one season to the next.
  • Historical examples like the Dallas Cowboys' defensive drop-off were cited to argue that turnovers are random.
  • Despite randomness and unpredictability in football, the importance of preparation and strategizing was emphasized.
  • The conversation included references to the Puka Nacua catch and a quarterback named Brosmer.
