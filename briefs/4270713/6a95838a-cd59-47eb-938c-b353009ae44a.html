Bloomberg Daybreak: US Edition

ICE in Chicago and Trump-Epstein Letter; Traders Await Labor Revisions

Key Takeaways

  • Trump administration targets Chicago with "Operation Midway Blitz" immigration crackdown.
  • House Democrats release alleged 2003 Trump-Epstein birthday note, raising pressure.
  • US markets await key labor and inflation data, influencing Federal Reserve rate cut outlook.
  • Apple launches new iPhones, watches, and Vision Pro headset; stock is down 5% year-to-date.
  • Israel orders 1 million Gaza City residents to evacuate before a potential major offensive.

Deep Dive

  • Operation Midway Blitz targets "criminal illegal aliens" in Democratic-run cities.
  • The Department of Homeland Security blames Illinois Governor JB Pritzker's sanctuary policies for shielding lawbreakers.
  • Enforcement operations are set to continue daily to ensure safety.
  • This initiative is seen as politically advantageous for President Trump.

  • Israel has ordered Gaza City's 1 million residents to evacuate ahead of a potential major military offensive.
  • Former Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu issued a warning for residents to leave the city.

  • U.S. markets are nearing record levels for the S&P 500, with investors focusing on upcoming labor market and inflation data.
  • The Labor Department is set to release a benchmark payrolls revision, with economists anticipating a significant downward adjustment, potentially reducing the March jobs count by nearly 1 million.
  • A weaker job market may prompt the Federal Reserve to cut rates next week.
  • Bloomberg Economics anticipates a hot headline Consumer Price Index, potentially curbing enthusiasm for a larger rate cut.

  • Investors are focused on Apple's product launch event today, where new iPhone, Apple Watch, and Vision Pro headset models are expected.
  • Growth from these updates may not be sufficient to boost the company's performance.
  • Apple stock is down 5% year-to-date.

  • Doctors at Mass General in Boston have successfully transplanted pig kidneys into three human patients.
  • The FDA has approved a clinical trial for this development.
  • This breakthrough could potentially reduce wait times for organ transplants.
