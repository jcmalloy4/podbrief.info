Bloomberg Daybreak: US Edition

Instant Reaction: Jay Powell's Jackson Hole Speech

Key Takeaways

  • Fed Chair Powell opened the door to a September rate cut, citing labor market risks.
  • The Fed's policy reflects a cautious balance between employment and inflation concerns.
  • Market confidence in the Fed's independence is crucial for currency and yields.
  • Wall Street reacted positively, with equities surging and bond yields falling post-Powell's speech.
  • Tariffs generate substantial revenue and indicate a new era of global trade fragmentation.

Deep Dive

  • Former St. Louis Fed President James Bullard stated that Fed Chair Jay Powell's Jackson Hole remarks solidified expectations for a 25 basis point rate cut in September.
  • Bullard's assessment was influenced by a recent soft labor market report.
  • He maintains his preference for 100 basis points in rate cuts by 2026, advocating for a slow, data-driven adjustment.
  • Bullard noted the difficulty of addressing income and wealth disparity with broad interest rate policy changes.

  • The host highlighted headlines about former President Trump's threat to fire Fed Governor Lisa Cook and comments on Fed Chair Powell's rate remarks.
  • Former St. Louis Fed President Jim Bullard emphasized the importance of due process for such charges, expressing concern over a "Wild West" scenario.
  • Bullard warned that significant rate cuts could lead to rising long-end yields if markets lose confidence in the Fed's credibility.
  • He advocated for replacing the "dot plot" with a quarterly monetary policy report and forecast to improve communication.

  • Wall Street reacted positively to potential rate cuts, with equities surging and the Russell 2000 leading gains.
  • 10-year yields fell by approximately six basis points, and front-end yields dropped by 10 basis points.
  • Citi Wealth CIO Kate Moore cautioned that despite improved earnings ratios, current market positioning in crowded segments could lead to weakness.
  • Moore highlighted a "K-shape" recovery, indicating disparate growth across industries and consumer groups.

  • The labor market exhibits a "curious balance" due to slowing worker supply and demand, potentially increasing downside risks to employment.
  • Former St. Louis Fed President Jim Bullard noted the "fungibility" of tariffs, indicating a more settled trade situation for markets.
  • Citi Wealth CIO Kate Moore observed that markets have "looked through" the inflationary impact of tariffs throughout the summer.
  • Moore emphasized the ongoing importance and sticky nature of "sectoral tariffs" for overall earnings.

  • Former Fed Vice Chair Rich Clarida concluded that Fed Chair Powell "opened the door pretty wide" for a September interest-rate cut.
  • Powell's remarks emphasized a weaker outlook for the labor market, shifting the "balance of risk."
  • The Fed is balancing its dual mandate, with a focus on avoiding recession despite a stable unemployment rate and weak private sector job growth.
  • Clarida noted Powell's speech reinforced central banking independence by focusing on the Fed's congressional mandate.

  • The dovish stance of Fed Chair Jay Powell contrasts with the inflation-focused approach of German central bank's Joachim Nagel, creating currency implications.
  • PIMCO highlighted valuation divergences between US and European equity markets, noting Europe is closer to its 2% inflation target.
  • Former Fed Vice Chair Richard Clarida stated Trump tariffs generate substantial revenue, potentially "$200 or $300 billion a year."
  • Clarida noted a significant shift towards a fragmented global trading system, moving away from 30 years of globalization.
