Bloomberg Daybreak: US Edition

Instant Reaction: Trump Says US to ‘Run Venezuela’ in Interim After Maduro

Key Takeaways

  • U.S. forces captured Venezuelan leader Nicolas Maduro in "Mission Absolute Resolve."
  • President Trump stated the U.S. would administer Venezuela post-capture, deploying U.S. oil companies.
  • Congressional members questioned the operation's legality and lack of briefings from the administration.
  • Experts critically analyzed the administration's justifications, citing problematic overdose data connections.
  • The intervention is being framed with an "America First" emphasis on U.S. energy interests.

Deep Dive

  • Congressman Brad Schneider (D-Illinois) questioned the lack of congressional briefings following the Venezuela operation.
  • Schneider emphasized Congress's constitutional authority to declare war and authorize military force.
  • He asserted President Trump lacks blanket authority for intervention without Congressional authorization, contrasting the situation with the 1989 U.S. invasion of Panama.
  • Schneider criticized the administration for usurping congressional authority in war powers and other areas.

  • The possibility was raised that legal justification and a clear strategy for a swift transition might temper congressional opposition.
  • President Trump's stated intention to use Venezuelan oil revenue for U.S. personal benefit or reimbursement is a point of contention.
  • Congressman Schneider believes some Republican colleagues will voice concerns about the administration's actions in Venezuela.
  • Concerns were raised regarding the potential for U.S. troop deployment and congressional notification.

  • Justin Logan, Director of Defense and Foreign Policy Studies at the Cato Institute, questioned the military standpoint of the operations in Venezuela.
  • Logan critiqued the administration's justification, citing a discrepancy in overdose statistics and dubious connections between Venezuelan cocaine and U.S. overdose deaths.
  • He noted a blurring of rationales, mixing law enforcement concerns with national security concerns for the Venezuela policy.
  • Logan also questioned the political viability of President Trump's statement about protecting American oil companies' revenue streams in Venezuela.

  • Nathan Hager reported on President Trump's statement that the U.S. is running Venezuela following airstrikes and the capture of Nicolas Maduro and his wife.
  • Maduro and his wife are reportedly en route to New York to face charges.
  • President Trump stated the U.S. will manage Venezuela until a "safe, proper and judicious transition" is organized, indicating U.S. oil companies would be deployed.
  • Trump also asserted that opposition leader Maria Corina Machado lacks the necessary support to lead Venezuela.

  • A U.S. operation, codenamed "Mission Absolute Resolve," resulted in the capture of Venezuelan leader Nicolas Maduro.
  • President Trump announced the U.S. would manage Venezuela until a "safe, proper and judicious transition" is organized.
  • Trump indicated U.S. oil companies would be deployed to the country, though the embargo "on all Venezuelan oil remains in full effect."
  • The administration of Venezuela by the U.S. was suggested to last for approximately one year, covering costs and reimbursing damages.

  • Panelists discussed the "America First" aspect of the intervention, noting the president framed the action to resonate with his base.
  • The strategy emphasizes U.S. interest in Venezuelan oil and energy independence.
  • This framing is seen as a direct response to potential criticism regarding the intervention.
  • Discussions included the political analysis of whether the action was justified and the logistics and legality of the U.S. administration.
