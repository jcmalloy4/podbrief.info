Bloomberg Daybreak: US Edition

Instant Reaction: Tesla Earnings Miss Despite A Sales Surge

Key Takeaways

  • Tesla's Q3 profit missed Wall Street expectations despite record electric vehicle sales.
  • Shifting federal policies, rising costs, and tariffs negatively impacted Tesla's earnings.
  • Tesla is investing in future technologies like humanoid robots and new assembly lines.
  • Investor sentiment on Tesla is mixed, with some reducing holdings due to valuation and FSD viability concerns.
  • Tesla's Full Self-Driving (FSD) strategy faces questions on revenue, competition, and safety.

Deep Dive

  • Tesla's third-quarter adjusted earnings were 50 cents per share, falling short of analysts' average expectation of 54 cents.
  • The earnings shortfall occurred despite the company achieving record electric vehicle sales during the period.
  • Tesla's free cash flow for the third quarter was $3.99 billion, significantly exceeding the estimated $1.25 billion.
  • Promotional activities and discounting were utilized to clear inventory, influenced by the expiration of federal EV tax credits.

  • Tesla's bottom line was impacted by various factors including tariffs, fiscal policy, R&D costs, and product mix.
  • Costs associated with installing new assembly lines, such as for the Optimus humanoid robot, affected financial results.
  • Company filings indicate a transition period influenced by changing macroeconomic and fiscal policies, necessitating investment in future technologies.
  • Tariffs were identified as a direct negative impact on Tesla's profits across both its EV and energy storage businesses.
  • Record deliveries and higher-volume energy products in the third quarter lacked current U.S. policy support.

  • Ross Gerber, CEO of Gerber Kawasaki Wealth and Investment Management, managing over $3 billion, discussed his firm's reduced Tesla position.
  • Gerber Kawasaki is not recommending new Tesla purchases due to premium valuation, categorizing the stock as a 'hold'.
  • A commentator expressed skepticism regarding the viability of Tesla's Full Self-Driving (FSD) and vision-only systems.
  • Personal excitement for Tesla's future is contingent on FSD reliably enabling autonomous commutes.

  • Tesla cited lower Full Self-Driving (FSD) revenue recognition due to previous generation releases.
  • The market is divided on Tesla's strategy of transitioning from selling driver-assist software to operating a proprietary robo-taxi service.
  • Building a ride-hailing service is characterized as difficult and expensive, citing Uber's decade-long struggle.
  • Significant competition from existing services like Uber, Lyft, and Waymo presents challenges for Tesla's robo-taxi ambitions.
  • Bloomberg Tech Co-Host Ed Ludlow stated he would not be comfortable letting his car drive itself with his child, despite tracking FSD's generational improvements.

  • Tesla's Full Self-Driving (FSD) technology relies on a vision-only system, using cameras to gather real-world data.
  • The company explains that real-world data from current FSD use helps improve the future robo-taxi version.
  • Competitors like Waymo employ multiple sensors, including LiDAR and radar, for enhanced redundancy and safety.
  • Tesla argues its sensor-limited approach offers greater scalability and affordability for widespread adoption.
  • Rival firms emphasize the critical need for sensor redundancy to ensure safety in autonomous driving systems.
