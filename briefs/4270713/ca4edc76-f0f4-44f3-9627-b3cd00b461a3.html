Bloomberg Daybreak: US Edition

Walz Clashes With Noem Over ICE Shooting Probe; Trump’s Tariffs Face Supreme Court Test

Key Takeaways

  • A federal investigation into an ICE-involved shooting in Minneapolis is facing state objections over impartiality concerns.
  • The US Supreme Court is expected to rule on the legality of President Trump's tariffs, potentially marking a significant legal defeat.
  • Rio Tinto is reportedly in talks to acquire Glencore, aiming to create the world's largest mining company.
  • The White House is preparing "Plan B" tariff authorities should the Supreme Court rule against President Trump's current levies.
  • Experts detail specific alternative legal frameworks for implementing tariffs if the current emergency powers are invalidated.

Deep Dive

  • The FBI took control of the investigation into a US citizen's killing by an ICE agent in Minneapolis, overriding state objections.
  • Minnesota Governor Tim Walz questioned the federal probe's impartiality, citing Trump administration officials' comments that seemed to exonerate the officer.
  • State authorities were initially invited to participate but later denied access to evidence or interviews by federal officials.
  • Separately, in Portland, a border patrol agent fired a defensive shot after a vehicle was driven at them, resulting in two individuals sustaining gunshot wounds.

  • The Supreme Court is expected to issue a ruling on the legality of President Trump's tariffs, potentially as soon as today.
  • Lower courts previously ruled in 2025 that these levies, imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, were issued illegally.
  • The tariffs in question range from 10% to 50% on most imports and could face a significant legal defeat for the administration.

  • Rio Tinto is reportedly in talks to acquire Glencore, a deal that would create the world's largest mining company.
  • The potential tie-up is estimated to have a market value exceeding $200 billion.
  • If successful, this would represent the largest deal in mining history.

  • White House Trade Advisor Peter Navarro confirmed that a "Plan B" exists should the Supreme Court rule against President Trump's emergency tariffs.
  • The Supreme Court may rule today on these tariffs, which lower courts previously found illegal.
  • Potential alternative authorities for implementing tariffs include Section 122, 301, or 232.

  • Bloomberg News Global Trade Editor Brendan Murray explained that Section 232 and 301 tariff authorities are legally tested.
  • Section 122, while less used, is considered a viable alternative by legal experts and lower courts.
  • White House officials, including Peter Navarro, indicated that alternative tariff authorities would be implemented within days if the president loses the Supreme Court case.
