Bloomberg Daybreak: US Edition

Instant Reaction: Amazon Boosts Spending Far Ahead of Estimates

Key Takeaways

  • Amazon's fourth-quarter EPS of $1.95 missed estimates, with first-quarter operating income forecasts also below expectations.
  • The company announced a significant increase in capital expenditures, projecting $200 billion for 2026, far exceeding analyst estimates.
  • Investor concerns over the return on investment for Amazon's massive AI and infrastructure bets led to a notable drop in share price.
  • Despite CapEx concerns, Amazon reported solid Q4 net sales and strong growth in AWS and advertising segments.

Deep Dive

  • Amazon reported fourth-quarter EPS of $1.95, a penny below estimates, with an operating margin of 11.7%, in line with expectations.
  • For the first quarter, Amazon forecasts net sales between $173.5 billion and $178.5 billion, slightly below estimates of $175.54 billion.
  • Operating income for the first quarter is projected at $16.5 billion to $21.5 billion, falling short of the $22.24 billion expected by analysts.
  • Following the earnings report, Amazon shares dropped approximately 4.4% in after-hours trading.

  • Amazon projects $200 billion in capital expenditures for 2026, significantly exceeding the street estimate of $146.11 billion.
  • This increased spending targets areas such as AI chips, robotics, and low-Earth orbit satellites.
  • The CapEx plan, combined with a potentially lower first-quarter net sales forecast, caused Amazon's shares to drop over 10% in after-hours trading.
  • Despite strong Q4 net sales of $213.39 billion and solid AWS (24% growth) and advertising (22% growth) performance, investor sentiment was negatively impacted.

  • Amazon's stock, which had fallen as much as 11% post-market, was down approximately 7.5% as investors awaited further clarity.
  • Investors seek more insight into the allocation of the $200 billion 2026 capital expenditure, AWS backlog, and AI integration into consumer workflows.
  • Amazon's advertising business demonstrated 22% growth, noted as admirable and important for funneling retail sales.
  • This segment, with its high profit margins, is highlighted as funding retail growth, including in grocery.

  • Significant capital expenditures by hyperscalers like Microsoft, Alphabet, and Amazon on cloud and AI infrastructure are raising questions about the return on investment (ROI).
  • The market is now re-evaluating financial performance, free cash flow, and return on spend for these massive infrastructure investments.
  • New AWS agreements with major companies, including OpenAI, Visa, NBA, and Salesforce, suggest a trend of businesses increasingly tapping into Amazon's cloud infrastructure.

  • James Cakmak of Clockwise Capital states Amazon's $200 billion capital expenditure will primarily benefit the AI ecosystem, focusing on semiconductor scarcity like memory and manufacturing.
  • Cakmak identifies Intel and Micron as strategic holdings due to their position in areas of scarcity and pricing power.
  • AWS is actively transforming its data centers for AI environments and developing its own chips and accelerators to increase platform utilization and customer stickiness.

  • James Cakmak's investment strategy includes increasing positions in semiconductors, aerospace, defense, utilities, and staples.
  • He views Bitcoin's significant decline as an indicator of high leverage in the financial system that is currently unwinding.
  • Cakmak emphasizes focusing on companies with pricing power within the broader market context.
