Bloomberg Daybreak: US Edition

Judge Blocks Trump Bid to Fire Cook; President Trump Sues NY Times

Key Takeaways

  • An appeals court temporarily blocked Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook's dismissal by President Trump, citing due process.
  • President Trump has filed a $15 billion defamation lawsuit against The New York Times.
  • The Securities and Exchange Commission is prioritizing a proposal to reduce corporate earnings reports frequency.
  • Stephen Myron received Senate confirmation to the Federal Reserve Board despite concerns about his independence.

Deep Dive

  • An appeals court blocked the White House from removing Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook, allowing her to continue her duties.
  • The 2-1 ruling cited President Trump's social media attempt to fire her, suggesting due process rights violations.
  • This decision occurred just hours before the Federal Reserve's meeting to vote on interest rates.
  • President Trump retains the option to appeal the ruling to the Supreme Court.

  • President Trump has filed a $15 billion defamation and libel lawsuit against The New York Times in Florida.
  • Trump accuses the publication of acting as a "mouthpiece" for the Democratic Party.
  • He cited its endorsement of former presidential candidate Kamala Harris among his concerns.
  • This suit follows previous legal actions against media companies including Dow Jones, ABC, and Paramount Global.

  • President Trump is considering sending National Guard troops to Memphis, stating an obligation to prevent cities from being "destroyed by savagery."
  • Local officials and Congressman Steve Cohen argue that federal agents skilled in policing methods would be more beneficial.
  • The proposed deployment aims to address ongoing public safety concerns in cities.

  • Stephen Myron received Senate confirmation to the Federal Reserve Board.
  • His confirmation came despite Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer's warnings regarding his independence and economic understanding.
  • Bloomberg's Valerie Titel noted Myron's rapid confirmation and potential influence on upcoming interest rate decisions.
  • Myron has previously voiced criticisms of tariffs and expressed concerns about inflation.

  • The appeals court ruling on Lisa Cook's dismissal is significant as it marks the first presidential attempt to remove a Fed governor.
  • The case raises critical questions about the legal definition of "cause" for dismissing a Federal Reserve official.
  • This legal precedent could influence future political interactions and potential interventions with the Federal Reserve's independence.
