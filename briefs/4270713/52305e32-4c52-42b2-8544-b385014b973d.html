Bloomberg Daybreak: US Edition

Instant Reaction: Jay Powell on the Fed Decision

Key Takeaways

  • Fed Chair Powell indicated a December interest rate cut is not guaranteed.
  • Markets reacted negatively to Powell's remarks on policy uncertainty.
  • Dissenting opinions within the Fed committee are challenging consensus decisions.
  • Economists are surprised by the lack of expected stagflationary shocks.

Deep Dive

  • Equities and bond markets reacted negatively after Federal Reserve Chair Powell's remarks on a December interest rate cut.
  • The S&P 500 fell 0.2%, while small caps dropped 0.8% following the policy decision.
  • Treasury yields increased, with the 10-year yield up 7-8 basis points.

  • Federal Reserve Chair Powell's post-meeting comments lacked clear forward guidance, emphasizing data dependency for future policy.
  • Analysts questioned the rationale for a rate cut given current PCE at 2.8% and GDP tracking around 3%.
  • The Fed appears to be recalibrating policy based on academic concepts like 'neutral rate' and 'output gap'.

  • The role of the Federal Reserve chair has evolved from unilateral authority, exemplified by Alan Greenspan, to a more collegial, consensus-driven approach under recent chairs.
  • Commentators suggest Chair Powell may be losing his grip on the committee due to unusual dissenting opinions on interest rate policy.
  • Speculation arises about Powell's potential waning influence as a 'lame duck' chair if a replacement is known ahead of the December meeting.

  • Jeffrey Rosenberg of BlackRock identified Chair Powell's comments on market pricing and 'driving in the fog' as a key takeaway.
  • Market expectations for a December rate cut are being repriced due to increased awareness of two-sided risks and dissent within the committee.
  • The Fed is described as reactive to tightening financial conditions but reportedly does not factor them into rate cut decisions.

  • Economists expressed surprise at the absence of expected stagflationary shocks and downside GDP pressure following earlier forecasts.
  • The wealth effect is playing a significant role in supporting surprisingly strong consumption, contrasting with expected inflationary pressures.
  • The dual nature of the wealth effect is highlighted as a potential concern if financial conditions were to tighten.
