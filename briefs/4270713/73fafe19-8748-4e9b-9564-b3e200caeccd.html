Bloomberg Daybreak: US Edition

Instant Reaction: Trump Nominates Warsh for Fed Chair

Key Takeaways

  • President Trump nominated Kevin Warsh as the next Federal Reserve chairman via Truth Social.
  • Warsh previously advised Trump and aligns with desires for lower interest rates.
  • Markets reacted with initial falling stocks and shifting dollar movements, later showing recovery.
  • Warsh faces Senate confirmation hurdles and questions regarding his policy stance.

Deep Dive

  • President Donald Trump nominated former Fed Governor Kevin Warsh as the next Federal Reserve chairman on Truth Social.
  • Warsh is slated to succeed Jerome Powell when his term concludes in May.
  • He previously advised Trump on economic policy and his family are notable Republican donors.
  • Warsh's recent public statements advocating faster growth with lower inflation, attributed to AI and deregulation, align with the President's preference for low interest rates.

  • Tom Keene and Paul Sweeney compared Warsh's strong views and confidence to Jerome Powell's more soft-spoken approach and lobbying skill.
  • Warsh was previously passed over for the top job by President Trump in 2017.
  • Powell's term as chair ends on May 23rd, with discussions about potential quick Senate confirmation for Warsh.
  • The Senate could quickly confirm Warsh, potentially resolving the Justice Department's subpoena of Fed records.

  • Initial market reaction included falling stocks and a slight rise in treasury yields following the announcement.
  • The dollar initially found a bid, with Warsh perceived as more hawkish than other potential nominees, impacting gold prices.
  • Later, the dollar's weakness provided a boost to gold, and S&P futures recovered from session lows.
  • Markets appeared to take the news in stride, suggesting it was largely anticipated as a "sell the rumor, pair back the news" event.

  • Questions persist regarding Kevin Warsh's potential dovishness and alignment with President Trump's low interest rate stance, contrasting his reputation as an inflation hawk.
  • Warsh faces potential Senate confirmation hurdles, including Senator Tom Tillis's stance, which is tied to the Jay Powell investigation.
  • Concerns about the president's influence on the Federal Reserve's traditional insulation from elected officials have been raised.
  • Ahead of the market open, NASDAQ futures were down half a percent, reflecting pre-market jitters.
