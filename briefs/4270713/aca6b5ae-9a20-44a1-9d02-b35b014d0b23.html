Bloomberg Daybreak: US Edition

Instant Reaction: Jay Powell on the Fed Decision

Key Takeaways

  • Federal Reserve cut rates by 25 basis points; more reductions projected.
  • Fed Chair Powell cited labor market weakness for policy decision.
  • Committee deeply divided on future rate path, causing market uncertainty.
  • Inflation target credibility faces scrutiny amid persistent delays.
  • Monetary policy impacts low-income households disproportionately.
  • Future Fed leadership and political influences may sway policy.

Deep Dive

  • The Federal Reserve lowered its benchmark interest rate by a quarter percentage point.
  • Former New York Fed President Bill Dudley characterized the cut as a risk management move due to increased downside risk in the labor market.
  • The Fed's Summary of Economic Projections revealed a split committee, with nine members favoring one or no further cuts and nine favoring two more.
  • Analysts noted a 'political dot' projecting lower rates for 2026, contributing to market confusion and signaling a potential end to clear forward guidance.

  • The Federal Reserve's rate cut was described as an "insurance rate cut," not a strong predictor of future policy.
  • Concerns were raised about inflation, referencing rising sewage rates in East Hampton, Massachusetts.
  • Fed actions are seen as negatively impacting low-income households due to their inability to benefit from stock market gains and vulnerability to higher tariffs.
  • Monetary policy is viewed as a blunt instrument, raising questions about the Fed's mandate and its equitable impact.

  • The credibility of the Fed's 2% inflation target is questioned, as projections for achieving it are consistently pushed back.
  • Concerns were raised about inflation expectations becoming unanchored if progress is not made towards the 2% target.
  • BlackRock's Jeff Rosenberg explained that if the Fed loses credibility on its inflation target, it could lead to higher long-term interest rates and increased inflation risk premiums.
  • The Federal Reserve faces an "uncomfortable, unfamiliar position" as inflation persists above target while the labor market weakens.

  • The Federal Reserve committee is described as divided, with Chair Powell attempting to convey uncertainty about future policy without explicitly stating it.
  • The dot plot provided less clear guidance than in previous meetings, creating tension between rate cut projections and economic forecasts.
  • Discussion raised the potential for future Fed appointments to be politically motivated, aiming to push rates lower regardless of economic justification.
  • Chair Powell's communication strategy was noted for its market credibility, navigating contradictory economic signals while projecting a need for more rate cuts.

  • Jeff Rosenberg of BlackRock noted the bond market validated earlier critiques regarding labor market data and aligned with the Fed's outlook for year-end.
  • A disconnect emerged between the Fed's forward-looking 2026-2027 projections and bond market pricing.
  • Statistical uncertainty in Fed projections highlights a 'no risk-free path' trade-off between pursuing maximum employment and combating inflation.
  • The limited dissent at the meeting was seen as validating a shift in risk assessment, aligning near-term market expectations.

  • Speculation arose about potential appointments of Chris Waller or Governor Myron as Fed chair affecting monetary policy and financial markets, including yield curve control.
  • A central bank prioritizing the labor market over inflation could lead to higher risk premiums on long-term bond yields.
  • Market participants need to see actual policy actions rather than just potential innovations to price them accurately.
  • The possibility of the Fed being 'overcome by events' was raised, especially if economic data evolves and the relative trade-off between inflation and labor markets shifts.
