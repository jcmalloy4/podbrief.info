Invest Like the Best with Patrick O'Shaughnessy

Justin Ishbia - Lessons from Acquiring 586 Companies [Invest Like the Best, REPLAY]

Key Takeaways

  • Shore Capital specializes in microcap private equity, executing hundreds of acquisitions through a system-driven approach.
  • The firm's competitive advantage stems from high transaction volume and operational excellence, yielding significant returns.
  • Shore Capital structures boards with diverse industry experts, compensating them with stock options for alignment.
  • Deep industry knowledge and pre-acquisition board recruitment are crucial for validating investment theses.
  • Negotiation focuses on psychology and trust, utilizing a 'green, yellow, red' system for deal term management.
  • The firm builds platforms by acquiring single businesses and scaling them with operational 'factory floor' resources.
  • Co-owner Justin Ishbia applies a community-focused, systems-oriented philosophy to his Phoenix Suns ownership.

Deep Dive

  • Shore Capital invests in micro-cap businesses (sub-$10 million EBITDA) within specific industry niches.
  • The firm has deployed $7 billion with an average transaction size of $12 million, completing nearly 600 acquisitions in three years.
  • The "system is the star" philosophy emphasizes codified processes across all investment stages to yield differentiated results.
  • This approach navigates the inefficient micro-cap market and empowers early-career professionals.

  • Shore Capital's investment strategy focuses on operational excellence and a margin of safety, aiming for high returns (e.g., 70% gross, 50% net IRRs).
  • The primary competitive advantage is volume, which generates consistent deal flow and allows for the reuse of experienced executives.
  • Mistakes on initial, smaller deals are less critical compared to larger, higher-capital investments.
  • This volume creates an ecosystem for deal flow, ensuring a pipeline of businesses and talent.

  • Shore Capital closes 12-13 platforms annually, involving approximately 90 unique board members.
  • Over 80% of CEOs backed by Shore Capital are first-time CEOs, supported by a structured ecosystem.
  • Boards are composed like "basketball teams" with diverse expertise, compensating members with stock options (potential $250,000 over five years).
  • The firm focuses on industrial subsectors, particularly in healthcare, providing operational support to clinicians lacking business acumen.

  • Investment professionals at Shore Capital become industry experts, researching and developing detailed white papers for specific sectors.
  • Incentive structures align compensation and carry to the success of deals originated and managed, encouraging rigorous vetting.
  • Shore Capital recruits a board of industry experts *before* acquiring a company to validate the sector and target business.
  • This approach demonstrates preparedness and belief in the industry, reassuring sellers during the acquisition process.

  • Negotiation is primarily about psychology and sales, not just legal documents; Shore Capital has completed nearly 900 transactions with zero lawsuits.
  • The firm uses a 'green, yellow, red' system to manage and score key terms across all material transaction documents.
  • This system provides autonomy to deal teams, allowing junior members to negotiate standard terms independently.
  • Key negotiation points include catastrophic downside protection and structuring deals to unwind problematic partnerships.

  • Shore Capital operates like a venture capital firm, building platforms by acquiring a single business and then scaling it through strategic hires.
  • They target businesses in growing, inefficient market segments, aiming to improve them from single-digit to double-digit EBITDA.
  • The firm leverages resources akin to "billion-dollar companies" for "million-dollar companies," creating a 'factory floor' for operational improvements.
  • Centers of Excellence (e.g., marketing, data) facilitate knowledge sharing and best practice implementation across portfolio companies.

  • Justin Ishbia, co-owner of the Phoenix Suns, views sports ownership through a lens similar to private equity.
  • His philosophy prioritizes community and fan experience over purely financial metrics for the team.
  • Four key pillars for the Phoenix Suns include creating a raving fan experience, fostering a great workplace, acting as community stewards, and winning championships.
  • Ishbia noted the collaborative nature of NBA ownership, where owners share knowledge to benefit the entire league.
