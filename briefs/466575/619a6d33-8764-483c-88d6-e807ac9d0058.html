The NPR Politics Podcast

What's the 'biggest' thing on our political radar for next year?

Key Takeaways

  • The NPR Politics Podcast is releasing a bonus episode, typically subscriber-exclusive, for all listeners.
  • Reporters identify affordability, election administration, and redistricting as key issues for the 2026 midterms.
  • Significant demographic shifts among voters of color towards Republican candidates are noted, driven by cultural and foreign policy concerns.
  • Journalists Sam Gringlas and Sarah McCammon discussed influential mentors who shaped their career decisions.

Deep Dive

  • The NPR Politics Podcast is releasing a special bonus episode for all listeners, typically reserved for Politics Plus supporters, offering a preview of exclusive content.
  • Listeners can support NPR and access sponsor-free episodes by subscribing to Politics Plus at plus.npr.org/politics.
  • Reporters Sarah McCammon and Sam Gringlas introduce their 'Political Mosts' series, where they answer superlative questions about their careers in journalism.
  • Sam Gringlas returns to NPR as a Congress reporter after previously producing for the network and covering politics in Georgia.

  • Affordability, including healthcare costs, is identified as a significant political issue, with Democrats aiming to make it a winning platform, especially if enhanced ACA subsidies expire.
  • President Trump has shown mixed signals on affordability concerns, sometimes downplaying them and other times acknowledging them.
  • Senator Alyssa Slockin cited healthcare affordability as a personal issue, a concern also mentioned by Representative Marjorie Taylor Greene regarding the White House's impact on daily life.

  • Sarah McCammon's reporting beat covers politics and culture, including reproductive rights, gender, and demographics, focusing on upcoming midterms and their implications for the 2028 presidential election.
  • Demographic shifts observed in the 2024 election, particularly among voters of color moving towards Donald Trump, raise questions about a lasting realignment for the Republican Party.
  • McCammon's reporting included visits to Dearborn, Michigan, a heavily Arab and Muslim area, and conversations with Hispanic voters in Pennsylvania and Asian American voters in Nevada to understand these shifts.

  • Interviews with Hispanic and Arab/Muslim voters revealed concerns about cultural issues, LGBTQ rhetoric in schools, and dissatisfaction with Democratic engagement.
  • Foreign policy issues, specifically impacting Middle East policy and a desire for peace, also played a role in voter choices.
  • The ability of Republicans to maintain support from these shifting demographics in the upcoming midterms is uncertain, with early signs suggesting potential difficulties.

  • Off-year elections in Virginia and New Jersey in 2025 showed Democratic overperformance driven by affordability issues, offering potential signals for future trends.
  • LaShawn Jordan, an African-American voter in Norfolk, Virginia, who previously voted for Trump in 2024, expressed feeling 'bamboozled' by his rhetoric and shifting priorities.
  • Sarah McCammon expresses curiosity about the future of the diverse Latino vote in the 2026 election, noting that while some Latino voters initially aligned with Trump, recent polling suggests a shift away.

  • Sam Gringlas credits his high school journalism teacher, Nikki Schuler, for teaching him about the power of storytelling and human connection.
  • Sarah McCammon identifies Bill Norton, the editor of The Kansas City Star's teen page, as a significant mentor who instilled rigorous journalistic standards and resilience.
  • Listeners are invited to submit future 'most-ish' or superlative questions to nprpolitics@npr.org for upcoming bonus episodes.
