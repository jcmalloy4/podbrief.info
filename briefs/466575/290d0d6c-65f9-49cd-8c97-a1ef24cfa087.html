Year In Review: Congress' Successes & Failures

Key Takeaways

  • Narrow Republican majorities hindered legislative progress in 2025, resulting in limited bipartisan action.
  • The "Working Families Tax Cut" was a significant legislative achievement, though public awareness of its contents remains low.
  • Expiring Affordable Care Act subsidies present a critical legislative and midterm challenge for 2026, threatening premium increases.
  • President Trump significantly utilized executive authority, impacting Congress's traditional power of the purse and oversight.

Deep Dive

  • Narrow Republican majorities in both the House and Senate created legislative difficulty in 2025.
  • Speaker Mike Johnson faced challenges maintaining conference unity, impacting bill passage.
  • A large tax bill, initially called 'the one big beautiful bill' and later rebranded the 'Working Families Tax Cut,' passed via reconciliation, requiring only a simple majority.
  • Public awareness of the 'Working Families Tax Cut' contents and effects remains largely limited.

  • Republican members in competitive districts sought targeted legislation on issues like energy, healthcare, and grocery prices.
  • President Trump focused on executive authority rather than legislative details, preventing the codification of his actions.
  • The House experienced extended absence and a government shutdown due to Congress's failure to pass annual spending bills.
  • Congress passed a $9 billion rescissions package, clawing back previously approved funds for foreign aid and public media.

  • Affordable Care Act (ACA) subsidies, which enhanced tax credits implemented in 2021, are set to expire at the end of December.
  • Republicans largely oppose extending these subsidies, citing their opposition to the ACA since its passage in 2010.
  • Democrats insist on the renewal of the subsidies, projecting significant premium increases in January without intervention from President Trump.
  • The expiration of these subsidies is anticipated to be a major issue in the 2026 midterm elections.

  • The President circumvented Congress through actions including threats to fire agency leaders, enacting tariffs, and withholding funds.
  • Republican lawmakers largely enabled these actions, viewing the President as the party leader and fearing retribution through social media attacks and primary challenges.
  • The implementation of tariffs, which many traditional Republicans historically opposed, led to negative economic impacts for farmers and meat producers, requiring a presidential bailout.
  • The administration's actions weakened Congress's power of the purse due to Congress's inability to pass spending bills.

  • A bipartisan effort successfully forced a vote to release Epstein files, compelling presidential action due to constituent and base pressure.
  • Debate arose in Congress regarding the administration's boat strikes against alleged narco-traffickers, raising questions about a lack of a war powers debate.
  • Some Republicans expressed concerns about the boat strikes and vowed oversight, though partisan divisions emerged after a briefing on the actions.
  • This highlights the careful line Republicans must walk to avoid publicly criticizing the President.

  • Democrats face challenges maintaining unified messaging, risking being sidetracked by focusing on former President Trump's personality.
  • Their success in 2026 elections may depend on presenting positive proposals on issues like housing, healthcare, and energy costs to appeal to independents.
  • Little significant legislative progress is expected on Capitol Hill due to thin margins and Republican members prioritizing independence for re-election.
  • A key observation for 2026 will be whether former President Trump is welcomed in swing districts by at-risk candidates.
