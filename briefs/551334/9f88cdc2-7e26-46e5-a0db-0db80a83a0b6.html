Planet Money

Capitalism (Taylor's Version) (25-minute Podcast Version)

Key Takeaways

  • Taylor Swift's new album achieved record sales through strategic variant releases despite critical divisiveness.
  • The music industry's adoption of album variants as a new norm maximizes first-week chart performance.
  • Public perception of Swift shifted, leading to backlash concerning lyrical quality and perceived authenticity.
  • Debate exists regarding Swift's artistic motivation, whether financial gain or control of her public narrative.

Deep Dive

  • Taylor Swift's new album achieved record-breaking sales while generating critical divisiveness.
  • The episode analyzes Swift's 'capitalist genius' in leveraging business choices to boost profits and chart numbers.
  • The host and Vox's 'Today Explained' discuss how musicians strategically 'game the charts' for commercial success.

  • Taylor Swift's album sold over 4 million copies in its first week, surpassing Adele's 2015 record of 3.5 million.
  • The strategy included releasing numerous album variants, with Swift offering 27 different physical editions and accompanying merchandise.
  • This led to 1.2 million vinyl copies being sold in 24 hours, boosting first-week sales by appealing to fans willing to spend more on collectibles.

  • Digital album variants, often including bonus tracks or alternate artwork, are quickly released to influence close chart races, such as between Sabrina Carpenter and Travis Scott.
  • This strategy, aimed at maximizing first-week sales, has drawn criticism from fans who view it as exploitative and environmentally wasteful.
  • Some fans question if artists like Taylor Swift, given their established success, genuinely need such tactics to break records.

  • Music reporter Elias Light described some of Taylor Swift's new album lyrics as 'cringy'.
  • NPR Music critic Ann Powers noted a backlash against 'The Tortured Poets Department', with some critics questioning its lyrical quality and authenticity.
  • The perception of Taylor Swift has shifted, contrasting the positive reception of her Eras Tour with the critical response to her latest album.
  • The album's backlash is partly linked to a leaked lyric from 'thanK you aIMee', perceived as a crass attack on Charlie XCX.

  • Growing public irritation with Taylor Swift is fueled by online discussions about her wealth juxtaposed with her continued underdog persona.
  • Swift's response to criticism, including her statement about being a mirror, was perceived by some as dismissive.
  • Die-hard fans are publicly questioning Swift, partly due to a perceived lack of empathy towards struggling individuals.
  • The host interpreted Swift's song 'The Smallest Man Who Ever Lived' as a 'villain' role, expressing negative emotions in a 'swaggering macho' manner.

  • The episode discusses whether a wealthy and happy artist can create 'great art', drawing parallels to Beyoncé's 'Cowboy Carter'.
  • A critique suggests Taylor Swift is not perceived to be growing as an artist, with some questioning the expectation for pop stars to undergo a 'life journey of success'.
  • A theory proposes Swift's latest album was motivated not by financial gain, but by a desire to control her public narrative and musical legacy.
  • The album is suggested to be a 'marker on the highway' signifying Swift's current state of happiness, control, and power, moving beyond past misery.
