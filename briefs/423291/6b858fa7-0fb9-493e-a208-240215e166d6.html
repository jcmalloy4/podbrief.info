Making Sense with Sam Harris

#439 — How to Lose a Democracy

Key Takeaways

  • Damon Linker describes U.S. democracy's erosion, citing Trump's authoritarian potential.
  • Concerns include Trump's use of unrest as pretext for increased presidential power.
  • Discussion scrutinizes Trump's immigration and urban crime policies and tactics.
  • Claims of widespread left-wing terrorism are deemed unsubstantiated by the guest.
  • The host and guest explore differing interpretations of Trump's executive actions.

Deep Dive

  • Damon Linker, a writer for 'Notes from the Middle Ground' and a University of Pennsylvania lecturer, specializes in political theory.
  • He describes his political evolution from conservative to center-left.
  • This shift was driven by disillusionment with the Bush administration and the modern Republican party.

  • Linker expresses concern over President Trump's potential to establish authoritarian control to 'vanquish his enemies'.
  • He fears Trump and allies like Stephen Miller may use civil unrest as a pretext to suppress political dissent.
  • Concerns include the alleged use of the Insurrection Act for militarized intervention in cities like Portland and Chicago.

  • The host suggests a right-of-center view sees Trump's actions as a serious response to urban crime and immigration promises.
  • The guest acknowledges some validity but highlights aggressive tactics, such as masked ICE officers and employer raids.
  • These tactics are seen as alarming and potentially provocative, distinct from deportation numbers.

  • The guest criticizes Democratic approaches to homelessness and crime, agreeing on the importance of prosecuting offenses.
  • However, he notes current crime rates in major cities like New York and Philadelphia are significantly lower than in the early 1990s.
  • Claims of widespread left-wing terrorism are dismissed as unsubstantiated and delusional, unlike the 1970s.

  • One speaker contends that Trump's approach is more like 'reality TV' and theater, not indicative of authoritarianism or fascism.
  • The other speaker explains masked ICE agents due to doxxing risks and justifies forceful tactics based on immigration laws.
  • The conversation explores differing interpretations of the Trump administration's approach, with one speaker dismissing scholars of totalitarianism as hysterics.
