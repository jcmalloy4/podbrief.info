Adam Carolla Show

Trump wants to sue Beyonce + Ja Rule + Comedian Adam Ray

Overview

  • The podcast features comedian Adam Ray discussing his live comedy experiences, including his upcoming tour dates, performing at Dave Chappelle's Yellow Springs venue, and his philosophy that true performers will perform anywhere with gratitude for audience attention.
  • A significant portion explores how celebrity endorsements have evolved from being stigmatized to becoming highly lucrative opportunities, with examples like Matthew McConaughey's Lincoln commercials and Johnny Depp's Sauvage ads commanding enormous payouts.
  • Guest Ja Rule shares insights about his journey in the music industry, from humble beginnings rapping in school lunchrooms to touring large venues, while also addressing his involvement with the infamous Fyre Festival and his current whiskey brand venture.
  • The conversation examines how entertainment careers thrive on uncertainty and breaking rules, with Adam emphasizing the importance of taking creative risks and avoiding complacency rather than seeking guaranteed outcomes.
  • Throughout the episode, nostalgic discussions about 90s sitcom catchphrases, childhood experiences, and changing social dynamics provide cultural context and comedic relief.

Content

Adam Ray's Comedy Shows and Celebrity Encounters

  • Adam Ray discusses his upcoming live comedy shows in June (Kansas City, Cleveland, Indianapolis)
  • His shows are longer than typical stand-up performances (around 2.5-3 hours)
  • A recent Austin show featured performers like Tom Lennon, Big J, Oakerson, Grace O'Malley, and Casey Rocket
  • Matthew McConaughey was in the audience at the Austin show
  • Later shows mentioned include Kevin Nealon, John Stamos, Eric Andre, and Rich Eisen
  • Adam shares a comedic anecdote about a bit with John Stamos involving a Full House-themed interaction with a kid

Celebrity Endorsements and Commercials

  • The conversation shifts to celebrity endorsements, focusing on Matthew McConaughey's Lincoln and Sauvage ads
  • They note that celebrities now freely do commercials without stigma, unlike in the past
  • Some actors like McConaughey get paid enormous amounts for minimal work (e.g., $275K per syllable)
  • Specific examples discussed include Johnny Depp's Sauvage cologne ad and McConaughey's Lincoln car commercials
  • They contrast past celebrity commercial practices (hiding overseas ads) with current ubiquitous endorsement culture
  • Agents now easily pitch commercials to actors, and once one respected actor does a commercial, others follow suit

Dave Chappelle's Yellow Springs Shows

  • Adam Ray performed at Dave Chappelle's Firehouse venue in Yellow Springs, Ohio:
- Did two standup shows - Chappelle hosted and performed - Venue capacity around 250-300 people - Chappelle unexpectedly came on stage during the show
  • Adam reflects on his previous visit to Yellow Springs during COVID, which featured outdoor comedy shows
  • Notable performers at those shows included John Mayer, Sarah Silverman, Common, Louis CK, Tiffany Haddish, and Donald Rawlings

Professional Growth and Performance Philosophy

  • Adam reflects on not being invited to perform at a past event, emphasizing self-reflection rather than blaming others
  • When not invited to an opportunity, he focuses on understanding why instead of getting angry
  • He views performing as a "blessing" - having an audience's attention is valuable
  • True performers are passionate and will perform anywhere, anytime
  • He shares a personal anecdote about a dog rescue benefit show where he got into a confrontation after making jokes about cats

Embracing Uncertainty in Comedy Careers

  • Adam discusses the unpredictable nature of comedy and entertainment careers
  • He reflects on his early experience with the Dr. Phil character, noting he didn't know it would become successful
  • Key insights shared: "There are no more rules" in entertainment, and success often comes from trying things without guaranteed outcomes
  • He's developing a new character show called "Jeremy's House" - featuring a 38-year-old man-child character, to be performed at the Comedy Store on May 28th
  • His philosophy emphasizes trying things that scare you and not getting stuck in the same routine

Upcoming Show and Celebrity Guest Strategy

  • The speakers discuss their upcoming show/podcast project ending in August
  • Remaining shows include stops in KC, Cleveland, Indianapolis, Pittsburgh, Atlantic City, Reno, and Cape Cod
  • They're reaching out to various celebrities via Instagram (mentioned Teddy Swims, Johnny Knoxville, John Stamos, Scott Stapp)
  • They observe that celebrities are attracted to projects with "heat" or momentum
  • One speaker reflects on live podcasting being novel when he started 16 years ago

90s TV Sitcom Catchphrases and Characters

  • The discussion turns to TV sitcom catchphrases from the 1990s, including:
- Steve Urkel (Jaleel White) from "Family Matters" with catchphrases like "Got any cheese?" and his distinctive laugh - Joey Lawrence's "Whoa!" - Joey Tribbiani's "How you doing?" - Stephanie Tanner's "How rude!"
  • They specifically discuss the Urkel talking doll, its marketing, and potential merchandising profits

Personal Updates and Nostalgia

  • Dave Coulier recently beat stage 3 non-Hodgkin's lymphoma and is now cancer-free as of April
  • Jimmy Kimmel's daughter Katie is now a mother, prompting nostalgic reflection about building her a playhouse
  • The speaker shares a humorous anecdote about drunkenly urinating in Kimmel's daughter's sandbox
  • This leads to reflection on childhood entertainment, specifically sandboxes as a primary form of entertainment pre-digital age

Childhood Memories and Socioeconomic Context

  • Discussion of childhood summer activities, particularly playing in sandboxes in extremely hot Reseda weather
  • Reflection on limited recreational options for kids in the valley, including lack of water parks
  • Memories of using neighbors' pools and raiding neighbors' pantries as a child
  • Description of growing up with limited financial resources
  • Mentions specific neighborhood families like the Ari Emmas, Brights, and Garmeyers
  • Recalls a German kid named Canude who was considered a "trouble kid"

Pool Ownership and Household Technology

  • Conversation about above-ground pools and their inherent structural risks
  • Discussion of pool ownership dynamics among different socioeconomic groups
  • Water weight is consistent regardless of pool type (11 pounds per gallon)
  • Proper pool maintenance requires income, sobriety, and technical knowledge
  • One speaker strongly values the microwave as a life-changing technology, arguing it's more impactful than phones or cars

Hotel Incidents and Travel Experiences

  • Speaker recounts accidentally spilling Beefaroni in a hotel room
  • Hotel front desk called about blood-like stains on sheets (which were actually Beefaroni)
  • He was caught on security footage taking 3 Beefaroni cans from the hotel's sundry shop
  • Discussion about inconsistent policies for paying for small items in hotels and on flights
  • They mention a unique bar in Phoenix called "Carry On" that simulates a 1970s private plane experience

Ja Rule Interview Segment

  • Ja Rule discusses his early rap beginnings as a hobby with low barriers to entry
  • Started in lunchrooms with informal "ciphers" and rhyming
  • Compares rap to sports like soccer and basketball - accessible to anyone
  • Recalls starting to rap around age 15-16, gaining confidence through peer feedback
  • Encourages discovering and developing natural talents rather than focusing on weaknesses

Social Dynamics and Generational Differences

  • Discussion of high school dances and changing social norms across different eras
  • Conversation about modern challenges in social interactions due to technology
  • Exploration of the "creepy guy" vs. "cool guy" concept in social scenarios
  • Observation that social confidence dramatically changes how interactions are perceived
  • Crude hypothetical discussions about social perception and workplace dynamics

Independent Artistry in the Digital Age

  • Pros: Independent artists now have more opportunities to be seen
  • Cons: Lack of gatekeepers means many unqualified artists gain attention
  • Critique of "shortcut" culture in entertainment, particularly in comedy and music
  • Emphasis on "doing the reps" and studying one's craft
  • Observation about performance expectations: comedy audiences want fresh material while music fans typically want established hits

Ja Rule's Tour and Whiskey Brand

  • Upcoming American tour starting in July covering 40 cities
  • Performing in large outdoor amphitheaters (20,000-30,000 seat venues)
  • Traveling primarily by tour bus, with comfortable accommodations
  • Nelly will be closing out the shows
  • Discussion of Ja Rule's whiskey brand - Amber and Opal:
- Black-owned brand created by Ja Rule and three close friends - "Amber" refers to the liquor's color, "Opal" represents inclusivity - Available in Total Wines, Reserve Bar, Seal Box, and Flavia.com - Unique bottle service approach with lookbooks and mixologists

Fyre Festival Discussion

  • Ja Rule describes himself as a "victim" of the Fyre Festival situation
  • Initially had an innovative idea to create a unique festival experience
  • Suggests the event was derailed by greed
  • Was not criminally charged or sent to jail, though he lost reputational value
  • As an officer of the company, he did not take any salary
  • Has no aspirations to do another large festival

Closing Segments

  • Discussion of a jail escape in New Orleans where Sterling Williams helped 10 violent inmates escape
  • Conversation about prison escape methods and coyote encounters
  • Commentary on celebrity endorsements in politics and campaign spending
  • Speculation about Beyoncé potentially being paid $11 million for an endorsement
  • Anecdote about receiving a chopper (motorcycle) as payment for a commercial
  • Mention of upcoming shows: Adam Ray's remaining 8 shows, Ja Rule tour starting July 23rd, and Adam Crowl performing in Bellflower, Tacoma, and Spokane
