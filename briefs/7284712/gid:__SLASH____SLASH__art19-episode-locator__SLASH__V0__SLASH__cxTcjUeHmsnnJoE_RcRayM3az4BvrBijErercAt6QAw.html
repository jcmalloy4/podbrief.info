Adam Carolla Show

Jasmine Crocket & Newsom’s Performative Confidence + Comedian Monique Marvez

Overview

  • Adam Carolla reflects on the profound impact of his podcast on listeners' lives, including helping a woman cope with grief after losing her husband, highlighting the meaningful connection between content creators and their audience.
  • The group analyzes the unrealistic portrayal of physical capabilities in action movies, particularly Tom Cruise's superhuman grip strength scenes, while acknowledging that Cruise actually performs many of his own dangerous stunts.
  • Discussion explores how authentic relationships require accepting people as complete "packages" with both positive and negative traits, while still being open to small behavioral adjustments rather than expecting fundamental change.
  • The conversation critiques modern society's breakdown of traditional mentorship dynamics, where younger generations increasingly dismiss expert knowledge, alongside a concerning trend of adults abandoning mature responsibilities.
  • Guest Monique Marvez shares her journey through three failed marriages and career success, emphasizing the importance of making decisions from wisdom rather than fear and the value of maintaining professional boundaries.

Content

  • Adam Carolla begins by reflecting on a meaningful interaction with a fan in Florida who shared how his podcast helped her cope with grief after losing her husband to a bee sting. He acknowledges that hearing from listeners who say his show has helped them through difficult times is the "best part" of his job.
  • Carolla appreciates listeners who tune in during various activities like hiking, commuting, or walking dogs, and thanks them for their support.
  • The conversation shifts to action movies, with a focus on the unrealistic "grip" abilities of action stars, particularly Tom Cruise:
- Action stars consistently demonstrate superhuman ability to hang onto surfaces - Without these grip scenes, movies like Mission Impossible would be extremely short - Tom Cruise especially is known for hanging off airplanes, buildings, and other surfaces - Grip scenes are more unrealistic than fighting, running, or driving scenes - Characters often casually react after being saved from extreme heights - Tom Cruise "spends more time outside airplanes than inside them"

  • The discussion moves to military parachuting and an action movie scene involving vehicles in a C-130 aircraft:
- Static line parachuting involves being attached to the plane during jump - The speaker critiques a Fast and Furious scene where cars are loaded facing forward when they should be backed in - He argues vehicles should logically be loaded facing the exit for better weight distribution, easier orientation, safer exit, and practicality

  • The conversation returns to grip strength:
- Discussion about different types of grip strength, including natural and trained grip - One participant describes how jiu-jitsu training can dramatically improve hand strength - They acknowledge that Tom Cruise actually performs his own stunts, including hanging off planes

  • The participants analyze an Aspercreme commercial featuring a 56-year-old man in a garage band:
- They find the tagline "Don't feel your age. Act your age" contradictory - They argue the man is actually acting like a teenager, not his age - Discussion about whether middle-aged fathers typically spend nights in garage bands

  • The speaker criticizes societal trends of adults not acting like adults and trying to be like their children.
  • Discussion of "performative confidence" as a communication style, particularly among certain groups, characterized by dramatic, assertive speech that may signal dishonesty.
  • The conversation turns critical of Elon Musk's involvement with government contracts, suggesting it was a self-serving "sham" rather than an effort to improve government efficiency.
  • Analysis of California Governor Gavin Newsom's speech on homelessness:
- Portrayed as performative and potentially hypocritical - Criticism of apparent inconsistencies in his statements about homeless individuals - Commentary on his seemingly casual way of discussing serious issues like death and homelessness

  • The speaker shares a personal encounter with a young meth-addicted individual in San Diego who was with his wife and newborn in a tent, highlighting the severe street addiction problem.
  • Further criticism of Newsom suggests he:
- Previously denied street addiction issues - Is now suddenly addressing the problem - May be changing his approach due to political calculations, possibly preparing for a presidential run

  • The discussion shifts to a reference about Vince Champ, a serial rapist who was also a standup comedian.
  • A female comedian (Monique Marvez) discusses her experiences in comedy:
- She was focused on work and not particularly aware of her attractiveness when younger - She had a "Wonder Woman" attitude about her career - Her father advised "there's no free lunch" - be cautious of offers with potential hidden costs - She avoided drinking, smoking, drugs, and situations that could put her in vulnerable positions

  • The conversation takes a tangential turn to male body care, specifically focused on testicle/scrotum maintenance and sensitivity.
  • Discussion transitions to broader relationship dynamics:
- People cannot fundamentally change their core nature - Individuals often temporarily modify behavior early in relationships - People ultimately return to their authentic selves - Accepting a partner's "package" of characteristics is important

  • The conversation continues on the theme of accepting people as "packages" with both positive and negative traits:
- Being open to small behavioral adjustments in relationships is reasonable - People have different responses to coaching/advice based on their background and self-confidence - Those with sports/coaching experience are often more receptive to guidance - Small behavioral tweaks in relationships can be valuable

  • The speaker emphasizes the value of mentorship and learning from others:
- Importance of "deferring to the bigger brain" when someone has more knowledge - Criticism of a current cultural trend where people dismiss experts - Notes a breakdown in traditional mentorship, with younger generations often believing they know everything - Advocates for making oneself interesting and building genuine relationships

  • The conversation shifts to a personal anecdote about a past hookup between Monique Marvez and Mike August in Montreal in 1992 during a comedy festival:
- Took place at the Four Seasons Hotel during a professional networking event - Monique was traveling with a younger female friend/assistant - She had a high-paying day job selling medical malpractice insurance in Miami while doing comedy

  • Monique discusses her father:
- Describes him as a womanizer and an architect who lived life on his own terms - Shares his quote: "I can forgive you being a happy go lucky slut, but I will never forgive you being a whore" - His life philosophy was to "live fast, die young" though he died of congestive heart failure - In his final weeks, he leased a new Cadillac, ate at a mob-owned restaurant, drank Negronis - Her stepmother cared for him in his final years

  • Discussion of the "Sons and Moons" metaphor from her father:
- People are like "sons" (energetic, productive) or "moons" - Two "sons" can create a powerful partnership - Two "moons" create conflict because each thinks the other is problematic

  • Monique reflects on her three failed marriages:
- First marriage: High school sweetheart who became a CPA - Second marriage: She put her husband through flight school - Third marriage: Motivated by desire to have a child at age 40, not love - Paid nearly $250,000 in the divorce, plus $3,500 monthly alimony and $700 for health insurance - Takes responsibility for making a "decision made in fear"

  • She discusses her career achievements:
- Worked on radio show "Monique and the Man" - Improved station ratings from 13 to 1 - Received development deal with Dick Wolf at NBC Universal - Nearly secured a sitcom (lost to 30 Rock) - Had a book deal and was earning around $500K per year - Working extremely long hours (17-20 hours daily)

  • The speaker describes a frustrating interaction with an entitled friend who occupied his warehouse with multiple cars and parts, refusing to move his car from a hoist despite requests.
  • Discussion about Ben & Jerry's co-founder being arrested protesting at a Senate hearing, with Monique offering perspective that protests often reflect deeper personal issues.
  • Conversation about a Starbucks dress code controversy where over 1,000 baristas were striking over new restrictions requiring black shirts and specific trouser colors.
  • The speakers discuss societal degradation, focusing on graffiti and urban decay:
- They view graffiti as a "middle finger" to the community and a source of depression - Argue that poverty does not justify destructive behavior - Emphasize that environment impacts mental well-being

  • The narrator shares a story about discovering graffiti with the tag "Egbert" and tracking down the person responsible:
- They confront the tagger (Greg Besner) at his home with his parents present - Greg's parents immediately hold him accountable, making him repaint the entire alley - The narrator reflects that this type of swift, direct parental accountability would be unlikely today

  • Monique shares a childhood story about sassing a neighbor and being disciplined by her parents, suggesting this type of discipline is important.
  • The conversation concludes with commentary on Bruce Springsteen's criticism of the Trump administration during his tour, with the speaker strongly criticizing Springsteen's political statements as exaggerated and without real consequences.
  • The hosts wrap up by promoting upcoming comedy shows by Monique Marvez and Adam Carolla.
