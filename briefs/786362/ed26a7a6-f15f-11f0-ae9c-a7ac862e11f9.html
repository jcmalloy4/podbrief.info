TIVP055: Churchill Downs, Inc. (CHDN): Investing in the Greatest Two Minutes in Sports w/ Shawn O’Malley & Daniel Mahncke

Key Takeaways

  • Churchill Downs combines the iconic Kentucky Derby with growing Historical Racing Machines (HRMs) and B2B wagering.
  • The company leverages strategic asset management and political influence to secure tax advantages and local monopolies.
  • HRMs are a primary financial driver, generating billions in annual handle across key states like Kentucky and Virginia.
  • A costly mobile gaming venture highlights diversification risks in the complex gambling regulatory landscape.
  • Strong management with significant stock ownership demonstrates a long-term focus and effective capital allocation.

Deep Dive

  • Churchill Downs leveraged its political influence and relationships, including $800,000 in lobbying efforts in 2022, to protect its business interests.
  • The company saved millions in annual property taxes by transferring the Churchill Downs racetrack title to the city of Louisville in 2002 via a 30-year lease.
  • Arrangements have included foregoing property taxes in exchange for reinvesting savings into local schools, with a buyback clause for reacquiring facilities.
  • HRM revenues also fund horse race purses, strengthening the industry's interconnectedness and argument for political support.

  • Churchill Downs pivoted its TwinSpires platform to a business-to-business model, licensing content to other sportsbooks and avoiding high marketing costs.
  • The gambling industry's complex regulatory landscape, including unique requirements like live Hyli performances, creates significant barriers to entry for competitors.
  • A 2021 law decoupled previously linked activities, granting Churchill Downs local monopolies for certain gaming types within specific zones.

  • Churchill Downs' diversification into mobile gaming with 'Big Fish Casino' led to significant legal challenges, alleging illegal gambling through its freemium model.
  • The venture resulted in a costly $124 million pre-tax settlement, highlighting risks of diversification in shifting regulatory environments.
  • This experience contributed to the company's subsequent focus on historical racing machines (HRMs).

  • The Kentucky Derby, a "trophy asset," generates substantial revenue via high-priced tickets and luxury suites, ranging from $165 to $2,800.
  • It draws over 20 million TV viewers, surpassing many NFL games, NBA Finals, or golf tournaments in popularity.
  • Despite its global brand and cultural significance, the Derby's implied multiple of operating profits is surprisingly low.
  • Temporary factors like weather or renovations can impact short-term financial results and stock performance, but the 150-year-old brand remains resilient.

  • Churchill Downs has significantly focused on Historical Racing Machines (HRMs) since selling Big Fish Games in 2018, growing its HRM count to over 10,000 by 2024.
  • The Derby City Gaming Facility opened in 2018 with 900 HRMs, marking a strategic shift in the company's gaming operations.
  • HRMs are regulated under horse racing statutes as parimutuel wagering, distinct from slot machines, generating substantial revenue.
  • The Richmond location averages $470 per machine per day from 1,200 HRMs, yielding approximately $200 million annually.

  • The horse racing industry faces secular headwinds, marked by a decline in thoroughbred races and betting handles despite overall sports betting growth.
  • Recent horse fatalities have negatively impacted the public image of the sport.
  • Churchill Downs, however, continues pragmatic, localized HRM expansion into states like Virginia, Kentucky, and New Hampshire.
  • Management targets a five-year payback for historical racing machines, achieving attractive returns on capital.

  • Churchill Downs' TwinSpires operates as both a B2C horse racing app and a B2B infrastructure provider for other sportsbooks.
  • It is a leading U.S. online horse racing platform, processing billions in annual handle and offering 24/7 wagering on global races.
  • While the overall horse wagering market is flat or declining, the online segment, where TwinSpires holds significant share, is growing.

  • Churchill Downs' management team, including CEO Bill Carstanjen with $190 million in stock, demonstrates substantial "skin in the game."
  • They possess a strong track record of delivering shareholder returns via opportunistic acquisitions, strategic capital expenditures, and share buybacks.
  • The company maintains a long-term focus, exemplified by its decision not to provide quarterly or annual guidance, similar to Berkshire Hathaway.
  • Management has shown mastery of the legislative process, leveraging the Kentucky Derby's ownership for business opportunities.

  • A back-of-the-envelope valuation suggests Churchill Downs is an attractive investment if HRMs and regional casinos are valued at typical industry multiples.
  • The Kentucky Derby's ownership is seen as a crown jewel, potentially valued at a fraction of multiples paid for other iconic sports assets.
  • One guest recommended adding CHDN to a watchlist, expressing hesitation to invest at current prices due to industry inexperience and perceived risks.
  • Cyclical and idiosyncratic factors, like adverse weather during the Derby, can temporarily impact profits and stock performance but the brand remains resilient.
