TIVP049: DoorDash (DASH): Can It Keep Delivering Returns? w/ Shawn O’Malley & Daniel Mahncke

Key Takeaways

  • DoorDash achieved U.S. market dominance by cracking positive unit economics in meal delivery.
  • The COVID-19 pandemic significantly accelerated DoorDash's order volume and market share capture.
  • DoorDash is expanding beyond restaurant delivery into broader local commerce, including groceries and retail.
  • Past tipping practices and worker classification have generated ethical scrutiny and lawsuits for the company.
  • Strategic acquisitions and diversification into services like Dashmarts aim to ensure long-term growth.
  • Current valuation reflects optimistic future growth, with the stock not considered cheaply priced for immediate investment.

Deep Dive

  • DoorDash classifies its workers as contractors, leading to controversy over the lack of employee benefits like healthcare.
  • California's Proposition 22 favored contractor status, setting a precedent many states follow due to cost complexities.
  • New York City implemented a minimum earnings floor of $21.44 per hour for delivery workers, with tips not counting towards this minimum.
  • Increased prices for consumers could result if drivers were classified as full-fledged employees.

  • DoorDash's revenue streams include restaurant commissions (15-30%), customer fees, and DashPass subscriptions.
  • Advertising, a $1 billion run rate in 2024, is a key driver of margin improvement and increased net revenue margin to 13.4%.
  • Improved logistics and order batching have increased customer order frequency by 10% annually since 2020.
  • The DashPass subscription program offers free delivery, aiming to increase order frequency and loyalty across local merchants.

  • Consumers show willingness to pay a premium for delivery convenience, despite potentially doubling food costs compared to pickup.
  • DoorDash's net revenue margin increased to 13.4%, partly due to higher-margin advertising and improved logistics.
  • The service's stickiness is attributed to consumer habit, making it difficult to revert to self-pickup or home cooking.
  • Restaurants and retailers face high fixed costs and unpredictable demand, making owned delivery challenging.

  • DoorDash achieved profitability and rapid top-line growth despite historically challenging food delivery economics.
  • The company has recently shown operating profit after burning billions of dollars for over a decade.
  • Profitability was reached while managing significant variable costs inherent in human delivery services.
  • Future growth could be enhanced by further scale and the potential adoption of autonomous delivery, reducing costs.

  • The COVID-19 pandemic caused an unprecedented surge in DoorDash's order volume, enabling significant market share capture.
  • DoorDash leveraged its success in suburban markets and strategically accelerated growth during the pandemic.
  • The company's strategic vision extends beyond restaurant delivery to leadership in local commerce, encompassing groceries and prescriptions.
  • Despite early operational failures in September 2013, DoorDash recovered customer trust through refunds and direct action.

  • DoorDash faced a past tipping scandal for allegedly using customer tips to subsidize driver earnings.
  • The incident led to customer deception and numerous lawsuits, with the company paying millions in settlements.
  • Concerns about DoorDash's past ethical integrity and 'win-at-all-costs' management approach persist.
  • Tips now directly go to Dashers, a resolution enacted after driver protests and transparency issues.

  • DoorDash expanded into first-party retail with Dashmarts, acting as micro-fulfillment centers, and explored ghost kitchens.
  • Key acquisitions include Caviar for its high-end market and Volt for European expansion.
  • The recent acquisition of Deliveroo further broadened its international presence and customer base.
  • International revenue growth now exceeds U.S. growth, addressing past global expansion limitations.

  • Over 90% of non-CEO compensation is tied to Restricted Stock Units (RSUs), viewed skeptically as tenure-based rather than performance-based.
  • DoorDash has a substantial stock-based compensation expense, with over $2 billion in unrecognized expenses expected to vest.
  • The total share count has risen by a third over the last five years, contributing to shareholder dilution.
  • The company authorized a $5 billion buyback but has not yet repurchased shares.

  • The addressable market for DoorDash is vast, encompassing $3 trillion in global restaurant commerce and potentially another $3 trillion in groceries.
  • A target of $300 billion in gross order value suggests a 5% capture rate of global restaurant and grocery spending.
  • With scaled growth, the company could achieve 15% operating margins and $6 billion in operating profit within a decade.
  • Current equity value projections range from $150 billion to $210 billion, but the stock is considered not cheaply priced, reflecting an optimistic outlook.
