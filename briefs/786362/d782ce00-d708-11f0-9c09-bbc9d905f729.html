TIVP050: Portfolio Review: Performance and New Positions w/ Daniel Mahncke & Shawn O’Malley

Key Takeaways

  • The Intrinsic Value Portfolio returned approximately 9% in its first year, despite significant cash drag.
  • Google and Reddit were key portfolio winners, with Google up over 50% and Reddit nearly 120%.
  • New positions were initiated in Crocs and Uber, with Adobe allocation increased.
  • Ulta Beauty was sold from the portfolio to reallocate capital to more compelling opportunities.
  • The portfolio strategy includes small, asymmetric positions for significant upside potential.

Deep Dive

  • The Intrinsic Value Portfolio achieved an approximate 9% return in its first year.
  • This return occurred despite starting with 50% cash, now at approximately 25%.
  • Google, the largest position, was up over 50%.
  • Reddit significantly outperformed, becoming a nearly 120% gain on its initial 2% portfolio allocation.

  • An alternative strategy to invest in Ferrari is through Exor, an Italian holding company.
  • Exor holds a significant stake in Ferrari, alongside assets like CNH, Stellantis, and Philips.
  • Stellantis is noted as a potentially favorable entry point during a cyclical downturn.
  • Exor's significant buyback program provides a margin of safety and can help close valuation discounts.

  • Crocs stock decreased significantly, prompting a re-evaluation after Q2 earnings.
  • The company features attractive metrics, including an 18% free cash flow yield and 16% buyback yield.
  • A valuation model suggests a fair value of $78 per share, close to current trading prices.
  • Despite declining sales, core Crocs brand volumes are increasing, with pullbacks attributed to promotional activity and HeyDude brand weakness.

  • Nu Bank, a current portfolio holding, has increased approximately 30% since its purchase.
  • The bank's non-performing loans are slightly higher than competitors but balanced by significantly faster growth.
  • Nu Bank is diversifying into safer lending and expanding into higher-income markets, with rapid growth in Mexico since 2019.
  • The company has applied for a US bank charter, potentially targeting the Latin population for remittances.

  • Uber is a core holding, representing 4.8% of the portfolio, with an average purchase price around $75.
  • The company is seen as a potential dominant aggregator of autonomous vehicle demand.
  • Uber's forward price-to-free-cash-flow is approximately 18 times, with a forward P/E around 27.
  • Profitability and margins are increasing, illustrating operating leverage with linear earnings growth.

  • Nike, an early portfolio addition, is up 10% from its entry price of $56.
  • An opportunity to sell at $80 for a 40% gain was missed.
  • The hosts plan to sell if the stock reaches $80 again without significant fundamental improvement.
  • Mid-$50s is seen as a margin of safety for a turnaround bet.

  • PayPal's pitch has not performed as expected, with the position down significantly.
  • Sentiment remains low despite new partnerships and a recent stock increase after earnings.
  • Venmo and Bino Pay Later are scaling successfully, and share buybacks are a possibility.
  • The company's current valuation is considered attractive relative to its fundamental strength.

  • Salesforce is affected by the AI narrative but could potentially benefit from it, despite AI not being an immediate threat to its core business.
  • The company is deeply integrated into corporate America, providing high switching costs.
  • Salesforce trades at a forward PE of 20 and a forward price to free cash flow per share of 15.
  • A base case valuation with typical margins suggests a fair value of $280, falling short of a 12% hurdle rate.

  • Adobe's stock price declined nearly 30% year-to-date due to concerns that AI may diminish the need for its creative tools.
  • Major corporations rely on Adobe for creative and marketing analytics, solidifying its market position.
  • The company is currently trading at a forward Price-to-Earnings and Price-to-Cash Flow ratio under 14, an all-time low.
  • A conservative model suggests an 18% annualized return over five years with a 25x exit multiple, exceeding the 12% hurdle rate.
