TIVP040: Manchester United (MANU): Valuing a Football Empire w/ Daniel Mahncke & Shawn O’Malley

Key Takeaways

  • Manchester United, a global brand, faces a valuation gap despite high Forbes estimates.
  • European football operates open leagues with relegation risks, unlike closed US sports franchises.
  • NFL's economic model, with shared TV revenue and salary caps, ensures financial stability.
  • On-pitch success heavily impacts European club finances, especially broadcasting revenue.
  • Manchester United's revenue pillars are commercial, broadcasting, and stable matchday income.
  • Sports franchises are increasingly valued as "trophy assets" for the ultra-rich.
  • A sale or regulatory changes are potential catalysts for Manchester United's valuation.
  • Current Manchester United stock is not an attractive investment without clear catalysts.

Deep Dive

  • Manchester United's public trading status raises questions about its value investment potential.
  • The club broke the €100 million player transfer fee barrier in 2016.
  • Neymar Jr.'s record transfer for over $250 million exemplifies inflated player market values.
  • Player transfers often utilize release clauses as a common mechanism.

  • U.S. franchises dominate valuation lists despite football's global popularity, due to fragmented European leagues and higher US revenue per user.
  • Manchester United, a global brand, faces monetization challenges from its fanbase spread across less lucrative markets.
  • The profitability of the NFL is contrasted with European leagues, where Manchester United's Forbes valuation is significantly lower than top NFL franchises.
  • American businessman Todd Boehly's significant spending spree at Chelsea FC highlights differing investment approaches.

  • The fragmented European market, with diverse cultures and languages, limits the premium earned by football clubs compared to the unified U.S. market.
  • The English Premier League's dominance is partly due to its use of English, making it more accessible internationally.
  • Forbes' valuations of sports teams are considered reliable, showing growth between 2021 and 2023 for team valuations.
  • European football ownership stakes sell at an average 17% premium to Forbes' valuations, driven by international attention.

  • US leagues are closed systems with fixed franchises guaranteeing TV contract revenue, unlike open European leagues with promotion/relegation risks.
  • The NFL uses salary caps (approx. $270 million/season), while Premier League teams like Manchester United can spend $250 million on wages without a cap.
  • American leagues prioritize structural balance with draft picks aiding weaker teams, contrasting with dominant clubs in some European leagues.
  • NFL's entertainment product maximizes advertising opportunities through camera angles and commercial breaks, effectively monetizing fan engagement.

  • The NFL ensures financial parity by collectively selling and equally splitting TV revenue among all teams.
  • Its limited 17 regular season games enhance economic value compared to 38 games in European football.
  • NFL broadcasts allocate approximately 25% of airtime to commercials, generating over $10 billion annually in TV rights, significantly more than the Premier League's $2 billion.
  • The draft system and salary caps create an egalitarian system, attracting investors by ensuring consistent revenue for all franchises.

  • On-pitch performance significantly impacts European football finances, contrasting with the NFL's stable revenue distribution.
  • Manchester United's performance declined after Sir Alex Ferguson's 2013 departure, resulting in no league titles and a near-relegation season in 2023.
  • Relegation could cause Manchester United an $80 million loss in broadcasting revenue and $100 million in commercial revenue.
  • The Glazer family's leveraged buyout 20 years ago burdened the club with significant debt, sparking fan protests.

  • Manchester United's revenue streams are commercial (45%), broadcasting (one-third), and stable matchday income.
  • Player wages and contract amortization consume approximately 80% of the club's income.
  • Recent one-off costs led to a loss exceeding $100 million despite a $1.6 billion investment for a 27% stake.
  • The club's unprofitability and a market valuation of $3 billion compared to a potential buyer's $6 billion interest highlights a valuation gap.

  • The enduring nature of sports makes teams appealing investments for the ultra-rich, who value appreciation and bragging rights over cash flows.
  • Manchester United's valuation has increased approximately five-fold from 2004 to present, driven by growing global billionaire wealth.
  • Chelsea FC's 2023 sale for $5.4 billion provides a comparable valuation point for Manchester United.
  • A potential new stadium project, estimated to cost billions, is a future catalyst that could significantly impact the club and shareholders.

  • Potential Premier League regulatory changes or an independent regulator could prompt a Glazer family sale.
  • Increased interest from Middle Eastern investors, including Saudi Arabia and Qatar, presents a significant catalyst for franchise acquisitions.
  • Infrastructure investments like new stadiums are exempt from financial regulations and can boost matchday revenue.
  • Manchester United owns its stadium, providing a potential margin of safety, though an investment relies on a future sale at a higher price rather than organic business compounding.
