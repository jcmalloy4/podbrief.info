OpenAI to Support Adult Content, CoreWeave’s AI Ranch, Apple's M5 Vision Pro | Zachary Perret, Panos Panay, Eric Wittman, Brian Baumgartner, Adam Ryan, Adit Abraham, Dan Shipper & More

Key Takeaways

  • OpenAI's decision to allow explicit AI content signals a shift in strategy, prompting debate over revenue versus reputational risk.
  • New AI-driven solutions are transforming finance with Plaid's LendScore and legal services with Finch Legal's AI-augmented paralegals.
  • Amazon is deeply integrating conversational AI and Large Language Models into its device ecosystem, enhancing user interactions.
  • The surge in AI demand is fueling massive data center investments and speculative valuations in related energy and infrastructure companies.
  • Niche professional networking platforms like Workweek are gaining value by offering verified, peer-to-peer communities over broader, open networks.

Deep Dive

  • VSCO is integrating AI tools to enhance the artistic process, supporting creatives rather than replacing them.
  • The new AI Lab suite includes a mood-boarding tool with generative AI and community content, and VSCO Hub for visual search connecting brands with photographers.
  • AI is seen as a tool for streamlining mundane tasks like culling and editing large photo batches, preserving the integrity of the craft.
  • The company emphasizes AI's role in augmenting lighting and composition, helping photographers achieve their vision.

  • OpenAI's decision to permit erotica for verified adults in text-based AI generated significant online debate and comparison to tech giants like Apple (PG) and Google (PG-13).
  • Disappointment was expressed regarding the 'porn bots' potential, while also acknowledging the adult industry's estimated $1 billion annual revenue.
  • Analysis suggests this revenue-maximizing strategy may indicate AGI is not imminent, shifting focus from brand prestige to valuation justification.
  • OpenAI reported $13 billion ARR but incurred an $8 billion loss in the first half of the year, projecting a $20 billion run rate loss.

  • Anthropic is reportedly projecting $26 billion in annualized revenue by 2026.
  • Speculation centers on OpenAI's future revenue drivers, including commerce and ads, given their current $13 billion run rate.
  • OpenAI's delayed product updates, allowing for public reaction to policy changes, are speculated as a strategy to absorb negative sentiment before implementing adult content options.

  • Plaid introduced LendScore, a new credit scoring system utilizing real-time cash flow and network data for accurate borrower assessment.
  • LendScore analyzes up-to-date income and spending habits, aiming to improve lending outcomes by approximately 20% and increase loan eligibility.
  • The business model involves selling the service on a per-loan application basis to lenders, similar to traditional credit scores.
  • Plaid also enhanced its 'Protect' suite, an anti-fraud model using AI to identify risky user behavior and enable faster onboarding for legitimate customers.

  • Amazon launched new Echo devices with Alexa Plus, an enhanced conversational AI, alongside color Kindle Scribe models and 4K Ring cameras.
  • Alexa's transition to Large Language Models (LLMs) involves re-architecting from deterministic interactions to a more conversational, personalized experience with memory and empathy.
  • Panos Panay stated that Alexa Plus orchestrates existing skills and services, aiming to benefit developers by handling more general user requests.
  • Increased adoption of Alexa Plus is noted across Amazon's current device lineup, including smart glasses and earbuds.

  • Workweek creates specialized professional networking platforms offering verified, peer-to-peer conversations within specific industries.
  • The platform verticalizes by profession, preventing spam and fostering trusted discussions in fields like healthcare and marketing.
  • Targeted impressions on Workweek's verified professional audiences are thousands of times more valuable than on general platforms like LinkedIn.
  • The model draws from the success of Doximity, a $10 billion professional network for doctors that uses verification and minimal funding.

  • Poolside, an AI company, plans to build large-scale data centers in the Permian Basin, valued at $14 billion, utilizing natural gas resources.
  • This move towards vertical integration into physical infrastructure is noted as distinct from companies focusing solely on the application layer.
  • The Wall Street Journal indicates a 'frothiest AI bubble' in energy stocks, with non-revenue generating companies like Oklo ($26 billion valuation) experiencing soaring valuations.
  • A $16 billion, two-gigawatt data center project's cost distribution between physical structure and components is under debate.

  • Campfire secured $65 million in Series B funding, bringing their total funding to $100 million for its AI-native ERP system.
  • The company aims to modernize accounting processes for the mid-market by developing its own foundational model trained on accounting data.
  • Campfire automates transactional tasks like bank reconciliations and handles large volumes of data that legacy systems cannot.
  • Demand stems from aging ERP systems and the desire for consumer-grade software with advanced AI integration.

  • Finch Legal raised $20 million in Series A funding led by Redpoint Ventures, aiming to enhance personal injury law firms' efficiency.
  • The company integrates experienced paralegals with AI agents to manage pre-litigation tasks.
  • This AI-integrated approach enables law firms to handle up to four times more cases effectively.
  • Finch Legal has achieved a tenfold growth in the past six months, attributing success to the direct economic incentive for firms on contingency fees.
