Weekly Recap: Apple Debuts iPhone 17, Einstein of Wall Street, YC Demo Day, Declining AI Adoption

Key Takeaways

  • Enterprise AI adoption shows a reported decline in large firms but an increase in embedded usage.
  • YC Demo Day introduced a range of AI startups from brand agents to defense systems.
  • Peter Tuchman, 'Einstein of Wall Street,' discussed IPO dynamics, including the WeWork failure.
  • Apple's iPhone 17 Pro features enhanced graphics and an LLM-optimized chip, enabling on-device AI.
  • The podcast details on-device AI's potential for developers to create viral applications.
  • Apple is shifting over 51% of US iPhone assembly to India amid supply chain changes.
  • OpenAI's restructuring into a public benefit corporation includes a $100 billion nonprofit equity.
  • Microsoft's 20% revenue share deal with OpenAI until 2030 raises sustainability questions.

Deep Dive

  • The discussion critiqued Apple's marketing of each new iPhone as its "most powerful ever" and noted the design's camera bump.
  • Debate covered whether design choices like the camera bump are driven by accessory sales or user preference for thinner phones.
  • Speculation explored the potential of on-device LLMs on the new iPhone, possibly running GPT-4 locally with 12 gigabytes of RAM.

  • A U.S. Census Bureau chart indicated declining AI adoption among large firms (over 250 employees) from 14% to 12%.
  • AI adoption increased among small firms (1-4 employees), contrasting with the trend in larger companies.
  • A government estimate of 8% business AI usage conflicted with a Ramp estimate of 43% for companies with paid AI subscriptions.
  • Despite a potential decrease in self-reported AI usage, actual adoption is expected to increase as AI integrates into operations like fraud detection, often without explicit user recognition.

  • Reacher builds AI agents for brands to find creators and generate viral content scripts.
  • Gauss provides personal AI investment analysis for retail investors, helping avoid emotional trading decisions.
  • Perseus Defense develops 16-inch missiles to counter drones, aiming to fill a gap in existing counter-UAS solutions.
  • Meteor is an AI-native browser designed to replace Google Chrome by automating redundant web tasks and acting as a co-pilot for users.

  • Nazomio offers an API that gives AI agents more context from documentation or codebases, addressing LLM memory persistence and context window pollution.
  • Veritas Agent builds AI agents specifically for consumer lending, streamlining financial processes.
  • Agent Mill introduces 'email for your AI,' enabling AI agents to communicate and manage tasks, a need discussed by Sequoia and Lang Chain.
  • Kalinda provides a deep research tool for class action law firms, designed to process millions of pages of data for litigation.

  • Peter Tuchman, known as the 'Einstein of Wall Street,' increased his TikTok following from 260,000 to over 419,000 through his 'Einstein Elementary' series.
  • He detailed his experience with IPOs, including LinkedIn and Alibaba, and emphasized the importance of a structured price discovery process.
  • Tuchman described the WeWork IPO as a turning point that negatively impacted the market for years after its abortion due to misrepresented financials.

  • Apple's iPhone 17 Pro announcement highlighted graphics power surpassing an M2 MacBook Air and camera advancements like a square sensor.
  • Despite upgrades including a vapor chamber for cooling and 50% more RAM, the device's lack of a major form factor change led to some disappointment.
  • The new iPhone chip incorporates LLM transformer architecture, signaling Apple's move towards AI optimization, mirroring strategies used by OpenAI and NVIDIA.

  • On-device AI, utilizing 7 billion parameter models, is feasible for tasks like email assistance, enabling free, meterless inference comparable to GPT-4 for consumer applications.
  • Apple Intelligence is criticized for lacking a 'killer app' and offering incremental features, creating an opportunity for third-party developers.
  • The ease of mobile app distribution, unlike Mac apps, is highlighted as a factor that could fuel viral growth through user-generated content and 'magical LLM experiences.'

  • Over 51% of iPhones designated for the American market are now assembled in India, a strategic move by CEO Tim Cook to mitigate tariffs and geopolitical risks.
  • Apple faces ongoing battery life challenges, with reports indicating a 'plateau' being added to the back of Air and Pro models to accommodate larger chips, increasing device thickness.

  • OpenAI is restructuring into a Delaware public benefit corporation, with its nonprofit arm controlling its for-profit arm and holding over $100 billion in equity.
  • This structure aims to enable large-scale capital raising while adhering to OpenAI's mission of benefiting humanity.
  • Microsoft's deal grants them 20% of OpenAI's revenue until 2030, raising concerns about sustainability due to OpenAI's high costs, including payments to Broadcom and Oracle.
