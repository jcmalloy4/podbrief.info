Full Interview: Eric Seufert on AI and Advertising

Key Takeaways

  • OpenAI is strategically launching a basic ad platform, leveraging ex-Meta talent for future sophistication.
  • Meta's substantial investments in generative AI are driving re-accelerated ad growth and increased return on ad spend.
  • Google is integrating Gemini into its search functionality, aiming to expand ad surface area through AI overviews.
  • Apple faces scrutiny regarding its AI strategy, with its partnership with Google Gemini raising privacy concerns.
  • User behavior in Europe indicates a preference for free services supported by personalized ads over subscription paywalls.

Deep Dive

  • ChatGPT initiated ad rollout, a move anticipated due to the hiring of Fiji Simo, who previously led Facebook's mobile newsfeed ads product.
  • The current ad offering is described as a basic testing phase with high CPMs and limited targeting and measurement capabilities.
  • This initial ad strategy is likened to Netflix's early ad-tier launch, indicating a phased approach to platform development.

  • OpenAI's 4% instant checkout fee is viewed as unworkable for retailers and primarily serves as a 'stalking horse' to gather conversion data.
  • The guest predicts OpenAI will likely reduce or eliminate the 4% fee to boost conversions, eventually transitioning to a bidding system for ad placements.
  • Retailers using the instant checkout feature cannot capture customer data for remarketing, as OpenAI's terms prohibit using customer emails for future marketing.

  • Compounding effects of AI-driven advertising improvements are critical for digital advertising success, particularly for direct-response platforms like Meta.
  • Meta's growth is re-accelerating due to reinvestment of ad profits, driven by increasing Return on Ad Spend (ROAS) and quarterly performance bumps.
  • Mark Zuckerberg's significant capital expenditure in generative AI positions Meta as a major player in AI monetization alongside Google.

  • Future advertisements are expected to be hyper-personalized, representing a significant shift from traditional mass advertising.
  • User behavior indicates a tolerance for such personalized ads if they provide access to free services.
  • Low opt-in rates for subscription services in Europe suggest users prefer free access, even with personalized ads, over paywall models.

  • TikTok is reportedly re-engaging with its e-commerce strategy after a period of uncertainty, potentially aiming to fill a gap left by Meta's reduced focus.
  • The guest expresses skepticism about the viability of this e-commerce model for Western audiences, favoring an ad-driven approach for TikTok.
  • Concerns are raised regarding whether new owners will continue ByteDance's previous subsidies for TikTok's e-commerce, given the high revenue share with ByteDance.

  • Apple's AI strategy is described as 'embarrassing,' potentially stemming from difficulties in reconciling AI development with its privacy-focused brand image.
  • The partnership with Google for Gemini is viewed as outsourcing data sourcing and monetization, which raises questions about Apple's commitment to privacy.
  • Apple's AI development is perceived as slow, and the long iPhone replacement cycle, particularly with secondhand devices, challenges the introduction of compute-intensive AI features.
