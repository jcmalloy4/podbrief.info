#1010 - Bernie Sanders - Who Actually Runs the US Government?

Key Takeaways

  • Extreme income and wealth inequality fosters an "oligarchy," leaving 60% of Americans living paycheck to paycheck.
  • Average American wages have stagnated for 52 years, while essential costs like healthcare and education have skyrocketed.
  • The Democratic Party shifted away from its working-class base, driven by reliance on wealthy donors and corporations.
  • Campaign finance reform is critical, with the Citizens United decision enabling billionaires to dominate politics.
  • Declining birth rates are a concern, linked to high childcare costs and economic anxieties among young people.
  • The Democratic Party is perceived as neglecting issues affecting men, including rising suicide rates and loneliness.
  • The guest expresses deep concern for U.S. democracy's future, citing rising authoritarianism and "Trumpism."

Deep Dive

  • Bernie Sanders highlights unprecedented income and wealth inequality, with 60% of Americans living paycheck to paycheck.
  • Real inflation-adjusted weekly wages are lower than in the early 1970s, while $75 trillion transferred from the bottom 90% to the top 1% over 52 years.
  • Critical costs like healthcare and education have skyrocketed over 200% since 2000; 20 million households spend over 50% of income on housing.

  • The Republican Party exhibits "Stalinist type allegiance," with fear of backlash from Trump and billionaires deterring dissent.
  • Senator Tom Tillis reversed a stance on a Trump bill under pressure, illustrating this dynamic.
  • A similar dynamic exists in the Democratic Party regarding opposition to President Biden's policies on Gaza funding.

  • The "corrupt campaign finance system" and the Citizens United Supreme Court decision enable billionaires to fund Super PACs.
  • Elon Musk reportedly spent $270 million to help elect Donald Trump.
  • The guest proposes overturning Citizens United and implementing public funding for elections to level the playing field for challengers.

  • Since the 1970s, the Democratic Party shifted away from its working-class base by accepting money from wealthy donors and corporations.
  • This led to supporting trade agreements causing job losses and neglecting critical issues like healthcare and education costs.
  • The guest describes this reliance on wealthy funding as a "tragic era" that alienated working-class voters.

  • The guest questions the immense wealth of individuals like Elon Musk, who holds more wealth than the bottom 52% of American households.
  • He argues against the necessity of billionaires possessing tens or hundreds of billions while many struggle, despite acknowledging innovation.
  • A Pew Research Center poll indicates 58% of Americans feel worse off today than 50 years prior.

  • The guest expresses concern over declining birth rates, attributing it to high childcare costs and economic anxieties among young people.
  • Projections show significant population decreases in countries like South Korea.
  • He suggests Democrats have not adequately promoted family values or created conditions for families to feel good about having children.

  • While social justice issues are crucial, the Democratic Party's focus on identity politics has neglected the economic needs of the working class.
  • This approach, without adequately addressing immigration, crime, and DEI concerns, makes it difficult to resonate with the electorate.
  • Divisive "us vs. them" tactics are used to distract from core issues like income inequality and the climate crisis.

  • The guest expresses opposition to abolishing ICE but criticizes its "authoritarian" operations under Trump, likening them to vigilante actions.
  • He emphasizes the disregard for due process rights in current actions.
  • He advocates for a comprehensive and sane immigration policy for the United States.

  • The guest acknowledges that the Democratic Party has overlooked issues concerning men, historically focusing on women's inequalities.
  • Statistics indicate rising male suicide rates, lower college enrollment, and increased loneliness, especially among lower-income men.
  • Progressive institutions are perceived to have a "blind spot" regarding male issues, sometimes viewing men as "the problem."
