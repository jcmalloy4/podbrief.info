Modern Wisdom

#1001 - Ryan Holiday - Stoicism’s Lessons on Becoming Wise

Key Takeaways

  • Wisdom is an active, continuous pursuit involving learning from accumulated knowledge and personal experience, distinct from mere intelligence.
  • Humility, empathy, and challenging existing beliefs are crucial for developing wisdom and avoiding intellectual complacency.
  • Historical accounts and Stoic philosophy offer frameworks for understanding human experience, developing resilience, and navigating adversity.
  • Over-reliance on past successes, ego, biased information, and unresolved personal issues can prevent intelligent individuals from achieving true wisdom.

Deep Dive

  • The guest refused to alter a talk on ethics and courage at the Naval Academy, leading to revocation of their speaking invitation.
  • The academy removed hundreds of books from its library and censored discussions, mirroring past attempts to ban J.K. Rowling's series.
  • Admiral James Stockdale's study of Marxism to understand enemy ideology was presented as a counter-example to censorship.

  • Wisdom is described as the most elusive virtue, encompassing experience, knowledge, intelligence, and insight.
  • It is a continuous loop of learning from humanity's accumulated knowledge, applying it, and repeating the process, rather than passive learning.
  • Humility is crucial for continuous learning, as increased knowledge expands the 'shoreline of ignorance'.

  • Traditions and precedents often represent hard-won solutions to complex problems, which modern society tends to discard.
  • The Gordian knot analogy illustrates how superficial solutions can miss underlying complexity, contrasting with acknowledging established systems.
  • Silicon Valley's risk tolerance differs from geopolitics, where failures lead to catastrophic outcomes, underscoring the importance of established procedures.

  • Wisdom is essential for navigating future difficult decisions and challenges, distinguishing it from innate intelligence.
  • It must be accumulated proactively over time, not acquired through shortcuts or external sources like artificial intelligence.
  • The anecdote of a wealthy Roman hiring literate slaves illustrates that borrowed knowledge is not genuine understanding.

  • When individuals experience significant loss, the paradox of choice can cause disorientation.
  • A Stoic approach suggests returning to formative experiences and reassessing them with a new self.
  • Journaling helps maintain a connection with one's past self to gain perspective during difficult times.

  • Admiral James Stockdale endured years of torture and solitary confinement as a prisoner of war in the Hanoi Hilton during the Vietnam War.
  • He possessed sensitive information about the Gulf of Tonkin incident, making him a high-value target for his captors.
  • Stockdale self-maimed by beating his face and cutting his scalp to become 'unfilmable' and resist psychological warfare.

  • The guest reflects on evolving motivations, shifting from seeking extrinsic rewards like bestseller status to valuing the intrinsic satisfaction of the writing process.
  • He recounts a moment of uncertainty awaiting book success, choosing presence over dwelling on outcomes.
  • This illustrates managing focus, similar to disciplined physical training.

  • Intelligent individuals often fail to achieve wisdom due to ego, which filters information based on self-perception rather than reality.
  • Consuming biased information that confirms existing beliefs also hinders growth.
  • Complacency, or the cessation of learning, is a significant obstacle, as one cannot learn what they believe they already know.

  • External factors such as sleep deprivation, drug use, stress, and loneliness can cumulatively impair cognitive function, leading to breakdowns.
  • Highly intelligent individuals, especially those accustomed to being right, are vulnerable to catastrophic failures when over-relying on past successes.
  • Resisting feedback can stem from a '15-year-old's defiance' rather than genuine insight.
