#997 - Bonnie Blue & Louise Perry - The Modern Sex Work Debate

Key Takeaways

  • Bonnie Blue asserts agency and happiness in sex work, driven by financial independence and lifestyle choices.
  • Louise Perry analyzes Bonnie Blue as an extreme manifestation of the sexual revolution's contradictions.
  • The discussion explores the cultural and societal impacts of Bonnie's work, including potential misrepresentation of women.
  • Bonnie Blue's psychological resilience to criticism and her unique personality are highlighted.
  • Past fertility struggles influenced Bonnie's life trajectory, but not her view of sex as pleasure.
  • The ethical challenges of sex as a commodity and its market dynamics are debated.
  • Bonnie employs a controversial social media strategy to provoke engagement and highlight relationship issues.
  • Bonnie refutes victim narratives, asserting her control and agency despite online accusations.

Deep Dive

  • Bonnie Blue states she is not traumatized by her sex work, attributing well-being to personal choice and success factors like travel and family time.
  • She argues women marrying for financial security engage in a similar transaction to sex work, claiming her approach is more honest.
  • The conversation contrasts the precarious financial situation of women dependent on husbands with the contractual nature of sex work, where workers retain agency.
  • Bonnie asserts personal control over her career, focusing on present happiness and not being bound by past declarations about quitting.

  • Bonnie Blue transitioned into sex work for financial independence after disliking a recruitment job, beginning after a short stint on Camin and OnlyFans.
  • She recounts the accidental origin of her group sex participation during a spring break, finding a gangbang more humorous than sexual.
  • A journalist suggested pursuing a world record for group sex events, which Bonnie later attempted in January after growing her social media presence.
  • Bonnie implemented a controversial social media strategy targeting women and partners, aiming to provoke reactions and highlight underlying relationship issues.

  • Louise Perry suggests Bonnie Blue is an extreme manifestation of the sexual revolution, holding a mirror to societal contradictions regarding sexual freedom and capitalism.
  • Bonnie argues her actions align with societal messages of liberation and personal priority, seeing them as a natural consequence of modern feminism.
  • She highlights a double standard, noting that actions acceptable for men are condemned when she performs them, particularly regarding the 'teen' category in adult content.
  • Louise Perry questions the term 'empowering' for Bonnie's work, acknowledging financial liberation but citing collateral damage to others.

  • The discussion addresses the potential for broader cultural damage from Bonnie Blue's work, referencing her 'thousand in' event in January.
  • Louise Perry expresses concern that her high-profile sex work might overshadow critical societal issues and be misconstrued as representative of all women.
  • The conversation explores the perception that Western pornography, particularly featuring white women, could negatively influence foreign men's views, leading to harmful stereotypes.
  • Perry clarifies her personal actions do not represent all British women and actively portrays women as diverse, not inherently 'sluts.'

  • Louise Perry's past work successfully changed the law regarding consent in BDSM deaths, addressing a loophole where men claimed consent for fatal acts.
  • Reduced manslaughter sentences for men were linked to domestic violence, with pornography's normalization of rough sex, including choking, potentially influencing jury perceptions.
  • The conversation shifts to the cultural impact of pornography, suggesting trends in sexual techniques popularized by figures like Bonnie Blue can lead to harmful behaviors.
  • Bonnie Blue asserts her content differs from mainstream pornography by explicitly discussing consent on camera and showing partners communicating about sexual acts.

  • Statistics show an increase in men under 25 supporting stricter online pornography access.
  • The conversation explores why performers like Bonnie Blue remain successful despite this trend, attributing it to a dedicated audience and potential negative impacts on younger generations.
  • Negative impacts of porn addiction and unrealistic expectations for men are discussed, advocating for open discussions about sexual health.
  • It is argued that increased friction in accessing pornography leads to decreased consumption, with a reported 47% drop.

  • Bonnie Blue explains that while sex work offers financial and time flexibility, it may limit future career choices and impact her children.
  • She details downsides of her fame, noting constant approaches for photos by fans, including families, which necessitates security measures.
  • Bonnie contrasts intense online hate with positive in-person reception, attributing her resilience to a lack of concern for others' judgments.
  • She notes a significant increase in confidence since starting her work, attributing it to positive feedback outweighing the negative.

  • Bonnie Blue addresses backlash from within the porn industry, attributing it to her making others' content seem less extreme and claims of promoting rape culture.
  • Louise Perry notes the economic realities of the sex industry, where treating sex as a commodity creates ethical challenges through market dynamics.
  • Perry suggests Bonnie Blue's work highlights fundamental problems with the worldview supporting sexual liberation and pornography by taking arguments to their logical conclusion.
  • Bonnie discusses challenges of content creation due to frequent social media bans and restrictions, requiring adaptation.

  • Bonnie discusses her past struggles with fertility and her decision not to lie about pregnancy rumors, finding them hurtful due to her IVF difficulties.
  • Her 18-month fertility struggle led to a realization that redirected her career and future outlook, grateful she did not become pregnant then.
  • Bonnie clarifies that infertility did not alter her fundamental views on sex, which was always a pleasure-seeking activity for her.
  • She recounts public perception and media twisting her words, even when she helped others with IVF, leading to negative labels like 'psychopath.'

  • Bonnie attributes public fascination to her unusual nature and self-chosen role as a controversial figure, despite claiming normalcy in daily life.
  • Chris Williamson describes Bonnie as a 'high-agency person' who achieves her goals, an assessment Bonnie agrees with.
  • Bonnie expresses strong disapproval of lazy individuals who avoid responsibility, contrasting with her own proactive approach.
  • She reflects on her financial success, aiming for five million per month, but emphasizes happiness and experiences over sheer numbers.

  • Bonnie Blue expresses disgust at people fabricating victim stories and creating fake assault narratives based on old photos.
  • She describes a TikTok video that falsely accused her deceased grandfather of childhood sexual assault, highlighting the online harm to her family.
  • Louise Perry states she does not view Bonnie Blue as a victim, acknowledging Blue's unusual nature compared to most in the sex industry.
  • Bonnie observes that people, particularly women, are motivated to view her as a victim, disturbed by the idea that she genuinely enjoys her work.

  • The host introduces 'steelmanning' arguments, asking guests to present the strongest cases for and against Bonnie's lifestyle and worldview.
  • Louise Perry outlines her steelman argument against Bonnie's work, which would involve criminalizing her actions and removing her financial gains.
  • Bonnie discusses her desire for greater open-mindedness towards sex workers, emphasizing they are not inherently disgusting or harmful.
  • The host likens Bonnie to 'LeBron James of Porn' and the 'reductio ad absurdum of the sexual revolution,' highlighting public fascination.
