The Prof G Pod with Scott Galloway

Scott Galloway Answers Your Questions on Resist and Unsubscribe

Key Takeaways

  • The 'Resist and Unsubscribe' campaign advocates for economic pressure over outrage as a lever for change.
  • Targeting subscription revenue is considered more impactful due to its direct effect on a company's market capitalization.
  • The campaign acknowledges potential downsides including job losses, political backlash, and personal risks for participants.
  • Efforts to pressure advertisers on platforms like Meta are deemed less effective compared to consumer-led action.

Deep Dive

  • Scott Galloway launched the 'Resist and Unsubscribe' campaign, emphasizing economic pressure over outrage.
  • The campaign directs listeners to a website for guidance on unsubscribing from specific companies.

  • Potential downsides include job losses, though large companies already face AI-driven layoffs.
  • Galloway is concerned the campaign could backfire if politicized, leading opposing groups to increase support for targeted companies.
  • The host acknowledges personal and professional risks, such as canceled speaking gigs and advertisers fleeing the platform.

  • The podcast segment shifted to discussing former President Trump's approval ratings.
  • Clips from Trump voters presented their defense, citing economic performance and border policy as successes.
  • Voters expressed skepticism about media narratives surrounding recent events.

  • Targeting advertisers is difficult for platforms like Meta, where no single advertiser controls a significant portion of spending.
  • Past attempts to pressure Meta advertisers failed as advertisers shifted spending to other platforms like YouTube.
  • The host continues to use Meta platforms despite advocating for their breakup due to their extensive reach.

  • Reducing subscription revenue has a significantly greater impact on a company than reducing spending on other goods or services.
  • A small change in subscription revenue can substantially affect a company's market capitalization.
  • Meta derives 98% of its revenue from advertising, contrasting with Amazon's growing ad arm versus subscription revenue impact.
