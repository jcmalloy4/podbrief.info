The Prof G Pod with Scott Galloway

No Mercy / No Malice: Resist and Unsubscribe

Key Takeaways

  • A national economic strike, specifically targeting tech and AI companies, is proposed to counter government actions.
  • The administration is perceived to respond primarily to market performance, not public protests or outrage.
  • Consumers hold significant power to influence the economy and policy, particularly through concentrated spending reductions.
  • Corporate leaders are hesitant to challenge the president due to fear of retaliation, contrasting with activism from rank-and-file workers.
  • The proposed strike emphasizes a quiet, month-long economic slowdown as a more potent form of protest than traditional marches.

Deep Dive

  • Attorney General Keith Ellison sued the Trump administration over ICE activity in Minneapolis, raising concerns about federal troops.
  • Scott Galloway introduced the concept of a 'crisis of stupid,' questioning societal intelligence.
  • A national economic strike for February is proposed as a weapon against government actions, specifically targeting tech and AI companies.

  • The movement draws inspiration from tech CEOs like Sam Altman and Tim Cook, suggesting their companies' growth could impact valuations.
  • A month-long economic strike, proposed to start February 1st, aims to reduce consumer spending, particularly targeting tech and AI companies.
  • The US economy's heavy reliance on AI and seven major tech companies, constituting over one-third of the S&P 500, makes them vulnerable to such a strike.

  • Proposed actions include unsubscribing from services like ChatGPT, Claude, Amazon, and Apple, and delaying new device purchases like iPhones.
  • The strike could expand to target companies with ICE contracts, such as AT&T, Dell, and FedEx, with options detailed on Resist and Unsubscribe website.
  • Participants are encouraged to document their actions on Instagram by sharing cancellation screenshots and 'unsubscribed selfies'.
  • A 10% spending reduction by wealthy households and 5% by middle/lower income households could significantly curb US GDP.

  • Economic data, rather than the death toll, was identified as the primary driver of government spending during the COVID-19 pandemic.
  • Americans in the top 10% income bracket, who represent half of all consumer spending, are deemed crucial for the strike's effectiveness.
  • An October estimate projected that a 3% cut in consumer spending could lead to a 1% GDP decline, excluding economic multipliers.
  • President Trump's 'taco trade' negotiation tactics are characterized by aggressive initial stances followed by reversals when markets react negatively.

  • President Trump filed a lawsuit against JPMorgan Chase, indicating corporate leaders' reluctance to challenge him due to fear of retaliation.
  • Over 450 tech employees signed a letter urging CEOs to push back against ICE policies and cancel contracts.
  • This worker action is contrasted with executive outrage over a wealth tax versus ICE's actions, highlighting a divergence in courage.
