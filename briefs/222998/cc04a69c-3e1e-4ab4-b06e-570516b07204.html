Capital Allocators – Inside the Institutional Investment Industry

CIO Greatest Hits: Endowments – Andy Golden (Princo)

Key Takeaways

  • Princeton's endowment strategy evolved from trustee-led decisions to a staff-centric model empowering investment professionals with full discretion.
  • Princo emphasizes a 'beyond the long term' investment horizon, valuing enduring discomfort and theoretical conviction over short-term market data.
  • The firm's core philosophy centers on partnering with highly motivated external managers, leveraging their specialized expertise across a diverse, equity-biased portfolio.
  • Decision-making at Princo employs a unique 'bull bear' process, fostering robust debate and depersonalizing investment choices to mitigate emotional bias.
  • Success hinges on 'alignment of appetites' with managers, focusing on shared motivation for competitive returns rather than solely contractual fee structures.

Deep Dives

The Evolution of Princo's Investment Philosophy

  • Andy Golden's career at Princeton Investment Management Company involved transforming its investment approach from a committee-driven model to one empowering staff with full discretion.
  • The firm adopted a core philosophy centered on strategic use of external managers, a long-term horizon, and the principle of buying low and selling high.
  • This evolution was critical in establishing a robust decision-making framework that could withstand market volatility and adapt to changing investment landscapes.

Princo's Distinctive Portfolio Construction and Manager Selection

  • Princeton's investment strategy is built upon an equity-biased policy portfolio, with significant allocations to hedge funds and private equity, balanced by midterm adjustments.
  • The firm embraces a 'beyond the long term' philosophy, valuing enduring discomfort and theoretical conviction over short-term market data, and seeks to go beyond typical 10-year outlooks.
  • Manager evaluation emphasizes understanding the people, their motivations, and execution, rather than relying solely on short-term performance or 'black box' strategies.
  • Princo seeks an 'alignment of appetites' with managers, focusing on shared motivation for competitive returns rather than prioritizing contractual fee structures alone.

Cultivating a High-Performance Investment Culture

  • Princo fosters a unique investment culture, emphasizing intellectual challenge, a shared sense of mission, and a long-term horizon to attract and retain talent.
  • The firm employs a 'one team, one dream' approach, with a bottom-up asset allocation process and junior staff exposed to diverse investment ideas to foster comparative thinking.
  • Major decisions are made through a 'bull bear' session, a system developed post-Global Financial Crisis to depersonalize debate, encourage robust arguments, and mitigate power dynamics.

Strategic Use of External Managers and Fee Considerations

  • Princo's primary strategy involves extensive use of external managers, viewing them as crucial collaborators, leveraging the firm's attractiveness as a client and its supportive governance structure.
  • Despite significant allocations to alternative assets, Princo focuses on optimizing fees to ensure managers are sufficiently incentivized to generate superior returns rather than simply minimizing them.
  • The firm prioritizes transparency and justification for all fees, questioning charges that do not directly align with genuine value creation beyond basic incentive structures.
