Imperfect Leaders

Michael Paul, CEO of MaraBio - Finding 'Ability' in Disability

Key Takeaways

  • MARAbio utilizes maternal immune science to identify and potentially prevent severe forms of autism, targeting up to 20% of cases.
  • CEO Michael Paul advocates for trust over invulnerability, fostering psychological safety and purpose-driven leadership within his organization.
  • The company balances scientific innovation with ethical considerations, offering preconception and postnatal testing while developing treatments for early intervention.
  • Authentic company values, developed through employee involvement, are foundational to MARAbio's culture and its mission to serve the neurodiverse community.

Deep Dive

  • MARAbio, short for Maternal Autoantibody Related Autism, was founded based on Dr. Judy Van de Water's discovery at UC Davis.
  • Research indicates that up to 20% of women with autistic children possess specific maternal autoantibodies.
  • These maternal antibodies can transfer across the placenta around 100 days of gestation, potentially impacting fetal brain development.
  • The prevalence of autism is rising, affecting approximately 1 in 30 newborns and impacting hundreds of thousands of families.

  • MARAbio is developing a precision immunology platform to test for and neutralize maternal antibodies, aiming to reduce the incidence of a specific autism subtype.
  • Ethical considerations guide MARAbio's approach, currently limiting prenatal testing until effective treatments are available.
  • Testing is offered preconceptionally and postnatally to provide accurate, clinically relevant information for families.
  • Early diagnosis and intervention for autism are crucial, supported by tools like MCHAT and emerging technologies such as eye-tracking and hair analysis for risk identification.

  • Michael Paul holds a PhD in biochemistry and molecular biology, transitioning from scientific research to the biotech industry's business side.
  • His career includes roles at Amgen and founding Lineagen (2006-2007), a company focused on genetic testing for autism, which was sold in 2020.
  • Paul's motivation for working in the autism space is rooted in a belief in choosing trust over invulnerability, influenced by Patrick Lencione.
  • MARAbio's stated purpose is to eradicate the most severe forms of autism, a clear mission that unites the company's vision.

  • Trust is considered essential for effective internal debates, leading to organizational commitment, alignment, and clear communication of decisions.
  • The company's decision-making process emphasizes psychological safety, diverse viewpoints, designated owners, and accountability for collective results.
  • Discussions with the board of directors are approached as debates, requiring over-communication, particularly concerning challenges.
  • Balancing investor expectations for profitability with the unpredictable timeline of scientific discovery is a challenge, highlighting the critical role of early investors.

  • The CEO's role demands continuous personal growth and adaptation as the company progresses from discovery through commercialization stages.
  • Core values and company culture are paramount, leading to difficult decisions such as declining to hire a 'superstar' candidate if they do not align with company principles.
  • Authentic company values, including 'grit,' are developed through broad employee involvement rather than superficial statements.
  • The guest is currently involved in transitioning MARAbio towards product development, leveraging past experience leading a company from startup to exit.

  • MARAbio aims to significantly impact autism prevalence by reducing the incidence of a specific subtype, potentially affecting up to 20% of individuals worldwide.
  • The company actively recruits neurodiverse talent and aims to make testing family-friendly, developing tools like a buccal swab for DNA collection based on community feedback.
  • Ongoing CEO development focuses on continuous improvement in communication, adapting strengths, and maintaining 'dogged persistence.'
  • Protecting authentic company values, developed with employee involvement, is critical for organizational health and mission achievement.
