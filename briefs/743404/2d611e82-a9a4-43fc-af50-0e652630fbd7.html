The Indicator from Planet Money

Can American cities grow AND stay affordable?

Key Takeaways

  • Rapid urban growth can significantly increase housing costs, threatening affordability.
  • Proactive city planning, like Huntsville's, emphasizes increasing housing supply to manage growth.
  • Diversifying housing types, including 'missing middle' options, addresses varied housing demands.
  • Addressing low-income housing affordability often requires subsidies beyond market-rate construction.

Deep Dive

  • Rapid growth in cities such as Austin and Atlanta has led to significant increases in housing costs.
  • Austin's home prices have more than tripled in the last two decades, significantly outpacing the national average.
  • This illustrates an economic paradox where a low cost of living attracts growth, which then erodes affordability.

  • Huntsville city planners, including Dennis Madsen, began anticipating a housing crisis about 10 years ago.
  • Their strategy has focused on increasing housing supply, with the city adding 16,000 apartment units since 2020.
  • The broader North Alabama region has seen substantial new home construction, partly due to fewer building regulations and available land.

  • Huntsville permitted 900 traditional homes, 1,400 apartments, and 200 townhomes in the past year.
  • Diversification of housing types, including 'missing middle housing' like quads, addresses evolving family structures.
  • Building more housing, even expensive units, can indirectly increase affordability by diverting demand from existing lower-cost stock.

  • UNI Freemark of the Urban Institute states that affordable housing for low-income individuals requires additional subsidy.
  • The construction of expensive apartments alone is insufficient to address the specific needs of low-income housing affordability.
