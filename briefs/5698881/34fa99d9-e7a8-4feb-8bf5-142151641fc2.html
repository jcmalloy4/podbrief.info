The Mel Robbins Podcast

#1 Mindset Expert: Simple Mindset Shifts That Transform Your Body, Energy, & Life

Key Takeaways

  • Mindsets, acting as mental 'settings,' fundamentally shape personal reality, influencing biology, emotions, and behavior.
  • The 'milkshake study' demonstrated that beliefs about food can physiologically alter hunger hormones and metabolism.
  • Consciously shifting mental settings can help individuals overcome fears, enhance physical performance, and improve health habits.
  • The placebo effect highlights the significant power of belief in treatment efficacy, even when substances are inactive.
  • Adopting an 'indulgent' mindset towards healthy eating and lifestyle choices can reduce cravings and foster consistency.

Deep Dive

  • Dr. Alia Crum defines 'mindset' as 'settings of the mind' or 'lenses' through which experiences are filtered.
  • These mental settings are often rooted in core beliefs and oversimplified judgments about the world or personal capabilities.
  • Dr. Crum's research explores how specific mindsets impact various situations, including health diagnoses and job interviews.

  • A Yale University milkshake study gave participants the same 350-calorie shake but with different labels: 'indulgent' (620 calories) or 'sensible' (140 calories).
  • Participants believing they consumed an 'indulgent' shake experienced a threefold drop in ghrelin, the hunger hormone.
  • This research demonstrates how mindsets about food can biologically impact hunger and metabolism, challenging the simple 'calories in, calories out' model.

  • Mind settings influence individuals through four core mechanisms: focus, emotions, motivation, and physiological responses.
  • The example of a production assistant overcoming a decade-long fear of flying illustrates how reframing a fear as an 'old setting' can alter these mechanisms.
  • Awareness of unhelpful beliefs can be sufficient to change mental settings, not always requiring extensive therapeutic intervention.

  • The brain continuously forms simplified assumptions, or 'settings,' from birth until death to make sense of the world.
  • These internal settings are often influenced by external factors such as upbringing and cultural norms.
  • Individuals possess the power to consciously change these settings to create more beneficial and empowering internal frameworks.

  • The total effect of medication or food is a combination of the substance itself and the belief in its efficacy.
  • A study on the migraine pill Maxalt demonstrated that altering its label, even with the same active ingredient, significantly affected its reported effectiveness.
  • Research indicates that placebos, even when patients know they are inactive, can positively impact health outcomes, highlighting the powerful influence of belief.

  • For serious conditions like cancer, research suggests that acceptance and a belief in manageability are more effective mindsets than simple positive thinking.
  • This mindset shift has been shown to improve patients' quality of life and reduce physical symptoms during cancer treatment.
  • The guest advises combining the best available medical treatments with supportive mindsets about the illness and care received.

  • Research indicates that a 'restraint' mindset during eating triggers the body's scarcity response, leading to increased hunger.
  • Conversely, an 'indulgent' mindset, focusing on the pleasure of food, signals the body appropriately and can reduce cravings.
  • This shift from restrictive 'shoulds' to enjoyment physiologically impacts hunger signals, a concept proven by research.

  • Food industry marketing often associates unhealthy options with pleasure and happiness, while healthy foods typically lack similar indulgent promotion.
  • The guest advises reframing healthy foods, such as a salad with appealing toppings, to be perceived as indulgent.
  • This shift in mindset can make the body anticipate and crave healthier options, aiding in dietary adherence and goal achievement.

  • The host demonstrated how an initial mindset of perceived weakness made a 30-pound weight seem unliftable.
  • After consciously shifting to a mindset of capability, the host was able to lift the same 30-pound weight with relative ease.
  • Research indicates that an individual's mindset can even alter the perceived physical heaviness of an object, directly impacting physical performance.

  • The guest contrasted a past mindset of scarcity and fear around money with a current mindset of abundance and wanting to give it away.
  • Beliefs about money, encompassing scarcity versus abundance perspectives, directly influence an individual's financial behavior and outcomes.
  • Dr. Crum advises acknowledging financial goals and problems before consciously adopting supportive mindsets to effectively address them.
