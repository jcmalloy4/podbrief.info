The Alabama Murders - Part 7: The Second Warrant

Key Takeaways

  • Kenny Smith received a second execution warrant 35 years after a 1988 murder.
  • Alabama proposed nitrogen gas asphyxiation, an untested method for judicial execution.
  • Smith's legal team challenged the nitrogen gas method, citing Eighth Amendment violations.
  • The execution by nitrogen hypoxia involved observable violent, seizure-like movements.
  • Alabama officials described the execution as professional despite observed struggles.

Deep Dive

  • Kenny Smith faced a second execution warrant in November 2023 for a January 2024 date, 35 years after the murder of Elizabeth Sennett.
  • He experienced severe nightmares of execution, prompting his legal team to hire psychologist Kate Porterfield.
  • Porterfield testified about Smith's severe Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder if subjected to another execution, deeming it 'cruel and unusual'.
  • Upon receiving the second warrant, Smith was anxious and reportedly 'falling apart' while also focusing on fighting the execution.

  • Just over a month before his second scheduled execution, Kenny Smith's legal team filed a lawsuit against Commissioner John Q. Hamm of the Alabama Department of Corrections.
  • Alabama's execution appeal proposed nitrogen gas asphyxiation, a method never before used in a judicial execution.
  • Smith's lawyers argued that the novel nitrogen gas method violated the Eighth Amendment's prohibition against cruel and unusual punishment.
  • An anesthesiologist explained that while nitrogen is inert, pure nitrogen causes physiological changes, theoretically leading to unconsciousness and death without awareness.

  • Complications with nitrogen gas execution include potential brain death if oxygen seeps into the mask, or choking on vomit if the prisoner becomes nauseous.
  • Unlike lethal injection, gas execution necessitates prisoner participation, increasing potential complications.
  • Holding one's breath during nitrogen gas execution increases carbon dioxide, potentially leading to seizures and a cascade of physiological changes instead of instant unconsciousness.
  • A study on rats found carbon monoxide and nitrogen gas resulted in longer times to loss of consciousness, seizures, increased stress, and lung damage, deeming them inhumane euthanasia alternatives.

  • Alabama's proposed protocol for Kenny Smith's execution by nitrogen gas was presented on December 20th, 2023, mirroring a previous lethal injection attempt.
  • A state witness admitted the protocol did not consult medical personnel regarding issues like vomiting into the mask or external air seepage.
  • The mask used was identified as an industrial type, not designed for executions, and the witness's knowledge stemmed from internet research.
  • Testimony also revealed the protocol lacked specific procedures for ensuring a proper mask fit on the inmate.

  • Kenny Smith arranged his affairs before his January 25th execution date, naming witnesses including his mother, Linda, and legal counsel.
  • Witnesses were processed and transported to Holman Correctional Facility, awaiting the procedure.
  • Smith's mother recounted his final words of love and acceptance, noting he smiled as he was taken away.
  • Witnesses observed Smith strapped to a gurney and masked in the execution chamber as the death warrant was read.

  • Kenny Smith's final statement declared that Alabama was 'taking a step backward,' before the execution procedure began.
  • The execution differed from advertised rapid unconsciousness, as Smith exhibited violent, seizure-like movements for minutes.
  • He was observed straining against restraints and appearing to gasp for air before falling still.
  • A witness expressed feeling 'awful' about the unsuccessful 18-year effort to prevent the execution.

  • Kenneth Eugene Smith was executed by nitrogen hypoxia at Holman Correctional Facility for the 1988 murder of Elizabeth Sennett.
  • The Commissioner of the Alabama Department of Corrections described the execution as professional, despite observed shaking and involuntary movements.
  • The Alabama Attorney General's office issued a press release calling the execution 'historic' and a 'blueprint' for other states, despite international criticism.
  • The host criticized the state official's use of 'historic,' contrasting it with the narrative of the 'cascade' of events leading to the controversial execution.

  • A letter was written to Kenny Smith, expressing appreciation for his 'irrepressible spirit' and ability to build relationships within prison walls.
  • A guest recalled their last conversation with Kenny, discussing legal options, unaware it would be their final interaction.
  • The guest acknowledged the human tendency to make meaning by considering personal losses and empathy for all involved in such events.
