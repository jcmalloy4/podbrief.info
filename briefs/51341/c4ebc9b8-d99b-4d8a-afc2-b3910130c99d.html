Revisionist History

From Medal of Honor: Showing Up On Veterans Day

Key Takeaways

  • The 'Medal of Honor' podcast returns for Season 3 in May 2026.
  • J.R. Martinez's military service began after 9/11, leading to severe combat injuries.
  • His recovery journey fostered a new identity and purpose through helping others.
  • The episode emphasizes the critical role of storytelling for veteran healing and public understanding.
  • Martinez reflects on his evolving identity from soldier to veteran, expressing pride and gratitude.

Deep Dive

  • Martinez enlisted as an infantryman following the events of 9/11 during his senior year of high school.
  • He deployed to Iraq and initially felt insignificant before a unit briefing shifted his perspective on individual roles.
  • He realized his value to the team approximately three and a half weeks into his Iraq deployment.

  • On April 5, 2003, Martinez's Humvee struck a roadside bomb in Karbala, Iraq.
  • He was trapped in the burning vehicle, sustaining third-degree burns and inhalation damage while three others were ejected.
  • For five minutes, Martinez remained conscious, actively fighting to survive and telling himself not to give up.

  • After a medically induced coma, Martinez awoke in San Antonio, Texas, three weeks later, facing a medical discharge.
  • He grappled with a loss of military identity, initially focusing on daily survival rather than long-term pressures.
  • A pivotal moment occurred when he began visiting other patients, which helped him regain purpose and identity through service.

  • Martinez's participation in 'Dancing with the Stars' helped promote his veteran status and personal identity beyond his service.
  • His emotional rumba performance to Tim McGraw's 'If You're Reading This' involved a package about his recovery and deeply resonated with the audience.
  • Both the host and guest underscore the importance of veterans sharing their stories for public understanding and connection.

  • Martinez reflects on his evolving identity from soldier to injured service member and finally to veteran.
  • He expresses pride in his initial military decision and gratitude for the self-discovery and leadership lessons it provided.
  • The host and guest express excitement for 'Medal of Honor' Season 3 in May 2026, inviting listener feedback for future episodes.

  • Host J.R. Martinez announces Season 3 of 'Medal of Honor' is scheduled for May 27, 2026.
  • Listeners are encouraged to provide feedback for the show via a survey available at bit.ly/MOHsurvey.
  • Producer Ryan Sweikert suggests approaching conversations with veterans by allowing them to lead the discussion.
