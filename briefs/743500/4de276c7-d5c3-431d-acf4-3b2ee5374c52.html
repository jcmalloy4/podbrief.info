The McCarthy Report

Episode 326: Unrest in Iran

Key Takeaways

  • The Iranian regime uses force and internet blackouts to suppress protests while adhering to its "revolutionary jihadist" doctrine.
  • Experts suggest regime change is the only viable solution for Iran, with targeted military strikes considered given intelligence penetration and weak air defenses.
  • A shooting involving an ICE agent in Minneapolis, coupled with ongoing unrest, has led to discussions about invoking the Insurrection Act.
  • Legal debates focus on the definition of insurrection and the President's Article 2 authority to deploy military forces in domestic policing.
  • U.S. policy towards Venezuela faces scrutiny for its unpredictability, lack of direct action against indicted officials, and controversial oil sales without congressional oversight.

Deep Dive

  • Iran's regime responds to protests by cutting internet and media, using force.
  • Host Andy McCarthy contrasts Trump's focus on hanging as an execution method with Saudi Arabia's beheading practices under Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman.
  • The regime's "revolutionary jihadist doctrine" drives its actions, including hostility towards the U.S. and Israel, rather than solely desperation.

  • President Trump's "deal-making" approach may lead him to underestimate ideological adversaries like Iran.
  • The conclusion is presented that regime change is the only viable solution for Iran, as the current regime is unlikely to alter its "death to America" stance.
  • Analysis suggests regimes like Iran, Russia, and China engage in negotiations as a stalling tactic, not adhering to commitments, to play for time.

  • The Iranian regime is vulnerable due to a lack of effective air defenses and intelligence penetration, particularly by Israeli agencies.
  • A potential course of action involves striking regime targets that enable power projection, specifically damaging the Bassij internal police force.
  • U.S. military action against Iran could be justified by Iran's historical support of al-Qaeda and destabilizing actions in the Middle East, serving American interests.

  • The discussion touches on military asset deployment, questioning the rationale behind their placement in the Gulf versus the Caribbean.
  • A prior brief operation in Venezuela involving Maduro occurred after military assets were strategically in place.
  • The possibility of a cyber operation against Iran is raised as an alternative to military action.

  • A shooting involving a Venezuelan illegal immigrant and ICE agents in Minneapolis, resulting in the immigrant being shot in the leg, amplified unrest.
  • President Trump is reportedly considering invoking the Insurrection Act in Minneapolis due to rioting, which would involve using military troops.
  • Legal debate centers on defining "insurrection," contrasting historical events like the Civil War with Minneapolis events described as a campaign of coercion.

  • The President possesses inherent Article 2 authority to protect federal facilities, personnel, and law enforcement functions, including using military forces for ICE raids.
  • The potential invocation of the Insurrection Act would unlock authorities to deploy military forces to arrest individuals, raising questions about Posse Comitatus Act restrictions.
  • A concurring opinion by Justice Kavanaugh is cited, highlighting the inherent dangers of vehicles and the need for police leeway in using force during law enforcement operations.

  • Minneapolis operations involve "trained professional agitators" who understand police tactics and use vehicles, whistles, and horns to create auditory chaos and establish "no-go-zones."
  • Concerns are raised about a potential overreliance on military intervention and the risks associated with militarizing domestic policing.
  • Speakers argue military forces, trained for combat, lack expertise for civilian law enforcement tasks like investigations or arrests, underscoring a preference for traditional policing methods.

  • Congressional scrutiny is suggested to clarify whether agencies like ICE face insufficient numbers or inadequate preparation of resources.
  • Speakers advocate for understanding ICE's mission and prioritizing targets, drawing a parallel to how other law enforcement manages resource limitations.
  • While broad support exists for border security, the idea of rounding up and deporting every undocumented immigrant is not widely supported.

  • Trump's Venezuela strategy is criticized for its unpredictability, including not pursuing legal actions like indicting Maduro and leaving figures like Diosdado Caballo in power.
  • 50 million barrels of Venezuelan oil were reportedly sold via international traders Traffigora and Vital to prevent storage issues, lacking clear legal authority or congressional oversight.
  • Proceeds from the Venezuelan oil sales are placed in escrow accounts in Qatar, then transferred to Venezuelan banks and sold for Bolivars to stabilize the currency, raising transparency concerns.
