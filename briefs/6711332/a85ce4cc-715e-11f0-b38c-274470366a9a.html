The Tucker Carlson Show

Margaret Roberts Exposes the True Story of the Oklahoma City Bombing and the Ongoing Cover-Up

Key Takeaways

  • The official Oklahoma City bombing narrative is deeply flawed.
  • Crucial evidence, like John Doe II and surveillance tapes, was suppressed.
  • The attack's financing and foreign links prompt unanswered questions.
  • Key figures linked to the case died under highly suspicious circumstances.

Deep Dives

Flawed Narrative

  • Initial reports assumed Islamic terror, quickly replaced by a lone-wolf narrative.
  • Government alleges McVeigh, fueled by rage over Waco, acted largely alone.
  • Carlson and Roberts find the official account overly simplified and illogical.

Suppressed Evidence

  • The Ryder agency initially identified McVeigh with an unidentified 'John Doe II' from a composite sketch.
  • FBI initially offered a $2 million reward for 'John Doe II' before reversing, despite numerous eyewitness accounts.
  • The FBI admitted under oath to possessing surveillance footage of the bomb delivery, but this crucial tape remains unreleased.
  • Carlson notes fundamental aspects of the government's case remain unexamined or unexplained to the public.

Unexplained Funding

  • Timothy McVeigh maintained a mysterious 'road warrior' lifestyle without visible income sources for years.
  • Terry Nichols, though penniless, undertook expensive, unexplained trips to the Philippines.
  • Roberts highlights Nichols' alleged meeting with a 1993 World Trade Center bomber in the Philippines, contradicting the official narrative.

Suspicious Deaths

  • Kenneth Trentadue, resembling 'John Doe II,' died brutally in solitary confinement shortly after his transfer to Oklahoma City.
  • Richard Guthrie, an Aryan Republican Army leader, died suspiciously in prison days before his testimony.
  • Alden Gillis Baker, a witness to Trentadue's murder, was found hanged in his cell two months before testifying.
  • Roberts notes the unusual frequency of 'suicides' among key figures, contrasting with typical profiles of convicted criminals.
