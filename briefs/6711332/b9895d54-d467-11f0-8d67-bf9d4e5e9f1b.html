John Leake: The Demonic Rituals to Replicate God and Mankind’s New Religion of Science

Key Takeaways

  • Public health authorities aggressively discouraged COVID-19 treatments while pushing vaccines.
  • The episode frames vaccine promotion with religious iconography and messaging, likening it to a religion.
  • Vaccine manufacturers have had legal immunity from liability since 1986, which is argued to reduce safety incentives.
  • Early COVID-19 treatment suppression and vaccine mandates are questioned for their rationale and psychological impact.
  • The medical industry is criticized for alleged dishonesty and prioritizing orthodoxy over factual accuracy.
  • Philosophical and historical concepts are used to explain the erosion of public trust in institutions.
  • Concerns are raised regarding mRNA vaccine technology, including potential long-term effects and risks for young people.
  • Historical parallels illustrate resistance to scientific truth and how powerful interests can obscure facts.
  • The "vaccine ideology" is presented as a divisive force, intentionally separating adherents from the hesitant.

Deep Dive

  • Public health authorities framed vaccine hesitancy as a form of heresy while discouraging COVID-19 treatments.
  • A 2022 Vatican coin and a 1930s Diego Rivera mural depicted vaccines using traditional religious imagery.
  • A church in South Africa displayed a banner stating 'even the blood of Christ cannot protect you. Get vaccinated,' implying vaccine superiority.
  • The Biden administration's 'COVID vaccine core' allegedly disbursed billions of dollars to sports leagues and religious institutions to promote vaccination.

  • The medical industry is criticized for perceived dishonesty, particularly in downplaying natural immunity acquired after COVID-19.
  • Professionals like Dr. Sanjay Gupta and Dr. Paul Offit retained positions despite making statements deemed technically inaccurate.
  • Medical science is suggested to operate with adherence to orthodoxy and a doctrinal view over factual accuracy in complex human health areas.
  • Human affairs and decision-making in medical science are explained as shaped by presuppositions and interpretive frameworks, not pure objectivity.

  • Vaccine manufacturers gained full legal liability immunity in 1986 for injuries or deaths caused by their products.
  • The 1986 Childhood Vaccine Injury Act was upheld in the Bruzowitz v. Wyeth Supreme Court case, denying liability for a child's severe injury.
  • Justice Sotomayor's dissenting opinion argued that removing product liability litigation eliminates incentives for safety improvements.
  • Pfizer's history of fraud and misleading safety data is questioned in the context of this legal immunity and corporate conscience.

  • Claims that hydroxychloroquine, an FDA-approved drug used for decades, was dangerous were presented as absurdities designed to demoralize the public.
  • Vaccine mandates, initiated during the Biden administration, were posited as intentionally designed to eliminate inquisitive minds and demoralize the public.
  • Participation in 'humiliation rituals' through compliance was suggested to diminish individuals, illustrated by Novak Djokovic's quarantine.
  • A BBC reporter's interview with Djokovic was characterized as a 'struggle session,' demanding he receive the vaccine.

  • Motivations beyond financial gain, such as religious or metaphysical forces, are explored as explanations for actions within the medical industry.
  • The removal of Dr. McCullough's interview from YouTube prompted discussion about unseen, powerful influences that defy simple explanation.
  • Dostoevsky's 'The Grand Inquisitor' is cited as a profound cultural text contrasting with superficiality.
  • The Grand Inquisitor narrative, within 'The Brothers Karamazov,' explores humanity's need for control and obedience over free will.

  • Themes from 'The Grand Inquisitor' are connected to modern issues, suggesting that questioning vaccines leads to persecution, akin to religious heresy.
  • Vaccine mandates are discussed as a ritualistic act and a form of coerced obedience, described as a lack of informed consent.
  • The mandates are paralleled to a perverted form of communion, where participation is coerced without full understanding.
  • mRNA vaccines are linked to a scientific archetype of transcending limitations, drawing parallels to mythological figures like Prometheus and Lucifer.

  • Tribalism is presented as an anthropological constant impacting public discourse, with a reference to Wittgenstein's philosophy.
  • An anecdote from the Dalmatian coast illustrated intense animosity between groups attributed to demonic or insane motives.
  • The speaker noted how millions of German-Americans fought against Germany in WWII due to the discouragement of tribal identity in the U.S.
  • The 'vaccine ideology' is compared to tribalism, intentionally dividing adherents from the hesitant and leading to restrictions for unvaccinated individuals in public spaces.

  • Messenger RNA (mRNA) technology is described as a 'Luciferian' scientific advancement, with its molecular biology processes explained.
  • Francis Collins' 2006 book "The Language of God" is referenced for the idea that scientists were reading God's code through molecular biology.
  • The discussion suggests scientists are now attempting to 'write' in God's language using mRNA to instruct the human body.
  • The paradox of free will and obedience is explored, drawing parallels to Ivan Karamazov's 'The Grand Inquisitor.'
  • The assertion of complete knowledge in science, especially concerning mRNA technology, is critiqued as hubris, referencing Oliver Wendell Holmes Sr.'s 'topography of ignorance'.

  • Concerns were raised about mRNA vaccines potentially altering the genetic code, impairing fertility, and being passed to offspring, questioning their legality.
  • The risk-benefit analysis of COVID-19 vaccines for young people was contrasted with potential benefits for frail individuals, citing myocarditis in an adolescent.
  • Dr. Eric Rubin, editor of the New England Journal of Medicine and an FDA advisor, acknowledged a potential safety signal for myocarditis in younger individuals.
  • Senator Ron Johnson's hearing investigated an alleged cover-up of myocarditis risks by U.S. government agencies, which are argued to avoid admitting catastrophic errors.
  • A case study published by the McCullough Foundation found mRNA in cancerous bladder cells, suggesting potential integration, though its heritability remains unknown.

  • The story of Ignaz Semmelweis in 1840s Vienna is recounted; he hypothesized that physicians were transferring 'corruption' from cadavers to patients.
  • Semmelweis's implementation of handwashing reduced childbed fever cases from 20% to near zero, yet European medical authorities rejected his findings.
  • Semmelweis was declared insane and committed to an asylum, illustrating historical resistance to evidence-based scientific findings.
  • The historical struggle for scientific acceptance also includes the delay in acknowledging tobacco's link to lung cancer, with public warnings appearing only in 1964.
