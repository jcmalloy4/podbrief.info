Episode

Key Takeaways

  • President Trump's comments questioning NATO allies' contributions in Afghanistan sparked significant criticism.
  • First trilateral talks on the Russia-Ukraine war began in Abu Dhabi, highlighting territorial disagreements.
  • The UN's human rights chief urged Iran to cease its "brutal repression" of anti-government protests.
  • A rare 1699 slang dictionary is heading to auction, offering unique historical linguistic insights.
  • Zimbabwe's $3.5 billion land compensation agreement for white farmers remains largely unpaid.
  • Political scientist Francis Fukuyama analyzed the growing fragility of global liberal democracy and the international order.

Deep Dive

  • President Trump's remarks questioning NATO allies' contributions in Afghanistan drew strong criticism, including from British Prime Minister Keir Starmer, who called them "insulting and frankly appalling."
  • Former US defense official Mick Mulroy highlighted NATO allies' professionalism and sacrifices, noting hundreds of British personnel died after serving on front lines for eight years.
  • The White House, via Deputy Press Secretary Anna Kelly, defended Trump, asserting paramount US contributions to NATO and the success of increased European defense spending.
  • Former Trump administration officials, including H.R. McMaster and Kirk Volcker, deemed the remarks insulting and inaccurate, with an apology considered unlikely.
  • NATO invoked its mutual defense clause, Article 5, only once: in support of the United States following the 9-11 attacks.

  • The first trilateral talks between Russia, Ukraine, and the US commenced in Abu Dhabi to end the war, with territory as a central agenda item.
  • Russia demands Ukrainian surrender in the Donbas, while Ukraine deems this impossible, indicating no immediate peace breakthrough.
  • Russia's continued attacks on Ukraine's power infrastructure are causing a humanitarian crisis with widespread blackouts and lack of heating amid freezing temperatures.

  • The UN's human rights chief, Volker Turk, called on Iran to end its "brutal repression" of anti-government protests.
  • The UN cited excessive force and thousands of deaths, prompting the UN Human Rights Council to initiate an inquiry.
  • Iran attributed the unrest to terrorists and had its representative walk out of a UN meeting in protest of the inquiry.

  • A rare first edition of 'A New Dictionary of the Terms Ancient and Modern of the Canting Crew', a 300-year-old slang dictionary, is slated for auction.
  • The dictionary uniquely includes non-criminal slang, featuring terms like 'ambidexter' and 'ninny hammer', offering historical linguistic insight.
  • Lexicographer Jonathan Green noted that recorded slang history spans 500 years, serving as a repository of synonyms for various concepts.

  • Donald Trump previously supported white farmers in southern Africa, particularly in Zimbabwe, concerning land seizures.
  • Zimbabwe's controversial land redistribution, initiated around 2000, led to a $3.5 billion compensation agreement signed in 2020, which remains largely unpaid.
  • A group of farmers has engaged a US company with ties to President Trump, seeking lobbying support for economic growth and compensation.

  • The post-World War II international order appears increasingly unstable, exacerbated by strained US-European relations and President Trump's actions, including a threat to acquire Greenland.
  • Political scientist Francis Fukuyama assessed the fragility of liberal democracy, suggesting European allies must view the US as a potential threat and strengthen independent capabilities.
  • Fukuyama argued Europe's perceived weakness encourages nationalistic stances and observed younger generations lack urgency for international cooperation, lacking memory of past global conflicts.
  • He posited that technology, enabling belief disconnected from empirical reality, fuels current extremism in ways not seen in a pre-internet era.
