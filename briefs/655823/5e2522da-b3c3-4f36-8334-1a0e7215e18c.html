How To Recruit The Best Engineers And Sales Reps

Key Takeaways

  • Hiring is a sales process focused on pitching and closing top talent.
  • Candidates evaluate offers across big tech, growth-stage companies, and startups.
  • Personalize outreach and pitches based on candidate motivations and role.
  • Proactive sourcing, like outbound sales, requires pipeline management and conversion tracking.
  • Founders must prioritize and actively participate in all stages of the hiring process.
  • Speed and tailored offers are crucial for winning top candidates.
  • Avoid generic outreach and neglecting to sell your company's vision.
  • Leverage recruiting tools and unconventional sources to find hidden talent.

Deep Dive

  • Candidates fall into three categories: big tech (stability, compensation, slower pace), growth-stage (predictable upside, good base, faster pace), and startups (equity variance, high risk, impact).
  • Founders must identify a candidate's company type preference and motivations, such as mission-driven work, equity upside, or interesting problems, to tailor their pitch.
  • The guest outlines four key reasons people join companies: challenging deep tech problems, team culture, and personal referrals.

  • Sourcing involves three steps: searching for candidates (e.g., LinkedIn Recruiter, Juicebox), crafting multi-step outreach messages, and converting candidates to interviews and hires.
  • Proactive sourcing is comparable to outbound sales, requiring pipeline building and conversion tracking, mirroring initial revenue-generating sales efforts.
  • Sourcing channels include maximizing referrals (with potential bonuses up to $20,000) and distributing job postings on platforms like YC's 'work at a startup' and LinkedIn.

  • Outreach involves personalized, multi-channel campaigns using email, LinkedIn, and Twitter DMs.
  • For sales roles (Account Executives), the pitch should highlight career trajectory and potential for higher compensation, offering faster advancement than larger organizations.
  • For Software Engineers (SWEs), emphasis should be placed on ownership, challenging problems, and autonomy.
  • Juicebox's CTO handles all SWE outreach, with an example email strategy including personalization, company legitimacy (metrics, funding), conciseness for mobile, and a calendar link.

  • Personalizing outreach emails significantly impacts final hiring steps; Juicebox's pre-Series A metrics showed reply rates between 10-18% for roles like AE and SWE, with SWEs having the lowest at 11%.
  • While 10-20% reply rates are considered good, some companies achieve over 40% through strong branding or highly personalized outreach.
  • Beyond reply rates, the 'interested rate' is crucial, aiming for roughly half the reply rate, as an increase in reply rate without a corresponding rise in interested rate may signal messaging issues.
  • Founders should prioritize outreach, aiming to send 100 emails weekly to speak with at least 10 candidates, and all founders should be involved in hiring.

  • The interview process begins with selling the company vision and understanding candidate motivations, including other companies they are considering.
  • The second interview round is for assessment, where candidates might demo a product; for PLG companies, candidates can demo the startup's product while the interviewer plays the customer.
  • At the offer stage, speed is a key advantage for early-stage startups compared to larger companies.
  • Founders should personalize offers based on candidate interests and leverage co-founders or investors to reinforce the value proposition.

  • Two common hiring mistakes are not selling the company vision and using generic outreach messages that fail to convey excitement.
  • Founders are advised to craft personalized messages, using themselves as the benchmark for an engaging email, rather than relying on typical, ineffective recruiting templates.
  • Achieving high response rates (over 40%) relies on creative sourcing and personalized messaging, with each outreach explaining why the individual is being contacted.

  • To effectively use recruiting tools like Juicebox, users should begin with natural, descriptive prompts for the role, similar to writing a system prompt, to define search parameters.
  • Juicebox utilizes features like 'Autopilot' to filter candidates based on defined criteria, such as academic publications, and allows custom filters to create personalized outreach with detailed candidate information.
  • Email is recommended for automation and data tracking due to its multi-step campaign capabilities, though LinkedIn may yield higher response rates for sales roles, while email is often more effective for engineers.

  • Founders should understand what candidates truly want and avoid aggressively pursuing those with strong preferences for Big Tech due to factors like compensation or stability.
  • Job descriptions should be concise, engaging, and opinionated, dedicating at least 30% of content to why the company is a great place to work.
  • Elite founders continuously recruit, building relationships over time, and aggressively pursue desirable candidates committed to joining a startup.
  • It is important to have the discipline to move on from candidates who are not a good fit, recognizing that hiring is a repeated game.
