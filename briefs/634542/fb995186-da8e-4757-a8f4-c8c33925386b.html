Alex Morgan: Soccer, Sexualization, & Suing US Soccer

Key Takeaways

  • Alex Morgan's early career greatly benefited from strong parental support for her professional aspirations.
  • The USWNT's 2019 World Cup victory marked a pivotal shift toward assertive confidence and challenging norms.
  • Female athletes face intense public scrutiny regarding behavior and appearance, unlike their male counterparts.
  • Morgan championed support systems for mothers in professional sports, establishing new league standards.
  • The USWNT secured over $20 million in back pay after a six-year equal pay lawsuit against U.S. Soccer.
  • Morgan's retirement signifies a transition to new life chapters and continued investment in women's sports.

Deep Dive

  • At age seven, Alex Morgan informed her mother of her goal to become a professional soccer player; her mother provided strong support.
  • Her father, despite limited soccer knowledge, coached her team and instilled confidence.
  • Morgan overcame early criticism from a coach who later apologized years after her success.

  • Morgan felt imposter syndrome as a rookie on the 2011 U.S. World Cup team.
  • Winning the 2015 World Cup was a turning point, boosting the team's confidence.
  • The 2019 World Cup team adopted a confident, celebratory, and assertive approach, simultaneously filing an equal pay lawsuit against U.S. Soccer.
  • The team challenged expectations for female athletes to be demure, embracing their success.

  • Female athletes face higher standards of behavior, expected to be role models, often overshadowing their athletic achievements.
  • Morgan became more guarded after the 2012 Olympics due to intensified pressure to maintain a positive public image.
  • Post-game demands included extended autograph and photo sessions for women, contrasting with male athletes' routines.
  • Minor boundary-setting, like declining a sweaty photo, could lead to accusations of being difficult.

  • Throughout her career, media and fans frequently focused on Morgan's appearance over her athletic achievements, with fans presenting swimsuit photos for autographs.
  • Morgan experienced significant acne from constant sunscreen and makeup combined with sweat and sun, making her self-conscious.
  • While she participated in projects like the Sports Illustrated swimsuit issue, she adopted a mentality of not overanalyzing external pressures.
  • Some teammates negatively commented on her wearing makeup, prompting Morgan to advocate for mutual respect regardless of grooming choices.

  • Announcing her first pregnancy at age 29 during the height of her career generated intense public reaction and societal skepticism.
  • Morgan noted an increasing trend of younger players like Mel Swanson and Sophia Smith having children early.
  • She faced challenges managing the fear and secrecy surrounding her pregnancy due to potential internet leaks.

  • Morgan highlighted the lack of established rules and support systems for mothers in the NWSL and national team.
  • She drafted agreements for her daughter's travel, presence at team meals, and locker room access, encountering resistance.
  • An incident where her young daughter was initially denied a separate bus seat underscored the challenges.
  • Her advocacy helped establish a new league standard, ensuring children always have their own seats.

  • In 2019, nearly all USWNT players filed a lawsuit against the U.S. Soccer Federation, originating from pay disparities observed after the 2015 World Cup win.
  • The fight required extensive effort from 25-30 players, involving weekly calls with lawyers, depositions, and multiple failed settlements over six years.
  • Players risked career repercussions and loss of sponsorships, which constituted 90-95% of Morgan's income.
  • U.S. Soccer's legal arguments claimed men deserved higher pay due to "inherent speed and strength," a statement later retracted.

  • The USWNT's lawsuit resulted in over $20 million in back pay, with Morgan describing the emotional moment of signing the settlement while holding her daughter.
  • She devoted over 500 hours of personal time to the fight, aiming to impact future generations.
  • The NWSL has experienced significant financial growth, with starting player salaries increasing from $6,000 to over $500,000, influenced by advocacy and Commissioner Jessica Berman's commitment to change.

  • Morgan's decision to retire from professional soccer was prompted by an epiphany with her husband and a desire to expand her family.
  • She found relief in the decision, though informing others was challenging, citing the physical toll of keeping up with younger teammates and overall exhaustion.
  • Her retirement was accelerated by a surprise pregnancy.
  • Morgan is investing in the San Diego team, aiming to put money back into the NWSL and provide her children with an affiliation to the team.
