Call Her Daddy

Demi Lovato (FBF)

Key Takeaways

  • Disney's image control fueled Demi's severe eating disorder and substance abuse.
  • Childhood trauma and intense industry pressure deeply impacted Demi's early mental health.
  • Demi's new album, "Holy Fvck," confronts past trauma and celebrates her authentic self.

Deep Dives

Extreme Control

  • Management's relentless focus on maintaining her thin image involved barricading hotel rooms and removing sugar, exacerbating her eating disorder.
  • Despite throwing up blood, her team denied treatment, fearing public backlash, a refusal that preceded her overdose less than a year later.

Early Adversity

  • She experienced suicidal ideation at age seven and faced severe bullying, including a 'suicide petition,' leading to homeschooling.
  • Early exposure to beauty pageants and a mother's emphasis on perfection contributed to a developing eating disorder by age 12.
  • Her biological father's mental illness and addiction created a detached relationship, influencing her trust dynamics with future managers.

Authentic Voice

  • Her album "Holy Fvck" directly addresses anger from recovery, later shifting to embrace sexuality and love.
  • The song "29" reflects on a past manipulative relationship with an older partner, aiming to warn young girls.
