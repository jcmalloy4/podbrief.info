The Rewatchables

‘Quiz Show’ With Bill Simmons and Brian Koppelman

Key Takeaways

  • 'Quiz Show' (1994) is reevaluated for its prescience on media's influence and societal shifts.
  • The film explores themes of manipulated viewers, corporate malfeasance, and lost innocence.
  • Robert Redford's direction and key actor performances are analyzed.
  • The podcast debates the film's aging, historical accuracy, and its place in 1994 cinema.
  • Discussions include casting "what-ifs," career trajectories, and enduring thematic relevance.

Deep Dive

  • 'Quiz Show' (1994) reflects a 1950s turning point, exploring manipulated viewers, corporate malfeasance, and celebrity cult.
  • Released in September 1994, it was overshadowed by 'Pulp Fiction' and 'Shawshank Redemption'.
  • The film's merit was recognized despite awards season competition from 'Forrest Gump' and 'Shawshank Redemption'.

  • Redford directed 'Quiz Show' from his distrust of television authenticity after witnessing the actual event.
  • His directorial career includes 'Ordinary People' and 'A River Runs Through It'.
  • The screenplay, adapted by Richard Goodwin, allowed Redford to cast highly sought-after actors.

  • Ralph Fiennes, John Turturro, and Rob Morrow's lead performances are central to the film.
  • Martin Scorsese and Barry Levinson deliver praised acting roles, with Scorsese's scene with Morrow noted for its intensity.
  • Rob Morrow's career trajectory is discussed, questioning why he didn't achieve bigger movie stardom after 'Northern Exposure'.

  • Herb Stemple's introduction is identified as a highly rewatchable scene, effectively establishing the character and 1950s context.
  • Other nominated scenes include the game show's setup and Van Doren's pressure interview.
  • The ethical dilemma of selling principles for financial gain and social acceptance is explored.

  • The portrayal of wealthy social circles and father-son dynamics in 'Quiz Show' has aged well.
  • The film's depiction of rigged reality shows, considered novel in 1994, is now commonplace, citing examples from 'The Hills'.
  • The 1950s setting is defined by elements like Geritol and the term "beatnik" to describe a gay artist.

  • Ralph Fiennes' intentional flubbing was visually evident to modern viewers but unnoticed initially.
  • The film takes liberties with the true story, compressing events and misrepresenting NBC's role in Van Doren's incorrect answer.
  • Dick Goodwin's sole involvement in the investigation is potentially overstated, as other individuals performed significant legwork.

  • Rob Morrow's career trajectory is questioned; he didn't become a major movie star, potentially due to a 1990s glut of acting talent.
  • A "hottest take" suggests 'Quiz Show' would have won Best Picture and Director if released in 1995, potentially avoiding 'Redford fatigue'.
  • Paul Newman reportedly turned down the role of Van Doren's father, which Paul Scofield later earned a Best Supporting Actor nomination for.
  • Tom Cruise and Tom Hanks were considered for Charles Van Doren, with debate on Cruise's "bookish appeal." Philip Seymour Hoffman was suggested for Van Doren or his father, with Tom Hanks as a strong choice.

  • David Paymer and Hank Azaria are highlighted for their strong supporting roles.
  • Hank Azaria's impactful, anonymous performance, noted for his role in 'Rain Man', is considered for an award.
  • Christopher McDonald's versatility and career, including 'Shooter McGavin' and 'Quiz Show', are discussed, questioning why he didn't achieve greater stardom.

  • The film's themes of media deception, nepotism, and classism remain relevant in 2025, paralleling the advent of AI.
  • The hosts discuss the life lesson: "Speculation in Our Society Has a Way of Becoming Fact."
  • 'Quiz Show' is seen as Robert Redford's potentially last significant directorial effort in a decade that saw his career renaissance.
