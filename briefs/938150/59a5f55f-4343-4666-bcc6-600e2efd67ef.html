20Growth: How Wix Built a $100M Marketing Machine | Why LTV is BS and Why Time Return On Investment is the Most Important Metric | How to 10x Your Growth: What is the Next Great Channel with Omer Shai, CMO @ Wix

Key Takeaways

  • Wix CMO Omer Shai manages a marketing budget exceeding $100 million annually.
  • Wix is investing in two Super Bowl commercials, including one for its new acquisition, Base 44.
  • The company has shifted from Lifetime Value (LTV) to Time to Return on Investment (TROI) for marketing measurement.
  • Marketing strategy involves balancing investment in established channels with exploring new arbitrage opportunities.
  • Wix is increasing its investment in SEO and related AI search methods, despite broader industry discussions about SEO's decline.
  • Brand marketing is Wix's strongest channel, while TikTok and celebrity endorsements have not met performance expectations.
  • Marketing efforts should prioritize the product at their core, focusing on value over abstract 'why' narratives.
  • AI is significantly accelerating content creation and execution, reducing concept-to-test times from months to hours.
  • An 'AI-first' mindset is crucial for marketers to remain competitive as AI reshapes the labor market.

Deep Dive

  • Wix CMO Omer Shai announced two Super Bowl commercials for the current year, one for Wix (their sixth) and another for recently acquired Base 44.
  • Base 44 is noted as potentially the youngest company to air a Super Bowl commercial, with the ad spot purchased rapidly after its acquisition announcement.
  • Shai views brand activities, including Super Bowl ads, as interconnected with acquisition marketing, contributing to performance goals.
  • A past Super Bowl spot cost $4.8 million, plus mandatory co-investment in network advertising, though current prices are higher and still deemed worthwhile.
  • Wix's Super Bowl commercial this year will focus on the product and its users, shifting away from celebrity endorsements and elaborate productions.

  • Wix's annual marketing spend exceeds $100 million, taking significantly longer to achieve than for a new platform like Base.
  • The CMO does not separate performance from brand marketing within this budget, instead focusing on activities measured by what he calls TRI.
  • This contrasts with the traditional Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) metric, which the CMO expresses frustration with due to its ambiguity.
  • TROI (Time to Return on Investment) is favored, with a personal example of a new ad format yielding a 5x increase in one day in 2012.
  • Wix primarily uses shorter cohorts, such as one-day or seven-day, to quickly measure performance and impact results.

  • Wix maintains an 11- to 12-month Time to Return on Investment (TROI), which comprehensively accounts for user retention, upgrades, and churn.
  • The CMO emphasizes diversifying traffic sources across multiple channels, including social media, education, YouTube, paid ads, and SEO, to mitigate risk.
  • This strategy acknowledges the 80/20 principle (focusing on dominant sources) but also aims to explore new channels to find arbitrage opportunities.
  • Diversification is positioned as crucial for building a sustainable business, reducing vulnerability compared to relying on a single traffic source.

  • Marketing metrics and channel effectiveness between Wix and Base are compared using TROI, which accounts for churn and user behavior differences.
  • A rigorous approach to managing marketing channels involves constant testing and data analysis to determine effectiveness and budget allocation.
  • Campaigns are modified based on incremental impact, even for established channels like YouTube.
  • The strategy prioritizes data-driven decisions, with a daily challenge to assess whether to increase investment, cut losses, or pivot strategies.

  • Wix is increasing its investment in SEO and related AI search methods, despite discussions about SEO's decline due to AI, as significant traffic remains on traditional search engines.
  • Brand marketing is identified as Wix's top-performing channel.
  • Conversely, TikTok and sports endorsements have not met performance expectations.
  • The CMO expresses determination to improve performance on TikTok and LinkedIn, viewing them as significant untapped growth areas.

  • The CMO states Wix operates as a product company with a marketing department, deeply integrating customer feedback into product development.
  • Marketing efforts often fail by neglecting to place the product at the center of their messaging.
  • While acknowledging Simon Sinek's 'sell the why' philosophy, the CMO argues only a few companies like Apple can effectively use it.
  • Most companies, including Wix, need to focus on clearly communicating their product's value and relevance to users.

  • Despite its Israeli origins, Wix's global brand perception often places its headquarters in New York or San Francisco.
  • Wix's most efficient traffic source is its brand, built through hundreds of global campaigns and Super Bowl commercials.
  • The CMO expresses indifference to competitor valuations and focuses on driving growth, aiming to exceed $2 billion in sales for Wix.
  • He respects competitors' social media and personal branding efforts but emphasizes his strengths in data analysis and commercial storytelling.

  • AI accelerates both the ideation and execution phases of content creation, significantly reducing time from initial concept to tested results from months to days or hours.
  • The CMO predicts that individuals who are 'AI-first' will become key players in the marketing field.
  • AI is expected to drive changes in the labor market and potentially increase unemployment, necessitating individuals to embrace AI to remain competitive in marketing roles.

  • The Wix CMO describes his job as enjoyable, driven by pressure and the excitement of winning.
  • He recounts a past costly Super Bowl ad campaign and a failed celebrity endorsement involving a famous athlete creating a website.
  • Reflecting on 18 years as CMO, he would advise his younger self that he would succeed because he thinks differently.
  • He emphasizes prioritizing users over money when evaluating team members, looking for a scientific approach and deep understanding of capital flow.
