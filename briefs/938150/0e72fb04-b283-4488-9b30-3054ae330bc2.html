20Growth: The $6.6B Growth Engine Behind ElevenLabs | Why ElevenLabs Do Not Have PMs | The 7 Part Launch Playbook to Crush All Launches with Luke Harries, Head of Growth @ ElevenLabs

Key Takeaways

  • ElevenLabs achieved a $6.6B valuation with a horizontal product strategy using AI audio models.
  • The company scales growth by sharding into specialized product teams with dedicated marketing efforts.
  • A structured seven-part launch playbook, emphasizing video and broad distribution, is critical for product visibility.
  • ElevenLabs operates without traditional Product Managers; engineers own the product lifecycle, aided by AI.
  • AI customer support agents are viewed as superior to human agents due to efficiency and knowledge.
  • Organic LinkedIn is an underrated growth channel, contrasting with high CPMs of LinkedIn ads.
  • For B2B/prosumer, prioritize product-market fit before heavy paid marketing investment.
  • Mastering copywriting is fundamental for all growth activities, from ad campaigns to blog posts.

Deep Dive

  • ElevenLabs co-founder Matty and Luke Harries met at a Cambridge hackathon at age 19, where they won a Microsoft Prize.
  • Harries initially doubted ElevenLabs' go-to-market strategy for its audio AI models, making only a small investment.
  • Approximately six months after the initial discussion, ElevenLabs reached one million users, prompting them to recruit Harries to lead growth.

  • ElevenLabs employs a horizontal foundational API for text-to-speech, speech-to-text, and voice cloning, contrary to niche ICP advice.
  • The company expands with discrete products like a reader app and a text-to-audio book generator, driven by ambition.
  • Growth is managed by sharding the company into specialized teams (e.g., consumer app, creator growth, developer marketing), each with its own growth focus.
  • A hybrid growth team structure includes horizontal channel specialists and vertical product growth leads, with the enterprise marketing team projected to reach 20 members by year-end.

  • ElevenLabs uses a structured seven-part launch playbook, categorizing releases into three tiers for major models or product lines.
  • Tier 1 launches are led by a growth lead who defines the audience, key performance indicators, and core value propositions.
  • Core messaging is translated into assets like tweet threads and videos, distributed broadly across X, LinkedIn, and Product Hunt to maximize visibility.

  • The guest emphasizes the enduring relevance of blog posts for technical audiences, highlighting the need for detailed content and benchmarks.
  • A 'mini tools' strategy is used to showcase product value, especially for enterprise offerings, by hiring a growth engineer to build and expose small, valuable features quickly.
  • ElevenLabs utilizes interactive text boxes on their homepage as an example of these mini-tools to demonstrate product capabilities.

  • The guest states that CAC to payback period is a more critical daily metric than CAC to LTV, with payback periods varying between 12-36 months.
  • ElevenLabs structures growth teams by assigning marketing leads to different channels, accelerating spending when a positive CAC to payback ratio is observed.
  • A successful consumer product acquisition strategy helps lower CAC, enabling upsells to creator and enterprise tiers.

  • Startups are long-term games requiring intentionality about personal excitement and brand identity to avoid damaging public perception for enterprise sales.
  • The guest cautions against controversial marketing tactics, such as highlighting tools for cheating, for serious B2B brands like ElevenLabs.
  • ElevenLabs aims to be a trusted player for both voice actors and enterprises, contrasting with competitors through a counter-positioning strategy.

  • ElevenLabs operates without traditional Product Managers; engineers are responsible for the entire product lifecycle, from ideation to shipping and analysis.
  • AI coding tools like Cursor facilitate this, with 60-70% of core engineering code being AI-generated, while research engineering remains untouched.
  • The guest predicts a decrease in PM roles due to AI, with a shift towards growth roles or more technical product engineers.

  • The guest noted Lovable's revenue tripled between funding rounds, leading to a 10x revenue multiple on a prior investment, which he now considers 'nuts.'
  • The potential for AI to automate the entire software development lifecycle is discussed, positioning companies like Lovable to own the core stack.
  • For enterprise, Net Revenue Retention (NRR) over 100% is considered strong, even if individual seat retention is lower, with virality driving consumer account growth.

  • The guest initially doubted conversational AI agents but now acknowledges their effectiveness, leading ElevenLabs to develop a platform to support them.
  • AI customer support agents are considered superior to human agents due to their knowledge, efficiency, and lack of need for politeness.
  • Inbound Sales Development Representatives (SDRs) may become obsolete as BANT screening can be automated through forms or conversational AI, potentially using cloned Account Executive voices.
