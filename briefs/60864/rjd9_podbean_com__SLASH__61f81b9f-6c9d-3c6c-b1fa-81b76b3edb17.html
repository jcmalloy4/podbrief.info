Episode

Key Takeaways

  • Jake Heller's decorated military background significantly shaped his leadership and management philosophy.
  • KKR's growth technology strategy leverages extensive firm resources to accelerate growth for category-leading companies.
  • Growth equity targets established businesses with scalable products in large markets, differentiating from venture capital and buyouts.
  • The O9 Solutions investment exemplifies KKR's growth equity in a high-impact supply chain software company.
  • Heller's leadership style emphasizes team development and encouraging calculated risk-taking, influenced by key mentors.

Deep Dive

  • Jake Heller, head of Tech Growth Equity Americas at KKR, served as a detachment commander in the Navy's special boat teams.
  • His role involved leading two Mark V high-speed assault boats, primarily for inserting and extracting special operations forces.
  • Heller pursued a Navy ROTC scholarship to fund his education at Harvard, where he also played football.
  • His military experience in Afghanistan and Iraq, including receiving the Bronze Star, profoundly shaped his perspective on leadership and management, emphasizing adaptability.

  • Jake Heller joined KKR in 2019 to help build a new growth technology franchise, a strategy that commenced in 2014.
  • The growth technology strategy is now 10 years old, managing its third fund, and focuses on scalable, repeatable franchise building.
  • KKR applies its extensive firm resources to category-leading companies to accelerate growth and minimize risk.
  • An integrated approach is fostered by a shared compensation pool across KKR teams, promoting global collaboration and market trend insights.

  • Growth equity involves investments in established businesses with scalable products in large markets where product and market risks are mitigated.
  • KKR targets companies with revenues between $5-7 million, becoming particularly interested when they reach $15-20 million.
  • The firm often prefers minority stakes and seeks alignment with management and existing shareholders.
  • This approach differentiates from venture capital, which focuses on earlier stages, and buyouts, which rely more on financial engineering.

  • O9 Solutions, a supply chain and business planning software company, serves as an example of a KKR growth equity investment.
  • Its founders, Sajeev Sidhu and Chakri Guramakala, previously built and sold I2, and O9 Solutions was bootstrapped until KKR's investment.
  • The company serves major clients like Walmart and Starbucks, demonstrating significant ROI and saving customers over $10 million annually.
  • KKR invested in March 2020, just before the COVID-19 pandemic, with conviction in O9's platform to disrupt supply chain planning.

  • KKR's investment approach emphasizes a 'pull not push' mentality, focusing on category-leading businesses.
  • The firm leverages its comprehensive platform, including global reach, strategic advisors, and the Capstone operating group, to support portfolio companies.
  • This strategy aims to add significant value, accelerate growth, and minimize risk for invested businesses.

  • Jake Heller cites his father, a Little League coach, for instilling philosophies of preparation and setting people up for success.
  • Captain Rick Rushton, a Navy officer, profoundly influenced Heller by transforming ship morale through individual engagement and explaining the 'why' behind sailors' work.
  • This approach of fostering a supportive environment and encouraging risk-taking during a 'zero defect' Navy era now applies to his work at KKR.
