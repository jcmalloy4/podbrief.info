The Daily Beans

Refried Beans | Riptide Thinking; Current Messaging (feat. Phil Williams; Amy McGrath) | 12/26/2023

Key Takeaways

  • Investigative journalism uncovers local political deception and challenges powerful interests like school privatization.
  • The decline of local journalism threatens democratic accountability and amplifies special interest influence.
  • Well-funded right-wing media effectively shapes public perception, contrasting with struggling legacy outlets.
  • Operation Save Democracy combats authoritarianism and potential threats from a second Trump presidency.
  • Protecting democracy requires supporting journalism, advocacy, and strategic voter outreach in swing states.

Deep Dive

  • Investigative journalist Phil Williams recounts his career path, inspired by the Watergate scandal.
  • Williams grew up reading columnist Jack Anderson, which motivated him to pursue investigative reporting.
  • His early inspirations shaped his 25-year career, earning national recognition and multiple awards, including Peabody and DuPont awards.

  • Phil Williams's investigative work in Tennessee revealed groups claiming to support charter schools are pushing for full privatization.
  • These groups aim to achieve privatization through vouchers and homeschooling subsidies.
  • The efforts are reportedly funded by wealthy donors, impacting public education resources.

  • Investigative reporter Phil Williams highlights the collapse of traditional journalism's business model.
  • There has been a significant decrease in reporters covering local communities, which challenges democracy.
  • This reduction in coverage allows special interests to exert more influence at the statehouse level.
  • Williams attributes the strain to the consolidation of legacy media and declining funding, likening it to a 'riptide.'

  • Phil Williams expresses a personal obligation to fight disinformation despite the exhausting nature of the work.
  • He faces attacks from right-wing media outlets and 'angry mobs' on social media, especially after reports on figures like Gabrielle Hansen and Andy Ogles.
  • Williams manages these attacks by largely ignoring them and finding fulfillment in the gratitude of the public, not the loudest hateful voices.

  • Investigative reporter Phil Williams emphasizes letting his work speak for itself and countering misinformation with lived experiences.
  • He highlights his commitment to continuing his work despite criticism, especially personal attacks, including those targeting his late wife.
  • Williams advocates for increased support for local journalism and attracting new talent to the field to defend democracy and the free press.

  • Amy McGrath, co-founder of Operation Save Democracy, describes the super PAC's mission to address national security concerns from a potential second Trump presidency.
  • The group, launched in September, comprises bipartisan national security experts.
  • They believe the threat to democracy posed by Trump and 'Trumpism' is a critical issue transcending political affiliation.

  • Operation Save Democracy aims to defeat 'Trumpism' at the ballot box by targeting Republican voters who prioritize national security.
  • The organization uses independent expenditures to communicate with these voters in key swing states, including Georgia and Arizona.
  • The strategy highlights the Republican Party's perceived shift away from traditional national security principles, contrasting with past figures like Ronald Reagan.

  • Journalism and advocacy are emphasized as crucial in countering misinformation and protecting democratic institutions.
  • The current political climate is framed as a fight for democratic values, requiring immediate action and support for advocacy and get-out-the-vote efforts.
  • The Republican Party's stance on foreign aid, particularly concerning Ukraine, is critiqued for potentially undermining the global order and the United States' role as an ally.
