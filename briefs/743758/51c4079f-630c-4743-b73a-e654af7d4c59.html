The Daily Beans

Minnesota Lawfare

Key Takeaways

  • A federal judge sanctioned Trump appointee Lindsey Halligan; DOJ subpoenas Minnesota officials under a potential Insurrection Act pretext.
  • A Texas judge permitted a campus drag ban, comparing drag to blackface, while Congress excluded anti-transgender riders from funding bills.
  • The stock market declined following Trump's EU tariff threat, and the DOJ confirmed Social Security data misuse by a Trump administration team.
  • Congress released a funding bill with contested ICE allocations, and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney criticized Trump's global economic policies.

Deep Dive

  • Federal prosecutors subpoenaed at least five Democratic officials in Minnesota, including Tim Walls and Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey.
  • The ongoing criminal investigation targets their response to the Trump administration's immigration crackdown, also including Hennepin County District Attorney Mary Moriarty and County Attorney Keith Ellison.
  • This probe is considered a potential pretext to invoke the Insurrection Act and follows the fatal shooting of Renee Goode by a federal immigration agent.

  • Judge Matthew Kacsmaryk ruled West Texas A&M University can ban drag performances on campus, citing the university president's 2023 ban based on religious mission.
  • The ruling compared drag to blackface and disregarded First Amendment precedent, preventing the LGBTQ student organization Spectrum WT from holding its drag show.
  • The drag show was intended to raise funds for suicide prevention hotlines.

  • Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney delivered a critique of Donald Trump at the World Economic Forum in Davos.
  • Carney stated the world is experiencing 'a rupture, not a transition' due to great powers weaponizing economic integration.
  • His speech followed Trump's announcement of new tariffs against European allies and rhetoric regarding Greenland.

  • The U.S. stock market experienced significant losses, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropping 900 points.
  • The S&P 500 fell 2%, and the NASDAQ composite slid 2.3%.
  • These declines were attributed to Trump's intensified rhetoric on Greenland and threats of new tariffs, causing a flight from U.S. assets and a decline in the U.S. dollar.

  • The Justice Department conceded a Trump administration team, referred to as DOGE, may have misused Social Security data.
  • Two employees on Elon Musk's Doge team at the Social Security Administration are under investigation for potential data misuse and Hatch Act violations.
  • Emails suggest DOGE members accessed SSA data to match voter roles for an unidentified advocacy group, and a senior advisor to Elon Musk was copied on a password-protected file with private social security information.
  • The DOJ also revealed DOGE members had access to private social security profiles after a court order prohibited it and used an unapproved third-party server, Cloudflare.

  • Congressional negotiators released a massive, thousand-page funding bill to prevent a government shutdown on January 30th.
  • Progressive Democrats, including Representatives Ted Lieu and Ro Khanna, oppose the bill's $18 billion inclusion for ICE, calling it a 'surrender to Trump's lawlessness.'
  • The bipartisan package maintains ICE funding at $10 billion for the remainder of the fiscal year, despite an additional $75 billion from a previous bill.
  • Representative Rosa DeLauro endorsed the package, highlighting $20 million for ICE body cameras and a reduction in ICE enforcement funding and detention beds.

  • A Trump-appointed judge barred Lindsey Halligan from acting as the top federal prosecutor in the Eastern District of Virginia.
  • The judge cited Halligan's inexperience and misrepresentations, deferring disciplinary action.
  • Separately, Congress released final appropriations bills for Labor, Health and Human Services, and Education, notably excluding anti-transgender riders.
  • This means transgender individuals will likely be spared new congressional attacks for the fiscal year.
