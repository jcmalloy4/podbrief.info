Trump Cabinet gets Instant Repudiation in Tariff Ruling

Key Takeaways

  • Federal Appeals Court ruled Donald Trump's global tariffs unlawful, citing lack of executive authority.
  • The court affirmed tariffs are a congressional power under the Constitution, not an executive one.
  • Trump's cabinet reportedly attempted to pressure the court, warning of humiliation and foreign policy harm.
  • Cabinet members recognized tariffs contributed to slowed economic growth and rising inflation.

Deep Dive

  • Donald Trump's cabinet, including Treasury Secretary Scott Besant and Commerce Secretary Howard Luttnick, sent letters to the Federal Circuit Court of Appeals.
  • These letters warned against ruling Trump's global tariffs unlawful, citing potential embarrassment and harm to U.S. foreign policy.
  • The administration also filed statements from officials like Besant, Luttnick, and Marco Rubio, requesting a stay on any ruling until a Supreme Court decision.

  • Appeals Court Rejects Tariff Power
  • bullets
  • The Federal Circuit Court of Appeals upheld a lower court's decision, ruling Donald Trump lacked authority under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) to impose tariffs.
  • The court explicitly affirmed that tariffs are a core congressional power, not an executive one, referencing the Constitution's grant to Congress to tax and regulate commerce.
  • The ruling indicated IEEPA did not explicitly delegate tariff authority to the president and emphasized that Congress would need to clearly state such a power.
