Furious India Checkmates Trump and Refuses His Calls

Key Takeaways

  • India's Prime Minister Modi is reportedly refusing Donald Trump's phone calls, citing past US tariffs and perceived disrespect.
  • Modi publicly challenged claims of US mediation in the India-Pakistan conflict, recounting warnings from US officials.
  • Indian officials explicitly denied US mediation in de-escalating conflict with Pakistan, contrasting Trump's claims.
  • Former National Security Advisor Jake Sullivan criticized Trump's foreign policy for damaging the US-India relationship.

Deep Dive

  • Prime Minister Modi is reportedly not returning Donald Trump's phone calls after Trump's administration imposed tariffs.
  • US tariffs on India, starting at 10% and escalating to 50%, were perceived by India as humiliation.
  • India rejected Trump's claims of brokering an India-Pakistan ceasefire, viewing it as an infringement on their sovereignty.
  • The host criticized Trump's foreign policy for damaging bilateral relations and US interests.
  • Modi's refusal coincides with his upcoming meeting with Xi Jinping, viewed as a strategic move toward China.
  • India's rejection is attributed to Trump's false peace claims and alleged financial ties to Pakistan through a crypto venture.

  • Prime Minister Modi addressed parliament about India's conflict with Pakistan.
  • Modi recounted a call from JD Vance regarding a potential Pakistani attack, stating his response included a threat of worse retaliation.
  • He also stated the US vice president had warned him of an imminent Pakistani attack.
  • Modi claimed Pakistan's military strength was devastated by the morning of the 10th following a potential attack on the night of the 9th.
  • Modi recounted declining an invitation from President Trump to meet in Washington D.C. after the G7 summit in Canada.

  • Donald Trump claimed he mediated a potential nuclear war between India and Pakistan.
  • Indian Foreign Secretary Vikram Siri stated military action was halted at Pakistan's request through direct communication, not US mediation.
  • Prime Minister Modi affirmed India has not and will not accept mediation, citing complete political unanimity on the issue.
  • Trump described dangling trade deals and intervening in potential India-Pakistan conflict to resolve it within hours.

  • Former National Security Advisor Jake Sullivan discussed Trump's claims about India and Pakistan.
  • Sullivan criticized Trump for taking credit for resolving the conflict and for his interactions with Pakistan.
  • He stated Trump's dealings with Pakistan have harmed the US-India relationship, calling it a strategic setback.
  • Sullivan argued this approach makes allies wary of relying on the US.
