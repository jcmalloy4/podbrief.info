Can a Democrat Win Ohio’s Governor Race? Dr. Amy Acton Says Yes

Key Takeaways

  • Ohio voters are shifting against Trump and MAGA ahead of the 2026 governor's race.
  • Democratic candidate Dr. Amy Acton leads Vivek Ramaswamy in 2026 Ohio gubernatorial polling.
  • Acton emphasizes Ohioans' economic struggles and the need for affordability.
  • She criticizes Ramaswamy's policies, lack of presence, and 'Project 2025 doctrine'.
  • Crossover voters seek problem-solving public servants over culture war issues.

Deep Dive

  • Republicans and Independents in Ohio are reportedly turning against Donald Trump and MAGA ahead of the 2026 governor's race.
  • Polling data indicates Trump is underwater with Ohio voters, and Governor Mike DeWine has low approval.
  • Democratic candidate Dr. Amy Acton leads perceived MAGA Republican candidate Vivek Ramaswamy in the 2026 Ohio gubernatorial race, especially among independent voters.
  • Trump's policies, including trade wars and attempts to repeal the Affordable Care Act, are cited as negatively impacting Ohio.

  • Dr. Acton focuses on making life more affordable for Ohioans, addressing economic struggles.
  • She criticizes the focus of Trump, Vivek Ramaswamy, and MAGA on issues other than affordability.
  • Acton's background includes personal experiences with hunger and homelessness, informing her public service.
  • She has spent a year listening to Ohioans' concerns about rising costs for groceries, gas, healthcare, and agriculture.

  • Dr. Acton notes a trend of crossover voters in Ohio seeking problem-solving public servants over culture war politics.
  • She links rising energy bills, housing, childcare, and property taxes to a "corrupt statehouse."
  • Host Ben Meiselas highlights that affordability, education, and healthcare are issues transcending party lines.

  • Dr. Acton outlines a vision for an economy built from communities up, focusing on education, healthcare, and housing.
  • She criticizes her opponent, Vivek Ramaswamy, for accusing Ohioans of laziness and for his lack of presence, contrasting it with her grassroots campaign.
  • Acton highlights Ohio's 45th ranking in GDP growth and the export of young people as evidence of failed policies.
  • She states current elected officials have unsuccessfully imposed "Project 2025 doctrine," arguing their opportunity has passed.

  • Dr. Acton's public service is motivated by a difficult childhood in Youngstown, marked by abuse, neglect, and hunger.
  • She aims to address systemic issues leading to disparate outcomes for children, citing a childhood memory of receiving breakfast from a neighbor.
  • Acton observes Ohioans are exhausted by political chaos and seek public servants who listen.
  • She notes the widespread need for assistance, including seeing a food pantry serve 400 cars with 65 still waiting.
  • She invites support for her 2026 gubernatorial campaign at ActinForGovernor.com, emphasizing her role as a lifelong public servant and doctor.
