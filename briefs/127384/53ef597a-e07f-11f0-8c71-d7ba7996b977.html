All Hell Breaks Loose as SCOTUS Rules Against Trump

Key Takeaways

  • ICE and Border Patrol are accused of escalating enforcement actions, including alleged mistreatment and unlawful detentions.
  • Reports detail specific incidents of alleged misconduct, such as trespassing and confrontations with U.S. citizens recording events.
  • One Indigenous woman was nearly deported due to mistaken identity, raising concerns about citizen rights.
  • The White House reportedly launched a propaganda campaign promoting voluntary self-deportation with unreliable financial incentives.

Deep Dive

  • Following a Supreme Court ruling, ICE and Border Patrol reportedly initiated a "reign of terror," involving alleged snatching of individuals off streets and mistreatment of women and children.
  • A Bulgarian business owner died in ICE custody at a Michigan private prison after being arrested at his green card appointment; his family claims inadequate diet for his diabetes contributed to his declining health.
  • The Trump regime is utilizing a White House propaganda campaign encouraging voluntary self-deportation before December 31, 2025, with financial incentives that have been unreliable in the past.

  • ICE agents were reportedly involved in trespassing in backyards without permission and entering women's bathrooms at a New York nutrition bar plant under a warrant limited to employer documents.
  • An ICE agent is accused of unlawfully detaining a U.S. citizen woman who was advising immigrants of their rights, with video footage capturing her distress.
  • Separately, ICE agents detained an individual dressed as Santa Claus who was advocating for children at a Portland, Oregon, detention center, appearing to tackle and restrain the person.

  • In Brighton Park, Chicago, ICE agents allegedly pointed guns at U.S. citizen Viviana Corral while she was filming them detaining her neighbor.
  • The incident raises concerns regarding First Amendment rights and the ability of citizens to record official actions.

  • Letitia Jacobo, an Indigenous woman, faced near-deportation due to an ICE detention hold based on mistaken identity, despite possessing a tribal ID.
  • Officials later acknowledged the incident as "human error" and released her.
  • Legal experts confirm Native Americans born in the U.S. are citizens and cannot be deported, advising them to carry tribal identification and not consent to searches without a warrant.

  • A New York Times report, supported by video, details Border Patrol agents allegedly causing a traffic accident, then lying and arresting a U.S. citizen without clear reason.
  • In a separate incident, footage shows a blue SUV, identified as possibly belonging to ICE, veering into a college student's lane, stopping abruptly, and its agents not immediately acknowledging a collision before confronting the student.
  • An ICE agent in Minneapolis reportedly stopped and questioned a U.S. citizen and CIU member, raising questions about racial profiling and the purpose of the stop.
