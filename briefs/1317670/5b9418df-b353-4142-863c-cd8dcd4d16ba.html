The Megyn Kelly Show

FBI Director Kash Patel Reveals NEW Details of Pipe Bomber Arrest, and Ongoing Mysteries, with John Solomon and Jim Fitzgerald | Ep. 1207

Key Takeaways

  • FBI arrested Brian Cole Jr., suspected January 6 pipe bomber, who is now cooperating with authorities.
  • The investigation involved millions of data points, including cell phone, credit card, and enhanced video evidence.
  • Suspect's motives, including alleged belief in 2020 election conspiracy theories, are under ongoing investigation.
  • FBI leadership changes are cited for refocusing resources, leading to resolutions in high-profile cases.
  • Analysts discuss a potential new profile of 'disillusionment criminals' among isolated young men.

Deep Dive

  • Brian Cole Jr. was arrested, facing charges including transporting an explosive device.
  • FBI Director Kash Patel confirmed Cole is cooperating with the FBI, with details remaining confidential due to the ongoing investigation.
  • Cole allegedly worked for a company that sued the Trump administration over immigration policies.
  • The prior FBI's failure to solve the case was attributed to leadership decisions and resource diversions to investigations like 'Russian Gate'.

  • The FBI reviewed 3 million lines of evidence, including cell phone data, to identify leads in the pipe bomber case.
  • Investigators obtained search warrants for credit card data, social media, and email accounts, correlating purchases of bomb components with cell phone data and surveillance footage.
  • Cell phone pings placed the suspect at the RNC and DNC, and purchase records for bomb-making materials began as early as October 2019.
  • Additional bomb-making materials were found in the suspect's home, necessitating a bomb tech squad for the search.

  • FBI Director Kash Patel asserted the current leadership refocused the bureau on core tasks, including apprehending violent offenders.
  • The FBI reported a 510% rise in arrests for individuals preying on children online and apprehended four of the FBI's top 10 most wanted fugitives in nine months.
  • Search warrants have been issued for the January 6 pipe bomber's social media accounts, with information being processed with service providers.
  • The FBI is supporting Utah authorities in the murder investigation of Charlie Kirk's alleged assassin, Tyler Robinson, with no credible foreign government links found.

  • Reports indicate the suspected January 6 pipe bomber confessed to FBI investigators, stating a belief in 2020 election conspiracy theories.
  • Retired FBI profiler James Fitzgerald noted the suspect's alleged social phobia and withdrawn nature.
  • The pipe bomber's purchases of specific components from Home Depot stores occurred between October 2019 and November 2020, predating election challenges.
  • Lingering questions remain about the extended timeline of the suspect's planning and potential involvement in other plots.

  • Details about the suspect's background indicate his grandmother described him as naive and possibly on the autism spectrum.
  • The suspected motives include a possible intellectual challenge or thrill-seeking behavior rather than purely political motivations.
  • New video footage revealed the suspect kneeling near the Congressional Black Caucus Institute building for an extended period, where his phone pinged multiple times.
  • The discussion draws parallels to historical cases like Leopold and Loeb, where crimes were committed for thrill or to test boundaries.

  • The pipe bombs used unsophisticated components like kitchen timers and homemade black powder, raising questions about the suspect's DIY knowledge source.
  • An FBI explosive expert suggested the January 6 pipe bombs were designed to look functional but had imperfections preventing detonation.
  • A witness reported seeing the device with 20 minutes remaining on the timer, questioning the established timeline.
  • Analysts suggest the bombs may have been intended for less destructive effects, such as noise or lights, rather than significant damage.

  • Former FBI profiler James Fitzgerald suggested the suspect might be a serial bomber, noting their tendency to take pride in their work and leave a signature.
  • The discussion explored if the suspect's actions, including walking around for hours, indicated a mental health issue or thrill-seeking motive.
  • The complexities of early statements in investigations, like the DC sniper, were highlighted as not always accurate for motive analysis.
  • The timeframe of purchases and assembly processes raised questions about the suspect's motives and plans.

  • Parallels are drawn between the pipe bomber, Charlie Kirk's alleged assassin, and Trump's attempted assassin, noting similarities like being young, socially isolated, and connected to gaming.
  • A potential syndrome affecting isolated young men in the 21st century is discussed, characterized by loss of identity, unusual gaming habits, and a disconnect from reality.
  • Factors like lack of masculinity, socioeconomic status, and influence from online communities (e.g., 'furry,' gaming) are considered to contribute to violent acts.
  • The conversation raises the possibility of 'incel' status contributing to violent acts among these individuals.

  • Early reports linking the suspect to Antifa or anarchist ties are now questioned, with no confirmed proof of ideological affiliations.
  • The current assessment leans towards the suspect being a loner who acted impulsively or for thrill.
  • Ryan Cole Jr. made his initial court appearance, with prosecutors seeking his detention ahead of trial.
  • The direct link between Cole Jr.'s belief in 2020 election conspiracy theories and his motives remains under investigation.
