The Megyn Kelly Show

The Truth About the ICE Shooting in MN, and Dangers of Marijuana, with Dave Aronberg, Phil Holloway, Ashleigh Merchant, and Alex Berenson | Ep. 1226

Key Takeaways

  • An ICE agent's fatal shooting of a driver in Minneapolis sparks legal and public debate.
  • Legal experts discuss the 'objective reasonableness' standard for officer use of force.
  • The ICE agent's prior injury in a dragging incident is presented as relevant context.
  • Media and officials are criticized for narratives potentially fueling public tension.
  • Nick Reiner's attorney withdrew from his case, raising questions about financial or defense strategy.
  • High-potency cannabis is linked to psychosis, violence, and long-term mental health risks.
  • President Trump's reclassification of marijuana raises public health concerns among experts.
  • Societal denial and attitudes towards substance use are highlighted as problematic.

Deep Dive

  • An ICE agent fatally shot Renee Nicole Goode, described as blocking traffic to impede ICE investigations in Minneapolis.
  • Video footage shows Goode reversing, then accelerating forward toward an agent before a shot was fired through the front windshield.
  • The agent had a prior incident in June 2025 where he was dragged over 100 yards by a suspect, sustaining serious injuries.
  • Over 100 incidents of ICE agents being rammed or dragged by individuals were cited.

  • Legal experts emphasize the 'objective reasonableness' standard from Graham v. Connor (1989) for assessing officer force.
  • This standard requires evaluating force from the officer's perspective in tense, uncertain, and rapidly evolving circumstances.
  • Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey's 'inflammatory statements' regarding the shooting were contrasted with these legal standards.
  • Experts expressed doubt about the likelihood of success for civil or criminal lawsuits due to the reasonableness standard.

  • Debate surrounds whether state authorities have jurisdiction to prosecute a federal officer, citing the Supremacy Clause.
  • One perspective suggests state charges could proceed if the officer acted outside official duties, possibly leading to federal removal.
  • A state attorney noted that while prosecution is legally possible, the current case is complex, criticizing officials for escalating tensions.
  • Comparison was drawn to Trump cases lacking federal protection, implying the ICE agent may have presidential pardon potential.

  • Media reporting, specifically CNN, was criticized for relying on eyewitness accounts providing legal interpretations deemed unreliable.
  • Local politicians and media rhetoric in Minneapolis were suggested to be exacerbating tensions, potentially seeking civil unrest.
  • Jaylani Hussein of CAIR called the suspect a 'hero' and 'patriot,' drawing criticism for being irresponsible and escalating the situation.
  • An official was criticized for using expletives and potentially escalating the situation rather than de-escalating.

  • Nick Reiner's attorney, Alan Jackson, withdrew from the case, citing ethical rules that prevented him from explaining the reasons.
  • A public defender has been appointed for Reiner, whose arraignment was postponed due to the attorney withdrawal.
  • Speculation arose that Jackson's departure was due to financial reasons, possibly a disagreement over fees or the estate's inability to cover costs.
  • The host criticized Jackson's assertion of Reiner's 'legal innocence,' differentiating it from factual innocence.

  • California has a high burden of proof for an insanity defense, where evidence of hiding behavior could undermine the claim.
  • A successful insanity plea might lead to longer institutionalization in a mental facility than a standard prison sentence.
  • The significant costs associated with mental health experts were noted as a factor influencing legal strategy and attorney departure.
  • Discussion differentiated between competency to stand trial and an insanity defense, questioning Reiner's awareness of right and wrong.

  • Author Alex Berenson asserts mounting evidence links cannabis use, especially in teens, to severe mental illnesses like psychosis and schizophrenia.
  • Psychosis is identified as a clear risk factor for violence, including acts against family members and strangers.
  • President Trump's executive order to reclassify marijuana as a Schedule III substance raises concerns about societal impacts given cannabis-related harms.
  • The discussion refutes the idea that cannabis is significantly safer than alcohol, noting links to violence, suicide, mental illness, and heart attacks.

  • Modern cannabis products contain significantly higher THC content, with some joints holding up to 100mg THC compared to a few milligrams 50 years ago.
  • Non-flower products like vapes and tinctures are described as pure, extracted, and sometimes chemically altered THC, challenging the 'natural plant' narrative.
  • Regular cannabis use is asserted to lead individuals to become 'utter losers,' with lives stalled in their mid-20s, often centered around pot use.
  • The cannabis industry is criticized for marketing pot as medicine to vulnerable individuals with anxiety or depression, who are most susceptible to negative effects.

  • A clip features Nick Reiner discussing cannabis and Adderall use as a preventative measure against harder drugs, illustrating individual and societal denial.
  • Heavy cannabis use is linked to violent psychotic breaks, citing Nicholas Cruz and the Minneapolis school shooter as examples.
  • The combination of cannabis and amphetamines like Adderall is considered particularly dangerous, as both can independently induce psychosis.
  • Concern is raised about a societal link between increased school and mass shootings, heightened cannabis potency, and widespread Adderall prescriptions in the U.S.
