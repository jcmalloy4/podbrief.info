Anti-ICE Violence Grows as Dem Politicians Cheer, with Adam Carolla, and Why Tish Hyman is Running For LA Mayor | Ep. 1231

Key Takeaways

  • An illegal immigrant attacked an ICE officer in Minneapolis, prompting protests and criticism from local politicians.
  • Adam Carolla discussed anti-ICE rhetoric, linking it to political opportunism, media bias, and societal narcissism.
  • Polling data indicates declining public approval for ICE enforcement, potentially impacting upcoming midterm elections.
  • Tish Hyman, an artist and activist, detailed multiple confrontations with men in women's locker rooms.
  • Hyman's experiences in locker rooms and dissatisfaction with California laws motivated her independent run for Mayor of Los Angeles.

Deep Dive

  • An illegal immigrant attacked an ICE officer during a Minneapolis traffic stop; the suspect was released by the Biden administration over three years prior.
  • Protests escalated at the scene, with demonstrators throwing fireworks and snowballs at law enforcement.
  • Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey and Minnesota Governor Tim Walz criticized federal immigration enforcement, calling it 'organized brutality'.

  • Adam Carolla likened rhetoric used against ICE to propaganda that historically incited violence against occupying forces.
  • The host and Adam Carolla debated the concept of 'heroes' in contemporary culture, citing a perceived lack of genuine impactful actions.
  • Carolla criticized media outlets like the LA Times and New York Times for perceived pro-activist bias in reporting ICE incidents, often omitting key details.

  • A theory linked unhappiness and a lack of sexual relationships to individuals engaging in destructive public behavior, focusing on 'gyno-fascism' among women.
  • Behaviors such as 'edibles, celibacy, and video games' were criticized as indicators of narcissism and idleness.
  • A woman's video about an ICE incident, featuring her shaving her head as protest, was critiqued as a narcissistic act for attention.

  • The host criticized online content creators for producing 'empty videos' contrasted with historical figures' impact.
  • Megyn Kelly questioned a CBS host's emotional interview as performative, seeking attention.
  • The discussion covered changing perceptions of masculinity, debating if modern men are becoming less traditionally masculine due to societal shifts.

  • YouGov and The Economist polling indicated a majority of respondents had seen footage of the Minneapolis ICE incident.
  • A CNN poll showed 53% of Americans believe an ICE agent involved in a shooting should face criminal charges.
  • ICE's approval rating in cities dropped by 17 points, potentially costing Trump political points and impacting 18 House races in midterms.

  • Senator Josh Hawley questioned a doctor regarding whether men can get pregnant.
  • The host and Adam Carolla criticized the doctor's hesitant response, praising Hawley for perceived accountability.
  • Carolla stated biological men cannot get pregnant, emphasizing respect for transgender individuals.

  • Artist and activist Tish Hyman recounted an initial encounter with a man in a Gold's Gym women's locker room in Los Angeles.
  • Hyman described a second encounter with the same individual at a different gym, leading to a police report yielding no action.
  • She identified the individual as Grant Freeman, who adopted the name Alexis after allegedly assaulting his former ex-wife.

  • Hyman recorded and posted a video of her confrontation with a man in a Gold's Gym women's locker room, which went viral.
  • She expressed anger at men's presence and felt gym staff treated her as if she was in the wrong.
  • Hyman was escorted out of Gold's Gym by police, and her membership was eventually terminated under new ownership.

  • Hyman witnessed a man allegedly masturbating in a Planet Fitness women's locker room, captured on video.
  • These incidents led her to confront lawmakers like Eric Swalwell and Scott Weiner regarding policies allowing men in women's spaces.
  • Hyman announced her independent run for Mayor of Los Angeles, aiming to unite the city and address political division by voting based on legislation.
