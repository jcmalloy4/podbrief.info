The Megyn Kelly Show

Kamala Celebrates Herself, 1/6 Pipe Bomber Details, and Future of Film Industry, with Stu Burguiere and Zachary Levi | Ep. 1209

Key Takeaways

  • Kamala Harris's public statements and outreach signal potential 2028 presidential ambitions, drawing criticism for vague policy.
  • The January 6th pipe bomber investigation faced scrutiny over FBI claims of corrupted cell data and delayed suspect apprehension.
  • Debate arose regarding Piers Morgan's interview with Nick Fuentes and the journalistic value of platforming controversial figures.
  • "Morning Joe" hosts Joe Scarborough and Mika Brzezinski were criticized for frequent absences and perceived elitist conduct.
  • Actor Zachary Levi discussed his new film "Not Without Hope," a true survival story highlighting friendship and perseverance.
  • Discussions covered media consolidation impacts, streaming's role, and skepticism towards vaccine safety and pharmaceutical influence.

Deep Dive

  • Vice President Kamala Harris described herself as a "historic figure" destined for a marble bust in Congress during a New York Times profile.
  • She avoided concrete policy positions, opting for vague statements like needing to 'stand for the people' and prioritizing a 'survival instinct'.
  • Harris reportedly contacted recent Democratic election winners, interpreted as signs of a potential 2028 presidential campaign.
  • Harris criticized Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro in her book, leading Shapiro to call her account "bullshit" and "blatant lies" in an interview with The Atlantic.

  • Zachary Levi expressed concern over potential media consolidation, specifically a Netflix acquisition of Warner Bros.
  • He argued such a takeover would de-emphasize theatrical releases, which he believes are crucial for shared experiences and social cohesion.
  • Dissatisfaction was noted with content consolidation within streaming, citing the loss of physical media ownership and the ephemeral nature of digital content.
  • The conversation highlighted the diminishing value of the shared community aspect of watching films in a theater.

  • The alleged January 6th pipe bomber, Brian Cole Jr., reportedly had an online obsession with "My Little Pony."
  • The guest suggested "My Little Pony" conventions often attract "pervy men" rather than children.
  • The discussion explored alleged connections between interests in communities like bronies and furries, and acts of violence or detachment from reality.
  • The suspect Tyler Robinson and potential connections within the furry and trans communities were also mentioned.

  • The FBI faced questions regarding delays in locating the January 6th pipe bomb suspect, Brian Cole Jr., despite having cell phone data from January 5, 2021.
  • FBI official Steve Dantuono testified that corrupted cell data from a provider hindered the investigation, a claim later contradicted by cell phone carriers to Congress.
  • The investigation's slow progress and unusual circumstances, including claims of blurry surveillance footage, were highlighted.
  • Concerns were raised about the FBI's priorities, comparing the pipe bomb case to actions against parents at school board meetings and monitoring U.S. Senators.

  • Piers Morgan interviewed Nick Fuentes, who made controversial statements about women's rights and attraction.
  • The host expressed skepticism about the value of interviewing Fuentes, suggesting that those who dislike him should ignore him.
  • Fuentes's views, including alleged admiration for Hitler and offensive remarks about Jews, women, and Black people, were described as abhorrent.
  • The discussion contrasted the strategic value of interviewing subversive figures like Fuentes with interviewing those in power.

  • Joe Scarborough and Mika Brzezinski, hosts of "Morning Joe," reportedly took frequent time off during summer and fall, appearing together on only 56% of episodes between May and November.
  • Brzezinski missed 41 shows and Scarborough missed 29, leading to frustration among staff and "C-list hosts" filling in for 16 shows.
  • They returned to perfect attendance for three weeks after November 12th, with their compensation estimated at $25-30 million.
  • The host criticized their perceived elitist behavior, suggesting a disconnect from their audience and influence on their reporting style.

  • Actor Zachary Levi discussed his new film, "Not Without Hope," a true story about four friends lost at sea.
  • The film explores themes of friendship, perseverance, and hope, contrasting human vulnerability with nature's power.
  • Levi plays survivor Nick Schuyler, detailing the harrowing real-life events, including an anchor malfunction and a storm.
  • The challenging production involved shooting in a cold water tank in Malta for three and a half weeks.

  • The guest critiqued the recommended Hepatitis B vaccine for newborns, arguing it is unnecessary for most infants and citing a questionable study.
  • Claims were made that diseases like polio and measles were declining due to improved sanitation and antibiotics before widespread vaccine administration.
  • The 1986 law granting immunity to vaccine manufacturers was questioned, suggesting a need to scrutinize vaccine safety and efficacy.
  • Allegations were raised about well-meaning doctors spreading misinformation due to financial incentives, and the suppression of a study showing unvaccinated children had fewer health issues.
