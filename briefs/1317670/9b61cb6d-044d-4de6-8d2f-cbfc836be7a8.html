The Megyn Kelly Show

Charlie Kirk's Legacy of Compassion, and Kamala and Katie Porter's Inauthenticity, with Jack Posobiec and Ana Kasparian | Ep. 1172

Key Takeaways

  • Megyn Kelly reflects on Charlie Kirk's Presidential Medal of Freedom ceremony and President Trump's support.
  • Jack Posobiec discusses Charlie Kirk's legacy as a conservative movement "bridge" and Rush Limbaugh's influence.
  • The media's focus and alleged tactics against conservatives and Charlie Kirk are scrutinized.
  • Concerns are raised regarding societal division, political violence, and the role of godlessness in culture.
  • Ana Kasparian criticizes Katie Porter's interview performance and readiness for leadership.
  • Kamala Harris's public speaking and perceived lack of authenticity are debated.
  • The importance of authenticity in political figures and media interactions is discussed.
  • Bari Weiss reportedly faces backlash from CBS News staff following a recent email.

Deep Dive

  • Megyn Kelly reflected on attending the Presidential Medal of Freedom ceremony for Charlie Kirk on his 32nd birthday in the Rose Garden.
  • President Trump and Kirk's widow, Erica Kirk, spoke at the event, which many major networks broadcast, though MSNBC notably did not.
  • Kelly noted parallels to Rush Limbaugh's posthumous award, highlighting Limbaugh's significant influence on conservatism.

  • Jack Posobiec described Charlie Kirk as a "bridge" uniting conservative factions, akin to Rush Limbaugh's pioneering media role.
  • Limbaugh was revealed as a significant, undisclosed donor to Turning Point USA, influencing Kirk's vision for direct audience connection.
  • Since his passing, Kirk's podcasts and books have topped charts, and new Turning Point chapters are forming, indicating continued demand for his message.

  • A news release critical of Charlie Kirk was reportedly timed to coincide with his memorial service as a media and political strategy.
  • The discussion critiqued the media's focus on minor Republican figures' text messages compared to serious threats from a Virginia Attorney General candidate.
  • Speakers argued MSNBC's narrative relied on an "inverted hierarchy of victimhood," portraying successful white Christian males like Kirk as enemies.

  • Jack Posobiec linked recent acts of political violence, including attempts on President Trump and Charlie Kirk's murder, to a perceived decline in societal standards.
  • He suggested the absence of God contributes to resentment, envy, and disaffection, particularly among young men.
  • Charlie Kirk's daily Bible verse sharing was highlighted as a unifying force, contrasting with the historical removal of God from public discourse since the 1960s.

  • A clip featured Charlie Kirk advising a young person on engaging with politics while honoring parents with differing views, emphasizing biblical obligation.
  • Megyn Kelly highlighted Kirk's consistent message of love and respect across political divides, contrasting with media portrayals.
  • Kirk's approach focused on connecting with individuals personally, which was contrasted with what was described as a left-leaning tendency to ostracize opponents, even family members.

  • Ana Kasparian criticized Katie Porter's tense demeanor during a Nexstar interview when questioned on past incidents and her stance on Proposition 36.
  • Porter avoided a direct "no" when asked if more videos might surface regarding staff situations, stating she focuses on earning voters' trust.
  • Kasparian stated Porter's irritation with follow-up questions demonstrated a lack of leadership and preparedness for the California governor role.

  • The host critiqued Kamala Harris's public speaking and communication style during her book tour and a recent interview with Kara Swisher.
  • Questions were raised about Harris's ability to articulate policy points, contrasting with her past performance during the Brett Kavanaugh hearings.
  • The host suggested pressure for validation may hinder Harris's authentic communication, leading to increasingly strange public behavior.

  • The host observed that genuine politicians, like Donald Trump, resonate with voters even if their statements are controversial.
  • She contrasted this with a perceived struggle by the Democratic establishment to be authentic, often maintaining a rigid left vs. right paradigm.
  • Katie Porter's handling of a viral video was discussed, with the host suggesting she should embrace her authentic, albeit abrasive, personality rather than offering apologies.

  • Megyn Kelly reported Bari Weiss faced backlash at CBS News after sending an email requesting staff project information, leading to union intervention.
  • CBS assured employees they would not face discipline for not responding to Weiss's email, which Kelly viewed as a sign of trouble for Weiss's tenure.
  • The guest suggested the backlash stemmed from prior reports about potential CBS ownership changes and David Ellison's interest in acquiring the company for $150 million, noting its pro-Israel bias.
