Pardon My Take

Draymond Green, SB Talk With Scott Zolak, NBA Trades + Guys On Hank

Key Takeaways

  • Draymond Green's extensive interview covers his career, Warriors dynamics, and trade discussions.
  • The NFL's international game expansion and the effectiveness of the Rooney Rule drew criticism.
  • Major NBA trade deadline moves involving the Grizzlies, Bulls, and Celtics were analyzed.
  • Super Bowl Media Night was critiqued for perceived loss of appeal and bizarre nature.
  • Guest Scott Zolak provided insights on the Patriots and a Super Bowl historical game.

Deep Dive

  • Hosts expressed skepticism regarding the Rooney Rule's effectiveness in promoting diversity in coaching hires.
  • The rule's intent was noted to be failing, exemplified by two sets of brothers holding head coaching positions.
  • NFL officiating quality was debated, with a guest humorously quoting Commissioner Goodell about officials being 'amazed at how good' they are.

  • Major NBA trades occurred around the deadline, including the Memphis Grizzlies reportedly trading Jaren Jackson Jr. to the Utah Jazz for multiple players and draft picks.
  • A multi-team deal involved the Chicago Bulls, Minnesota Timberwolves, and Detroit Pistons, sending Jaden Ivey and Mike Conley Jr. to Chicago.
  • The Chicago Bulls also traded Nikola Vucevic and a second-round pick to the Boston Celtics for Anthony Simmons and a second-round pick.

  • Kawhi Leonard's snub from NBA All-Star reserves was discussed as a 'Hot Seat' topic.
  • Speculation arose that the snub might be linked to a past controversy involving him.
  • The NBA All-Star selection process was debated, with one host blaming coaches and another detailing the weighted voting system (fans, players, media).

  • Draymond Green discussed his basketball IQ, ranking himself among the top five alongside LeBron James, Nikola Jokic, Chris Paul, and James Harden.
  • He described observing when teammate Steph Curry is poised for a highly productive scoring night, highlighting the defensive challenges this creates.
  • The conversation analyzed unique defensive strategies against Curry and the art of setting effective screens, particularly higher screens for Curry due to tight coverage.

  • The discussion focused on the Golden State Warriors' consistent third-quarter dominance, where they frequently converted tied games into significant leads.
  • The guest attributed this to their ability to adapt to opposing defenses and a unique mentality that thrived under pressure.
  • Draymond Green drew parallels to Klay Thompson's 'Game 6 Klay' phenomenon, where belief in a pattern influences performance.

  • Draymond Green explained the decrease in his three-point attempts, citing the arrival of Kevin Durant and hip and shoulder injuries sustained in 2018.
  • The lingering effects of these injuries led to a loss of confidence in his shot, creating mental hurdles that took years to overcome.
  • Hosts and Green discussed his shooting ability in practice versus games, and a hypothetical high-stakes basketball challenge was proposed.

  • Kevin Durant's arrival was described as a 'cheat code' that, alongside Steph Curry, created an 'invincible' Golden State Warriors team.
  • The hosts noted an initial adjustment period to integrate their playstyles.
  • Green highlighted the effectiveness of the pick-and-roll with Curry and Klay Thompson, and how Durant's addition created an 'unguardable' offense.

  • The hosts discussed the potential impact of Sam Darnold starting at quarterback for the Seahawks, suggesting his less mobile style might benefit the Patriots' defense.
  • A reporter recounted calling Super Bowl XLIX, highlighting the challenge of learning opposing players' names and his reaction to Malcolm Butler's game-winning interception.
  • Bill Belichick later agreed with the reporter's assessment of the Seahawks' final play call as a poor decision.
