Why a Weird NBA Season Finally Went Full Weirdo On Us, With Tim Legler

Key Takeaways

  • The 2023-24 NBA season is marked by unusual parity and unpredictable outcomes, with all 30 teams separated by only 10 games.
  • The league is experiencing a significant influx of young, analytically minded coaching talent that is transforming strategy.
  • Teams with limited playoff experience, such as Toronto or Phoenix, face scrutiny regarding their viability in seven-game series.
  • Acquiring star players with extensive injury histories and high career mileage presents substantial trade risks for NBA franchises.
  • Concerns persist regarding Zion Williamson's consistently low rebounding numbers and recurring injury history.
  • Despite defensive struggles, the Los Angeles Lakers exhibit a unique ability to create momentum swings within games.
  • The Philadelphia 76ers, led by Joel Embiid, are considered a legitimate Eastern Conference threat, despite durability questions.
  • The Minnesota Timberwolves' lack of a traditional point guard is identified as a critical personnel gap for deep playoff runs.

Deep Dive

  • The top team holds a 17-8 record, while the bottom team is 7-18 in the last 25 games, with all 30 teams separated by only 10 games.
  • Player availability fluctuations and inconsistent team effort are cited as reasons for this being the 'weirdest year.'
  • The season is predicted to stabilize after the All-Star break, with a clearer hierarchy emerging by March.
  • Increased three-point shooting year-over-year also contributes to unpredictable game outcomes and the possibility of upsets.
  • Many teams now possess strong benches, reflecting improved depth across the league, exemplified by the Toronto Raptors.

  • Questions are raised regarding whether regular-season success for teams like Toronto or Phoenix will translate into playoff wins.
  • Teams lacking a playoff track record face uncertainty when attempting to compete in seven-game series against top opponents.
  • The Detroit Pistons, despite performing well against the Knicks last year, face a different challenge in winning multiple playoff series.
  • Young teams often struggle to navigate the intense playoff environment and potential slumps in player performance.

  • Acquiring star players with significant injury histories and career mileage carries inherent trade risks for franchises.
  • Historical parallels are drawn to past NBA stars like Kevin Garnett and Shaquille O'Neal to illustrate long-term viability concerns.
  • The long-term impact and value of such acquisitions are questioned given their potential for future health issues.

  • Concerns are raised about Zion Williamson's low rebounding average, specifically questioned for averaging only 6 rebounds per game.
  • His tendency to backpedal on defense rather than pursuing offensive rebounds is cited as a factor in his low numbers.
  • Discussion highlights his limited games played over six seasons, raising broader questions about his health and consistency.
  • Jaren Jackson Jr. is noted for potentially sacrificing rebounding opportunities by challenging shots at the rim, drawing a parallel to Williamson.

  • The Dallas Mavericks are considered better than their 19-27 record indicates, partly due to injury luck.
  • It is estimated that Kyrie Irving could be worth approximately 12 additional wins if he had played the entire season.
  • A hypothetical record of 30-16 for the Mavericks is suggested with Irving's full participation.

  • The Charlotte Hornets averaged 125 points per game in their last three outings, with notable wins against Oklahoma City, Sacramento, and the Lakers.
  • Individual offensive contributions from players like Miles Bridges and Brandon Miller are highlighted.
  • LaMelo Ball's consistency is questioned despite his confidence against top players.
  • Discussion includes the upcoming trade deadline, with assets like multiple draft picks, and the potential to acquire a veteran shooter or stretch four to expedite development.

  • The Philadelphia 76ers, with a 24-21 record, are considered a legitimate Eastern Conference threat, particularly for home playoff games.
  • Their size and speed advantages are noted as alarming for opponents, despite inconsistencies and potential injuries to Joel Embiid.
  • Embiid's statistical performance, including 29.8 points in his last 14 games, is highlighted despite initial doubts about his durability.
  • A recent game against the Knicks exposed weaknesses in rebounding and energy, which could pose playoff challenges.

  • The Cleveland Cavaliers are noted for their recent strong performance, with an 11-4 record in their last 15 games.
  • An upcoming five-game West Coast road trip is identified as a key indicator for the Cavaliers' potential.
  • The New York Knicks have experienced a recent slump (6-9 in their last 15 games), though the guest believes they will perform better in the playoffs.
  • Both teams are discussed as part of a larger group of contenders, acknowledging the difficulty in definitive rankings.

  • The Minnesota Timberwolves' ability to close out games in the playoffs is questioned due to a perceived lack of a traditional point guard.
  • This personnel issue forces Anthony Edwards to manage the game's flow, potentially detracting from his scoring focus.
  • The team's inconsistency is noted, with strong defense on some nights contrasted by the heavy burden on Edwards to carry the offense.
  • Concerns are also raised about their three-point shooting, as they rank 27th in attempts and 17th in percentage.
