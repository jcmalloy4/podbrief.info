The Bill Simmons Podcast

Arch Manning, NFL Announcers, a New Deal for 'PTI', the Ballmer Scandal, and a Crawford-Canelo Megafight With Van Lathan Jr., Bryan Curtis, and Chris Mannix

Key Takeaways

  • Arch Manning's early college football performance is under scrutiny despite significant hype.
  • Conference realignments are changing traditional college football rivalries and creating logistical challenges.
  • The Los Angeles Clippers face a "scandal" involving alleged irregular payments to Kawhi Leonard.
  • Sports media is evolving with new personalities, changing broadcast formats, and emerging platforms like YouTube.
  • The anticipated Canelo Alvarez vs. Terence Crawford fight presents significant weight-class and strategic considerations.
  • Professional boxing's health is debated amidst streaming, Saudi investment, and fighter activity.

Deep Dive

  • Arch Manning's performance for the University of Texas is being compared to Chris Sims's early career, with his numerous NIL endorsements noted.
  • Concerns arose about Manning's apparent shoulder discomfort during a recent game, with the coach's defensive response at a press conference adding to fan anxiety.
  • Despite a seemingly good stat line against San Jose State, analysis of game tape revealed throws were often into wide-open windows and not precisely on target.
  • Guests questioned the hype surrounding Manning based on his surname and prior playing time, noting other highly touted quarterbacks in the same recruiting class are also not yet meeting expectations.

  • The college football media landscape is expanding with larger pre-game shows, exemplified by an incident involving Lee Corso and Barstool Sports at a Game Day event in Columbus, Ohio.
  • ESPN's College GameDay personalities are contrasted, specifically the dynamic between Chris Corso and Lee Corso with the current pairing of Nick Saban and Pat McAfee.
  • Nick Saban is noted for his surprising aptitude for television broadcasting after his coaching career.
  • Bill Belichick's brief media tour is contrasted with Saban's, suggesting Belichick lacked the energy for sustained media engagement.

  • The decline and eventual move of 'Inside the NFL' to Twitter is discussed, reflecting on its historical significance as a 'cool' football discussion platform.
  • Reasons for its diminished presence include market correction, the rise of podcasts, and the instantaneous availability of clips online.
  • The panel questioned whether the current emphasis on technology and constant data has diminished the art of game preparation and viewer engagement seen in past broadcasts by Brent Musburger and Bob Costas.
  • YouTube's recent attempt to cover a football game did not go smoothly, notably resulting in Derek Carr being the broadcast talent.

  • The dynamics of live television emphasize the importance of trust among participants and the negative impact of intentional or unintentional undermining.
  • A specific instance where one participant felt put on the defensive during a live broadcast is analyzed, contrasting it with podcast interactions.
  • Speakers noted a trend towards louder, more aggressive on-air personalities, potentially sacrificing nuanced interaction, and praised Stephen A. Smith's ability to 'sell' his co-hosts' points.
  • 'Pardon the Interruption' (PTI) is highlighted as an example of successful, long-running sports commentary, enduring on ESPN since 2001.

  • The longevity of "Pardon the Interruption" (PTI) is discussed, attributing its enduring appeal to the chemistry between its hosts, Tony Kornheiser and Mike Wilbon, and the format's ability to capture genuine sports opinions.
  • The perceived value of PTI has increased as other similar shows have disappeared.
  • Questions are raised about PTI's future and whether it should continue without its original hosts, drawing parallels to "Inside the NBA" and the concept of lifetime contracts at ESPN.
  • The discussion notes how shows like "Cold Pizza" emerged while PTI maintained its relevance in the evolving business side of sports media.

  • The discussion centers on whether Los Angeles Clippers owner Steve Ballmer was aware of alleged underhanded dealings related to Kawhi Leonard's contract.
  • Chris Mannix expressed skepticism about the Clippers' situation, suggesting teams provide "sweeteners," but noted the "magnitude" of the alleged $7 million per year payment to Leonard for doing nothing was astonishing.
  • Participants debate the NBA's evidentiary standard for punishment, contrasting it with a legal trial and referencing past cases like the Minnesota Timberwolves situation.
  • Further analysis explores the potential consequences for the Clippers, considering the destabilizing effect of potential sanctions versus the league doing nothing, with financial implications for other owners discussed.

  • The specific financial details of the alleged violation are debated, questioning the amount and necessity of additional payments to Kawhi Leonard, especially considering his injury status.
  • The societal implications of the scandal are explored, with some viewing it as a microcosm of how the wealthy can bend rules.
  • Participants question the motivation behind paying Kawhi Leonard extra money to stay with the Clippers, debating whether the payments were to secure him on his first contract or a subsequent one, and if the amount was truly less than the maximum possible salary.
  • A hypothetical scenario suggests Uncle Dennis sought more money, and a 'random company' allegedly facilitated payment to avoid scrutiny, referencing Russell Westbrook's situation.

  • The discussion delves into theories that the Clippers might be trying to circumvent the salary cap by overpaying Kawhi Leonard, analyzing the discrepancy between his alleged $7 million annual payment for no work and other athletes' endorsement deals.
  • Participants discuss the possibility of NBA teams and executives facing scrutiny due to past questionable practices, citing instances like players using team planes or receiving questionable endorsements.
  • The conversation references Mark Cuban and the "Cuban defense," mentioning Dirk Nowitzki's past contract situation and other business dealings that may not 'pass the smell test.'
  • The idea that such questionable practices are common in the league is discussed, with examples like VIP tickets or luxury stays, and the specific amount of $28 million highlighted as significant.

  • Terence Crawford's inactivity, fighting only once a year, raises concerns about his performance, particularly against Israel Madrimov.
  • The matchmaking strategy for Crawford's move to 154 pounds is questioned, suggesting that facing an awkward stylistic opponent like Madrimov may have hindered his offensive capabilities more than the weight difference.
  • Panelists acknowledged historical examples of fighters moving up successfully (e.g., Sugar Ray Leonard, Thomas Hearns) but cautioned that size often remains critical, citing Jermell Charlo's performance against Canelo Alvarez as a cautionary tale.
  • Canelo Alvarez's recent performance is debated, with one guest suggesting a decline in his "meat and potatoes" style and inability to cut the ring off, while another counters that an early morning fight time in Saudi Arabia and potential lack of motivation affected his performance.

  • Strategies for defeating Canelo Alvarez emphasize the necessity of throwing power shots, specifically right hands, citing Gennady Golovkin and Dmitry Bivol as examples.
  • A jab-only strategy, like some have attempted, is deemed insufficient to beat Canelo, though a powerful jab can control the pace, with Gennady Golovkin's jab cited as controlling a fight.
  • Initial concerns are raised about Crawford's significant weight gain for the fight, as he is moving up from 147 pounds, with his current weight around 169 pounds.
  • Speculation suggests Crawford's weight gain could potentially impact his speed and stamina, contrasting with Canelo's experience fighting at higher weights.

  • Terence Crawford's personality is described as "ruthless" and singularly focused on winning, drawing comparisons to Kobe Bryant's mentality.
  • He is also characterized by having "rabbit ears," being highly aware of and responsive to public perception and criticism, even engaging with skeptics on social media.
  • Concerns are raised about Crawford's fight tendencies, specifically his inclination towards "firefights" and engaging in exchanges despite having a clear physical advantage, which could be disadvantageous against Canelo.
  • Crawford's slow starting tendencies are highlighted, raising questions about his ability to build an early lead against Canelo, who also has a history of slow starts.

  • The current state of professional boxing is questioned amidst changing business models, the rise of streaming platforms, and the influence of Saudi Arabian investment.
  • Recent mega-fights facilitated by Saudi money, like Usyk vs. Fury, are acknowledged as positive outcomes, but concerns are raised about logistical challenges of global event scheduling and fighters delaying bouts for potentially larger purses.
  • The importance of fighter activity for the sport's momentum is emphasized, contrasting boxing's sporadic major events with the UFC's consistent output, noting top stars like Canelo Alvarez and Terence Crawford fight only once or twice a year.
  • Floyd Mayweather, Oleksandr Usyk, Andre Ward, and Manny Pacquiao are identified as the four greatest boxers of the first 25 years of the 21st century, with Canelo Alvarez and Bernard Hopkins mentioned as close contenders.
