The Big Picture

‘Wicked: For Good’ Is No Good. But ‘Train Dreams’ Is.

Key Takeaways

  • New trailers for Charli XCX's 'The Moment' and 'The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping' were released.
  • The film 'Wicked: For Good' generated strong opinions due to its narrative choices and character portrayals.
  • Director John M. Chu's 'Wicked: For Good' faced scrutiny regarding its box office potential and Oscar chances.
  • Clint Bentley's film 'Train Dreams' received praise for its sincere depiction of grief and impressive cinematography.
  • Director Clint Bentley shared insights into adapting 'Train Dreams' and discussed the evolving landscape of independent film.

Deep Dive

  • The hosts discussed the trailer for 'The Moment,' a mockumentary about Charli XCX's recent tour; guest Juliet Litman found it charming.
  • Some hosts expressed confusion about the trailer but acknowledged Charli XCX's meta-commentary on pop stardom.
  • Charli XCX was noted for logging 219 movies on Letterboxd in November, showcasing an appreciation for director Paul Thomas Anderson.
  • The mockumentary style was compared to films like 'Waiting for Guffman,' suggesting a trend in pop star documentaries.

  • Amanda Dobbins and Juliet Litman introduced 'Wicked: For Good,' the second installment of the film adaptation by director John M. Chu.
  • Both hosts admitted to not being deeply familiar with the source material, experiencing the narrative in real-time through the films.
  • Juliet Litman expressed excitement for the film, citing her positive reception of the 'Wicked' stage musical's second act and its songs, particularly 'For Good'.
  • The returning cast includes Cynthia Erivo, Ariana Grande, Jonathan Bailey, Jeff Goldblum, and Michelle Yeoh.

  • The hosts expressed frustration with the plot and story choices, finding the retconning of the original 'Wizard of Oz' problematic.
  • Ariana Grande and Cynthia Erivo's performances were deemed adequate but not well-directed, leading to an unconvincing central relationship.
  • The film's focus on a character's internal world and dream sequences contrasted with the original 'Wizard of Oz' book's allegorical commentary on money and populism.
  • The narrative was criticized for characters appearing in isolation, leading to a lack of perceived interconnectivity and baffling integration of characters like the Tin Man and Lion.

  • The film's literal approach was seen as enervating and frustrating, despite elements like Glinda's costumes being appealing.
  • Dorothy was portrayed as a villain and a murderer, with hosts questioning this interpretation of the original 'Wizard of Oz' narrative.
  • Pacing issues and padding were noted, especially in the latter half, to balance runtime with the first movie.
  • The increased presence and role of animals, including a scene with a goat and cage confrontations, stretched the narrative.

  • Discussion focused on 'bizarre' plot contrivances, such as Nessa's transformation into the Wicked Witch of the East, driven by unrequited love.
  • Questions were raised about the motivation behind the Munchkins' happiness after Nessa's death and Nessa's role in creating the Tin Man.
  • The film made deviations from the musical and original 'Wizard of Oz' concerning characters like Nessa Rose, the Tin Man, and the Scarecrow.
  • The explanation for the ruby slippers' color change was also questioned by the hosts and Juliet Litman.

  • The ending of 'Wicked: For Good,' where Elphaba fakes her death and runs off with the Scarecrow, was critically analyzed.
  • Director John M. Chu's integration of 'The Wizard of Oz' elements was debated, with some perceiving a loss of camp and fun compared to the first film.
  • This approach, termed 'Avengersification,' was questioned for its impact on the story and potential box office, despite Universal's marketing.
  • Jonathan Bailey's portrayal of the Scarecrow was noted for limited screen time and a perceived lack of chemistry with his co-star.

  • The film is predicted to gross less than the original film's $758 million, with an anticipated $130-$150 million opening weekend.
  • A steep decline in box office performance is predicted after the opening weekend, despite strong pre-sales from affection for the first film.
  • The press tour for 'Wicked: For Good' was described as lower-key, attributed to incidents affecting Ariana Grande and Cynthia Erivo.
  • Repeat viewings, a factor in the first film's success, are questioned for the sequel.

  • The first 'Wicked' film received 10 Oscar nominations and two wins, appreciated by filmmakers.
  • The sequel's potentially lower critical reception and box office, coupled with a less robust press tour, could impact its Best Picture chances.
  • Ariana Grande is anticipated to receive a nomination for her performance, with confidence expressed in a potential win.
  • Nominations are predicted in technical categories such as costume design, production design, and makeup/hairstyling, with lower likelihood for score, sound, visual effects, and editing.

  • Clint Bentley's 'Train Dreams,' based on a Dennis Johnson novella, was described as a beautiful and moving portrayal of a logger's life.
  • Starring Joel Edgerton and Felicity Jones, the film spans the protagonist's life with a naturalistic style and beautiful cinematography.
  • The film effectively conveyed themes of grief and loss without explicit dialogue, highlighting the character's 'pained experience'.
  • The cast and cinematography were praised, including William H. Macy's performance and innovative camera work like a shot strapped to a log.

  • Director Clint Bentley discussed his initial encounter with Dennis Johnson's novella, which he initially thought was unadaptable.
  • He found a narrative shape by drawing inspiration from Paul Schrader's ideas about screenwriting originating from oral traditions.
  • Bentley's personal connection to themes of balancing passion for work and family, and experiences with grief and COVID-19's societal impact, resonated with the story.
  • Producers Marissa McMahon, Ashley Schlafer, and Will Janowitz initially sought a filmmaker for the project.

  • Bentley detailed the intentionality behind capturing authentic logging scenes, using working logging areas and constructed elements.
  • Extensive visual effects were employed to create seamless, environmentally conscious visuals, remaining undetectable to the viewer.
  • Digital photography was used for flexibility and a fast-paced shooting schedule, with collaboration with colorists to achieve a softer look.
  • Narration was included to convey the inner thoughts of the quiet protagonist, Robert Greinier, and capture the literary voice of Dennis Johnson.

  • Bentley reflected on his commitment to filmmaking as a vocation, making a conscious decision to pursue it as an art form despite potential lack of lucrative returns.
  • He expressed frustration over the difficulty of exhibition for independent films, even critically acclaimed ones like 'Jockey,' especially during the COVID-19 pandemic.
  • 'Train Dreams' secured a unique distribution model, including a theatrical release and a platform on Netflix, providing wider accessibility.
  • Bentley likened independent filmmakers to 'alley cats,' adapting to available resources in the evolving film industry.
