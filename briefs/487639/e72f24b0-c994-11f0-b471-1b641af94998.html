‘Marty Supreme’ Is the Moment, With Josh Safdie!

Key Takeaways

  • Sean Fennessey and Amanda Dobbins introduce 'Marty Supreme' as one of the year's best films.
  • The movie features a unique blend of 1950s setting, 1980s music, and 2020s editing.
  • Timothée Chalamet's performance as Marty is central, embodying fierce ambition and confidence.
  • Director Josh Safdie discusses post-'Uncut Gems' pressure and fatherhood's influence on 'Marty Supreme's' mature themes.
  • Box office projections for 'Marty Supreme' range from $36 million to $57 million domestically.
  • The film's casting is noted for its extensive and unconventional choices, including cameos from public figures.

Deep Dive

  • 'Marty Supreme' marks a shift to a period piece, blending a 1950s backdrop with 1980s-inspired music and 2020s editing.
  • The filmmaking style creates a 'Fantasia dream nightmare state,' distinct from the anxiety-inducing realism of 'Uncut Gems.'
  • Timothée Chalamet's performance as Marty embodies ferocious ambition, drawing comparisons to characters from 'King of Comedy' and performances by Al Pacino.

  • The film features a propulsive opening with an evocative shoe store robbery that rapidly transitions to a London scene.
  • Key characters introduced swiftly include Lloyd, Pico Iyer, the ITTA leader, and Kay Stone, portrayed by Gwyneth Paltrow.
  • Paltrow's performance and chemistry with Timothée Chalamet are highlighted amid the film's rapid plot developments.

  • Marty's post-Japan journey involves a hospital visit to Rachel and meeting his newborn son, signifying a profound life-altering transition.
  • In the nursery scene, Timothée Chalamet's performance as Marty conveys immediate, overwhelming emotion, a callback to 'Call Me by Your Name.'
  • The scene concludes with an extended close-up, featuring 'Everybody Wants to Rule the World' by Tears for Fears, enhancing the emotional impact with the sound of a crying baby.

  • Box Office Theory projects 'Marty Supreme's' domestic gross between $36 million and $57 million.
  • A $50 million outcome is considered positive, while $36 million is seen as "rough," especially given the holiday release and lack of adult competition.
  • Marketing aims to broaden the audience beyond typical A24 fans through celebrity endorsements and popular media appearances to generate strong word-of-mouth.

  • Director Josh Safdie described using characters as vessels in notebooks to obfuscate personal experiences following 'Uncut Gems.'
  • The success of 'Uncut Gems,' a project with a decade-long journey, left Safdie with a hollow feeling of emptiness and embarrassment regarding what to do next.
  • He notes that creative endeavors can feel lonely despite collaboration, a feeling that persisted until the onset of the pandemic.

  • The film's inspiration stems from "Confessions of a Table Tennis Champion and Hustler," leading to research into the sport's history.
  • Post-WWII Japan saw the invention of the sponge paddle, dramatically changing the game by making points quicker.
  • The rapid evolution of table tennis and its individual stories became the emotional narrative foreground for the film, paralleling post-war cultural shifts towards individualism.

  • Director Josh Safdie encouraged actors to use a slang index, avoiding strict historical language, to create a modern yet timeless feel.
  • Collaborated with production designer Jack Fist, costume designer Miyako Belizzi, cinematographer Darius Kanji, and hair/makeup artist Kay Giorgio.
  • The film utilized approximately 3,500 extras to maintain authentic period settings.
  • 'Everybody Wants to Rule the World' was used as an emotional 'needle drop,' connecting themes of anxiety and control, with the film opening on the word 'change.'

  • The film explores themes of control, particularly relevant to Marty's lack of it in the final scene.
  • The guest praised the entire cast, highlighting Lloyd's heartbreaking performance during a business card scene and Gwyneth Paltrow's performance.
  • Long takes in the Japan sequence captured nuanced performances, including Kevin O'Leary's reactions filmed simultaneously.
  • A hotel room conversation between Marty and Kay, involving rapid dialogue and underlying meaning, required extensive rewriting to mirror real-life complexity.
