The 25 Best Movies of the Century: No. 3 - '25th Hour’

Key Takeaways

  • Sean and Amanda ranked Spike Lee's '25th Hour' third on their 25 best 21st-century films list.
  • The 2002 film is an under-discussed masterpiece deeply resonant with the post-9/11 era in New York.
  • Edward Norton's subdued performance as Monty Brogan marked a career highlight, exploring resignation and regret.
  • Spike Lee's distinct stylistic choices and Rodrigo Prieto's cinematography contribute to the film's emotional impact.
  • '25th Hour' critiques corporate power, explores complex male friendships, and nuances morality in its narrative.

Deep Dive

  • Sean Fennessey and Amanda Dobbins discuss Spike Lee's 2002 film '25th Hour' as part of their "25 for 25" series.
  • The film was initially ranked second on their list of 25 best 21st-century movies but moved to third after a 'crisis of confidence'.
  • Hosts noted its surprising absence from audience guesses despite its high critical regard by themselves and others like Chris Ryan and Spike Lee himself.

  • '25th Hour' is analyzed as a 'loss of innocence' film, contrasting its bleakness with hopeful narratives like Sam Raimi's 2002 'Spider-Man'.
  • The film's direct engagement with immediate post-9/11 feelings of uncertainty and fear might make it difficult for audiences to revisit.
  • Amanda Dobbins views '25th Hour' as a major convergence of artistic and personal significance, reflecting the present moment.

  • Edward Norton's portrayal of Monty Brogan in '25th Hour' is cited as a career peak in 2002, one of four films he headlined that year.
  • His performance is described as subdued and internal, reminiscent of 1970s tragic characters like Al Pacino or Marlon Brando.
  • A scene with Monty's internal "fuck you" monologue externalized in a mirror highlights themes of self-loathing and New York's abrasiveness.

  • The film depicts collective rage and sadness post-9/11, particularly through a character's 'f--k you' rant and scenes near Ground Zero cleanup efforts.
  • It explores thematic richness, including a critique of corporate power, referencing Enron executives and the symbolism of the Twin Towers.
  • The narrative also introduces complex male friendships, contrasting Frank's insistence on survival with Jacob's initial acceptance of finality.

  • The film explores Monty Brogan's desire to be beaten before prison, signifying male expression and frustration regarding his arrest and potential betrayal.
  • It delves into the specific legal context of early 2000s drug trade, referencing mandatory minimums and the Rockefeller laws.
  • The narrative presents a nuanced morality, fostering affection for the flawed Monty despite his failures as a criminal and his impending seven-year prison sentence.

  • '25th Hour' is noted for its 1970s sensibility in exploring the "perils of masculinity," including "creepy" behavior towards underage women.
  • Sympathetic casting of actors like Philip Seymour Hoffman and Edward Norton makes morally questionable actions impactful, particularly Hoffman's club scene.
  • The film also critiques New York City's status-seeking culture and the "soulless commodification of people" observed in the financial world.

  • The film depicts a strained yet sincere father-son relationship, highlighting Monty's struggle to communicate his reality to his father, played by Brian Cox.
  • The ending features Monty's father deliberately orchestrating an assault to facilitate his son's escape from prison.
  • This leads to an imagined sequence of them driving towards an uncertain future, praised for its dreamlike quality and emotional impact with integrated flashbacks.

  • Rodrigo Prieto's cinematography is praised for giving '25th Hour' a unique texture and sensibility, working alongside collaborators like Terrence Blanchard.
  • The film received rave reviews from critics like A.O. Scott and a 3.5-star rating from Roger Ebert, who compared Monty to Enron's betrayal of trust.
  • Despite its critical acclaim, the film lacks the widespread recognition of other acclaimed 21st-century films, notably absent from The New York Times' list.
