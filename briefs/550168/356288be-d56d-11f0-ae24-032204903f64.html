The Joe Rogan Experience

#2424 - Jelly Roll

Key Takeaways

  • Jelly Roll achieved over 300 pounds of weight loss through a dedicated personal transformation.
  • He addressed food addiction and emotional eating via therapy and consistent lifestyle changes.
  • New health regimens dramatically improved his insulin, A1C, C-reactive protein, and testosterone levels.
  • Changing social circles and digital content consumption were crucial for his positive personal growth.
  • Hunting became a meditative outdoor pursuit, providing physical benefits and a connection to nature.
  • The guest received an official invitation to become a member of the Grand Ole Opry.

Deep Dive

  • The guest fluctuated between 480 and 560 pounds in 2022, previously attempting weight loss without sustainable approaches.
  • He recognized overeating as a biological loop, not just a willpower failure, drawing parallels to drug addiction.
  • Food addiction is uniquely challenging as the addictive substance (food) is also necessary for survival.

  • Initial blood work revealed a diabetic marker of 6.4 (pre-diabetes) and an insulin level over 40, significantly above the desired level under five.
  • High insulin levels were identified as preventing the body from accessing fat reserves, making weight loss difficult even with fasting.
  • He began a slow treatment plan under Dr. Denise and Gary Brecken, including a year of low-dose metformin to reduce insulin levels.
  • The guest initially struggled with being honest about his eating habits with his nutritionist, hindering progress.

  • The guest describes replacing harmful addictions with beneficial ones, such as exercise, after overcoming other vices.
  • He emphasizes the concept that individuals imitate their environment, highlighting the negative impact of associating with struggling or negative individuals.
  • Decision to distance himself from toxic relationships after realizing the detrimental effect on his own progress.

  • The guest aims to engage in hunting and camping by next winter, finding restorative and medicinal benefits in spending time alone in nature.
  • He describes nature as offering a pure existence, devoid of ego or societal pressures, contrasting it with social media.
  • His first hunting experiences included a rifle kill of a mule deer and a bow hunt for a black bear, with aspirations to hunt elk.
  • He refutes the misconception that bear meat is unpalatable, comparing its taste to beef.

  • The guest's pardon paperwork has been submitted to the governor, with hopeful anticipation for December, though he would be pardoned, not exonerated.
  • He advocates for legislative reform to restore rights like hunting, citing its positive impact on his mental and physical health.
  • He expresses a desire for the right to carry a firearm to reduce security costs and for hunting to feed his family and manage deer populations.
  • The discussion references a hunting location in South Texas, "Hunt Cactus Jack," known for large deer.

  • Archery is described as a "moving meditation" requiring intense concentration, clearing the mind of distractions and worries.
  • The practice demands complete presence and focus, leading to a meditative state beneficial for mental clarity.
  • Managing nerves and staying calm during the hunting process is crucial, akin to a cat hunting prey.
  • During a difficult deer hunt, the guest prioritized an ethical kill by not taking a questionable shot, a decision praised by his guide.

  • Conquering a difficult hill during a run became a metaphor for tackling the hardest tasks first in life.
  • Consistent effort in physical challenges transforms initial difficulties into enjoyment, fostering a sense of accomplishment and mental preparedness.
  • The guest applies this philosophy to parenting, guiding his son through fear by acknowledging his own past struggles and demonstrating readiness.
  • He encourages embracing challenges and discomfort to build resilience, attributing success to good decisions derived from experience gained through bad decisions.

  • Hunting is described as a rewarding pursuit, with the guest finding enjoyment and anticipating future successes through persistence.
  • Wild game offers significant nutritional benefits, providing twice the protein of beef with fewer calories, akin to an athlete's diet.
  • His first successful mule deer hunt led him to abandon vegetarianism and fully embrace hunting.
  • The activity provides a spiritual and historical connection, deeply ingrained in human DNA for survival, as evidenced by his daughter's excitement catching her first fish.

  • Significant weight gain is linked to underlying trauma or deeply ingrained patterns, with the guest recalling being singled out for the "Husky" clothing section as a child.
  • Childhood experiences of shame, reinforced by children's comments, contributed to his early struggles with weight.
  • Therapy helped him overcome shame and recognize food as a distraction from unresolved issues, like unresponded emails.
  • He now works to express feelings in real-time, moving away from using "the pantry" as a "gas station" for emotional avoidance.

  • The guest, now able to do 20 push-ups, compares his fitness to Bert Kreischer, noting Tommy Buns' significant weight loss journey.
  • Tommy Buns won a weight loss challenge against Bert, leading to Bert shaving his beard live on air, while Bert's struggles with yo-yo dieting persist due to alcohol and food.
  • Alcohol's role in unhealthy eating habits and weight gain is highlighted, leading the guest to quit drinking due to inflammation and other health consequences.
  • Despite health struggles, Bert Kreischer is noted for extraordinary genetics, capable of running a marathon without training, and his success is tied to genuine personality.

  • Modern technology, particularly smartphones, is discussed as fostering a false sense of connection, leading to increased loneliness and stunted social development in younger generations.
  • The guest values deep, personal conversations, drawing parallels to therapy and the unexpected rise of long-form podcasts in popular culture.
  • He emphasizes the necessity of longer, in-person interviews to move beyond prepared statements and delve into genuine insights about a person's life and motivations.
  • Extended conversations are seen as revealing a person's true self, fulfilling a fundamental human need for authentic connection that social media often fails to provide.

  • The guest performed his song 'Hard Fought Hallelujah' with Brandon Lake at the Vatican, specifically in St. Peter's Square, an event he considers a potential career-best vocal achievement.
  • This spiritual performance, organized by figures like Pharrell and Andrea Bocelli, drew hundreds of thousands and marked the first time live music was performed there.
  • Jennifer Hudson unexpectedly joined him, as he was the only artist initially scheduled without a guest.
  • His wife is releasing her book 'Stripped Down' in February, and the guest also introduced his song 'Son of a Sinner'.
