The Joe Rogan Experience

#2377 - Carrot Top

Key Takeaways

  • Carrot Top faced career setbacks from a Fox ban over a Taco Bell prop bit and Johnny Carson's 'variety' dislike.
  • The comedian discusses adapting his long-running Las Vegas Luxor residency, noting changes from touring life.
  • Societal views on physical appearance, from wigs and implants to the ethics of cosmetic surgery, were explored.
  • The conversation delves into the ethical questions surrounding AI-generated music and its implications for artists unable to perform.
  • Reflections on the pre-internet era contrast limited information and celebrity with current societal dynamics.
  • Discussions around weight loss drugs like Ozempic and extreme body modifications raise questions about health versus aesthetics.
  • The complexities of the music industry, artist struggles with labels, and discovery of new talent were examined.
  • Carrot Top’s experience performing politically charged prop comedy on 'The Tonight Show' for Dick Cheney prompted diverse reactions.
  • The evolution of comedy, from late-night TV's constraints to podcasts and large stadium tours, highlights industry shifts.

Deep Dive

  • The guest discussed the difficulty of keeping secrets, referencing his brother's silence about his work as an F-16 pilot.
  • He mentioned the consequences of revealing classified information, even if it were to be declassified.
  • The discussion occurred around 26:53, addressing the implications of security clearances.

  • The guest described the Luxor venue as 'breathtaking' before discussing its adjacent exhibits.
  • The 'Bodies' exhibit's origin from Chinese political prisoners and ethical concerns about the plastination process were noted.
  • The proximity of the 'Bodies' exhibit and the Titanic Museum to the comedy venue was highlighted around 40:21.

  • The host and guest reflected on the pre-internet era, noting widespread anxieties about potential conflicts like Russia.
  • They discussed limited global event awareness and the smaller number of famous public figures during that time.
  • The guest characterized people then as 'wild animals' learning to navigate technology and societal expectations around 55:16.

  • Discussion covered Burt Reynolds' acting range, contrasting his roles in 'Smokey and the Bandit' with his intense performance in 'Deliverance'.
  • The host recounted meeting Reynolds on 'The Tonight Show,' where Reynolds threw a drink on 'Double Dare' host Mark Summers.
  • The segment explored the movie 'The Bandit,' noting its groundbreaking use of the fourth wall break around 1:05:20.

  • The discussion covered Billy Joel's 1973 album 'The Stranger' and the evolution of music consumption from albums to digital streaming.
  • Specific focus was given to the lyrics of 'Scenes from an Italian Restaurant,' particularly 'bottle of red' and 'bottles of white,' sparking debate.
  • The guest recalled listening to 'The Stranger' as a child and recognizing its significance in music genres.

  • Speakers analyzed 'South Park's' comedic success, attributing it to its unrealistic animation style, which allows the show to tackle controversial subjects.
  • The show's unchanging characters, who remain perpetually in high school, were highlighted as an advantage for long-term comedic storytelling.
  • Discussion included a hypothetical final episode using hyper-realistic 3D AI animation for a violent and ridiculous conclusion around 1:33:45.

  • A comedian shared an experience where a new joke about a Las Vegas billboard received a significantly stronger reaction on a second night without apparent changes in delivery.
  • The host mentioned performing his show in Spanish on Tuesdays.
  • Comedian Tom Segura's ability to perform stand-up in Spanish was discussed around 1:49:52.

  • The discussion centered on the use of the word 'pussy' in comedy and audience perception, contrasting it with general profanity.
  • Eddie Murphy's bit about Bill Cosby's reaction to swearing was referenced.
  • The guest recounted seeing Richard Pryor perform live as a teenager, realizing the power of stand-up comedy around 2:02:28.
  • He also described opening for Steve Harvey in Birmingham, observing an all-black crowd's unique, enthusiastic laughter at 2:03:21.

  • The discussion covered the origin of weight loss drugs, initially for diabetes and then adapted for obesity, becoming a significant moneymaker.
  • Speakers debated their use, acknowledging benefits for the severely obese but expressing concern about overuse by those lacking discipline.
  • The conversation also touched on extreme body modification, including a person with a $21,000 back implant and discussions of fake abs and their side effects around 2:15:45.

  • The conversation highlighted Prince's past issues with his record label, including his name change to a symbol.
  • This was compared to Billy Joel's early career struggles, such as signing away rights for 15 years.
  • The host and guest explored contemporary artists like Jelly Roll, Oliver Anthony, and Teddy Swims, noting their unique origins around 2:30:10.
  • They also discussed the difficulty of discovering new artists amidst a vast musical landscape.

  • The guest recounted security concerns during rehearsal for a 'Tonight Show' appearance with Dick Cheney, involving Secret Service agents.
  • He performed a Cheney-themed comedy routine with props, including a rifle and an 'Operation' game featuring Cheney's face, noting Cheney's varied reactions.
  • A prop bit involving a piggy bank designed for gay men caused a significant reaction from Cheney's daughter around 2:43:10.
