JRE MMA Show #170 with Michael "Venom" Page

Key Takeaways

  • Michael "Venom" Page's unique point-fighting style and takedown defense challenge traditional MMA strikers.
  • MVP's childhood martial arts success, including an Ayaska World Championship win, profoundly impacted his fighting ability.
  • The MMA community faces frustrations over high-profile heavyweight matchups, notably the Jon Jones vs. Francis Ngannou fight.
  • Fighter marketability and personal branding are critical for success, with MVP deliberately crafting his "Venom" persona.
  • Weight cutting is a controversial practice in MMA, labeled "legalized cheating" by the guest, with calls for reform.
  • The mental aspect of fighting, including anxiety and resilience, is as crucial as physical prowess for athletes.
  • MMA training philosophies vary significantly, with ongoing debate between "meathead" and more technical, recovery-focused approaches.
  • Fighter injuries, such as MVP's ankle and shoulder issues, significantly impact performance and require complex management.

Deep Dive

  • The guest's fight against Cyborg resulted in a gruesome injury, compared by doctors to car accident trauma.
  • Despite the severity of the injury, the opponent initially expressed a desire to fight again.
  • The guest recalled an awkward encounter with the injured fighter two years later at an MMA event, where they exchanged a handshake.

  • The guest's early martial arts career involved frequent travel for competitions with his father, marked by initial struggles and losses over several years.
  • A significant turning point occurred at age 11 or 12, when he won an Ayaska qualifiers in Birmingham and subsequently the World Championships in Orlando, Florida.
  • This pivotal win led to an "instantaneous shift" in his martial arts abilities, enabling him to dominate previously challenging opponents and achieve an undefeated streak.
  • He progressed to fighting in senior divisions (18+) at age 13, winning a British championship for his weight class, despite his father's initial hesitations.

  • Tom Aspinall is highlighted for his speed and agility as a heavyweight, presenting a difficult matchup for Jon Jones due to his size and ability to defend takedowns.
  • Francis Ngannou, described as a "tank" with a powerful background from working in sand mines, is considered another formidable challenge for Jon Jones at heavyweight.
  • The host expressed frustration over the failure to materialize a fight between Jon Jones and Francis Ngannou, emphasizing Ngannou's marketability as a dominant heavyweight.

  • The conversation contrasts the perceived harshness and effectiveness of Cuban training systems with Western methods, noting a concept where athletes receive more food based on their caliber.
  • London Shoot Fighters, a prominent MMA gym in the UK since the mid-90s, is characterized by its demanding, disciplined coaching style where coaches lead by example through rigorous participation.
  • The guest emphasized the foundational principle of "iron sharpens iron" in producing elite fighters, acknowledging the rise of data analysts and scientific approaches in MMA while reaffirming traditional methods.

  • The guest expressed frustration with the UFC's slow matchmaking process, possibly due to over 674 contracted fighters and the integration of a new streaming partner.
  • He outlined a potential path to a title shot at 170 lbs, including fighting undefeated prospect Morales and potentially facing the winner of Leon Edwards vs. Pratas.
  • The host and guest discussed the increasing talent level in MMA, noting that younger fighters are developing advanced skills and integrating high-level techniques from childhood, making them formidable competitors at an early age.

  • The host and guest emphasized the importance of aggressive finishes over purely defensive tactics in MMA, agreeing that wrestling, while crucial, should ideally lead to a finish rather than just control.
  • They debated the entertainment value of grappling-heavy fights versus striking-focused bouts, acknowledging that while grappling is technically complex, it may not always excite viewers unfamiliar with its intricacies.
  • The guest expressed frustration with fighters who rely solely on takedowns and control to win decisions without attempting to inflict damage or finish the fight.

  • The Tyson Fury vs. Francis Ngannou fight was cited as an example where Fury likely underestimated Ngannou, who had no prior boxing experience, leading to a knockdown and an unexpectedly competitive bout.
  • The Mike Tyson vs. Buster Douglas fight was presented as another instance of underestimation, with Douglas delivering one of the greatest heavyweight title fight performances despite Tyson's perceived complacency.
  • The discussion touched on talented individuals, like UFC fighter Galore Bufon, who possess unique and unpredictable skills, such as a "Hadouken" slam KO and cartwheel front kicks, but may not always reach their full potential under pressure.

  • The guest recounted intense nervousness before his first professional fight against an opponent named Dishman and his second UFC bout against Ian Garry, attributing anxiety to promotion expectations and his established reputation.
  • He stated he does not need to be angry to compete, contrasting his approach with fighters who use anger, and attributing this to a childhood of fighting siblings where love and camaraderie were maintained.
  • The guest described "ultimate bravery" as seen in fighters like Khalil Roundtree facing defeat and noted how talented individuals who have not experienced significant struggle may become fearful and perform poorly under pressure, contrasting with his upbringing among older, competitive siblings like Jon Jones.

  • The guest's "720 kick" move, a complex aerial maneuver involving a 720-degree rotation, was highlighted as a rare and devastating technique that likely contributed to opponents pulling out of fights.
  • Following a viral fight clip that showcased his unique style, the guest found thousands of negative online comments, leading him to conclude that avoiding comment sections is wise due to prevalent negativity.
  • The host interpreted the viral clip as confirmation of his long-held belief that elite point fighters possess a different, almost untouchable skill set that prevents opponents from closing the distance effectively.
  • An early fight against Raymond, a successful Glory fighter, demonstrated Raymond's speed and power with counter-stamps, impacting the guest's mindset and making him hesitant to commit to his own strikes.

  • The guest strongly dislikes weight cutting, labeling it "legalized cheating" and expressing a preference for fighters to compete at a healthy, natural weight.
  • Strategic weigh-in tactics, such as those used by prominent fighters like Mayweather and Javante Davis, are employed to disadvantage opponents, often by the "A-side" fighter.
  • Proposed reforms include implementing 10-pound increments for weight classes to reduce extreme weight cutting and suggesting random weight checks during training camp with a six-pound allowance between weigh-ins and camp weight.
  • Examples of extreme weight cutting cited include Travis Lutter's severe dehydration before fighting Anderson Silva and TJ Dillashaw's visibly unhealthy appearance after dropping to flyweight.

  • Michael "Venom" Page discussed his shoulder issues, which required multiple surgeries, and his preference for non-surgical rehabilitation for his shoulder and lat injuries, even when it impacted his ability to perform exercises during Bellator fights.
  • He recounted a severe ankle injury sustained the day before his fight against Ian Garry, which, exacerbated by weight cutting, required extensive treatments including shock therapy and acupuncture at the UFC Performance Institute.
  • The conversation referenced the Ankalaev vs. Pereira rematch, where Pereira reportedly fought with a broken hand and rotavirus, leading to speculation about how physical condition impacts fight performance.

  • The guest's wife, a Pilates instructor, developed the Cloud9 Collective app, which gained popularity during COVID-19 and offers cycle syncing for women, aligning workouts with their 28-day menstrual cycles.
  • Pilates, initially developed for male soldiers recovering from war injuries, is highlighted for strengthening stabilizing muscles and its use by boxers like Sergey Kovalev to enhance strength.
  • The guest emphasized the need for more awareness and consideration of female physiology in sports training and scheduling, highlighting differences from the prevalent male-centric models.
  • His own training incorporates plyometrics and elements from martial arts gymnastics and kata training, allowing for explosive movements and complex maneuvers like 720 spins, aiding his strategy of observing opponents' reactions to feints.
