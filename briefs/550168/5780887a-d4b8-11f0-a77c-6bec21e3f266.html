The Joe Rogan Experience

JRE MMA Show #171 with Brendan Allen

Key Takeaways

  • Logistical complexities and environmental factors significantly challenge outdoor UFC events.
  • Fighters frequently battle through extreme weight cuts and compete with various injuries.
  • Mental fortitude, strategic coaching, and advanced recovery are crucial for fighter success.
  • UFC judging fairness and drug testing protocols remain subjects of ongoing debate.
  • The evolution of MMA, from its Gracie family roots to modern athleticism, is profound.

Deep Dive

  • Discussions included a potential UFC event at the White House with Donald Trump, questioning logistics for 8-9 title fights in 8 weight classes.
  • Practical challenges of outdoor fights involve security, weather, and slippery conditions on canvas, as seen in a "King of the Cage: Wet and Wild" event in Louisiana.
  • Past outdoor UFC events in Abu Dhabi featured fights like BJ Penn vs Frankie Edgar, and Anderson Silva vs Damian Maia.
  • Heat and humidity in Washington D.C. in June would significantly impact fighters, contrasting with the Sphere event's meticulous planning.

  • Fighters' walking-around weights can surprise, with some significantly exceeding their 185-pound weight class limit before cutting.
  • Weight cutting practices, including dehydration for weigh-ins and rehydration, raise health concerns and led to guest's difficulty making 185 pounds.
  • The guest fought with multiple foot fractures, initially misdiagnosed, and sustained a torn ACL and calf in a previous bout against Paul Craig.
  • Most fighters do not compete at 100%, with the guest detailing a period of sluggishness due to an undiagnosed thyroid issue before a fight in France.

  • Creatine is beneficial for enhanced body recovery, energy levels, and cognitive support, disproving early safety concerns spread by the guest's father.
  • UFC partnerships, such as with Thorne, aid athlete recovery; hyperbaric chambers are also discussed for recovery and potentially lengthening telomeres.
  • A study in Jerusalem indicated a 20-year biological age difference after 60 hyperbaric chamber sessions.
  • Approaching 30, the guest prioritizes recovery and stresses the importance of stretching and consistent effort for flexibility and cardio.

  • The guest emphasizes avoiding unnecessary conflict to protect his family and lifestyle, contrasting with his younger self's desire to prove himself.
  • Seeking conflict can lead to severe injury or fatality, with examples cited of bar fights with fatal outcomes.
  • An anecdote from comedian Kevin James's bouncer experience highlighted a fight resulting in a fatality and jail time.
  • A family story details a great-uncle who survived a near-fatal throat slashing in a bar after narrowly missing carotid arteries.

  • The importance of coaching in MMA is discussed, emphasizing "maestro" coaches like Eric Nicksick who synthesize striking, wrestling, and jiu-jitsu.
  • The guest expressed frustration with generic corner advice and values knowledgeable coaches who understand his specific needs.
  • Wearable technology like Whoop and Aura rings are used to monitor physical metrics such as resting heart rate, despite limitations during active training.
  • Silicone wedding bands are used by the guest during training and fights to prevent severe 'sheathing' injuries common with traditional rings.

  • Concerns were raised about judges lacking combat sports experience, leading to flawed decisions that impact fighter purses, exemplified by a judge not recognizing an Americana submission.
  • The host advocated for a system incentivizing good performance without financially penalizing fighters for subjective judging errors.
  • The guest expressed frustration with opponents' strategies of holding without engaging, as seen in fights against Merab Dvalishvili and Magomedov.
  • The opponent's unique wrestling style focused on hip control and cage work, rather than conventional shots, catching the guest off guard.

  • Khamzat Chimaev's dominant grappling is noted for a skill gap rarely seen at the elite level, with his strength enabling him to compete across middleweight and light heavyweight.
  • Chimaev's ability to maintain dominant positions and transition, even putting champions like Drickus in crucifix multiple times, highlights his effective exploitation of grappling weaknesses.
  • His training with elite strength and conditioning coach Sam Calavita, operating from a "garage" facility in Orange County, California, is described as extremely rigorous.
  • Chimaev battled COVID-19 but continued intense training, potentially worsening his condition and impacting performance.

  • The discussion highlights the dedication of young, hungry athletes who balance manual labor jobs with intense night training.
  • Training with driven individuals provides contagious energy and motivation, pushing fighters to break through difficult sessions.
  • Gordon Ryan's intelligence and conceptual understanding of Jiu-Jitsu are emphasized, noting his 365-day-a-year training and study commitment.
  • The guest contrasts this with his own need for time off to reinvigorate his mind and practice mental repetitions for automating correct movements.

  • Learning from setbacks and openly discussing mental states, including anxiety, has improved performance, contrasting with past suppression of emotions.
  • Working with a sports psychiatrist specializing in combat sports helped the guest manage pre-fight anxiety by recognizing emotions and dissecting root causes.
  • A pamphlet by Rob Gilbert, PhD, and philosophies from David Goggins and Jocko Willink provide mental tools for maintaining a positive mindset before fights.
  • Confronting and dissecting negative thoughts is crucial, as losing to oneself due to internal battles is considered more detrimental than losing to a superior opponent.

  • Kamaru Usman's knee injuries and treatments at bio-facilities in Colombia and Tijuana are discussed.
  • Limitations imposed by the FDA on stem cell treatments in the US are contrasted with international practices in Colombia and Panama.
  • Potential benefits for neurological conditions and injuries are highlighted, referencing Dr. Neil Riordan's work.
  • Personal anecdotes include the speaker's mother's significant knee improvement and Mel Gibson's father's successful hip treatment, both with stem cells in Colombia.

  • The origins of MMA and Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu are traced back to Brazil, noting the Gracie family's pioneering role and involvement in early UFC events.
  • Stories of family dynamics, rivalries, and disputes among figures like Carlos and Horian Gracie shaped the sport's early days.
  • Anecdotes include Hickson Gracie's street fights on the beach against Luta Livre practitioners in Rio de Janeiro, showcasing the raw, no-rules nature of early martial arts.
  • Royce Gracie was chosen for early UFC fights over his brother Hickson to demonstrate jiu-jitsu's effectiveness against larger opponents due to Royce's smaller stature.

  • The evolution of MMA competition levels from the 1990s to today is significant, with current fighters displaying complete skill sets, speed, and intelligence.
  • The host recounts starting backstage interviews for the UFC at UFC 12 in 1997, after beginning jiu-jitsu training at Carlson Gracie's academy.
  • "TRT Vitor" (Vitor Belfort) is remembered as one of the most formidable fighters due to his aggressive style, including a wheel kick against Luke Rockhold.
  • Many younger fans are unaware of legendary fighters like Vitor Belfort and Demetrious Johnson, whose accomplishments, like Johnson's flying knee KO against Adriano Moraes, define martial artistry.
