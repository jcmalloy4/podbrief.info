All-In with Chamath, Jason, Sacks & Friedberg

Can We Trust the FDA? Marty Makary on Science, Power & Patients

Key Takeaways

  • FDA Commissioner Makary is enacting reforms to rebuild public trust and accelerate medical advancements.
  • The FDA aims to streamline drug approvals by reducing trials, animal testing, and leveraging AI.
  • New FDA food guidance emphasizes protein while warning against refined carbohydrates.
  • The U.S. vaccine schedule is under review, proposing a core list of essential childhood vaccines.
  • Efforts are underway to lower drug prices and increase access to over-the-counter medications.

Deep Dive

  • In 10 months, the FDA enacted 42 major reforms, including making rejection letters public for accountability.
  • Reforms target removing artificial food dyes and speeding up drug approval timelines from 10-12 years.
  • The agency is hiring 1,050 new scientists to support ambitious goals for accelerated drug approval pathways.

  • The FDA is reducing pivotal clinical trials from two to one, projected to save companies $100-300 million and shorten timelines.
  • Animal testing requirements are being reduced, specifically eliminating chimpanzee studies for monoclonal antibodies, previously using around 144 chimpanzees per drug.
  • A new pilot program aims to accelerate drug approval decisions to as few as 55 days for drugs addressing unmet public health needs, U.S. manufacturing, or affordability.
  • Companies can receive a voucher for expedited review if their drug meets specific criteria, such as addressing a national priority or lowering drug prices.

  • The FDA is streamlining regulations for cell and gene therapies to accelerate drug approvals.
  • Manufacturing requirements are being customized based on the drug and patient population, moving away from rigid, costly standards.
  • This aims to enable faster access to potentially life-saving treatments for rare diseases.
  • Concerns remain about potential outsourcing of CAR T therapy manufacturing to countries like China, which the FDA has moved to shut down.

  • The FDA's updated food pyramid corrects decades of scientifically inaccurate guidance influenced by industry, which focused on saturated fat while ignoring refined carbohydrates.
  • Previous guidance contributed to an obesity and pre-diabetes epidemic in children by promoting mass-produced refined carbohydrates.
  • New guidance emphasizes protein intake and warns against overconsumption of refined carbohydrates, which function like sugar.
  • Discussion highlighted the importance of soil quality, farming practices, and including more vegetarian protein options like nuts and beans.

  • Concerns were raised regarding U.S. competitiveness with China in biotechnology and the funding of scientific research.
  • NIH funding is proposed for reallocation from traditional treatments to research on root causes of diseases, such as food quality, sleep, and environmental exposures.
  • Critique was made of NIH's traditional focus on genetics, advocating for more research into environmental factors and lifestyle choices contributing to diseases.
  • Approximately 14% of NIH grants, amounting to about $40 billion annually, were directed towards Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) research.

  • A review of the U.S. vaccine schedule under President Trump found it to be an international outlier, recommending 72 doses by age 18.
  • A comparison with 20 other developed countries revealed the U.S. recommends a significantly higher number of vaccine doses for children.
  • A list of approximately 38 core essential vaccines from age 0 to 18 was proposed to rebuild public trust and potentially increase vaccination rates.
  • Paternalism in medicine has damaged public trust, contributing to decreased childhood vaccinations and distrust in health authorities.

  • Drug costs in the United States consume 15% of GDP and are significantly more expensive than in other developed nations.
  • Reforms aim to accelerate biosimilar approvals, reducing R&D costs by over $100 million and shortening timelines from 5-8 years to 2.5 years.
  • The Trump administration's 'most favored nation status pricing' initiative aims to lower drug costs, citing a GLP-1 drug potentially priced at $149 for three months.
  • Transitioning more medications to non-prescription (over-the-counter) status is a major priority for 2026 to increase price transparency and reduce healthcare costs.

  • The FDA is developing guidance for AI decision support and wearables in healthcare diagnostics.
  • A clear consumer lane is being created, distinguishing between general consumer information and regulated medical applications.
  • Wearables providing physiological data will be deregulated, but claims of medical-grade accuracy will require FDA validation.
  • The FDA aims to provide predictability for developers and investors in this rapidly evolving market.

  • Autism diagnoses are increasing, with one in 12 boys in California currently diagnosed, a significant concern compared to previous generations.
  • Hypotheses suggest a potential autoimmune phenomenon where antibodies block folate receptors, hindering neural development, with Lukavorin discussed as a treatment.
  • The role of the microbiome in brain health is examined, noting gut bacteria produce a significant amount of the body's serotonin.
  • Factors like C-sections, infant formula, and antibiotic overuse are identified as detrimental to the microbiome, potentially leading to inflammation and discomfort.
