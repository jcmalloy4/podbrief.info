Everyone Saw ICE Kill Renee Good

Key Takeaways

  • An ICE operation in Minnesota led to a fatal shooting by an agent, captured on video and published by the Minnesota Reformer.
  • The incident ignited community outrage and distrust, prompting local self-protection efforts and political demands for ICE's departure.
  • Conflicting orders from ICE agents at the scene and the administration's defense of the agency highlight ongoing tensions.
  • The anti-ICE movement in Minnesota is broadening, drawing in a wider demographic beyond traditional activists.

Deep Dive

  • A video published by the Minnesota Reformer shows an ICE agent shooting the driver of a vehicle attempting to leave.
  • The vehicle crashed after the shooting, and the agent who fired the shot was observed walking away.
  • Jon Collins, a senior reporter for Minnesota Public Radio News, learned of the shooting via local signal chats around 9:30 AM.
  • Bystanders expressed outrage that medical professionals were prevented from reaching the victim for minutes after the incident.

  • Activists in the Twin Cities established a network using signal chats to track and respond to ICE actions.
  • Information is shared in real-time to deter arrests or harassment, with observers documenting ICE vehicle movements.
  • This network allows community members to follow and document federal agents' activities and attempt to deter them.

  • ICE officers at the scene reportedly issued contradictory orders, with one trying to extract the driver while another told them to leave.
  • The guest suggested these conflicting orders indicated a lack of training and proper procedures among agents.
  • Observers report frequent harassment by ICE agents, including license plate tracking and home visits, to intimidate them.
  • The administration, including DHS, publicly defended ICE and labeled the victim a 'domestic terrorist' through unconventional media channels.

  • Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey demanded ICE leave the city following the shooting, though the guest expressed uncertainty about ICE's actual departure.
  • Residents took self-protection measures due to distrust of local police, especially six years after George Floyd's killing.
  • Governor Tim Walz notified the National Guard about potential needs to defend residents from ICE, raising constitutional concerns.
  • The situation is seen as more tense than similar past events in other cities, partly due to Walz's recent withdrawal from his reelection bid.

  • Minneapolis neighborhoods, previously affected by unrest, repurposed self-patrol Signal chats to monitor ICE activity.
  • These communication channels are now used to protect immigrant neighbors from federal enforcement actions.
  • Discussions are ongoing regarding potential legal action against federal officers by local officials like Hennepin County Prosecutor Mary Moriarty and State Attorney General Keith Ellison.

  • Challenges exist in investigating ICE incidents, as seen in a Chicago case where the agency provided false information, leading to case dismissal.
  • Local Minneapolis authorities expressed outrage that the FBI took over the shooting investigation, distrusting federal self-investigation.
  • An agreement was reached for the state Bureau of Criminal Apprehension to collaborate with the FBI on the investigation.
  • A vigil at the shooting scene was declared an anti-ICE space, drawing parallels to the sustained occupation of George Floyd Square.
