Up First from NPR

Suspected D.C. Shooter Charged, Trump vs. Harvard, SCOTUS Ruling On Agency Firings

Overview

* A shooting at a Jewish museum in Washington D.C. left two Israeli embassy employees dead, with the suspect allegedly claiming he did it "for Palestine" and "for Gaza," prompting increased security around Jewish institutions and investigation as a potential hate crime.

* The Trump administration revoked Harvard University's ability to enroll international students, citing concerns about "fostering violence" and "antisemitism," creating uncertainty for nearly 7,000 international students and potentially signaling broader targeting of international education in the U.S.

* In a significant 6-3 ruling on presidential power, the Supreme Court determined that President Trump can fire members of independent agencies, while creating a special exception for the Federal Reserve, raising concerns about political interference in regulatory bodies.

* The incidents collectively highlight escalating tensions around issues of antisemitism, immigration policy, and executive power in the current political climate.

Content

Shooting Incident at Jewish Museum in Washington, D.C.

* A man named Elias Rodriguez is charged with murdering two Israeli embassy employees in Washington, D.C. during a young diplomats' mixer outside a Jewish museum. * Rodriguez allegedly stated "I did it for Palestine, I did it for Gaza" after the shooting. * The attack is being investigated as a potential hate crime.

Victims

* The victims were Iaron Lichinsky (German/Israeli) and Sarah Milgram (American). * They were a young couple, with Lichinsky planning to propose to Milgram the following week. * Milgram had recently taken the embassy job to combat rising anti-Semitism through diplomacy.

Suspect Details

* Rodriguez flew from Chicago to D.C. the day before the attack. * He declared a firearm in his luggage and bought a ticket to the museum event. * Witnesses reported he shot the victims from behind, continuing to fire even as one tried to crawl away. * Federal agents raided his home and are investigating his motivations.

Law Enforcement Response

* Interim U.S. Attorney Janine Pirro indicated potential additional charges. * D.C. police will increase law enforcement presence around faith-based groups and Jewish institutions. * Officials condemned the violence as an "act of cowardice."

Harvard University and International Students

* The Trump administration revoked Harvard's ability to enroll international students. * Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem cited reasons including "fostering violence, antisemitism, and coordinating with the Chinese Communist Party." * Harvard has nearly 7,000 international students (about 1/4 of student body). * International students expressed fear and uncertainty about their academic futures. * Harvard is suing the administration over the decision.

Broader Implications

* This action appears to be part of a pattern targeting international students. * One expert suggests Harvard may be a "test case" for influencing institutions. * International student applications to U.S. universities are declining. * International students contribute significantly to the U.S. economy (over $43 billion annually).

Expert Perspectives

* Sean Carver (UC Berkeley) noted this is potentially the most difficult time for international students. * Students are increasingly looking at alternatives in Europe, Asia, and Canada.

Supreme Court Ruling on Independent Agencies

* The Supreme Court ruled 6-3 that President Trump can fire two members of independent agencies. * The ruling came from the Court's emergency docket. * Liberal Justice Elena Kagan dissented, arguing the emergency docket shouldn't be used to revise existing law. * The case challenges the independence of federal agencies from political pressure.

Federal Reserve Exception

* The conservative majority created an exception for the Federal Reserve. * They described the Fed as a "uniquely structured quasi-private entity." * Kagan argued the Fed's independence is fundamentally similar to other independent agencies.

Potential Implications

* The order suggests how the Court might rule if/when they fully hear the case. * There are concerns about potential political interference with independent agencies. * The decision could impact the fundamental structure of federal regulatory bodies. * The Supreme Court has been gradually weakening protections for independent agencies. * The case stems from concerns about presidential power to fire agency leaders.
