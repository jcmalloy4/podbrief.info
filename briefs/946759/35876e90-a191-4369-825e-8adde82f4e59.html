Up First from NPR

One-on-one with Utah Governor Spencer Cox

Key Takeaways

  • Utah Governor Spencer Cox advocated for civility and against political polarization.
  • Cox's political brand combines conservative principles with pragmatic problem-solving.
  • He attributes loss of trust in government to declining community engagement.
  • The governor sees AI as a global competition but warns of societal risks.
  • Cox emphasizes forgiveness and faith as foundational to his public service.

Deep Dive

  • Utah Governor Spencer Cox discussed right-wing activist Charlie Kirk's assassination and its impact on national discourse.
  • He urged Americans to disengage from online conflict and engage in community.
  • Cox reported positive responses to his calls for depolarization, including personal anecdotes and international observations.

  • Governor Cox describes his political brand as conservative yet pragmatic, emphasizing listening to others and working together.
  • Following the assassination, he publicly aimed to send a message against demonizing entire groups, stressing individual accountability.
  • He noted that political violence is not exclusive to one side and emphasized the need for unity in condemning it.

  • Cox discussed the dangers of hyperbolic political rhetoric, where terms like "battle" and "war" can be misinterpreted, leading to real-world actions.
  • He attributed the loss of trust in government to a failure to deliver on promises, particularly concerning the cost of living and housing.
  • Cox noted a nascent 'abundance movement' emerging from discussions with left-leaning individuals and fellow governors, expressing optimism for bipartisan support for permitting and NEPA reform.

  • Governor Cox endorsed Donald Trump for 2024, despite not voting for him in previous elections.
  • His endorsement stemmed from Trump's expression of appreciation for Cox's call for civility and unity following the Charlie Kirk assassination attempt.
  • Cox noted Trump's view of political adversaries as enemies, contrasting it with Erica Kirk's forgiveness of the shooter, highlighting a fundamental difference in their worldviews.

  • Cox discussed the importance of forgiveness, even for enemies, stating it is a command within his faith and crucial for personal formation.
  • He stated his faith informs his politics, and while he believes in the separation of church and state, he does not separate his faith from his public service, aiming for transparency with voters.
  • As governor, he represents all constituents, seeking common ground and win-win solutions rather than viewing politics as a zero-sum game.

  • Governors at the Western Governors Association meeting are focusing on energy policy, including promoting energy for data centers supporting artificial intelligence.
  • Utah plans to build nuclear power plants, referencing the state's historical success in nuclear development during the 1950s and 1960s.
  • Cox expressed optimism that the U.S. can again lead in nuclear technology and that Utah can have new reactors within 12 years with federal help.
  • He acknowledges rapid AI expansion's significant energy needs and the potential for increased prices, warning of national security risks if the U.S. does not keep pace with countries like China and Russia.

  • Cox expressed concern about AI deployment, drawing parallels to the negative impacts of social media on mental health and societal division.
  • He noted that unlike past technological shifts affecting blue-collar jobs, AI is likely to disrupt white-collar professions, predicting significant societal upheaval.
  • Utah's approach to AI focuses on a human-flourishing model, requiring companies to improve human life, not just profit.
  • Utah established the first AI policy lab, employing experts to work with companies on product safety and legislation.

  • Governor Cox attributed unhappiness and unheard voices in the U.S. to a loss of connection, referencing Robert Putnam's book 'Bowling Alone' and a societal shift toward fewer friendships.
  • He observed a decline in community-based problem-solving, citing a decrease in service organizations like Lions and Rotary clubs, with government often becoming the default problem-solver.
  • Cox stated this decline in genuine connection leads to increased anxiety, depression, gambling, drug addiction, and pornography use, ultimately resulting in dangerous societal outcomes like extreme polarization.
