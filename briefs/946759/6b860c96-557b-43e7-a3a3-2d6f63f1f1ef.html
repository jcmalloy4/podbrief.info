Up First from NPR

The Families Hiding from ICE

Key Takeaways

  • Increased immigration enforcement under President Trump leads to widespread fear among undocumented families.
  • Families with undocumented members live in constant fear of detention and separation.
  • Fear of enforcement results in social isolation and disruption of children's lives and education.
  • Emergency guardianship arrangements are increasingly used as a legal precaution to protect children's futures.
  • Legal experts advise formal documentation for guardianship to prevent children from entering foster care.

Deep Dive

  • An undocumented mother, referred to as M, in Maryland takes precautions to protect her children from potential detention or deportation.
  • M and her husband, both from Guatemala, disagree on self-deportation; the husband considers leaving due to risks associated with his landscaping job.
  • The family's confinement and fear have led to anxiety for their children, with M's 7-year-old daughter experiencing panic attacks.

  • Latino immigrant families are living in heightened fear, illustrated by an incident where ICE agents appeared at a father's landscaping job.
  • Following the close call, one family isolated themselves for weeks, canceling summer activities and impacting a 12-year-old's planned STEM camp.
  • A parent explained to their U.S. citizen children that public places like aquariums or pools were off-limits due to deportation fears.

  • Professor Elora McKurji clarifies that while no confirmed ICE raids have occurred inside schools, previously protected sites are no longer explicitly off-limits for enforcement.
  • Immigration agents require a warrant to enter a school, and schools are not permitted to inquire about a student's immigration status.
  • One mother, Em, felt fear when seeing sheriff's deputies near her children's bus stop on the first day of school, rushing them home.

  • A mother, Em, established emergency guardianship with a U.S. citizen friend during her children's first week of school as a precaution against detention or deportation.
  • NPR correspondent Jasmine Garst notes that establishing guardianship offers parents a sense of security, control, and community support in uncertain situations.
  • The process allows parents to plan for their children's care should they be detained by ICE.

  • An American neighbor in Washington, D.C., agreed to be the temporary guardian for a 17-year-old U.S. citizen son of a Honduran immigrant family, prioritizing his education.
  • The Honduran father expressed profound sadness about the situation and fear for his son's future, despite the neighbor's agreement.
  • The neighbor's decision was not a question, due to their love for the family, who had been in the U.S. for 25 years.

  • NPR correspondent Jasmine Garsd reports a rising number of requests for guardianship arrangements among immigrant families.
  • Lawyers like Ginger Miranda of the Central Florida Hispanic Bar Association advise creating formal legal documents for guardianship and power of attorney.
  • These legal documents are crucial to prevent children from potentially entering the foster care system if parents are detained or deported.
