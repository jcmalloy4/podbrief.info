PDB Special Report | January 3, 2026: The Fall of Maduro: Inside the U.S. Operation That Toppled Venezuela’s Dictator

Key Takeaways

  • U.S. military operation Operation Absolute Resolve captured Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro in Caracas.
  • The operation involved extensive intelligence tracking, an aerial campaign, and a special forces raid lasting over two hours.
  • Maduro faces federal terrorism and drug trafficking charges in New York, with a court appearance anticipated.
  • International reactions to the capture were mixed, prompting diplomatic responses from several nations.
  • The U.S. administration aims to oversee Venezuela's transition, but powerful figures within the country still pose potential instability.

Deep Dive

  • Operation Absolute Resolve involved months of intelligence tracking and planning for Nicolás Maduro.
  • It commenced with a large-scale aerial bombing campaign utilizing approximately 150 aircraft targeting Venezuelan military facilities.
  • U.S. Special Forces conducted a raid on a fortified location in Caracas, capturing Maduro and his wife.
  • The entire operation, including extraction to the USS Iwo Jima, lasted approximately 2 hours and 20 minutes.

  • Nicolás Maduro and his wife are reportedly en route to New York to face federal charges including terrorism and drug trafficking, with a court appearance anticipated as early as Monday.
  • Venezuelan officials denounced the U.S. action as aggression, declaring a national emergency.
  • International reactions were mixed, with UN Secretary General Guterres expressing alarm and Mexico condemning the action, while Argentine President Javier Milei welcomed the capture.
  • President Trump stated the U.S. will oversee Venezuela's transition, aiming for a 'rich, independent, and safe' country, and indicated a willingness to deploy troops if necessary.

  • The U.S. administration faced criticism for not providing advance notice to Congress, a decision Secretary of State Rubio defended as a law enforcement action under extraordinary circumstances.
  • Three potential end states for Venezuela were outlined following an increased U.S. military posture, noting Maduro's attempt to negotiate his own departure failed due to unrealistic terms.
  • Analysis suggests Maduro's downfall resulted from direct U.S. intervention, but his absence does not automatically guarantee Venezuela's stability or democracy.
  • The speaker concludes this is a historic moment but marks the beginning of a complex phase, with coming days and weeks determining the transition's stability.

  • Key figures remaining in Venezuela include Defense Minister Vladimir Padrino Lopez, regime enforcer Diosdado Cabello, and the Rodriguez siblings controlling oil and propaganda, suggesting potential instability unless prior agreements were made.
  • The operation likely required inside help, possibly from recruited assets within Venezuela's security or government apparatus, given Maduro's increased paranoia and reliance on Cuban security personnel.
  • Maduro's removal will have significant ripple effects beyond Venezuela, particularly for Cuba, which relies on Venezuelan oil and economic support.
  • The operation also sends a message to other nations like Iran, with its leadership and the IRGC closely watching the outcome.
