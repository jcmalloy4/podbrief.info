The Ben Shapiro Show

Ep. 2324 - The Trump Administration CRACKS DOWN on Third World Migration

Key Takeaways

  • President Trump proposed halting "third world" immigration due to a D.C. attack and welfare fraud concerns.
  • An Afghan refugee who attacked National Guard members reportedly self-radicalized domestically post-arrival.
  • A widespread welfare fraud scandal in Minnesota involving the Somali community drew national political attention.
  • U.S. military actions near Venezuela targeting narco-terrorists sparked debate over their legality and potential war crimes.
  • Ukraine peace talks face complexities, including internal corruption allegations against top government officials.
  • Discussions covered "replacement theory" and the cultural impact of mass migration on Western civilization.

Deep Dive

  • President Trump announced plans to permanently halt immigration from "third world" countries following a D.C. attack and citing Somali welfare fraud in Minnesota.
  • Trump's proposed policy includes deporting non-assets, ending federal benefits for non-citizens, and denaturalizing or deporting migrants deemed a risk.
  • Homeland Security Secretary Kirstjen Nielsen affirmed the administration's commitment to pausing immigration for vetting purposes.
  • The State Department announced a pause on visa issuance for individuals traveling on Afghan passports after the attack, citing national security.

  • Rahmanullah Lakanwal, an Afghan refugee and former CIA 'zero unit' member, attacked National Guard members in D.C. after entering the U.S. in 2021 and being granted asylum.
  • Homeland Security stated Lakanwal self-radicalized domestically through local connections, rather than being radicalized upon arrival.
  • A community advocate warned a refugee organization in March 2023 about Lakanwal's deteriorating mental state and concerning behavior.
  • Senator Mark Kelly suggested re-vetting all 190,000 Afghans currently residing in the U.S.

  • The New York Times reported a large-scale welfare fraud scandal in Minnesota, with dozens charged for stealing hundreds of millions from a child nutrition program, totaling over $1 billion across three plots.
  • Public outrage intensified, leading President Trump to call Minnesota a hub for fraudulent money laundering and suggest deportation for Somali perpetrators.
  • Officials initially hesitated to investigate due to fears of appearing racist, with the nonprofit 'Feeding Our Future' threatening lawsuits based on racism claims.
  • Data indicates 140,000 to 167,000 Somali immigrants have settled in the U.S. since 1990, with Minnesota hosting the largest community.

  • The host linked the Minnesota welfare fraud to cultural differences and migration, suggesting large sums are stolen by malign actors, comparing it to the Rotherham scandal in the UK.
  • Officials at the Minnesota Department of Education continued disbursing funds despite early pandemic red flags, facing racism accusations when questioning invoices from 'Feeding Our Future'.
  • Kaisa Magan, a former fraud investigator, stated state officials were reluctant to take strong action on allegations within the Somali community.
  • President Trump commented on "third world" migration in Minneapolis, suggesting expelling Rep. Ilhan Omar and criticizing Governor Tim Walz.

  • A speaker discussed "replacement theory," arguing that mass migration inherently changes a country's cultural fabric and that some immigrants may hold beliefs conflicting with Western civilization.
  • The host connected recent events, including the D.C. attack and Minnesota incidents, to broader concerns about "third world migration" and domestic radicalization.
  • These issues were framed as existential problems for Western civilization, with the speaker differentiating between people and ideologies as "cancers."

  • Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth faced scrutiny over a Washington Post report alleging he ordered a 'kill everybody' directive on a suspected drug boat, leading to a second strike that killed two survivors.
  • Hegseth denounced the reporting as fabricated, asserting U.S. operations are lawful lethal kinetic strikes against narco-terrorists, supported by military and civilian lawyers.
  • Andrew McCarthy of National Review argued that, if reported accurately, the actions could constitute a war crime under federal law, as drug trafficking is a crime, not an act of war.
  • Former military lawyers consider orders denying quarter or using lethal force against those unable to fight as war crimes.

  • The Trump administration is pressuring Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro to step down, reportedly issuing an ultimatum demanding his resignation.
  • The U.S. responded by closing airspace and deploying the USS Gerald F. Ford and a Marine expeditionary unit offshore.
  • Senator Dave McCormick highlighted the flow of drugs, including fentanyl, from Venezuela, contributing to American deaths.
  • Venezuelan officials accuse the U.S. of colonial aggression and seeking oil reserves amidst the crisis.

  • U.S. and Ukrainian officials held talks in Florida regarding the Ukraine war, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio emphasizing the need for a peace deal ensuring Ukraine's independence and sovereignty.
  • A proposed peace plan, initially seen as Russian demands, was reportedly revised after intervention from Senator Rubio and Jared Kushner.
  • Andrei Yermak, a key Ukrainian peace negotiator, reportedly resigned following anti-corruption raids on his home, highlighting long-standing corruption within the Ukrainian government.
  • Former Ukrainian Foreign Minister Dmitry Kuleba criticized a U.S. peace plan, likening it to Russian ultimatums and questioning pressure on Ukraine to concede.
