The Ben Shapiro Show

How Gerrymandering is DESTROYING America!

Key Takeaways

  • Gerrymandering distorts representation by manipulating congressional district lines based on census data.
  • Supreme Court rulings mandated equal population districts and allowed judicial oversight to prevent racial gerrymandering.
  • Counting illegal immigrants in the census over-inflates representation for specific states, sparking legal debate.

Deep Dives

Redistricting Basics

  • Congressional districts are constitutionally based on U.S. Census state populations; gerrymandering, named after Elbridge Gerry, manipulates these lines to overwhelmingly favor one political party.
  • Before 1964, congressional districts often had unequal populations, which changed with the Supreme Court's "one-man, one-vote" principle, requiring regular redrawing.

Judicial Oversight

  • The Supreme Court's 1960s rulings enabled judicial review of district drawing, specifically targeting racial discrimination that historically diluted Black voting power in areas like the Jim Crow South.
  • While federal pre-clearance for redistricting was removed in 2013, courts continue to oversee racial gerrymandering but typically avoid political gerrymandering as a partisan issue.

Census Controversies

  • The accuracy of the 2020 census and the inclusion of illegal immigrants directly impact congressional representation, potentially shifting power to states with large undocumented populations.
  • Courts have historically included illegal immigrants in census counts, a practice contested by those who argue for counting only citizens to adhere to the "one man, one vote" principle.
