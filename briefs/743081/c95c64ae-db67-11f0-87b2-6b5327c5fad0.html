The Ben Shapiro Show

Ep. 2336 - How White Men Became The Targets Of America’s Most Powerful Institutions

Key Takeaways

  • A Compact Magazine report details decreased white male representation across various industries due to DEI initiatives.
  • Trump's chief of staff, Susie Wiles, criticized a Vanity Fair profile as a "disingenuously framed hit piece."
  • Obamacare subsidies face expiration in 2025, potentially doubling premiums and creating a political dilemma for Republicans.
  • The U.S. government has initiated "Manhattan Project 2.0" to bolster AI development as a national security priority.

Deep Dive

  • A Compact Magazine report indicates Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) initiatives, institutionalized around 2014, significantly decreased the percentage of white men in various industries, including TV writing and academic positions.
  • In professional American industries, particularly journalism, white male millennials have been systematically disadvantaged by DEI initiatives since the mid-2010s; major news outlets like The New York Times prioritized hires of women and people of color.
  • At Harvard, white men in tenure-track arts and sciences positions decreased from 49% to 27% between 2014 and 2024; in humanities, it dropped from 39% to 21%.
  • Berkeley's white male hires fell from 52.7% in 2015 to 21.5% in 2023, partly due to initiatives to reduce white male job offers.
  • Internal documents from a major talent agency in 2017 revealed mandates for 'female' or 'diverse' staffing in TV writers' rooms, impacting shows like Chicago Fire, creating a 'glass ceiling' for entry-level white males.

  • The host contended that the push for diversity across media, higher education, and Hollywood created a zero-sum game, leading to 'reverse identitarianism' on the political right.
  • Younger generations were reportedly taught colorblind meritocracy but then told it did not apply to them due to immutable characteristics, leading to a perception of being an oppressed group.
  • The host argued that returning to a colorblind meritocracy is the only way to escape the current identitarian moment, on both the left and the right.

  • A Vanity Fair profile of Susie Wiles, Trump's chief of staff, was discussed, with the host questioning the decision to grant the interview.
  • Wiles later called the article a 'disingenuously framed hit piece' that omitted positive context.
  • White House Press Secretary Caroline Lovitt dismissed the article as 'fake news' and 'bias of omission.'
  • President Trump defended Wiles, suggesting the interviewer may have been disingenuous.

  • The expiration of Obamacare subsidies at the end of 2025 could cause average out-of-pocket premiums for subsidized enrollees to more than double, increasing by approximately 110-115%.
  • KFF estimates this would raise annual payments from about $900 to $1,900.
  • Insurers are also proposing median premium hikes of 18-26% for ACA plans in 2026.
  • Representative Mike Lawler expressed frustration with Republicans, calling it 'idiotic' and 'political malpractice' not to vote on extending subsidies.

  • House Speaker Mike Johnson stated there would be no vote on ACA subsidies, despite calls from some Republicans like Mike Lawler to extend them.
  • Republicans have proposed a plan aimed at lowering healthcare costs by 11% through increased transparency and changes to insurance pools.
  • The host argues that while the Republican plan might be better on policy than Democratic proposals, it may not be enough to offset the political fallout from rising Obamacare premiums.
  • Representative Chip Roy criticized the Republican plan as 'milquetoast garbage' for not addressing healthcare costs more aggressively.

  • President Trump is expected to issue an executive order to reclassify marijuana from Schedule I to Schedule III.
  • The host expressed strong disapproval, viewing the move as prioritizing short-term political gain over potential detriment to young people.
  • The reclassification could enable more research into marijuana.

  • An investigation is ongoing into a shooting at Brown University, with police releasing videos of a suspect seen pacing through residential streets near campus.
  • Suspicion has arisen that the Brown University shooting may be linked to Jewishness, as the professor leading the study group was the head of the Judaic Studies program.
  • A nuclear scientist at MIT, Nunal Lorero, was shot and killed in his Brookline, Massachusetts home, with circumstances still under examination and no suspect or motive identified.
  • In Crown Heights, Brooklyn, a Jewish man was stabbed while attackers reportedly expressed anti-Semitic sentiments.

  • Cabot Phillips of Morning Wire explained "Manhattan Project 2.0," a U.S. initiative to bolster AI development as a national security priority, comparing it to the space race.
  • The project involves federal government funding, connecting 40,000 scientists at national labs, and creating a coordinated AI platform with government access to vast databases.
  • Smaller AI companies expressed concern that larger companies with more resources may disproportionately benefit from new regulations and government support.
  • Trump's administration aims to streamline AI regulations at the state level, threatening federal funding for states that enact 'onerous AI laws.'
