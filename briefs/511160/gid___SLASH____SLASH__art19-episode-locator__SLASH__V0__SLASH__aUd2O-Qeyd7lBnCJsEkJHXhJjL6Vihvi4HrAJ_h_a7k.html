Build Muscle Without the B.S. — A Straightforward Guide to Size and Strength

Key Takeaways

  • Building strength and muscle requires a phased approach, prioritizing foundational strength before focusing on leanness.
  • Novice lifters benefit from simple, consistent barbell programs and linear progression to build a solid foundation.
  • Advanced training can blend powerlifting principles for the lower body with bodybuilding techniques for the upper body to optimize aesthetics and joint health.
  • Achieving a lean body fat percentage, such as 10%, at least once helps understand the cutting process and improves future bulking efficiency.

Deep Dive

  • Paul Horn began training as a vegan, weighing 160 pounds, before discovering weightlifting.
  • His initial approach, following popular bodybuilding advice, led to injuries, including shoulder surgery.
  • Horn's understanding of barbell training profoundly changed after discovering Mark Rippetoe's book 'Starting Strength', leading him to pursue coaching.

  • New lifters aiming for muscle and strength should prioritize building a foundation of general strength and size using basic barbell lifts.
  • The 'Starting Strength' program is an effective novice program, focusing on four key lifts (deadlift, squat, bench press, shoulder press) performed three times a week.
  • Workouts are structured efficiently, typically lasting 30-45 minutes for beginners, and linear progression, adding weight gradually, provides crucial motivation.

  • Benchmark strength goals include a 1-rep max of 315 lbs for squat, 225 lbs for bench press, 405 lbs for deadlift, and 135 lbs for overhead press.
  • A secondary goal involves achieving these same weights for five repetitions, with 405 lbs deadlift for five reps serving as a phase two objective.
  • Nutrition is crucial, with a recommended daily protein intake of 200 grams for most individuals, and a weekly weight gain goal of one pound during bulking to avoid excessive fat accumulation.

  • Strategies for novice lifters vary by body type: underweight individuals aim to gain 20 pounds, while those untrained with some body fat target a slow 10-pound gain.
  • Overweight novices should maintain weight and focus on recomposition, which is the simultaneous gain of muscle and loss of fat.
  • Recomposition is primarily possible for brand new lifters, individuals carrying significant body fat, or those using performance-enhancing drugs.

  • For those past baseline strength, an 'Intermediate B' program involves four workouts spread over a three-day week, utilizing upper/lower body splits.
  • A significant modification from novice linear progression is the introduction of varied rep ranges, typically 3-5 reps, to stimulate muscle growth.
  • This shift to flexible rep ranges helps prevent burnout and maintains enjoyment in training, leading to better adherence.

  • A four-day training split for established trainees recommends training the lower body like a powerlifter, focusing on compound lifts such as squats and deadlifts.
  • The upper body is trained like a bodybuilder, incorporating more isolation exercises to emphasize specific features and reduce joint stress.
  • This approach is attributed to the greater structural integrity of lower body joints compared to the more sensitive upper body joints, like the shoulder.

  • Achieving 'arm vein leanness' is suggested as a visible indicator of fitness, often preferred over abs for those wearing shirts.
  • The primary cutting goal recommended is reaching 10% body fat at least once to learn the process, which allows for visible abs and veins and improves nutrient partitioning for subsequent bulking.
  • The strategy involves cycling between 10% and 15% body fat, with 15% still considered athletic, and occasional cuts to 10-12% for specific events.
