The Glenn Beck Program

Best of the Program | Guest: Stephen Moore | 11/4/25

Key Takeaways

  • The government shutdown is significantly stressing the repo market and broader economic liquidity.
  • New York is experiencing a substantial exodus of citizens and capital due to high taxes.
  • Financial centers are shifting, with Texas emerging as a competitor to traditional hubs like New York.
  • Former Vice President Dick Cheney's post-9/11 leadership led to expanded government powers and controversial policies.

Deep Dive

  • The government shutdown is stressing the repo market, indicating a cash shortage likened to a body starving for oxygen.
  • The Treasury General Account, holding nearly $1 trillion, is drawing essential liquidity out of circulation, causing systemic strain.
  • Reduced cash flow, tightening credit, and increasing repo rates are impacting smaller businesses and consumers.

  • New York has lost 2.5 million people over the last 10 years, according to data from 'Vote With Your Feet.'
  • This migration includes individuals taking their income with them, which impacts the state's tax base.
  • Further tax increases in New York City are predicted to exacerbate this trend of citizens leaving.

  • The creation of the Texas Stock Exchange challenges New York's historical financial dominance.
  • Billionaire Ken Griffin's move from Chicago to Palm Beach, Florida, created a $50 million hole in Illinois' budget.
  • Dallas, Texas, now has more financial services jobs than New York City, signaling a shift from traditional Wall Street.

  • Florida has seen over $1 trillion in income over a decade due to people relocating from other states.
  • This trend is described as the largest mass migration in U.S. history, driven by economic and quality-of-life factors.
  • California is experiencing a similar outflow of residents, with many moving to Texas.
  • The host expresses concern over potential federal bailouts for fiscally struggling states like New York and California.

  • Former Vice President Dick Cheney provided steady leadership in the immediate aftermath of the September 11, 2001, attacks.
  • This period saw an expansion of government powers, including the Patriot Act and the creation of the Department of Homeland Security.
  • These actions, while potentially well-intentioned, are noted as a departure from conservative principles.

  • Cheney's legacy includes the unchecked power granted by the Patriot Act and FISA courts.
  • The controversial practice of 'enhanced interrogation,' also known as torture, was part of this era.
  • The host argues these actions carry a heavy price and offer critical lessons for the nation.
