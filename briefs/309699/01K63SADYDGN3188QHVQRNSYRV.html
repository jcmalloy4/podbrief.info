Consumer spending outpaced income — again

Key Takeaways

  • U.S. consumers are maintaining spending by drawing down savings and increasing credit use amid tariff-induced price hikes.
  • Ongoing ICE raids in Los Angeles are creating fear among immigrant workers and causing economic uncertainty for local businesses.
  • A dispute has emerged over which organization will manage the U.S. Olympic surfing team for the LA 2028 Games.
  • Artist Jake Longstreth's work captures the changing landscape of brick-and-mortar retail in the age of e-commerce.

Deep Dive

  • The August personal consumption expenditures price index showed overall inflation at 2.7% and core inflation at 2.9% year-over-year.
  • Rachel Siegel and Amera Mokwe discussed the absorption of tariff-induced price increases into the economy.
  • Companies are managing costs and future pricing amidst ongoing economic uncertainty.

  • September consumer sentiment neared record lows due to high prices and job market concerns, despite revised GDP figures indicating resilient spring quarter consumer activity.
  • Wealth growth among higher-income individuals supports continued spending, masking underlying economic anxieties for lower-income households.
  • For the third consecutive month in August, U.S. personal spending exceeded income, leading to reduced savings and increased credit card reliance.
  • Economists note that while consumers generally remain resilient, those at lower to median income levels face increasing pressure from a softening labor market and rising prices.
  • Financing consumption through savings depletion and increased credit card use is highlighted as an unsustainable, temporary measure, especially with a slowing labor market.

  • Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) raids are impacting Los Angeles businesses, with a 12% dip in non-citizen private sector employment in California following summer raids.
  • Workers at LA businesses, such as a local car wash, are experiencing fear and anxiety due to ongoing ICE raids, affecting their ability to work and financial stability.
  • Community organizers are offering 'know your rights' training to workers and employers in Los Angeles, teaching them how to respond during ICE raids, including protocols for private property.
  • Small businesses in Los Angeles are bracing for the economic impact of ongoing ICE raids, which threaten tax revenue and jobs.

  • The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 299 points (2/3%) to 46,247, the NASDAQ added 99 points (over 4/10%) to 22,484, and the S&P 500 gained 38 points (6/10%) to 6,643.
  • For the week, the Dow fell over 0.1%, the NASDAQ dropped 0.66%, and the S&P 500 subtracted 0.3%.
  • Electronic Arts, the video game maker behind Madden NFL and FIFA, is reportedly the subject of a $50 billion leveraged buyout.
  • The yield on the ten-year T-note rose to 4.17%.

  • Visual artist Jake Longstreth paints landscapes of the built environment, focusing on retail chains like Walmart and Taco Bell.
  • Longstreth discusses how the meaning of his paintings of boarded-up big box stores has evolved, reflecting the shift from 'brick and mortar' retail to the e-commerce era.
  • He notes that he finds Amazon warehouses too 'brutal' to paint directly, preferring places not typically considered picturesque.
  • Longstreth's art aims to awaken new perceptions in viewers, with people recognizing his style in everyday settings like a Toys R Us parking lot.

  • A conflict has emerged regarding which organization will manage the surfing competition for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympic Games.
  • US Ski and Snowboard is attempting to take over Olympic surfing management, aiming to provide athlete support and branding.
  • The CEO of US Ski and Snowboard believes adding surfing will allow investment into grassroots and elite athletes, enhancing marketing.
  • USA Surfing, a nonprofit that previously managed the team, is mounting a competing bid, arguing against US Ski and Snowboard's potential diversion of millions in commercial rights and direct funding.
  • USA Surfing, which helped get surfing into the Olympics, claims to have resolved past financial issues and seeks to capitalize on the sport's home-turf spotlight.
